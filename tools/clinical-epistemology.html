The Question

Most knowledge systems ask: what is true?

This system asks: what can I trace, what have I felt, and what am I responsible for claiming?

Somnistics Research Labs is a clinical neuroscience company building autonomic regulation technology for high-stakes professionals. At its foundation is a living knowledge graph — a vault of 146 interconnected concepts, 80+ evidence notes, and 50+ clinical observations, organized by library science standards and governed by the nursing process.

This document describes the philosophy that structures that knowledge. Not the technology. Not the product. The way of knowing that makes both possible.

Three Philosophical Commitments

1

The Body Is Evidence

What a CRNA senses after 28 years of clinical practice is a form of knowledge — not anecdote, not intuition dismissed. Embodied pattern recognition with its own evidentiary status, sitting right next to PubMed citations in the same graph.

2

Provenance Is the Epistemology

Every claim carries a chain of custody. Knowledge without provenance is assertion. The origin of a claim determines how much weight it can bear. What Randy originated, what AI proposed, and what the literature established are tracked separately — because they carry different authority.

3

Knowledge Is Something You Take Care Of

The nursing process (ADPIE) isn't a metaphor bolted onto the system. It IS the system's operating logic. A concept enters as a draft (assessment), gets validated (diagnosis), sequenced (planning), created (implementation), and reviewed (evaluation). Knowledge work and clinical work share the same structure of care.

1. The Body Is Evidence

The vault has an observations layer — Layer 0.5, sitting between raw evidence and abstracted concepts. This is philosophically radical.

It says: what a clinician senses in the operating room — the felt shift in a patient's autonomic tone before the monitors alarm, the somatic recognition that a case is about to change — is a form of knowledge. Not reducible to the peer-reviewed literature. Not dismissible as "just intuition." A distinct epistemic category with its own validity criteria.

The Neuroscience of Embodied Knowing

This commitment stands on decades of research demonstrating that the body is not merely the brain's vehicle — it is an instrument of cognition.

A.D. (Bud) Craig
The anterior insular cortex integrates homeostatic signals into subjective feelings — constructing a "global emotional moment" that underpins self-awareness. Feeling IS knowing.
Craig, A.D. (2009). How do you feel — now? The anterior insula and human awareness. Nature Reviews Neuroscience.
Antonio Damasio
The somatic marker hypothesis: bodily signals tag decision options with affective valence, enabling rapid evaluation under uncertainty. The body reasons before the mind deliberates.
Damasio, A.R. (1994). Descartes' Error: Emotion, Reason, and the Human Brain.
Stephen Porges
Polyvagal Theory and neuroception: the autonomic nervous system evaluates environmental safety pre-consciously via interoceptive signals. The body detects threat before awareness catches up.
Porges, S.W. (2004). Neuroception: A subconscious system for detecting threat and safety. Zero to Three.
Sarah Garfinkel
The interoceptive trichotomy: accuracy (objective), sensibility (self-reported), and awareness (metacognitive calibration). Three distinct dimensions of bodily knowing, each measurable.
Garfinkel, S.N., et al. (2015). Knowing your own heart. Biological Psychology.
Wolf Mehling
The MAIA framework: eight subscales for measuring interoceptive awareness, bridging first-person phenomenology with empirical rigor.
Mehling, W.E., et al. (2012). Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness (MAIA). PLOS ONE.
Michael Polanyi
"We know more than we can tell." Tacit knowledge — the unarticulated expertise honed through practice — is not lesser knowledge. It is the foundation on which explicit knowledge rests.
Polanyi, M. (1958). Personal Knowledge: Towards a Post-Critical Philosophy.
The architectural implication: The SRL vault gives embodied knowledge a legitimate address in the knowledge graph. Observations sit at Layer 0.5 — between evidence (Layer 0) and concepts (Layer 1). A clinician's felt sense of cardiac drift has structural parity with a PubMed meta-analysis. Both are knowledge. They carry different provenance, different authority, different validation criteria — but neither is dismissed.

What This Looks Like in Practice

When a CRNA notices their heart rate drop during a silent 60-second Pausality session between cases — that's an observation. When a founder measures joules per heartbeat on a Peloton bike and recognizes it as a cardiac coupling efficiency index — that's an observation. When a 28-year veteran senses the OR team's collective autonomic state shift before a code — that's an observation.

These don't need a DOI to be real. They need a provenance chain, a clinical context, and the clinician's own judgment about what they mean.

2. Provenance Is the Epistemology

Every node in the knowledge graph knows where it came from, what it stands on, and who said it.

This is not metadata hygiene. It is a philosophical commitment: the origin of a claim determines how much weight it can bear.

Three Provenance Classes

Class Source Authority Validation
Randy-Originated 28 years of clinical practice, embodied expertise, original concept synthesis Clinical authority — the knowledge of a practitioner who has lived inside the domain Clinical interpretation fields, peer review by domain experts, SRB-60 assessment
Literature-Established Peer-reviewed studies, PubMed-verified, DOI-tracked Scientific authority — replicated, falsifiable, consensus-tested Citation verification, DOI validation, systematic review status
AI-Synthesized LLM-generated frameworks, cross-domain synthesis, pattern proposals Computational authority — useful for pattern detection, dangerous if unchecked PubMed cross-reference, domain expert review, never sole basis for clinical claims
Why this matters: A concept Randy coined from clinical experience carries different authority than a concept an LLM synthesized from training data. Both can be valid. Neither should be anonymous. The vault tracks provenance so that every claim can be interrogated: who said this, from what experience, and on what evidence?

Standards That Enforce Provenance

Dublin Core
Metadata Standard
Creator, date, source, subject on every note. Who made this, when, from what.
SKOS
Simple Knowledge Organization
Broader, narrower, related. How concepts connect to each other in the graph.
PROV-O
Provenance Ontology
wasDerivedFrom, reviewedBy. The chain of custody from evidence to claim.

These aren't academic exercises. They're the infrastructure that makes the vault trustworthy. When an investor asks "what's your evidence for this claim?" or a clinician asks "where does this protocol come from?" — the vault can answer with a traceable chain from assertion back to source.

The git history reads like a lab notebook because it IS one. Every commit tells the story of what was learned, extracted, validated, or wired. Six months from now, git log --oneline should explain exactly what happened and why.

3. Knowledge Is Something You Take Care Of

The nursing process — ADPIE — governs every operation in the vault. Not as metaphor. As method.

A
Assessment
Read extraction reports. Load schema. Check for existing duplicates. Gather the data before acting.
D
Diagnosis
Validate candidates against quality gates. Identify conflicts, gaps, and schema violations.
P
Planning
Sequence writes. Resolve dependencies. Concepts before relationships. Evidence before claims.
I
Implementation
Create notes via MCP tools. Wire SKOS relationships. Add evidence links. Log every action.
E
Evaluation
Verify schema compliance. Check audit trail. Promote through quality gates: draft → review → canonical.

This is a philosophical claim: knowledge work and clinical work share the same structure of care. You don't skip steps. You don't chart what you haven't assessed. You don't promote what you haven't evaluated. The same discipline that keeps patients safe keeps knowledge honest.

The clinical_interpretation field: Every concept note has a field reserved for the clinician's own judgment. AI bots write "Pending review." Only Randy fills this field. This is the structural guarantee that embodied knowledge has the final word — not the algorithm, not the literature, but the practitioner who has lived inside the domain for 28 years.

The Vault Architecture

Five epistemic layers, each with distinct validation criteria and authority.

Layer 0
Evidence
PubMed-verified studies, books, patents. DOI-tracked. The empirical foundation.
Layer 0.5
Observations
Embodied clinical knowledge. Pattern recognition. Craft knowledge. The body's data.
Layer 1
Concepts
SKOS-linked atomic ideas. One concept per note. Richly connected. 146 and growing.
Layer 2
Audiences
Who you're speaking to. Evidence depth + language register tailored per audience.
Layer 3
Outputs
Generated artifacts: NeuroMinutes, CE courses, decks, proposals, research packages.

Each layer builds on and transforms the one below it. Evidence becomes observations through clinical experience. Observations become concepts through synthesis. Concepts become outputs through audience translation. And every transformation preserves the provenance chain back to its source.

Epistemic layering: This is what distinguishes a knowledge graph from a database. A database stores facts. A layered epistemic system transforms facts through progressive abstraction into actionable knowledge — while maintaining provenance chains back to the original evidence. Every output can answer: "Show me the evidence chain that supports this claim."

The Closed Loop: From Body to Vault to Body

The system doesn't just store knowledge. It produces knowledge through a feedback loop between embodied practice and structured analysis.

The CRNA practices a 60-second breathing protocol silently between cases. Their heart rate drops measurably. That observation enters the vault. It gets cross-referenced against the dose-response literature (You & Laborde 2021: five minutes produces the same vagal activation as twenty). The concept of Five-Breath Re-Embodiment is strengthened. The protocol is refined. The clinician practices again with the refined protocol. The loop continues.

This is not theory about practice. It is practice that generates theory that improves practice.

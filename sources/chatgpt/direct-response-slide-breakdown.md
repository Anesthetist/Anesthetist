Deep dive  
1) The 7-part slide, what each part is really doing  
1. Headline (80% of work)  
This is the cognitive gate. If the headline does not instantly communicate “what changes for me,” the rest of the slide is wasted. A useful headline usually contains: outcome, timeframe, and a “new mechanism” or differentiator. The point is not cleverness; it’s comprehension.

2. Problem (quantify it)  
This is where you earn attention from serious readers. “Quantify it” means you name a measurable baseline: prevalence, frequency, cost, time loss, error rate, conversion drop, churn, missed KPI, etc. Quantification can be external (market data) or internal (your observed baseline). Even one solid number beats five adjectives.

3. Agitate (make vivid)  
This is not melodrama; it’s consequence mapping. You take the quantified problem and show what it feels like in lived experience and what it cascades into (second-order effects). This is where people recognize themselves and stop skimming.

4. Credibility  
Why should they believe you specifically? This can be authority (credentials), proximity (you live the problem), process (how you built/validated), or social proof (users, logos, outcomes, testimonials). The key is relevance to the claim you’re making in the headline.

5. Solution (transform)  
Name the transformation in plain language: “We turn X into Y.” If it’s a product, keep it conceptual first (the shift), then optionally add the mechanism (how you cause the shift).

6. Proof (specific)  
Proof is not “trust me.” It’s concrete evidence: numbers, before/after, a chart, a short case vignette, a quote with context, an experiment result, or even a crisp “what happened” story with metrics. The slide’s last section (“Specificity”) is basically a reminder that proof without numbers is weak proof.

7. CTA (benefit)  
This is the action you want them to take, framed as what they get (not what you want). Good CTAs reduce friction and increase clarity: what happens after they click, how long it takes, what they’ll walk away with.

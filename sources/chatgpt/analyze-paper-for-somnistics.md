## 1. High‑level takeaways (TL;DR)

- **Across cultures, the “regulatory” scales (Self‑Regulation, Body Listening, Trusting, Attention Regulation)** are the most stable, most central to the general interoception factor, and are the ones that reliably shift with effective mind–body/PTSD treatments.citeturn13view0turn11view0turn6view0  

- **The “symptom” scales (Noticing, Not‑Distracting, Not‑Worrying)** are psychometrically messy and culturally unstable; Not‑Worrying in particular often behaves more like a generic anxiety/neuroticism factor than interoception per se.citeturn7view1turn13view0  

- **In PTSD, the consistent interoceptive deficits are not about “can you feel your body?”** but about **how you appraise and use sensations to regulate affect**—exactly the domains of Self‑Regulation and Body Listening (and secondarily Attention Regulation, Trusting). These are the MAIA nodes that improve in yoga/exercise/PTSD trials.citeturn11view0turn5search0turn5search2turn5search10  

- **Dissociation & hyperarousal show opposite interoceptive patterns.** A subset of PTSD (15–30%) shows emotional over‑modulation with *diminished* interoceptive awareness and insula activation, while others show hyper‑insular activation and overwhelming sensations.citeturn11view0turn10view0  MAIA doesn’t fully resolve these subtypes.

- **Key holes for “solving PTSD”:**  
  1. MAIA barely touches **threat tagging of sensations** (which internal cues are wired to danger).  
  2. It’s largely **trait + self‑report**, not moment‑to‑moment or physiology‑linked.  
  3. It doesn’t encode **dissociation/hypoarousal** or **cultural idioms of distress** well.  
  4. It conflates “not worrying / not distracting” with health, even though **strategic distraction and concern can be adaptive** in early trauma recovery.

These holes are where Somnistics can build IP: dynamic, physiological, culturally‑sensitive “interoceptive regulation indices” that sit downstream of breath‑based one‑minute interventions.

## 2. Quick refresher: what MAIA actually measures

Original MAIA / MAIA‑2 gives eight subscales:citeturn7view1turn11view0turn14view0  

1. **Noticing** – “Do you notice sensations?” (valence‑neutral)
2. **Not‑Distracting** – “Do you *refrain* from distracting/ignoring discomfort?”
3. **Not‑Worrying** – “Do you *refrain* from worrying/ catastrophizing about discomfort?”
4. **Attention Regulation** – “Can you voluntarily sustain and shift attention to bodily sensations?”
5. **Emotional Awareness** – “Do you recognise links between body sensations and emotions?”
6. **Self‑Regulation** – “Can you use body attention to calm or regulate distress?”
7. **Body Listening** – “Do you listen to your body for insight and guidance?”
8. **Trusting** – “Does your body feel like a safe, trustworthy place to you?”

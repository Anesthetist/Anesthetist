## Reframing Burnout: It’s Not Psychological, It’s Physiological

Burnout is often described as emotional exhaustion or loss of meaning. Those are real experiences—but underneath them is something more basic:

**Interoceptive dysregulation.**

### What is interoception?

In Anatomy & Physiology terms:

**Interoception** is your brain’s ability to sense and interpret signals from inside your body.

These signals come from:
• heart rate and blood pressure  
• breathing and CO₂ levels  
• gut stretch and visceral sensation  
• temperature and internal tension  

They are carried largely through afferent pathways of the **vagus nerve**, spinal afferents, and brainstem nuclei to regions like the **insula** and **anterior cingulate cortex**.

Your brain uses these signals to answer one continuous question:
> “Am I safe enough to think clearly right now?”

### Interoceptive dysregulation and burnout

Research from groups such as **Khalsa and colleagues (NIMH / Laureate Institute for Brain Research)** shows that when interoceptive signals become noisy, blunted, or chronically biased toward threat, people experience:
• emotional exhaustion  
• anxiety or numbness  
• impaired focus  
• distorted threat perception  

In other words:  
**Burnout is what it feels like when your brain no longer trusts your body’s signals.**

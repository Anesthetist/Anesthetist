## 2. “Uncovering Demand” in plain language

**Uncovering Demand = reconstructing the movie of their life *without* your product.**

You’re only interested in three things:

1. **Struggles** – What is hard, costly, painful?
2. **Context** – When/where does it happen? With whom? Under what constraints?
3. **Outcomes** – What would “actually better” look and feel like *for them*?

No features. No benefits. No “we could…”. Just their lived world.

---

## 3. The 3–layer Demand Map (use this with Linnea, CRNAs, anyone)

Think of this as a one-page card you keep in your head.

### Layer 1 – Struggles (HEAD + HEART + GUT)

You’re mapping friction.

- “Walk me through a **typical day** with your autistic patients.  
  Where do things feel most *fraught* or *delicate* for you?”
- “In the last month, what’s a moment that **stuck with you** because it was so hard on you (or them)?”
- “When you go home after a rough day, what are the **scenes that replay**?”

You’re listening for:
- Overwhelm, shutdown, meltdowns
- Your counterpart’s felt sense: helplessness, vigilance, dread, guilt

### Layer 2 – Context

Anchor the struggle in time, place, and constraints.

- “When those moments happen, **when** in the visit does it tend to be?  
  Intake? Transition? Procedure? Leaving?”
- “What’s usually happening in the **room**—noise, lighting, parent state, time pressure?”
- “What’s the **clock** doing? Is this happening with 10 minutes left, or 90?”

You’re building a *3D map* of their world: system pressure, sensory environment, relationships.

### Layer 3 – Outcomes

Now we touch what “better” means—without offering how.

- “If we could magically change *only one thing* about those moments, what would be different?”
- “How would **you** know—somatically—that today went better?”
- “If your patients’ parents could describe their child’s appointments in one word,  
  what word do you *wish* they used?”

You’re trying to surface:
- Desired emotional state (for patient, parent, clinician)
- Behavioral correlates (less avoidance, more cooperation, fewer blow-ups)
- System metrics (fewer cancellations, shorter visits, less staff turnover)

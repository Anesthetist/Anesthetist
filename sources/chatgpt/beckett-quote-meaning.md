Beckett’s line — *“Play and lose, and have done with losing”* — is concise but packed. In practical terms, it’s about **cutting cleanly through cycles of failure, hesitation, or regret**.

### Meaning (Plain & Practical)
- **"Play"** → Engage, take your shot, step into the game.
- **"Lose"** → Accept the outcome if it doesn’t go your way, without clinging to it.
- **"Have done with losing"** → Don’t stew in it. Don’t keep replaying the failure. Once the loss is experienced, let it go and move forward.

### Practical Application
- **Decision-making**: If you hesitate too long, you stay in limbo. Better to commit, risk loss, and clear the slate than live in avoidance.
- **Business/Startups**: Launch the product, test the feature, run the experiment. If it fails, you’ve “done with losing” — you have information and can iterate. Not shipping is worse than losing.
- **Personal/Performance**: In stress-heavy fields (like anesthesia or leadership), clinging to errors corrodes performance. Acknowledge the miss, reset, and move into the next action.
- **Life Practice**: It’s about cultivating resilience: fail fast, integrate the lesson, and drop the emotional residue.

It’s a call to **experience loss fully and briefly**, then free yourself from it — not carry it around as identity or inertia.

# 1. What Chin Means by “Operations”
Chin defines *operations* as:  

> “The repeatable actions that turn strategy into outcomes.”  

It’s the **plumbing of execution**:  
- Making decisions faster.  
- Creating feedback loops.  
- Avoiding brittle one-off hacks.  
- Turning chaos into processes *without killing adaptability*.  

💡 Key idea: **Good ops make success more likely but never guarantee it.**  

---

# 2. Core Concepts to Learn
Here are the main takeaways from Chin’s Operations essays:

### a) **The OODA Loop at Org Level**  
- Observe → Orient → Decide → Act.  
- Most orgs are too slow at this.  
- Ops = designing systems so your OODA is tighter than competitors’.  

### b) **Tacit Knowledge in Ops**  
- A lot of operations can’t be documented perfectly.  
- You need people with **judgment in context**, not just SOPs.  
- Train for tacit skills (the “Somnistics equivalent” = teaching clinicians how to regulate in gap moments).  

### c) **Slack vs. Efficiency**  
- Over-optimization kills adaptability.  
- Keep intentional slack (time, cash, trust) so the team can respond to synchronicities.  

### d) **Metrics for Ops**  
- Metrics should support decision-making, not vanity.  
- Ask: *“What decision would this metric help me make?”*  

### e) **Cadence is King**  
- Weekly reviews, monthly retros, quarterly priorities.  
- Rhythm > one-off heroics.

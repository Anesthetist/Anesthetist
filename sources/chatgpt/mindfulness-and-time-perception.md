### Why mindful moments can feel *longer*

- **Main-street observation vs. your experience**  
  Most parents report that “the years fly by”—a retrospective compression that shows up in population studies. ### Is what you’re noticing **samādhi**?

- **Classical definition** – Samādhi (Pali/Sanskrit: *collectedness, unification*) denotes deeply absorbed, one-pointed states that culminate in the four *jhānas* or higher formless attainments. They are characterised by near-total withdrawal of sensory processing and a locked-in, effortless stability. citeturn0search1turn6view0  
- **Mindfulness vs. samādhi** – Mindfulness (*sati*) keeps the field of awareness open and discriminative. It can mature into *upacāra-samādhi* (access concentration), but full *appanā-samādhi* (“absorption”) normally requires sustained, object-exclusive focus—something harder to maintain while actively parenting.  
- **Your report** – Experiencing expansive, detailed time with children suggests heightened present-moment awareness and a well-regulated nervous system; it *may* hover around access concentration, but classic samādhi criteria (loss of interest in external stimuli, syntonic joy, immovability) aren’t implied. In short: you’re tasting the *fruits* of mindfulness, not yet the depth of formal absorption—and that’s perfectly aligned with day-to-day life practice.

The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) has established guidelines for monitoring patients during anesthesia. These guidelines include the use of several standard monitoring devices, including:

1. Electrocardiogram (ECG or EKG): This device monitors the electrical activity of the heart and can detect abnormal rhythms or changes in heart rate.

2. Non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP): This device measures blood pressure using a cuff placed around the patient's arm or leg. It can detect changes in blood pressure that may indicate changes in the patient's condition.

3. Pulse oximeter: This device measures the oxygen saturation of the patient's blood by shining a light through the patient's finger or earlobe. It can detect changes in oxygen saturation that may indicate respiratory or circulatory problems.

4. Capnograph: This device measures the carbon dioxide levels in the patient's exhaled breath, which can indicate changes in respiratory function.

5. Temperature monitor: This device measures the patient's body temperature, which is important for detecting changes in metabolic function or hypothermia.

6. Neuromuscular monitor: This device measures the patient's muscle function and can help ensure that the patient is adequately paralyzed during surgery.

7. Anesthetic gas monitor: This device measures the concentration of anesthetic gases in the patient's breath, which can help ensure that the patient is receiving the appropriate level of anesthesia.

The use of these standard ASA monitors is essential for ensuring patient safety during anesthesia. Additionally, monitoring the biometric changes in the patient using these devices can help healthcare providers adjust anesthesia levels and detect potential problems before they become more serious.

## 1️⃣ 30‑second mental model you can walk in with

- **Pain ≠ damage.** Pain is the brain’s *prediction* of threat to the body, built from nociceptive input + prior beliefs + context.  
- In many chronic cases, the hardware is fine; the **prediction system is stuck in “danger” mode** → *nociplastic pain* (central sensitization). citeturn0search29turn0search15  
- **Pain Reprocessing (or Processing) Therapy (PRT)** is a brief, structured psychotherapy that:
  - Educates that the pain is **brain-generated and reversible**
  - Helps the patient **reinterpret sensations as safe**
  - Uses **exposure + somatic tracking** to move while feeling safer, not braced  
  - Works best when there’s **no ongoing tissue threat** and the pain is primarily nociplastic.
- In the flagship RCT for chronic low back pain, ~**2/3 of patients on PRT were pain‑free or nearly pain‑free at 4 weeks**, vs ~20% placebo and 10% usual care, with benefits largely maintained at 1 year and persisting in 5‑year follow‑up analyses. citeturn0search0turn0search8turn0search12turn0search30

## 2️⃣ Mechanism + evidence (for your scientist brain)

### A. Pain types and where PRT fits

IASP now uses three mechanistic buckets: citeturn0search3turn0search29  

1. **Nociceptive pain** – normal signaling from actual tissue damage/inflammation (fracture, burn, post‑op).
2. **Neuropathic pain** – lesion/disease of somatosensory system (radiculopathy, neuropathy).
3. **Nociplastic pain** – *altered nociception* despite no clear tissue damage or nerve lesion; the CNS is amplifying noise into signal. Classic in fibromyalgia, some chronic low back pain, headaches, IBS. citeturn0search7turn0search11  

PRT is designed primarily for **primary/nociplastic pain**—where pain is out of proportion to findings and maintained by fear, avoidance, and incorrect beliefs (“if I bend, I’ll blow my disc”). citeturn0search0turn0search7  

### B. Predictive processing model (why reinterpretation works)

In predictive coding terms:

- The brain runs a **threat model**: “Am I safe?”  
- Nociceptive input is just *data*; the experience of pain is the **posterior belief**: “I am being damaged.”  
- With chronic pain, priors like *“my back is ruined”* + high arousal + catastrophic attention bias push the system to keep inferring danger even from normal signals.
- PRT intervenes at the level of **priors and precision**:
  - Reduces the belief that pain = damage  
  - Reduces fear/avoidance (less amplifying arousal)  
  - Increases attention to *non-danger* interpretations of sensation

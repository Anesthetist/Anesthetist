Berkeleyan idealism posits that the only things that exist are minds and the ideas perceived by them. According to this view, material objects do not exist independently of perception; they exist only as perceptions in minds. In the context of McGilchrist's work, the "blindness" of the left hemisphere to its lack of perception can be likened to Berkeley's idea that existence is fundamentally about perception. This ties into McGilchrist's broader thesis about the divided brain, where he argues that the two hemispheres have fundamentally different ways of experiencing the world, with the left hemisphere being more analytical and focused on details, and the right hemisphere being more holistic and open to new experiences.

## 1. Chronic pain as a computational problem

The current computational / predictive-processing view of chronic pain goes roughly like this:

- The brain is constantly **inferring** “am I in pain?” from:
  - Prior beliefs about the body (“my back is fragile,” “pain means damage”),
  - Incoming nociceptive/interoceptive signals,
  - Context (movement, stress, memory, emotion).citeturn0search23turn0search19turn0search26  
- In chronic pain, priors and precision weightings drift so that:
  - The **prior probability of pain** is too high.
  - Benign sensory noise is interpreted as threat.
  - The model learns “pain is the default explanation” for a wide range of interoceptive states.citeturn0search1turn0search19  

Eckert et al.’s Bayesian model explicitly formalizes this: a hidden “pain” state plus sensory observations; chronic pain emerges when priors and likelihoods are biased toward inferring pain from ambiguous input.citeturn0search1turn0search19  

From an interoceptive perspective, you can think of it as **maladaptive interoceptive inference**: the insula/ACC “homeostatic dashboard” keeps flagging danger even when the tissue status is safe.citeturn0search5turn0search16turn0search23

## 2. What PRT is actually doing, mechanistically

Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT) was designed for **primary / nociplastic** chronic pain—pain driven largely by central processes rather than ongoing peripheral damage.  

Key pieces (Ashar et al., JAMA Psychiatry RCT):citeturn0search21turn0search7  

1. **Reattribution of cause**
   - Education & dialogue to shift beliefs from “my back is damaged” to “this is a reversible brain-based pain signal.”
   - In later analyses, increases in “mind/brain attributions” mediated reductions in pain.citeturn0search11  

2. **Somatic tracking**
   - Guiding patients to **attend to pain sensations with curiosity and safety**, not as danger.
   - This is essentially **controlled exposure** to interoceptive prediction errors under a new model.

3. **Graded exposure to avoided movements**
   - Moving in previously feared ways while reappraising sensations as safe.
   - This directly confronts the old generative model (“movement = damage”) and updates it.

4. **Emotional processing**
   - Working with fear, anger, grief around the pain.
   - Affects the affective layer of the model (threat appraisal, salience tagging).

In the RCT, ~66% of PRT participants were pain-free or nearly pain-free after 4 weeks, versus ~20% placebo and 10% usual care, with effects maintained at 1-year—and now reported out to 5 years in follow-up.citeturn0search21turn0search4turn0search25turn0search28

“Value” is an inherently subjective assessment of how much an outcome is worth to someone. This means that ultimately definitions of value end up being circular: things that have value are valuable, etc.

Though we recognise that some outcomes have intrinsic value (e.g., “ensuring that human freedom is protected” or “ensuring that a human life is saved”) we seem to only be able to make sense of the value of outcomes in terms of proxies that are themselves valuable.

Fred values his salary at BankyBank enough to trade 60 of his waking hours each week to get it (valuable proxy: ⏳). Why is it important to understand not-knowing about value?
When we act with intention (vs acting accidentally or instinctively), it is in the context of a web of assumptions about what actions and outcomes are possible, how those actions are connected to outcomes, and which of those outcomes are desirable — that is to say, which outcomes we value and how much we value them. Much of the time, we assume that the value of these outcomes is a) stable, b) known in advance, and c) agreed among stakeholders.

Recognising the ways in which the value of outcomes could be unstable, incompletely known in advance, and/or heterogeneous among stakeholders certainly messes with existing theories of action — which maybe were unrealistically simplistic.

## 🔑 TL;DR for Operators
When you’re weighing outcomes, you’re usually assuming value is:  
1. Stable  
2. Knowable in advance  
3. Shared across stakeholders  

That’s rarely true. Five “not-knowing” traps distort value:  
- **Present heterogeneity** (people disagree now).  
- **Future scarcity** (what’s abundant today may be rare tomorrow).  
- **Future norms** (social/legal rules shift).  
- **Subjective judgment** (art, aesthetics, taste).  
- **Tradeoff thresholds** (different leaders accept different sacrifices).  

If you don’t map which kind of not-knowing you’re in, you’ll treat fragile assumptions as bedrock—and misallocate time, money, or trust.  

---

## 🚀 Deployment Guide (Rapid)
Here’s how to spot and handle each form of not-knowing in the field:

### 1. Present disagreements
- **Signal:** Stakeholders rank the same outcome differently.  
- **Tool:** Elicit assumptions explicitly (“What’s your coin-in-the-jar estimate?”).  
- **Deployment:** Run a “Boris process” or weighted decision matrix to surface divergence fast.  

### 2. Subjective judgments
- **Signal:** Debates over quality, beauty, or “fit.”  
- **Tool:** Switch from binary valuation → portfolio framing (“let’s carry 3 tastes to market, test them”).  
- **Deployment:** AB-test instead of argue.  

### 3. Tradeoff thresholds
- **Signal:** Hidden friction between departments over what to sacrifice.  
- **Tool:** Map resources as “currencies” (time ⏳, money 💰, reputation 🌍).  
- **Deployment:** Force explicit tradeoff swaps: “If we push X, we are not doing Y.”  

### 4. Future scarcity shifts
- **Signal:** Value of input/output hinges on uncertain supply (water, bandwidth, CRNA attention).  
- **Tool:** Scenario planning; stress-test with “what if it halves / doubles in scarcity?”  
- **Deployment:** Invest in options, not bets.  

### 5. Future norms shifts
- **Signal:** Dependent on law, culture, or ethical acceptance (privacy, AI, fossil fuels).  
- **Tool:** Horizon scanning; assign someone to watch early signals.  
- **Deployment:** Build reversible strategies; avoid lock-in.

2. **Am I treating it as if it were stable, known, and shared?**

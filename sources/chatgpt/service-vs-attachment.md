## 2. Category theory: you vs the network as a compositional system

### 2.1. Set-up: people as objects, introductions as morphisms

Think of a tiny social world as a category **C**:

- **Objects**: agents-in-context  
  - Person A with their current aims, bandwidth, emotional state  
  - Person B with theirs  
  - You (but just as *one* object among many)

- **Morphisms**: interactions and transformations  
  - A → B: “A sends B a message”  
  - You → A: “You share information with A”  
  - (You, A) → (A, B): “You introduce A and B around topic T”

Composition is just: **do one interaction, then another**. The category-theory power move is:  
> Complex behavior is built by composing simple morphisms.

Well-behaved social systems are *compositional* in that sense: your small act (forwarding something, lightly saying “you two might like each other”) composes with their subsequent acts in a way that doesn’t require you to stay in the loop.

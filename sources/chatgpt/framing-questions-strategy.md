## 🧭 Summary: “Framing” as Strategic Precision

Framing is the art of **shaping perception before information lands**. You already do this clinically—how you frame an induction sequence changes how your team behaves. In conversation or strategy, it’s the same: framing defines what *game* people think they’re in.

There are three master questions you can use in almost any context (team, investor, personal reflection) to align attention, emotion, and action.

---

## 🎯 The Three Core Questions

1. **Context Question (Why now?)**  
   *“What’s the real situation we’re in, and why does it matter right now?”*  
   - Purpose: Sets urgency and meaning.  
   - Example: “Given how burnout and vigilance loss are affecting CRNAs, why is this moment uniquely suited for Somnistics to define a new standard?”

2. **Choice Question (Where’s the leverage?)**  
   *“Of all possible moves, which one changes the game most?”*  
   - Purpose: Focuses attention on highest-yield intervention.  
   - Example: “What one feature, if perfected, would make every other feature easier or irrelevant?”

3. **Consequence Question (What becomes possible?)**  
   *“If we get this right, what future state does it unlock?”*  
   - Purpose: Pulls people forward emotionally; shows payoff beyond the task.  
   - Example: “If clinicians mastered one-minute physiological resets, what does healthcare culture look like five years from now?”

---

## 🔍 Deep Dive: Frame Construction Model

You can think of framing as a **three-part signal alignment** system:

| Layer | Function | Neurologic Parallel | Example |
|-------|-----------|--------------------|----------|
| **Meaning Frame** | Why this matters | Salience network | “We’re redefining vigilance.” |
| **Direction Frame** | What we’re doing | Dorsal attention network | “We’re building one-minute mastery.” |
| **Emotion Frame** | How it should feel | Default mode / limbic coupling | “Calm, clinical precision, reverent focus.” |

When all three line up, people *feel* the coherence before they *understand* it.

That line —  
> “What are the smallest repeatable patterns that, if trained into the system, would make excellence automatic?” —  

is a **keystone systems-thinking question**. It asks: *What micro-behaviors or feedback loops, once encoded, would cause excellence to emerge without constant oversight?*  

Let’s unpack it in your context:

---

### 1. **Definition**
- **Smallest:** atomic; a single, teachable action or rule (e.g., “breathe once before speaking,” “run 15-minute cash check at every L10”).  
- **Repeatable:** reliable across people, time, and context.  
- **Patterns:** coupling of triggers → actions → feedback → reinforcement.  
- **Excellence automatic:** you’ve embedded feedback so well that good behavior self-corrects faster than it decays.

---

### 2. **How It Works in Practice (Somnistics Examples)**

| Domain | Small Repeatable Pattern | Feedback / Reinforcement | Systemic Effect |
|---------|--------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| **Clinical / Product** | 60-second “pre-induction reset” (5-breath anchor) | HRV + CO₂ metrics | Improves vigilance consistency → fewer cognitive freezes |
| **Team Ops** | “2-minute gap before meeting decisions” | Slack bot prompt / reflection cue | Reduces reactivity, increases coherence |
| **Finance** | Weekly 15-minute cash review | Live dashboard + verbal summary | Turns financial awareness into ritual, builds safety & trust |
| **Culture** | Daily micro-gratitude or “state check” in standup | Peer acknowledgment | Maintains psychological safety + motivation |
| **Learning** | “After-action reflection” after demos | Shared Notion log | Converts mistakes into reusable learning assets |

Each is a **micro-ritual**—tiny enough to survive stress, yet powerful enough to compound.

---

### 3. **Design Formula**
1. **Observe:** Where does excellence *already* happen without effort?  
2. **Decompose:** What micro-pattern caused it? (Cue → Behavior → Feedback → Reward)  
3. **Encode:** Make that pattern explicit in a playbook, app, or ritual.  
4. **Reinforce:** Give it sensory, social, or data feedback.  
5. **Scale:** Teach it to the next node (person, team, system).  

When repeated, these patterns evolve into **organizational reflexes**—your nervous-system equivalent of procedural memory.

## Key Sources of Open Neuroscience Data

1. **Allen Institute for Brain Science**  
   - **What it offers:** Extensive data on brain structure and gene expression in mice, humans, and other species.  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Known for its open-science philosophy and high-quality, well-curated datasets, including the Allen Brain Atlas, cell morphology data, and transcriptomic data.  
   - [Website](https://www.alleninstitute.org/)

2. **Human Connectome Project (HCP)**  
   - **What it offers:** MRI, DTI (diffusion tensor imaging), and behavioral data from healthy adults.  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Provides a comprehensive map of human brain connectivity, helping you study neural circuits and brain-behavior relationships.  
   - [Website](https://www.humanconnectome.org/)

3. **OpenNeuro (formerly OpenfMRI)**  
   - **What it offers:** A platform hosting a wide variety of MRI, EEG, and behavioral datasets contributed by researchers worldwide.  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Includes standardized data with the BIDS (Brain Imaging Data Structure) format. Encourages reproducible research and easy data sharing.  
   - [Website](https://openneuro.org/)

4. **NeuroVault**  
   - **What it offers:** A repository for statistical maps (e.g., fMRI activation maps) and meta-analyses.  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Facilitates visual comparisons of activation patterns, supporting tasks like meta-analysis and machine learning research.  
   - [Website](https://neurovault.org/)

5. **OASIS (Open Access Series of Imaging Studies)**  
   - **What it offers:** Cross-sectional and longitudinal MRI data for aging, Alzheimer’s disease, and other neurological conditions.  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Ideal for anyone studying aging, dementia, or disease progression.  
   - [Website](http://www.oasis-brains.org/)

6. **NIMH Data Archive (NDA)**  
   - **What it offers:** A large-scale data repository on mental health conditions (e.g., autism, schizophrenia, mood disorders) from the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH).  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Includes clinical, imaging, genomic, and phenotypic data—very comprehensive resource.  
   - [Website](https://nda.nih.gov/)

7. **EEG Databases (e.g., Temple University Hospital EEG Data Corpus, BCI Competition Datasets)**  
   - **What it offers:** Scalp EEG recordings for multiple conditions and tasks, including seizure detection and brain-computer interface research.  
   - **Why it’s valuable:** Provides raw EEG signals for algorithm development, testing, and product prototyping.  
   - [Temple University Hospital EEG](https://isip.piconepress.com/projects/tuh_eeg/html/downloads.shtml), [BCI Competition Data](http://www.bbci.de/competition)

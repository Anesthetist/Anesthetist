### Commonly Tested Stress Biomarkers 

1. **Cortisol**  
   - A primary stress hormone that can be measured in blood, saliva, or hair.
   - Indicates hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis activity.

2. **Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA)**
   - Counteracts cortisol and reflects resilience to stress.

3. **Alpha-Amylase**  
   - An enzyme in saliva used as a marker for sympathetic nervous system activation.

4. **Heart Rate Variability (HRV)**  
   - Indicates autonomic nervous system balance, with low HRV linked to chronic stress.

5. **C-Reactive Protein (CRP)**  
   - A marker of systemic inflammation that can correlate with stress and PTSD.

6. **Catecholamines (Epinephrine and Norepinephrine)**  
   - Reflect acute stress responses via the sympathetic-adrenal-medullary axis.

7. **Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF)**  
   - Associated with neuroplasticity and impacted by chronic stress and PTSD.

8. **Oxytocin**  
   - May indicate recovery from stress or adaptive social bonding mechanisms.

9. **IL-6 and TNF-α (Pro-inflammatory cytokines)**  
   - Reflect inflammation associated with stress and PTSD.

10. **Hair Cortisol**  
    - A long-term marker of chronic stress exposure.

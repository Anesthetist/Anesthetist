### 1. **Lazarus & Folkman’s (1986) Transactional Model of Stress**
- **Core idea:** Stress is not just about an external event (“the stressor”) but about the **transaction** between the person and their environment.  
- **Key processes:**  
  - *Primary appraisal*: How a person evaluates whether something is threatening, harmful, or challenging.  
  - *Secondary appraisal*: How they evaluate whether they have the resources or coping strategies to manage it.  
- **Implication:** Stress = the mismatch between demands and perceived ability to cope.  
- **Relevance to programmes:** Interventions based on this model often focus on shifting appraisals (e.g., reframing stressors) and strengthening coping resources.

---

### 2. **Contemporary Research on Resilience**
- **Resilience:** The capacity to adapt well in the face of adversity, trauma, or significant stress.  
- **Modern framing (e.g., Chmitorz et al., 2018):**  
  - Resilience isn’t just a personality trait—it’s a **dynamic process** influenced by context, relationships, and learned skills.  
  - Programmes can target resilience factors (like emotional regulation, social support, or cognitive flexibility) to build long-term stress resistance.  

---

### 3. **Effectiveness of Online Interventions**
- **Why important:** The research cited (Ang et al., 2022; Diaz et al., 2021; Fischer & Law, 2021) shows that web-based or app-based interventions can successfully improve resilience, reduce stress, and support mental health.

- **Challenge:** Engagement and adherence (keeping people motivated to use the tools).

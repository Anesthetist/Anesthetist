Here’s the distilled guidance SONsiel gives for crafting a strong problem statement:  

---

### **SONsiel Problem Statement Principles**  

1. **Human-centered framing**  
   - Start with the lived experience of patients, families, or clinicians.  
   - Make it relatable, concrete, and anchored in a real-world challenge.  

2. **Clarity and brevity**  
   - 1–2 sentences, maximum.  
   - State *what the problem is* and *who is affected*.  
   - Avoid solutions in the problem statement (don’t “jump ahead”).  

3. **Evidence + emotion balance**  
   - Use a credible data point *or* a visceral story (ideally both).  
   - Judges respond well when they can feel the urgency and also see its scale.  

4. **Systemic scope**  
   - Show how this is not an isolated issue but a pattern.  
   - Example: “This challenge is repeated daily across X hospitals / in Y setting.”  

5. **Opportunity orientation**  
   - Frame the gap as an opening for innovation, not just a complaint.  
   - This sets up your solution as timely and necessary.  

---

### **Common SONsiel Pitfalls**  
- Too broad (e.g., “Nurses are burned out”) without specifying context.  
- Too narrow (one anecdote only, no scale).  
- Accidentally including the solution in the problem statement.  
- Jargon-heavy or academic phrasing.

#### 1.  The IP “Awareness Paradox”

| Concept | Why It Matters to Somnistics |
|---------|-----------------------------|
| **Duty of Disclosure (37 CFR § 1.56)** | Anyone substantively involved in prosecution must submit *all* known material prior art to the USPTO. The more references you personally study, the heavier your disclosure burden and the greater the risk of **inequitable conduct** accusations later. |
| **Doctrine of Obviousness (35 U.S.C. § 103)** | If you internalize a reference, a future opponent can argue your invention was “obvious to one of ordinary skill” in light of what you admittedly knew. Broad claims may then collapse. |
| **Internal Contamination** | Deep reading can unconsciously bias claim drafting—engineers start mirroring existing language, limiting scope. |
| **Publication/Use Bar (§ 102)** | Discussing specifics you learned (e.g., in a pitch deck) before filing can create a 12‑month ticking clock—or immediate bar in many foreign jurisdictions. |

> **Goddard’s point:** *Perform enough diligence to steer clear of landmines, but not so much that you (a) inherit undue disclosure obligations, (b) talk yourself out of broad claims, or (c) accidentally publicly disclose.*

---

#### 2.  Why Generative‑AI Era Makes the Trap Bigger

1. **Search Fire‑Hose** – LLMs surface *more* art, faster, and in plain language; easy to over‑consume.  
2. **Derivative Text Risk** – LLM‑generated summaries can echo claim language; copying them verbatim into your spec may import inadvertent prior‑art wording.  
3. **Metadata Trails** – Your prompts and results could be discoverable in litigation, proving you knew specific references.

---

A tighter, more neuro-precise version of your paragraph

Scientific (but readable)

Perception is not a direct readout of sensory input—it’s an inference. The brain combines prior expectations with incoming signals and weights them by their precision (reliability). When priors are strong or sensory evidence is uncertain, experience can be pulled toward what the brain expects. The same inferential machinery supports emotion and mood, which can be modeled as predictions about internal bodily states (interoception) in the service of allostasis. In depression and anxiety, computational accounts propose maladaptive patterns of priors and/or precision-weighting that bias interpretation and action. Cognitive “reframing” (reappraisal) works, in part, by updating high-level beliefs/meaning, which cascades down to alter predictions and thereby changes felt experience.  

Public-facing (short, punchy)

Your brain doesn’t just receive reality—it predicts it. What you experience is a “best guess” built from expectation plus sensation. Emotion and mood work the same way: they’re the brain’s predictions about what your body is doing and what it needs next. When those predictions get biased—toward threat, hopelessness, or depletion—anxiety, depression, and fatigue can follow. Change the meaning you assign to what’s happening (reframe), and you can change the prediction—so the experience shifts.  

Deep dive: what’s actually happening mechanistically

1) Why prediction can shape perception

In predictive coding terms, cortex is often framed as a hierarchy:
	•	Higher levels generate predictions (“what’s likely happening”).
	•	Lower levels compare predictions with incoming input and send prediction errors upward (“what didn’t match”).
	•	The brain updates its model to reduce future surprise / prediction error.  

Crucial nuance: it’s not “top-down beats bottom-up.” It’s an integration whose outcome depends on precision weighting—how much confidence the system assigns to sensory evidence vs. prior expectations (and attention is often modeled as precision control).  

Translation:
	•	If the signal is clear and reliable, sensation dominates and your model updates.
	•	If the signal is weak/ambiguous (low precision), priors dominate and experience can tilt toward expectation.

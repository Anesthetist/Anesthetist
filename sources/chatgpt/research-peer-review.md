Brainwaves are the result of the synchronized electrical activity of the neurons in your brain. This electrical activity is strong enough to be detected even outside the brain using a technique known as electroencephalography (EEG). The rate of repetition of these brainwaves, or their frequencies, can provide information about the current state of the brain【8†source】.

There are five main types of brainwave frequencies, each associated with different mental states:

1. **Gamma Brainwaves (32 – 100 Hz)**: These are the fastest measurable EEG brainwaves. They are associated with heightened perception, learning, and problem-solving tasks. Gamma brainwaves have been observed to be much stronger and more regularly observed in very long-term meditators, including Buddhist Monks【10†source】.

2. **Beta Brainwaves (13-32 Hz)**: These brainwaves are easiest to detect when we’re busy thinking actively. They are associated with alertness, normal alert consciousness, and active thinking. Examples of beta brainwave activity include active conversation, making decisions, solving a problem, focusing on a task, and learning a new concept【11†source】.

3. **Alpha Brainwaves (8-13 Hz)**: Alpha brainwaves are some of the most easily observed and were the first to be discovered. They become detectable when the eyes are closed and the mind is relaxed. They are associated with a state of being physically and mentally relaxed【12†source】.

4. **Theta Brainwaves (4-8 Hz)**: Theta waves indicate deep relaxation and occur more frequently in highly experienced meditation practitioners. They are associated with creativity, insight, dreams, and reduced consciousness. Theta brainwaves are strongly detectable when we’re dreaming in our sleep, during deep meditation, daydreaming, and when we’re doing a task that is so automatic that the mind can disengage from it. Research has shown a positive association of theta waves with memory, creativity, and psychological well-being【13†source】.

5. **Delta Brainwaves (0.5-4 Hz)**: These are the slowest of all brainwaves and are strongest when we are enjoying restorative sleep in a dreamless state. They are associated with sleep and dreaming【14†source】.

It's possible to change how much we experience these different brainwaves and the brain states and thought experiences associated with them. Training programs, such as neurofeedback, are intended to reinforce the brain states which produce increases in certain brain oscillations and decreases in others. Moreover, meditation deepens your ability to focus and control your attention, improving your ability to control your brainwaves【15†source】.

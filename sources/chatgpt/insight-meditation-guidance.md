Each stage (ñāṇa) represents a deeper penetration into the **three characteristics of existence**: **anicca** (impermanence), **dukkha** (unsatisfactoriness), and **anattā** (nonself). The path unfolds in a generally sequential, though sometimes nonlinear, way.

---

1. **Nāmarūpa-pariccheda Ñāṇa** – *Knowledge of Mental and Physical Distinction*

2. **Paccaya-pariggaha Ñāṇa** – *Knowledge of Cause and Effect*  
   > Recognition that intention precedes movement; cause-effect replaces self-narrative.

---

   > Increased clarity, brightness, and fluid insight into the transient nature of phenomena. Many vipassanā defilements (rapture, light, faith, pride) may arise.

---

5. **Bhanga Ñāṇa** – *Dissolution*  
   > All formations begin to break down; attention centers on vanishing. Can feel destabilizing.

6. **Bhaya Ñāṇa** – *Knowledge of Terror*  
   > The truth of impermanence becomes terrifying. Reality feels insubstantial.

7. **Ādinava Ñāṇa** – *Knowledge of Danger*  
   > Everything is seen as unsatisfactory and futile. Disillusionment deepens.

8. **Nibbidā Ñāṇa** – *Disenchantment*  
   > Profound boredom and revulsion with conditioned existence; a turning point.

9. **Muñcitukamyatā Ñāṇa** – *Desire for Deliverance*  
   > Intense desire to escape the cycle of suffering; inner struggle to break free.

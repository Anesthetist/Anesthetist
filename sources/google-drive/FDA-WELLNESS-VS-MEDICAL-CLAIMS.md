# FDA Wellness vs Medical Claims — Definitive Reference

*Last Updated: January 2026 (reflects FDA's January 6, 2026 updated guidance)*

---

## Executive Summary

The FDA distinguishes between **general wellness products** (not regulated as medical devices) and **medical devices** (subject to extensive regulation). The difference hinges on **intended use** and **claims made**.

**For Pausality:** We are positioning as a general wellness product. This document defines exactly what we can and cannot say.

---

## The Two-Factor Test

A product qualifies as a **general wellness product** (not regulated as a medical device) if it meets BOTH criteria:

### Factor 1: Intended Only for General Wellness Use
The product maintains or encourages a general state of health or healthy activity.

### Factor 2: Presents Low Risk to Safety
The product is:
- NOT invasive
- NOT implanted
- Does NOT involve technology requiring regulatory controls (lasers, radiation, etc.)

**If BOTH factors are met → General wellness product (not FDA regulated)**
**If EITHER factor is NOT met → Medical device (FDA regulated)**

---

## What is "General Wellness Use"?

The FDA defines general wellness claims in two categories:

### Category A: No Disease Reference
Claims that promote general health without mentioning any disease or condition.

**Examples:**
- "Promotes relaxation"
- "Helps manage weight"
- "Supports healthy sleep patterns"
- "Encourages physical activity"
- "Improves mental acuity"

### Category B: Disease Reference (With Healthy Lifestyle Caveat)
Claims that reference a chronic disease BUT frame the product as part of a healthy lifestyle that "may help" reduce risk.

**Examples:**
- "As part of a healthy lifestyle, may help reduce the risk of high blood pressure"
- "As part of a healthy lifestyle, may help living well with anxiety"

**Critical requirement:** The connection between healthy lifestyle and the disease/condition must be "well understood and accepted" (supported by peer-reviewed literature).

## What Pausality CAN Say (Wellness Claims) ✅

### Safe Claims — No Disease Reference
- "Promotes relaxation and stress reduction"
- "Helps manage daily stress"

- "Tracks and displays heart rate during breathing exercises"
- "Provides real-time biofeedback for stress management"

- "Breathing exercises, as part of a healthy lifestyle, may help reduce the impact of daily stress"
- "Regular practice, as part of a healthy lifestyle, may support relaxation and recovery"
- "Protocols based on peer-reviewed breathing and HRV research"

- "Techniques linked to peer-reviewed research"
- "Built by a clinician (CRNA)"

## What Pausality CANNOT Say (Medical Claims) ❌

### Diagnosis Claims (NEVER)
- ❌ "Diagnoses anxiety"
- ❌ "Detects stress disorders"
- ❌ "Identifies abnormal heart rate patterns"
- ❌ "Screens for cardiovascular risk"

### Treatment Claims (NEVER)
- ❌ "Treats anxiety"
- ❌ "Cures panic attacks"
- ❌ "Reduces symptoms of PTSD"
- ❌ "Treats burnout"
- ❌ "Therapeutic breathing for anxiety disorder"

### Prevention Claims (NEVER — unless with healthy lifestyle caveat)
- ❌ "Prevents anxiety attacks"
- ❌ "Prevents burnout"
- ⚠️ "As part of a healthy lifestyle, may help reduce the risk of..." (only if well-supported)

### Clinical Threshold Claims (NEVER)
- ❌ "Your HRV is abnormal"
- ❌ "Your heart rate indicates stress levels above normal"
- ❌ "Alert: Seek medical attention"
- ❌ Any reference to clinical thresholds or diagnostic values

### Clinical Equivalence Claims (NEVER)
- ❌ "As effective as medication"
- ❌ "Clinical-grade biofeedback"
- ❌ "Medical-grade HRV monitoring"
- ❌ "Equivalent to clinical stress assessment"

## The 2026 Guidance Updates (Important)

The FDA updated its General Wellness guidance on **January 6, 2026**. Key clarifications:

### Physiologic Parameters Are Now Explicitly Addressed
Products that estimate or display physiologic parameters (blood pressure, oxygen saturation, glucose, **heart rate variability**) CAN be general wellness products IF:

1. **Non-invasive** — Pausality uses Apple Watch optical sensors ✅
2. **Intended solely for wellness** — Not diagnosis or treatment ✅
3. **Avoid clinical claims, thresholds, or prompts for medical management** ✅
4. **If mimicking clinical values, must be validated** — HRV display should be supported by literature ✅

### Explicit Disqualifying Features
The following render a product a MEDICAL DEVICE (not wellness):
- References to specific diseases in diagnostic context
- Clinical thresholds (e.g., "abnormal HRV")
- Treatment guidance
- Claims of clinical equivalence
- Intended use for diagnosis, screening, monitoring, or management of disease

### Limited Professional Consultation Prompts Are OK
A wellness product CAN suggest users "consult a healthcare professional" IF:
- Does NOT name a specific disease
- Does NOT characterize outputs as "abnormal" or diagnostic
- Does NOT include clinical thresholds
- Does NOT make treatment recommendations

**Example (OK):** "If you have concerns about your health, consult a healthcare professional."
**Example (NOT OK):** "Your HRV is abnormal. Consult a doctor about anxiety disorder."

## Claim Ladder (Safe → Risky)

| Level | Claim Type | Example | Risk |
|-------|------------|---------|------|
| 1 | General wellness | "Promotes relaxation" | ✅ Safe |
| 2 | Evidence reference | "Based on peer-reviewed research" | ✅ Safe |
| 3 | Measurable physiology | "See your heart rate respond" | ✅ Safe |
| 4 | Healthy lifestyle + disease | "May help reduce impact of stress, as part of a healthy lifestyle" | ⚠️ Use carefully |
| 5 | Condition management | "Helps manage anxiety symptoms" | ❌ Medical claim |
| 6 | Diagnosis/treatment | "Treats anxiety" | ❌ Medical claim |

---

## Red Flag Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Diagnose | Inform, display, track |
| Treat | Support, help manage |
| Cure | Improve, enhance |
| Therapy / Therapeutic | Wellness, stress management |
| Clinical | Evidence-based, research-backed |
| Medical-grade | Designed by clinician |
| Abnormal | (Don't characterize values) |
| Disorder | Condition (with caution) |
| Symptoms | Feelings, experiences |
| Patient | User |

## Comparison: Wellness vs Medical Device

| Aspect | General Wellness Product | Medical Device |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------|
| FDA Regulation | None (enforcement discretion) | Full (510k, design controls, etc.) |
| Claims | General health, lifestyle | Diagnosis, treatment, prevention |
| Risk Level | Low | Any |
| Invasive | No | May be |
| Clinical Thresholds | No | Yes |
| Treatment Recommendations | No | Yes |
| Pausality | ✅ This is us | ❌ Not us |

## Practical Checklist for Marketing Materials

Before publishing ANY marketing content, verify:

- [ ] No diagnosis claims
- [ ] No treatment claims
- [ ] No disease-specific claims (without healthy lifestyle caveat)
- [ ] No clinical thresholds or "abnormal" characterizations
- [ ] No clinical equivalence claims
- [ ] No treatment recommendations
- [ ] Disclaimer included where appropriate
- [ ] "Stress management and performance support" framing used
- [ ] "Not a medical device" language available if needed

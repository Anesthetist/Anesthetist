# The 5 Breathing Protocols That Actually Work
### Science-backed techniques from the Huberman Lab

*Read time: 4 minutes. Practice time: 60 seconds.*

---

## Why Most Breathing Advice Fails

You've heard it before: "Just breathe."

Useless.

Your brain is spinning. Your chest is tight. Someone tells you to breathe and you want to scream.

Here's the problem: **most breathing advice is vague.**

"Take deep breaths" doesn't work because your nervous system needs specific patterns to shift gears.

Dr. Andrew Huberman, Stanford neuroscientist, has spent years studying exactly which patterns work—and why.

This guide gives you five protocols. Each one does something different. Pick the one you need right now.

---

## Protocol 1: The Physiological Sigh
**Use when:** You need to calm down *right now*

This is the fastest way to reduce stress. One breath. That's it.

**How to do it:**
1. Inhale deeply through your nose
2. At the top, sneak in a second short inhale (this reopens collapsed air sacs)
3. Long, slow exhale through your mouth

**Why it works:** The double inhale maximizes oxygen. The long exhale activates your parasympathetic nervous system—the "calm down" switch.

**Time:** 10 seconds

*"One physiological sigh can clamp stress in real time."* — Dr. Huberman

---

## Protocol 2: Cyclic Sighing
**Use when:** You want lasting calm (daily practice)

This was tested in a Stanford study. It beat meditation for reducing stress.

**How to do it:**
1. Do the physiological sigh (above)
2. Repeat for 5 minutes
3. That's it. No app. No guidance. Just breathe.

**Why it works:** Repetition trains your nervous system. Daily practice means you're calmer all day—not just during the exercise.

**Time:** 5 minutes/day

*"Cyclic sighing had the most robust effect in reducing stress, improving mood, and improving sleep."* — Stanford Study (Cell Reports Medicine, 2023)

---

## Protocol 3: Box Breathing
**Use when:** You need focus and calm (before a meeting, performance, or hard conversation)

Navy SEALs use this. It's simple math.

**How to do it:**
1. Inhale: 4 seconds
2. Hold: 4 seconds
3. Exhale: 4 seconds
4. Hold: 4 seconds
5. Repeat 4 times

**Why it works:** Equal timing creates balance. The holds build CO2 tolerance, which reduces panic response over time.

**Time:** ~2 minutes

*Start with 4-4-4-4. Work up to 6-6-6-6 as you improve.*

---

## Protocol 4: Extended Exhale
**Use when:** You can't sleep or need to wind down

Longer exhales = slower heart rate. It's physics.

**How to do it:**
1. Inhale: 4 seconds
2. Exhale: 6-8 seconds
3. Repeat for 2-5 minutes

**Why it works:** Your heart rate speeds up on inhale, slows on exhale. This is called respiratory sinus arrhythmia. Longer exhales tip the balance toward calm.

**Time:** 2-5 minutes

*Try this in bed. Most people are asleep before the 5 minutes are up.*

---

## Protocol 5: Cyclic Hyperventilation
**Use when:** You need energy, alertness, or to push through (use carefully)

This is the "Wim Hof style" breath. It wakes you up. Hard.

**How to do it:**
1. 25-30 deep breaths (inhale fully, exhale passively)
2. Exhale and hold (lungs empty) as long as comfortable
3. Inhale and hold for 15 seconds
4. Repeat 2-3 rounds

**Why it works:** Deliberate hyperventilation dumps CO2 and spikes adrenaline. You'll feel tingly. You'll feel alert.

**Time:** 5-10 minutes

⚠️ **Warning:** Never do this in water, while driving, or standing. You may get lightheaded. This is for controlled energy—not stress reduction.

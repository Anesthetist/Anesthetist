### Article 2: "HRV Is Not a Wellness Score"
**Hook:** The wellness industry got HRV wrong. Here's what it actually measures.

**Core Content:**
- HRV = beat-to-beat heart rate variation
- High HRV ≠ "healthy" (it's more nuanced)
- Context matters: morning baseline vs. real-time response
- The REAL value: measuring how fast you can shift state

**CTA:** Stop chasing HRV numbers. Start training responsiveness.

**Draft:**
```
HRV is not a wellness score. Stop treating it like one.

Heart rate variability measures beat-to-beat timing differences. Higher variance indicates parasympathetic activity. But:

- A 20-year-old athlete and a stressed executive can have the same HRV
- Morning baseline is not the same as real-time response
- The number matters less than the CHANGE

The real question: How fast can you shift your HRV during acute stress?

That's trainable. That's what matters. 🦞
```

### Article 4: "The Vagus Nerve Is Not Magic"
**Hook:** TikTok made the vagus nerve viral. And mostly wrong.

**Core Content:**
- What the vagus nerve actually does (parasympathetic efferents)
- "Vagal tone" = measurable via HRV
- Why most "vagus nerve hacks" are oversimplified
- What actually stimulates vagal activity: breathing, not ice

**CTA:** Skip the hacks. Train the system.

**Draft:**
```
The vagus nerve isn't magic. It's anatomy.

Cranial nerve X. Runs from brainstem to gut. Carries parasympathetic signals downward. That's it.

"Vagal tone" = tonic activity of the vagus. Measurable via HRV. Higher tone = faster shift from sympathetic to parasympathetic.

TikTok "vagus nerve hacks":
- Cold exposure: Stimulates diving reflex. Temporary. Not training.
- Humming: Activates laryngeal branches. Minor effect.
- Ear massage: Auricular branch. Minimal evidence.

What actually works:
- Slow breathing at 6 breaths/min
- Extended exhales (longer out than in)
- Repeated daily practice with biofeedback

The vagus nerve is trainable. But training requires measurement, not hope. 🦞
```

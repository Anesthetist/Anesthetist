## Ron's Core Thesis: Work Backwards from the Exit

Ron's entire framework inverts the typical pitch. Instead of starting with "here's our product," you start with: **How does the funder get their money back?**

The sequence:
1. Who are the acquirers in your space?
2. What revenue/metrics do they need to see to write a check?
3. What round size gets you there?
4. What milestones prove you're on track?
5. Now pitch THAT story.

**The investor is not buying your product. They are buying a return on capital.**

## Ron's Tactical Commandments for SRL

### 1. Know Your Numbers or Get Fired
"If we ask how many users you have and you have to look it up, you're fired as a CEO."

**Action:** Randy must know these numbers cold, daily:
- Active users (daily/weekly/monthly)
- Downloads, retention (Day 7, Day 30)
- MRR/ARR and growth rate
- CAC and LTV
- Pipeline value (enterprise)

### 2. Qualify VCs Before You Take the Meeting
Three questions to ask BEFORE accepting any VC meeting:
1. **"Are you allocating capital right now?"** (Not rude. Essential.)
2. **"What is your typical investment size?"**
3. **"Who are your LPs?"**

If they can't/won't answer these, don't waste the meeting.

### 3. Category Positioning
Ron was explicit: **Health & Wellness makes VCs glaze over.**

Options to explore:
- **Enterprise Performance Tech** (B2B SaaS for workforce optimization)
- **Neurotech / Digital Therapeutics** (clinical validation angle)
- **Workplace Safety / Risk Reduction** (healthcare error prevention)

The .ai domain question: Ron suggested NOT positioning as an AI company due to Bay Area concentration (4% of AI funding). Don't chase AI framing unless it's genuinely core IP.

### 4. Two Versions of Every Deck
- **Pitch deck**: Few words, lots of pictures, logos everywhere. Audience can't read and listen at the same time.
- **Takeaway deck**: Full detail for someone reading alone at their computer. This is what they forward to their partners.

### 5. The 3-Meeting Cadence
- **Meeting 1**: Entice them to want a second meeting
- **Meeting 2**: Deep dive, due diligence
- **Meeting 3**: Signing/terms

## SAFE Notes vs. Convertible Notes (Ron's Intel)

## Ron Weiner's Venture Scale Business Classification

For reference, Ron's framework classifies businesses into three types:

**Lifestyle Business**: Provide a living. Slow/steady growth. Self-funded. Lower risk. Long-term ownership. (Freelance services, blogs, artisanal)

**Growth Business**: Generate revenue with increasing profitability. Rapid growth at expense of work-life balance. May seek VC/angel investment. May consider IPO/acquisition. (Established small businesses, franchises, mid-sized companies)

**Venture Scale Business**: Disrupt an industry or create a new market. Rapid growth prioritizes investment over profitability. Relies on outside investment. Highest risk (untested ideas/technologies). Exit through acquisition or IPO is THE goal. (Tech startups, disruptive companies, high-growth ventures)

**SRL must position as Venture Scale** if seeking VC funding. Every slide must reinforce this classification.

## Founder Equity Dilution (Ron's Visual)

Ron showed the classic dilution waterfall:
- **Idea Stage**: Founder = 100%
- **Co-founder**: 50/50
- **Friends & Family**: Founders ~37.5% each, uncle 20%, option pool 5%
- **Angel Round**: Founder 1 ~31.2%, Founder 2 ~31.2%, angel ~14.7%, uncle ~4.2%, option pool ~14.7%
- **Seed Round**: Further dilution
- **Series A**: Founder 1 ~17.6%, Founder 2 ~17.6%, VC ~20.1%, etc.
- **IPO**: "100% of nothing is a lot less than 17% of a big company"

**Key message for Russ**: Cap table modeling must account for this trajectory. Show investors you understand dilution math inside and out.

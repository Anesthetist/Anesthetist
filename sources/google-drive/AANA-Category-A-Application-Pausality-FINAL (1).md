## SECTION 2: PROGRAM OVERVIEW

**Program Title:** Pausality: Interoceptive Training for CRNA Performance & Well-Being

**Program Description:**  
A 21-session progressive breathwork curriculum delivered via mobile application, designed to develop interoceptive awareness and autonomic self-regulation skills in CRNAs. The program integrates evidence-based respiratory techniques with real-time biometric feedback (Apple Watch HRV/HR) to build measurable competency in physiological self-regulation.

**Delivery Method:** Mobile application (iOS) with Apple Watch integration  
**Format:** Self-paced, asynchronous  
**Duration:** 3 weeks minimum (21 days)  
**Total Contact Hours:** 3.0 hours  
**Credits Requested:** 3.0 Class A credits  

**Target Audience:** Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetists (CRNAs)

**Prerequisites:** None. Active CRNA certification recommended.

## SECTION 4: PROGRAM GOALS & OBJECTIVES

### Program Goal
Develop CRNA competency in interoceptive awareness and autonomic self-regulation through progressive breathwork training with biometric verification.

### Terminal Objectives
Upon completion, participants will be able to:

1. **Demonstrate proficiency** in 21 evidence-based breathing techniques for autonomic regulation
2. **Apply appropriate techniques** for activation (wind-up) vs. recovery (wind-down) clinical scenarios
3. **Interpret personal biometric data** (HRV, HR) as indicators of autonomic state
4. **Achieve measurable improvement** in interoceptive awareness (MAIA-2 subscales)
5. **Implement sustainable practice** integrating breathwork into clinical workflow

### Enabling Objectives by Week

**Week 1: Awareness (Sessions 1-7) — 1.0 Contact Hour**
- Identify natural breathing patterns without modification
- Demonstrate diaphragmatic vs. thoracic breathing mechanics
- Perform box breathing (5-5-5-5 pattern) with accurate timing
- Explain physiological basis of slow breathing on vagal tone
- Achieve baseline MAIA-2 "Noticing" and "Attention Regulation" scores

**Week 2: Foundation (Sessions 8-14) — 1.0 Contact Hour**
- Apply mindful breathing principles (non-judgmental observation)
- Demonstrate breath counting for attention anchoring
- Perform 4-7-8 breathing for parasympathetic activation
- Integrate breath awareness with movement (breath walking)
- Improve MAIA-2 "Body Listening" and "Emotional Awareness" scores

**Week 3: Mastery (Sessions 15-21) — 1.0 Contact Hour**
- Perform alternate nostril breathing for hemispheric balance
- Demonstrate advanced breath retention techniques safely
- Apply activating techniques (bellows breath) appropriately
- Select optimal technique for given clinical scenario
- Achieve target improvements in MAIA-2 "Self-Regulation" and "Trusting" scores

## APPENDICES

### Appendix A: MAIA-2 Instrument

**Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness, Version 2 (MAIA-2)**

The MAIA-2 is a 37-item self-report questionnaire measuring eight dimensions of interoceptive body awareness:

1. **Noticing** (4 items) — Awareness of uncomfortable, comfortable, and neutral body sensations
2. **Not-Distracting** (6 items) — Tendency not to ignore or distract oneself from sensations of pain or discomfort
3. **Not-Worrying** (5 items) — Tendency not to worry or experience emotional distress with sensations of pain or discomfort
4. **Attention Regulation** (7 items) — Ability to sustain and control attention to body sensations
5. **Emotional Awareness** (5 items) — Awareness of the connection between body sensations and emotional states
6. **Self-Regulation** (4 items) — Ability to regulate distress by attention to body sensations
7. **Body Listening** (3 items) — Active listening to the body for insight
8. **Trusting** (3 items) — Experiencing one's body as safe and trustworthy

Items are rated on a 6-point Likert scale (0 = Never to 5 = Always). The instrument demonstrates strong psychometric properties with Cronbach's alpha ranging from 0.66 to 0.87 across subscales.

**Source:** Mehling, W.E., et al. (2018). The Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness, Version 2 (MAIA-2). *PLOS ONE*, 13(12), e0208034.

**Access:** https://osher.ucsf.edu/maia (freely available for clinical and research use)

### Appendix B: BOLT Test Protocol

**Body Oxygen Level Test (BOLT Score)**

The BOLT score is a simple, self-administered measure of CO2 tolerance and respiratory efficiency. It measures the comfortable breath-hold time after a normal exhalation, reflecting chemoreceptor sensitivity to carbon dioxide.

**Protocol:**
1. Sit upright in a comfortable position
2. Breathe normally through the nose for 2-3 minutes to establish baseline
3. After a normal, relaxed exhale (not forced), pinch the nose closed
4. Start timer
5. Hold breath until the **first distinct desire to breathe** (not maximum hold)
6. Stop timer at first: diaphragm contraction, swallowing urge, or air hunger sensation
7. Resume normal nasal breathing — you should be able to recover within 2-3 breaths

**Scoring Interpretation:**
- **<10 seconds:** Very poor CO2 tolerance, likely chronic overbreathing
- **10-20 seconds:** Poor tolerance, room for significant improvement
- **20-30 seconds:** Fair tolerance, typical for most adults
- **30-40 seconds:** Good tolerance, healthy respiratory function
- **>40 seconds:** Excellent tolerance, optimal for performance

**Key Principle:** The BOLT score reflects functional breathing patterns, not lung capacity. It measures when the brain's chemoreceptors signal the need to breathe based on rising CO2 levels.

**Clinical Relevance:** Higher BOLT scores correlate with improved HRV, better stress resilience, and enhanced parasympathetic tone. Progressive breathwork training typically increases BOLT scores by 5-15 seconds over 3-4 weeks.

**Source:** McKeown, P. (2015). *The Oxygen Advantage.* William Morrow.

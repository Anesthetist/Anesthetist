Crystal,

Quick update from the research side. We just completed our seventh "Notes from the Lab" essay — this one on the NeuroMinute, our 60-second micro-intervention format. Thought you'd want to see the evidence landscape it maps.

**The core finding:** You et al. (2021, *Int J Environ Res Public Health*, PMID: 34886206) tested slow-paced breathing at 5, 10, 15, and 20 minutes. No significant differences in RMSSD across durations. Five minutes produced the same vagally-mediated HRV shift as twenty. The dose-response curve flattens far earlier than the field assumed.

**What that means for us:** If 5 minutes equals 20, the research question becomes how far below 5 minutes the effect persists. Laborde et al.'s 223-study meta-analysis (2022, *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*, PMID: 35623448) confirmed that vagal activation begins with the first breath cycle, not after a warm-up period. We're designing a 14-day proof test — 10 CRNAs, five 60-second sessions distributed across shifts, RMSSD captured before and after each session via Apple Watch — to establish whether the effect holds at one minute.

**The convergence that matters for your program:** Hsu et al. (2025, *Br J Health Psychol*, PMID: 39542743) reviewed 62 just-in-time adaptive interventions and found them feasible and effective across behaviors and settings. The NeuroMinute fits squarely in the JITAI framework — brief, contextual, adaptive, moment-specific. Health behavior science and anesthesia practice arriving at the same design constraint independently.

**Three study-ready questions this opens:**

1. Does a 60-second breathing intervention produce a measurable RMSSD delta? (Dose-response below 5 minutes — nobody has published this.)
2. Does distributed practice (5×60s/day) outperform concentrated practice (1×5min/day) for autonomic adaptation? (Frequency vs. duration at the micro-intervention scale.)
3. Does the within-session delta compound across a shift? (End-of-shift HRV trending higher on intervention days vs. baseline.)

All three are testable with 10 CRNAs, Apple Watches, and 14 days. No IRB complexity beyond standard wearable data collection. The gap in the literature is wide open.

Happy to send the full essay if you'd like to see the mechanism section and the protocol design. Seven essays in the series now — Anterocept, Neurogating, Gap Moment Training, Vagal Tone, Interoception, Polyanchora, and NeuroMinute — all PubMed-verified, all building the evidence framework for the collaboration.

## Script

Welcome. Settle gently into your body.

Using your right thumb, softly close your right nostril.

Inhale through your left nostril for 4 counts.

Close left nostril, exhale right nostril 4 counts.

Inhale right nostril 4 counts.

Exhale left nostril 4 counts.

Continue slowly alternating sides.

Balance your brain and nervous system.

Feel harmony between mind and body.

Return gently to natural breath.

Thank you for taking this PAUSE.

## Neurobiology

Alternate nostril breathing (Nadi Shodhana) directly engages the corpus callosum, the largest white-matter commissure connecting the cerebral hemispheres. Each nostril preferentially activates contralateral cortical regions: left nostril breathing upregulates right-hemisphere activity (parasympathetic-dominant, spatial-holistic processing), while right nostril breathing upregulates left-hemisphere activity (sympathetic-dominant, verbal-analytic processing). The deliberate alternation forces repeated interhemispheric communication across callosal fibers, strengthening functional connectivity between bilateral prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, and insular regions.

The nasal cycle — the natural 90-120 minute alternation of nostril dominance — is governed by the hypothalamus and reflects autonomic balance. Nadi Shodhana overrides and regularizes this cycle, producing measurable bilateral EEG coherence within minutes. This is distinct from other breathing practices in that it creates a forced oscillation of lateralized autonomic tone, effectively "cross-training" both hemispheric activation patterns.

Key neurotransmitter effects include GABA upregulation in both hemispheres simultaneously and balanced norepinephrine distribution, avoiding the lateralized sympathetic bias that characterizes anxious states.

## Neurophysiology

Measurable biometric signatures of Nadi Shodhana include:

- **EEG bilateral alpha coherence**: Increased synchronization between left and right prefrontal alpha (8-12 Hz) bands, measurable within 5-10 minutes of practice. This is the primary electrophysiological marker of interhemispheric balancing.
- **Heart rate variability (HRV)**: Increased RMSSD and HF-HRV, reflecting enhanced parasympathetic tone. The alternating nostril pattern produces a distinctive HRV signature with slightly longer R-R intervals during left-nostril phases.
- **Blood pressure reduction**: Systolic and diastolic pressure decrease of 3-5 mmHg acutely (Telles et al., 2017), likely mediated by baroreflex resetting during the balanced autonomic oscillation.
- **Cognitive performance**: Improved performance on tasks requiring bilateral cortical integration (spatial reasoning + verbal fluency) following even a single session.
- **Galvanic skin response**: Decreased SCL (skin conductance level) bilaterally, reflecting reduced sympathetic arousal with balanced laterality.

The 4-count rhythm places this practice near the resonant breathing frequency (~0.1 Hz) for most adults, compounding the hemispheric effects with cardiovascular resonance.

## The Cool Stuff

The really fascinating dimension of Nadi Shodhana is that it exploits a neuroanatomical feature most people never think about: your nose is wired contralaterally to your brain, just like your hands. But unlike your hands, you can directly modulate which hemisphere gets more oxygen-rich airflow and which trigeminal nerve branch gets more stimulation — all without any equipment.

This means a CRNA sitting in a break room between cases can literally rebalance their cortical activation patterns in 60 seconds using nothing but their thumb and ring finger. The corpus callosum — the brain's information superhighway — gets a surge of cross-talk, creating a transient state of hemispheric cooperation that enhances both analytical precision and intuitive pattern recognition simultaneously.

For CRNAs specifically, this is relevant because high-acuity clinical environments tend to drive left-hemisphere dominance (checklists, dosing calculations, linear protocols). Over a shift, this can create a subtle hemispheric imbalance — the clinician becomes analytically sharp but loses access to the right-hemisphere gestalt pattern recognition that catches the "something doesn't feel right" signal. Nadi Shodhana restores access to both modes.

## Evidence Chain

2. **Shannahoff-Khalsa, D. (2004).** An introduction to Kundalini yoga meditation techniques that are specific for the treatment of psychiatric disorders. *Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine*, 10(1), 91-101. — Establishes lateralized autonomic effects of unilateral nostril breathing.
3. **Stancak, A., & Kuna, M. (1994).** EEG changes during forced alternate nostril breathing. *International Journal of Psychophysiology*, 18(1), 75-79. — Direct EEG evidence of bilateral coherence shifts during Nadi Shodhana.
4. **Jella, S. A., & Shannahoff-Khalsa, D. S. (1993).** The effects of unilateral forced nostril breathing on cognitive performance. *International Journal of Neuroscience*, 73(1-2), 61-68. — Demonstrates contralateral hemispheric activation from single-nostril breathing.

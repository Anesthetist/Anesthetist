Part 1: Intake Interview

30 minutes — conversational, not clinical — understanding the whole person

Racing Profile

Understanding the demands on your nervous system
  • What series do you race in? How many race weekends per season?
  • Walk me through a typical race weekend — travel, practice sessions, qualifying, race day. When do you feel sharpest? When does it start to degrade?
  • How long is a typical stint? What are the G-forces like in the car you're driving?
  • What does your team setup look like — performance coach, physio, engineer, data analyst?
  • How do you currently prepare mentally and physically before getting in the car?
  • After a race or long session, how long until you feel "normal" again? Hours? Next day?
  • Have you ever noticed your lap times getting less consistent later in a session? When does it typically happen?
  • Any incidents where you felt your reaction time or judgment was off — and you could feel it happening in the moment?

Physical Profile

Baseline fitness, sleep, recovery patterns
  • Age, height, weight?
  • What does your current fitness routine look like? (Strength, cardio, flexibility, frequency)
  • How's your sleep? Average hours, quality, any issues falling or staying asleep?
  • How do you sleep the night before a race compared to a normal night?
  • Do you track sleep with any wearable? (Apple Watch, Whoop, Oura, etc.)
  • Any current injuries, chronic pain, or physical limitations?
  • Caffeine intake — how much, when, does it change on race weekends?
  • Alcohol use? Nicotine? Supplements?

Health Screening

Safety first — ruling out contraindications
  • Any known heart conditions, arrhythmias, or cardiac history?
  • Any history of seizures, concussions, or traumatic brain injury?
  • Current medications? (Prescription or OTC)
  • Any history of panic attacks or anxiety disorder?
  • Any respiratory conditions — asthma, exercise-induced bronchospasm?
  • Blood pressure — have you been told it's high, low, or normal?
  • Family cardiac history? (Sudden cardiac events, arrhythmias)
Safety gate: If cardiac history, seizure history, or significant respiratory conditions are present, modify assessment protocol accordingly. EEG photic stimulation contraindicated in seizure history. Extended breath holds contraindicated in cardiac arrhythmia. Randy assesses and adjusts in real time.

Autonomic Experience

What has he tried, what's worked, what hasn't
  • Have you ever done any kind of breathwork, meditation, or mindfulness training?
  • Have you worked with a sports psychologist? What was the approach?
  • Do you use visualization or mental rehearsal before races?
  • Have you ever tried biofeedback or neurofeedback?
  • What do you currently do to "come down" after a high-intensity session?
  • On a scale of 1-10, how aware are you of your body's signals during a race — heart rate, breathing, muscle tension, grip pressure?
  • Have you ever intentionally used breathing to change how you feel in the car?

Goals & Expectations

What does success look like in his language
  • In your own words, what are you hoping to get out of this?
  • If this program worked perfectly, what would be different in 6 weeks?
  • Is there a specific race or event you're targeting?
  • Are you looking for something you do every day, or something specific to race weekends?
  • How much time per day are you willing to invest? (We'll need about 5 minutes.)
  • Do you have an Apple Watch? Which model?
  • How data-driven are you — do you want to see all the numbers, or just know it's working?

Part 2: Physiological Assessment

90 minutes — 11 measurement stations + SRB-60 baseline — full autonomic portrait
Station 01

Resting Vitals

10 min

5-minute seated rest, then measure: resting heart rate, blood pressure (seated), blood pressure (standing — orthostatic response), respiratory rate, SpO2.

Standing BP tests baroreflex sensitivity — how well his nervous system handles position changes. Orthostatic drop > 20mmHg systolic is a flag.

BP cuff, pulse oximeter, timer
Station 02

HRV Baseline

10 min

5-minute supine HRV recording. Chest strap for accuracy. Capture RMSSD (primary), SDNN, HF power, LF/HF ratio. Apple Watch parallel recording for device calibration.

This is the single most important number. RMSSD reflects vagal tone — the nervous system's recovery capacity.

Polar H10 chest strap, Apple Watch, HRV app (Elite HRV or Kubios)
Station 03

BOLT Score

5 min

Body Oxygen Level Test — normal exhale, then time to first involuntary breathing impulse. Tests CO2 tolerance, which correlates with autonomic flexibility and recovery capacity.

Elite athletes: 30-40s. Below 20s = significant room for improvement. Above 40s = strong CO2 tolerance baseline.

Timer, seated position
Station 04

MAIA-2 Survey

10 min

37-item validated questionnaire measuring 8 dimensions of interoceptive awareness: Noticing, Not-Distracting, Not-Worrying, Attention Regulation, Emotional Awareness, Self-Regulation, Body Listening, Trusting.

Identifies which channels of body awareness are strong vs. underdeveloped. Guides where to focus training.

iPad with MAIA-2 form
Station 05

EEG Baseline

15 min

3 conditions: eyes closed (2 min), eyes open (2 min), simulated cognitive load — countdown task or reaction time challenge (3 min). Capture alpha power, theta power, alpha/theta ratio, frontal asymmetry.

Alpha dominance at rest = good baseline. Theta intrusion under load = cognitive fatigue signature. Frontal asymmetry reveals approach vs. withdrawal tendency.

Muse 2 or Emotiv Insight, laptop with signal processing
Station 06

Resonant Frequency Discovery

15 min

Guided breathing at 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0, 6.5, 7.0 breaths/min — 2 minutes each. Chest strap captures HRV amplitude at each rate. The rate that produces the highest HRV oscillation is his personal resonant frequency.

This number is individual. One driver might resonate at 5.0 bpm, another at 6.5. Getting it wrong means suboptimal training for 6 weeks.

Polar H10, HRV app with pacing, Pausality app for guided pacing
Station 07

HR Response to Max Stress (Squat-Sit)

10 min

Deep squat-to-standing repeated to drive heart rate to near-max. Monitor HR in real time via chest strap. Capture: peak HR achieved, time-to-peak, and then the recovery curve — how fast HR returns to within 10% of resting baseline.

This is the single most race-relevant physical test. Simulates the rapid hemodynamic shifts of getting in and out of the car, G-load transitions, and acute physical stress. Recovery speed = return-to-ready speed between stints.

Polar H10 chest strap, Apple Watch, timer, open floor space
Station 08

Longest Nasal Exhale Expiration

5 min

Full nasal inhale, then timed maximal nasal exhale — slow, controlled, as long as possible. Measures total lung capacity utilization, diaphragmatic control, and the ability to sustain a controlled, extended exhale through the nose.

Directly predicts capacity for extended exhale protocols (4/7/8) and Anesthetist's Breath (10/0/10/0). A driver who can only sustain a 6-second nasal exhale needs a different starting protocol than one who can sustain 15+. Also reveals mouth-breathing patterns and nasal obstruction.

Timer, seated position, nose clear
Station 09

Cardiac Efficiency

10 min

Joules per heartbeat at standardized athletic load. Erg test at fixed wattage — divide power output by heart rate. Higher = more efficient cardiac output. Tracks improvement over the program.

Concept2 erg or Peloton with power meter, chest strap
Station 10

Physical Markers

10 min

Pull-up capacity (grip + upper body endurance — relevant to G-load tolerance). Grip strength (dynamometer, both hands). Basic postural assessment — cervical/thoracic position under simulated steering load.

Grip strength correlates with cardiovascular health and predicts autonomic reserve. Not just fitness — it's a biomarker.

Pull-up bar, grip dynamometer, posture assessment tools
Station 11

Subjective State Battery

5 min

Visual Analog Scales (0-10): Current readiness, current comfort level, typical pre-race anxiety, typical post-race recovery difficulty, sleep quality (last 7 days), energy level.

The numbers matter, but how he feels about his nervous system matters more. This is the baseline for "did it work?"

iPad with VAS forms

Part 2B: SRB-60 Baseline Assessment

The Somnistics Readiness Battery — 5 competency domains, each tested within a 60-second window

The SRB-60 is the standardized assessment battery for autonomic regulation competency. Originally designed for clinical certification, it maps directly to motorsport performance: Can your nervous system do what you need it to do, when you need it, in sixty seconds?

Why at intake: We run the SRB-60 at Week 0 to establish a baseline across all five domains. At Week 6, we run it again. The delta tells us not just whether your metrics improved, but whether your skill improved — can you regulate on demand, under pressure, and return to full performance immediately?
SRB Domain 1

Gap Moment Literacy

60-second event

What it tests: Can you detect that a transition is happening and classify your autonomic state?

Format: Simulated scenarios with embedded transition points. Massimo identifies: "A state shift is occurring now." Then classifies: hyperarousal / hypoarousal / cognitive tunnel / task-ready.

Scoring: Detection accuracy × Classification accuracy × Speed of recognition.

What it catches: The driver who is sympathetically overdrive and doesn't know it — running hot but reading "I'm fine." High confidence, low accuracy.

iPad with scenario prompts, Polar H10 for ground-truth state verification
SRB Domain 2

Micro-Interoceptive Skill

60-second event

What it tests: Can you rapidly sense, label, and calibrate internal body signals?

Format: Timed interoceptive tasks — heartbeat detection (count beats without pulse), breath-rate estimation, body tension mapping — under increasing cognitive load.

Scoring: Accuracy of interoceptive report vs. measured reality (wearable ground truth). At rest AND under simulated load.

What it catches: The driver whose body awareness collapses under stress — accurate in the paddock, blind in the cockpit. Exactly when it matters most.

Polar H10 (ground truth), timer, cognitive load task (dual-task paradigm)
SRB Domain 3

Protocol Selection & Fidelity

60-second event

What it tests: Given a detected state, can you choose the right breathing protocol and execute it correctly?

Format: Randy names a state (e.g., "You're hyperaroused, 5 minutes before getting in the car"). Massimo selects a protocol and executes it. Breath ratios, pacing, attentional focus scored.

Scoring: Selection appropriateness × Execution fidelity. Box breathing isn't the answer to every state. Protocol selection IS applied judgment.

At intake: This will likely score low — he hasn't learned the protocols yet. That's the point. The Week 6 delta is the measure of learning.

Protocol reference card (for Week 0, open-book; for Week 6, from memory)
SRB Domain 4

Measured State Delta

60-second event

What it tests: Did the protocol actually move the body? Did the nervous system respond?

Format: Pre/post biometric comparison across the 60-second window. HRV shift, heart rate change, respiratory rate change.

Scoring: Magnitude of state delta in the intended direction. Selecting an activating protocol and producing a measurable HR increase scores. Producing no change = the protocol didn't land.

What it catches: Going through the motions — perfect breathing form, zero physiological change. Ritual without regulation.

Polar H10 (continuous), Apple Watch, HRV software with 60-second window analysis
SRB Domain 5

Return-to-Task Readiness

60-second event

What it tests: After regulating, can you immediately perform at full cognitive capacity?

Format: Immediately following the protocol, Massimo performs a timed reaction task or decision-making scenario. Speed and accuracy compared to his pre-protocol baseline.

Scoring: Task performance post-protocol vs. baseline. The protocol should restore or improve performance, not impair it. If the driver is drowsy or spaced out after the breathing exercise, Domain 5 fails.

What it catches: The critical one. Regulation without sedation. A breathing protocol that produces calm but impairs reaction time is the wrong tool for motorsport. You need to be sharper after, not sleepier.

iPad with reaction time test, timer, Polar H10
SRB-60 Performance Tiers:
Bronze — Basic competency across all 5 domains (typical Week 0 target)
Silver — Consistent measurable state deltas + rapid protocol selection
Gold — Advanced skill under high-fidelity simulation
Platinum — Expert performance (instructor-level)

Part 3: Protocol Design & App Setup

15 minutes — close the session with a clear plan

Immediate Deliverables at End of Session

Massimo leaves with something in hand, not just data taken from him
  • His personal resonant breathing frequency (exact number)
  • Pausality app installed and configured on Apple Watch
  • First NeuroMinute completed together — he's felt what it does
  • Week 1 daily schedule: which 5 one-minute windows to use
  • Morning HRV tracking protocol explained and first reading taken
  • Next coached session scheduled (within 5-7 days)
The close: "You now have your resonant frequency — that's your number, personal to your nervous system. Over the next 6 weeks, we're going to train your nervous system to use that frequency on demand. Five minutes a day. Every metric we just measured, we measure again at Week 6. You'll see exactly what changed."

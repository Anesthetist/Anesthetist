Core Principles

The science behind the program design
5 × 60s

MED

Your resonant frequency

The research is clear: frequency and brevity beat duration for autonomic adaptation. Five 60-second sessions distributed across the day produce measurably better autonomic changes than a single 30-minute block (You & Laborde et al., 2021). One-minute paced breathing produces measurable HRV changes (Six Dijkstra, McCraty et al., 2019, N=877).

Every protocol is built around your personal resonant breathing frequency — discovered during your assessment. This isn't a generic 4-7-8 pattern. It's the exact rate where your cardiorespiratory system synchronizes and produces maximum HRV amplitude (Lehrer, Vaschillo & Vaschillo, 2000).

The approach: We're not teaching you to relax. We're training your nervous system to regulate the adrenaline you already have — channeling it into precision instead of burning it as physiological waste. Not less intensity. Better-managed intensity.

Week-by-Week Program

Phase 2 — Awareness Under Load

Race-Day Protocol

Designed in Week 5 — personalized to your race schedule and cockpit constraints

Race Weekend Sequence

Every transition is a training surface

Night Before — Pre-Sleep Recovery

Anesthetist's Breath (10/0/10/0) × 5 breaths. Deep vagal activation to maximize sleep quality. Not a sleep aid — giving your nervous system the best possible conditions to repair overnight.

With Apple Watch tracking. Compare sleep HRV to training baseline.

Morning — Baseline Check

Resonant frequency breathing × 1 minute. Morning HRV reading. This is your pit board for the day — tells you where your nervous system is starting from.

If morning HRV is significantly below trend, the day's protocols adjust: more recovery breathing, less activation.

Pre-Race (T-30 min) — Activation Priming

Box breathing (5/5/5/5) × 5 breaths, then natural breathing with Anterocept™ focus: breath → heartbeat → hands → peripheral vision. Priming the nervous system for high-load performance WITHOUT tipping into overdrive.

The goal is not calm. The goal is controlled elevation — sympathetic activation channeled through structured awareness.

In-Cockpit (Formation Lap / Safety Car / Red Flag)

Rapid Reg (3/3/3/3) × 3-5 breaths. Eyes open, hands on wheel. Works in 10-second windows. Prevents sympathetic escalation during waiting periods without dropping alertness.

This is the protocol you can use without anyone knowing you're doing it.

Between Sessions / Stints

Extended exhale (4/7/8) × 5 breaths. Active recovery. The faster you return to autonomic baseline between stints, the more reserve you have for the next one. This is the competitive edge.

Track HRV recovery time between sessions across race weekends. The trend should improve.

Post-Race — Deep Recovery

Anesthetist's Breath (10/0/10/0) × 5 breaths. Full vagal reset. Then resonant frequency breathing × 1 minute. Your nervous system just ran at redline — bring it back to baseline deliberately, not just by waiting for it to happen on its own.

This is where "hours to feel normal" becomes "minutes to feel returned." The single biggest quality-of-life improvement in the program.

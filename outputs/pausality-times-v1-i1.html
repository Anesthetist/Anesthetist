Theory

Somnistics Proposed as Middle-Range Nursing Theory

First formal theoretical framework for autonomic self-regulation in high-acuity clinical practice

Theoretical Research Desk  |  March 20, 2026

In what may become the session's most consequential output, somnistics has been formally articulated as a middle-range nursing theory—a theoretical framework narrow enough to be testable but broad enough to reframe clinical self-care. The theory extends the work of Patricia Benner's novice-to-expert model, Callista Roy's adaptation model, Jean Watson's caring science, Rosemarie Parse's human becoming theory, and Florence Nightingale's environmental theory into a domain none of them addressed: the clinician's own autonomic nervous system as a practice variable.

The framework proposes 13 falsifiable hypotheses, each amenable to the kind of prospective cohort or randomized controlled trial that nursing science demands. Central to the theory is a metaparadigm reframe: the clinician is not merely a cognitive agent delivering care but a physiological system whose autonomic state directly modulates clinical performance, decision-making under stress, and career longevity.

"The clinician is not separate from the physiology they manage. They are a physiological system operating within a physiological system." — Somnistics Theory Framework

This is not an incremental contribution. No existing nursing theory treats the practitioner's heart rate variability, baroreflex sensitivity, or interoceptive accuracy as modifiable practice variables. Somnistics fills that gap with a framework grounded in polyvagal theory, allostatic load research, and the emerging science of interoception—then translates it into the nursing process itself.

Science

Biomedical Validation Sweep Stress-Tests 44 Concepts Against Current Literature

Research Division  |  March 19–20, 2026

In the most rigorous evidence review the vault has undergone, 44 SRL concepts were systematically tested against the current biomedical literature through 12 BioMistral queries and 150+ PubMed articles. The results were sobering in places, vindicating in others, and universally useful.

Polyvagal Theory required significant reframing after Stephen Porges' core phylogenetic claims were challenged by Grossman's 2026 review. The vault now carries the nuanced position: PVT's clinical heuristics remain useful, but the evolutionary narrative is contested. Binaural beat entrainment fared worse—only 5 of 14 controlled studies showed significant effects, and those with the strongest designs showed the smallest effects. The concept was retained but downgraded.

"Interoceptive Simulation Hypothesis: zero competitors in the literature. The concept appears to be original." — BioMistral Validation Report, Wave 3

The brightest signal: the Interoceptive Simulation Hypothesis (ISH), Randy's proposal that interoceptive training works partly by building predictive models of one's own autonomic responses, returned zero matches in the competitive literature. No one has published this framing. Three costed study designs—targeting VCU as a research partner—are now ready for proposal, ranging from a $45K pilot to a $280K RCT.

Perhaps most practically, the sweep resolved a paradox that had been nagging the theoretical framework: how can breath-based regulation work when cognitive reappraisal theory says top-down processing should come first? The answer, supported by Arch & Craske (2006) and Garland (2015): breath first stabilizes the autonomic platform, then reappraisal becomes tractable. Bottom-up enables top-down. The sequence matters.

Research

Neurotagging: The Standard That Doesn't Exist Yet

Standards Research  |  March 20, 2026

A systematic mapping of 10 existing neuroscience and health data standards—BIDS, NWB, CDISC, FHIR, IEEE 1752, and five others—revealed a consistent gap: none of them connect a specific somatic or autonomic intervention to a specific neural target to a specific clinical outcome. Each standard handles one or two legs of the triad. None handles all three.

The closest precursors are Klaus Grawe's neuropsychotherapy framework, which maps therapeutic mechanisms to neural systems but lacks a tagging ontology, and Lisa Feldman Barrett's EPIC model (Embodied Predictive Interoception Coding), which provides the best available mechanism for how interoceptive predictions drive autonomic regulation but stops short of an intervention taxonomy.

"The intervention-target-outcome triad is the missing standard. Nobody has built it. SRL is in position to propose it." — Neurotagging Standards Analysis

The neurotagging concept, as formalized in the vault, proposes exactly this standard: a structured vocabulary that links what you do (resonant breathing at 5.5 breaths/min) to what it targets (baroreflex loop, vagal afferents) to what it produces (increased HRV coherence, reduced amygdalar reactivity). If adopted, it would allow the first systematic comparison of somatic interventions across studies, populations, and modalities.

## Top 3 Findings by Impact on SRL's Thesis

### 1. The Polyvagal Theory Debate Escalates (CRITICAL)

Grossman et al.'s 39-signatory critique declaring PVT "untenable" and Porges' rebuttal represent the most significant scientific challenge to polyvagal theory to date. **SRL must audit all materials referencing PVT** — not because the underlying interventions are invalidated (they aren't), but because relying on PVT-specific framing (ventral vagal state, dorsal vagal shutdown, neuroception hierarchy) carries increasing scientific risk. The interventions (HRV biofeedback, resonant breathing, interoceptive training) stand on their own evidence. **Recommendation:** Shift language toward mechanism-level descriptions of autonomic regulation and acknowledge the ongoing PVT debate where relevant.

### 2. Biofeedback for Healthcare Workers — The Field Is Wide Open

Cantone et al.'s systematic review confirms that biofeedback shows consistent stress reduction benefits for healthcare workers, but finds the evidence base is methodologically weak and heterogeneous. **This is Pausality's window.** No dominant protocol or product has captured this space. The lack of standardized approaches is exactly where Pausality's structured GMT methodology can differentiate. The review essentially maps the gap that Pausality is designed to fill.

### 3. Cognition–Autonomic Coupling Is Bidirectional

The 2026 Frontiers in Medicine paper demonstrates that cognitive performance and autonomic regulation are coupled via the central autonomic network. Higher cognitive scores predict better autonomic flexibility, and vice versa. **This directly validates SRL's core thesis:** training autonomic regulation enhances cognitive performance, not just reduces burnout. Pausality isn't a wellness app — it's a performance optimization system with wellness as a side effect.

## Findings That CHALLENGE or COMPLICATE SRL's Position

1. **Breathwork technique selection may not matter much** — The 2026 review suggests most techniques share core mechanisms. If true, Pausality's specific resonant-frequency protocol doesn't differentiate on efficacy alone. SRL must differentiate on *personalization via biofeedback* and *integration into a structured training system*, not on the breathing technique itself.

2. **HRV-burnout relationship is inconsistent** — The burnout biomarker systematic review (111 studies, 36 biomarkers) found no single biomarker sufficient for burnout diagnosis. Only 6 of 17 HRV studies showed the expected negative correlation. SRL should not overclaim HRV as a standalone burnout indicator — the composite battery approach (SRB-60) is the correct strategy.

3. **Three recovery nights needed after sleep curtailment** — The sleep-HRV meta-analysis shows autonomic homeostasis requires at least 3 nights of recovery after cumulative sleep loss. This has uncomfortable implications for CRNA call schedules and raises the question of whether micro-interventions (like NeuroMinutes) can meaningfully offset sleep debt, or whether schedule reform is the real lever.

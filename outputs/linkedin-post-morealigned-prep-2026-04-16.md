## Draft

A paper crossed my desk this week from the University of Alabama at Birmingham. Lopez Blanco and Tyler synthesize thirty years of research on the vagus nerve in Frontiers in Psychology, covering elite sport, anesthesia, meditation, and emergency medicine.

The finding that has stayed with me: more parasympathetic activity is not always better.

We have been taught to believe that "rest and digest" is the destination. Slow the heart, calm the mind, lower the numbers. Endurance athletes who push this too far develop what researchers now call parasympathetic overreaching. Elevated resting vagal activity, persistent fatigue, diminished performance. The body is not broken. The system has lost its range.

The literature is converging on a different organizing concept: autonomic flexibility rather than parasympathetic dominance.

Cardiac vagal control operates across three states. Resting tone is the reservoir. Reactivity is how quickly the brake releases when challenge arrives. Recovery is how fast it re-engages afterward. This is Laborde's Vagal Tank Theory, and it reframes the conversation. The question is not whether your HRV is high. The question is whether your autonomic nervous system can move.

Precision motor studies of dart-throwing under pressure make this visible. The athletes who score best are not the ones with the highest resting vagal tone. They are the ones whose vagal brake releases most crisply when the task demands it, then re-engages fastest afterward.

This maps onto what I see in the operating room and in the clinicians I train. The skill is not staying calm. The skill is shifting states deliberately. Upregulating when the case demands precision, downregulating before the next patient, doing both without conscious effort.

I describe this as a shift from relaxation to regulation. Relaxation is a state. Regulation is a capacity.

On Monday I am teaching a session for MoreAligned on interoceptive literacy, learning to read the body you have always had but may never have been formally taught to listen to. The paper I mentioned will sit in the references. For anyone working at the edge of what their system can handle, the ability to shift states is not a wellness feature. It is infrastructure.

Lopez Blanco C, Tyler WJ. The vagus nerve: a cornerstone for mental health and performance optimization in recreation and elite sports. Front Psychol. 2025;16:1639866. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1639866

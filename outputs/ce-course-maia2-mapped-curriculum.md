# Category A CE Course: Interoceptive Training for CRNA Performance

## MAIA-2 Mapped, Culturally Adaptive, 21-Session Curriculum

**Program Title:** Interoceptive Training for CRNA Performance & Well-Being
**Credits:** 3.0 AANA Category A Contact Hours
**Format:** 21 guided audio sessions (60 seconds each) + didactic content + assessments
**Delivery:** Web-based LMS (self-paced, 3-week minimum)
**Faculty:** Randy Graybeal, MSN, CRNA — 28 years clinical anesthesia

---

## Design Philosophy

### Core Principle: Awareness Before Technique

Every competitor starts with technique ("breathe 4-7-8"). This course starts with awareness ("can you notice your current state?"). The correct sequence is:

1. **Awareness** — Can you notice? (Most clinicians cannot — interoceptive suppression from training)
2. **Interoception** — Can you feel your breath, heartbeat, tension patterns?
3. **Breathing Pattern** — Now apply a specific pattern to what you can feel
4. **Vagal Toning** — The physiological effect emerges from the foundation

### MAIA-2 as Backbone

The 8 MAIA-2 subscales (Mehling et al. 2012) provide the validated measurement framework. Each session targets specific subscales. Pre/post assessment shows measurable improvement — not just "feeling calmer" but "perceiving the body more accurately AND regulating better."

### Cultural Adaptivity — Built Into the Architecture

CRNAs in the United States represent remarkable cultural diversity — Filipino, Indian, Nigerian, Vietnamese, Korean, Eastern European, Caribbean, Latin American, and many other backgrounds. The course must work across all of them.

**The problem with most mindfulness/breathing programs:**
- Assume Western individualist framing ("your personal journey")
- Use imagery from specific spiritual traditions (lotus, chakras, white light)
- Rely heavily on breath focus (some cultures associate deep breathing with distress, not calm)
- Command-based language ("close your eyes," "relax") triggers resistance in high-authority cultures
- Assume sitting meditation posture (inaccessible in OR scrubs between cases)

**Our design principles:**

#### Language Layer
- **Invitational, never directive:** "Notice..." not "Feel..." / "Allow..." not "Do..."
- **Physiological framing, not spiritual:** "Your diaphragm is a muscle" not "breathe into your center"
- **Clinical metaphors:** "Like titrating a medication" / "Like checking your airway cart" — language CRNAs already trust
- **Avoid absolute claims:** "This may support..." not "This will..." (FDA + cultural humility)

#### Anchor Diversity
Not everyone anchors to breath. The course offers multiple entry points per session:
- **Breath** (primary) — diaphragmatic, nasal, paced
- **Tactile** (alternative) — hand on belly, feet on floor, fingertip pulse
- **Auditory** (alternative) — ambient sound awareness, heartbeat rhythm
- **Proprioceptive** (alternative) — posture, weight distribution, muscle tension
- **Visual** (alternative) — soft gaze, peripheral vision, light awareness

Each session names one primary anchor and one alternative. Learners choose what works for their body and background.

#### Collectivist + Individualist Framing
Every session includes both:
- **Individual benefit:** "This supports your focus and decision-making"
- **Collective benefit:** "When you're regulated, your team and patients benefit"

This dual framing resonates whether the learner comes from an individualist (American mainstream) or collectivist (many Asian, African, Latin American) cultural context.

#### Trauma-Informed Design
- Never command eye closure ("If comfortable, allow your eyes to soften")
- Never assume physical safety ("If any position feels uncomfortable, adjust freely")
- Provide exit language in every session ("You can pause or stop at any time")
- Normalize difficulty ("If your attention drifts, that IS the practice — noticing is the skill")

#### Language Translation Readiness
Scripts are designed for clean translation:
- Short sentences (average 8-12 words)
- No idioms, slang, or culturally-specific metaphors
- Active voice, concrete nouns
- Universal sensory references (air, ground, rhythm, warmth)
- Marked pauses (... notation) serve as natural translation break points

## 21-Session Curriculum Map

### Phase 1: AWARENESS (Sessions 1-7)

**MAIA-2 Primary Targets:** Noticing + Attention Regulation
**Anterocept Domain:** 1 (Baseline Interoceptive Awareness)
**Neural Targets:** Posterior insula (raw sensory input), diaphragm proprioception
**Cultural Note:** Most accessible phase — physiological framing, minimal interpretation required

| Session | Title | Technique | MAIA-2 Focus | Anchor Options |
|---------|-------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Arriving** | Breath awareness only — NO manipulation | Noticing | Breath / Tactile (feet on floor) |
| 2 | **Depth** | Diaphragmatic breathing — belly expansion | Noticing | Breath / Tactile (hand on belly) |
| 3 | **Rhythm** | Box breathing (4-4-4-4) | Attention Regulation | Breath / Auditory (counting) |
| 4 | **Resonance** | Coherent breathing (5-5) | Attention Regulation | Breath / Auditory (heartbeat sync) |
| 5 | **Release** | Pursed lip exhale — controlled output | Noticing + Not-Distracting | Breath / Tactile (lip sensation) |
| 6 | **Balance** | Equal breathing (4-4) | Attention Regulation | Breath / Proprioceptive (posture) |
| 7 | **Precision** | Diaphragmatic with dual hand placement | Noticing + Attention Regulation | Breath / Tactile (chest vs. belly) |

**Phase 1 Learning Objective:** Demonstrate ability to notice breath without manipulating it, sustain attention to body sensation for 60 seconds, and identify at least one personal anchor point.

**Phase 1 Assessment:** MAIA-2 subscales Noticing + Attention Regulation (pre/post), breath awareness journaling prompt.

---

### Phase 2: FOUNDATION (Sessions 8-14)

**MAIA-2 Primary Targets:** Body Listening + Emotional Awareness + Not-Distracting
**Anterocept Domain:** 2 (Layered Interoception with Emotional Integration)
**Neural Targets:** Anterior insula (emotional-interoceptive integration), ACC (conflict monitoring), amygdala (emotional labeling)
**Cultural Note:** Emotional awareness varies significantly across cultures. Some cultures externalize (express readily), others internalize (contain). The course validates BOTH as adaptive strategies, not pathologizing either.

| Session | Title | Technique | MAIA-2 Focus | Anchor Options |
|---------|-------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| 8 | **Listening** | Body scan — systematic attention sweep | Body Listening | Tactile (sequential body regions) |
| 9 | **Naming** | Emotional labeling during exhale | Emotional Awareness | Breath / Auditory (internal voice) |
| 10 | **Staying** | Extended exhale (4-7) with discomfort tolerance | Not-Distracting | Breath / Tactile (hands) |
| 11 | **Alternating** | Alternate nostril awareness (not full Nadi Shodhana) | Body Listening + Attention Regulation | Breath / Tactile (nostril sensation) |
| 12 | **Connecting** | Heartbeat + breath coupling | Emotional Awareness + Body Listening | Cardiac / Breath (dual anchor) |
| 13 | **Grounding** | Feet-to-crown sequential awareness | Not-Distracting + Body Listening | Proprioceptive / Tactile |
| 14 | **Integrating** | Breath + emotion + body scan combined | Emotional Awareness + Body Listening | Multi-anchor (learner chooses 2) |

**Phase 2 Learning Objective:** Identify the connection between body sensation and emotional state, sustain attention to uncomfortable sensation without distraction for 30 seconds, and demonstrate ability to label one emotional state during breathing practice.

**Phase 2 Assessment:** MAIA-2 subscales Body Listening + Emotional Awareness + Not-Distracting (pre/post), 2 clinical scenario responses (see below).

**Phase 2 Cultural Caveat — Emotional Labeling:**
Emotional vocabulary varies dramatically across languages. English has ~6 basic emotion words most people use readily. Some languages (e.g., German, Japanese) have specific words for emotional states that English lacks. Others (some Southeast Asian languages) use fewer discrete emotion categories.

The course uses **physiological labeling** as the primary approach ("I notice tightness in my chest" / "My shoulders are raised") rather than requiring emotion words ("I feel anxious"). This is:
- Culturally neutral (body sensations are universal)
- More accurate interoceptively (body precedes label)
- Consistent with somatic marker theory (Damasio 1996)
- Accessible to alexithymic individuals (who struggle with emotion words but can detect body signals)

### Phase 3: MASTERY (Sessions 15-21)

**MAIA-2 Primary Targets:** Self-Regulation + Trusting + Not-Worrying
**Anterocept Domain:** 3 (Integrated Interoceptive-Exteroceptive Awareness)
**Neural Targets:** dlPFC (top-down regulation), vagal-social circuits (co-regulation), mirror neurons (team coherence)
**Cultural Note:** "Trusting the body" is the hardest subscale cross-culturally. Medical training in ALL cultures teaches body-signal suppression. This phase explicitly rebuilds trust.

| Session | Title | Technique | MAIA-2 Focus | Anchor Options |
|---------|-------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| 15 | **Choosing** | Physiological sigh (double inhale + long exhale) | Self-Regulation | Breath (physiological sigh) |
| 16 | **Activating** | Energizing breath — short sharp inhales | Self-Regulation + Not-Worrying | Breath / Proprioceptive (energy) |
| 17 | **Recovering** | Post-case reset — extended exhale (5-8) | Self-Regulation + Trusting | Breath / Tactile (ground contact) |
| 18 | **Expanding** | Peripheral vision + breath (Castle-Field) | Trusting + Not-Worrying | Visual / Breath (dual anchor) |
| 19 | **Attuning** | Team breath — synchronized exhale visualization | Trusting + Self-Regulation | Breath / Auditory (imagined team) |
| 20 | **Sustaining** | Full multi-anchor integration (breath + body + sound) | All Phase 3 subscales | Multi-anchor (learner chooses 3) |
| 21 | **Arriving Home** | Open awareness — no technique, just presence | Trusting | Learner's chosen anchor |

**Phase 3 Learning Objective:** Apply appropriate regulation technique (wind-up OR wind-down) to a clinical scenario, demonstrate ability to return to baseline within 90 seconds of a stressor, and report increased trust in body signals as a clinical decision-making resource.

**Phase 3 Assessment:** MAIA-2 full battery (all 8 subscales, pre/post comparison), 3 clinical scenario applications, 15-item knowledge exam.

**Phase 3 Cultural Caveat — Trusting the Body:**
"Trusting" means different things in different cultural contexts:
- In Western biomedical culture: trusting the body means accepting subjective sensation as valid data alongside objective monitors
- In Traditional Chinese Medicine context: trusting the body is assumed (qi flow, meridians) but the LANGUAGE differs
- In cultures with strong religious frameworks: the body may be viewed as separate from self — trust must be reframed as "the body provides useful information" not "the body is wise"
- In cultures that experienced medical racism: trusting the body may mean trusting YOURSELF in a system that historically dismissed your symptoms

The course frames "Trusting" as: **"Your body provides reliable information that supports your clinical decision-making."** This is:
- Clinically grounded (connects to patient safety)
- Culturally neutral (doesn't require spiritual or philosophical buy-in)
- Professionally validating (positions body awareness as clinical competency, not vulnerability)

## Assessment Battery

### Pre-Course (Baseline)
- MAIA-2 (37 items, 8 subscales) — interoceptive awareness baseline
- PSS-10 — perceived stress
- BOLT score — CO₂ tolerance (max comfortable breath-hold after normal exhale)
- Resting HR (self-measured, 60-second count)

### Weekly Check-ins
- 3-item MAIA-2 pulse (Noticing, Self-Regulation, Trusting — one item each)
- Session completion verification
- Brief reflection prompt (1-2 sentences, not graded)

### Post-Course (Outcome)
- MAIA-2 full battery (all 8 subscales — compare to baseline)
- PSS-10 (compare to baseline)
- BOLT score (compare to baseline)
- Resting HR (compare to baseline)
- 15-item knowledge exam (80% pass threshold)
- 5 clinical scenario applications (see below)

### 5 Clinical Scenario Applications

Each scenario presents a clinical moment and asks: (a) what autonomic state are you likely in? (b) which technique from the course would you apply? (c) what body signals would tell you it's working?

1. **Pre-induction for a high-risk cardiac case.** You have 90 seconds before entering the OR. Your heart rate is elevated and your hands are slightly trembling. → Wind-down technique selection + self-assessment

2. **Between cases, 10 hours into shift.** Your previous case had unexpected hemorrhage. You feel foggy but can't name why. Room turnover is 8 minutes. → Recovery technique + residual sympathetic bleed recognition

3. **Difficult intubation — second attempt needed.** Your first laryngoscopy attempt failed. The surgeon is watching. You have approximately 30 seconds before SpO₂ drops. → Activation technique (NOT calming) + performance under acute pressure

4. **Post-shift, driving home.** You managed a pediatric trauma case with a bad outcome. You notice you're gripping the steering wheel. Your jaw is clenched. → Self-regulation technique + somatic marker recognition + boundary between work and home

5. **Team conflict.** An attending made a dismissive comment about your clinical judgment during a case. You notice heat in your face and a desire to respond sharply. The patient is stable but you need to continue collaborating. → Co-regulation + emotional awareness + professional self-regulation

**Scoring:** Each scenario scored on (a) accurate autonomic state identification (b) appropriate technique selection (c) body signal awareness. 3 points per scenario, 15 points total, 12/15 to pass.

## 15-Item Knowledge Exam

1. What is interoception?
2. Name 3 of the 8 MAIA-2 subscales.
3. Why does the course start with awareness rather than breathing technique?
4. What is the approximate resonant breathing frequency for most adults?
5. What does RMSSD measure?
6. Describe the physiological sigh and when you would use it.
7. What is "residual sympathetic bleed" between cases?
8. Name two body signals that indicate sympathetic activation.
9. What is the difference between a "wind-up" and "wind-down" technique?
10. Why is nasal breathing preferred over mouth breathing in regulation protocols?
11. How does breathing at ~6 breaths/minute affect the baroreflex?
12. What role does the anterior insula play in interoceptive awareness?
13. Describe one way regulated clinicians affect their team's autonomic state.
14. What is a "gap moment" and how many occur in a typical CRNA shift?
15. How would you explain the benefit of this training to a skeptical colleague without using wellness language?

**Pass threshold:** 80% (12/15 correct)

## Cultural Adaptation Framework

For future translation/localization, each session carries metadata:

```yaml
cultural_adaptation:
  anchor_alternatives: [breath, tactile, auditory, proprioceptive, visual]
  imagery_type: universal  # nature, light, rhythm — no religious/cultural specifics
  emotional_vocabulary: physiological  # body sensations, not emotion labels
  authority_framing: invitational  # suggestion, not command
  benefit_framing: dual  # individual AND collective
  trauma_informed: true
  translation_ready: true  # short sentences, no idioms, marked pauses
  localization_notes: ""  # field for translator to add culture-specific adaptations
```

### Known Cultural Considerations by Region

| Region | Key Consideration | Adaptation |
|--------|-------------------|------------|
| Filipino CRNA community | Strong collective identity; "kapwa" (shared inner self) | Emphasize team/collective benefit; frame as caring for others through self-regulation |
| Indian subcontinent | Pranayama familiarity; may have existing breath practice | Acknowledge prior knowledge; position as clinical precision layer on top of existing practice |
| Nigerian/West African | Strong verbal/oral tradition; body-based expression | Audio delivery is natural fit; include call-and-response options; validate expressive style |
| Korean/East Asian | Emphasis on emotional composure; "nunchi" (reading the room) | Frame body awareness as enhanced professional composure; connect to social attunement skills |
| Latin American | Warmth + expressiveness valued; familismo (family orientation) | Emphasize family benefit ("bring this home to your family"); validate emotional expression |
| Eastern European | Directness valued; skepticism of "soft" interventions | Lead with evidence + mechanism; use clinical/physiological language exclusively |
| Military/veteran CRNAs | "Tactical" framing; resistance to vulnerability language | Frame as "performance maintenance" and "tactical reset"; avoid any wellness terminology |

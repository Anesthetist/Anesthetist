## Definition

Affective neuroscience studies the neural and physiological substrates of emotion — how feelings arise from body-brain interactions, how emotions are generated (or constructed), and how emotional states are regulated. It bridges interoception (body signal) to meaning (what the signal means for the organism), making it the interpretive layer between SRL's physiological interventions and behavioral outcomes.

---

## Sub-Questions (Chapter Outline)

1. **What is an emotion?** — The basic emotions debate (Ekman, Izard) vs. constructionist accounts (Barrett, Russell). Dimensional models (valence/arousal). Why this matters for SRL: if emotions are constructed from interoceptive signals, then training interoception changes emotional experience.

2. **Neural circuitry of emotion** — Amygdala, insula, anterior cingulate, prefrontal cortex. The limbic system concept and its problems. Panksepp's subcortical affective systems (SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY).

3. **Constructed emotion theory** — Barrett's "How Emotions Are Made." Emotion as prediction, not reaction. Affect as the brain's summary of the body's energy budget. Emotional granularity and its clinical implications.

4. **Emotion regulation** — Gross's process model (situation selection → modification → attentional deployment → cognitive change → response modulation). Reappraisal vs. suppression. Interoceptive regulation as a missing dimension in the Gross model.

5. **The feeling body** — Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis. Feelings as neural maps of body states. The as-if body loop. How interoceptive accuracy modulates emotional decision-making.

6. **Fear conditioning and extinction** — LeDoux's dual-pathway model (low road/high road). Fear learning, extinction, reconsolidation. Clinical relevance: how trauma neuroscience grows from this soil.

7. **Emotional granularity and alexithymia** — The spectrum from emotional blindness to high granularity. MAIA-2 connection: interoceptive sensibility predicts emotional awareness. Toronto Alexithymia Scale.

8. **Autonomic signatures of emotion** — Do discrete emotions have distinct physiological patterns? Kreibig (2010) meta-analysis. HRV and emotional states. Why SRL's HRV-BF work is affect-work whether or not it's labeled that way.

9. **Affect and time** — Emotional states distort time perception (Wittmann connection). Negative affect accelerates subjective time. Positive/flow states slow it. Bridge to D19.

10. **Clinical applications** — Emotion-focused therapy (Greenberg), acceptance and commitment therapy (Hayes), dialectical behavior therapy (Linehan). How interoceptive interventions fit the emotion regulation landscape.

## SRL Bridge Points

Why this discipline matters for SRL specifically:
- **If Barrett is right** (constructed emotion), then interoceptive training literally changes what emotions a person can construct. This is SRL's strongest theoretical claim.
- **Emotional granularity** predicts resilience under stress — directly relevant to CRNA performance.
- **The Gross model's gap:** emotion regulation theory underweights body-based regulation. SRL fills that gap.
- **Anterocept** is an SRL-original concept that lives at the intersection of interoception and affective prediction. It needs stronger evidence anchoring from this discipline.

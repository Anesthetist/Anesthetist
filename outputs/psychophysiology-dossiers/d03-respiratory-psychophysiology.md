## Definition

Respiratory psychophysiology studies how breathing patterns both reflect and regulate psychological states — the bidirectional link between respiration, autonomic function, affect, and cognition. It spans from respiratory physiology (gas exchange, chemoreceptors, breathing mechanics) through breath-based interventions (resonant breathing, Buteyko, pranayama) to the clinical science of breathing retraining. SRL's intervention stack is built on this discipline.

---

## Sub-Questions (Chapter Outline)

1. **Respiratory physiology fundamentals** — Mechanics of ventilation, gas exchange, hemoglobin-oxygen dissociation curve, Bohr effect. Chemoreceptors (central, peripheral). Respiratory control centers (medullary, pontine). What every SRL practitioner should understand about why breathing works.

2. **Breathing and autonomic regulation** — The respiratory gate: inspiration (sympathetic bias) vs. expiration (parasympathetic bias). Extended exhalation as vagal activation. Respiratory sinus arrhythmia as the embodiment of cardiorespiratory coupling.

3. **CO₂ tolerance and the Bohr effect** — Hyperventilation → hypocapnia → vasoconstriction → reduced O₂ delivery. The Bohr effect: CO₂ facilitates O₂ release. Why CO₂ tolerance is a legitimate physiological training target. BOLT score as metric (McKeown).

4. **Resonant breathing frequency** — Individual resonant frequency (~0.1 Hz / ~6 bpm ± 1). Lehrer's method for finding it. Vaschillo's mechanism. Why it maximizes baroreflex stimulation and HRV amplitude. Bridge to D02.

5. **Buteyko method** — Reduced breathing, nasal breathing, BOLT score. Evidence base (modest for asthma, mixed for other conditions). Where it overlaps with and diverges from SRL's approach.

6. **Pranayama traditions** — Alternate nostril breathing (nadi shodhana), kapalabhati, bhastrika, ujjayi. Physiological mechanisms where known. Streeter's GABA hypothesis. Scientific evidence for specific pranayama techniques.

7. **Diaphragmatic breathing retraining** — Mechanics of diaphragmatic vs. thoracic breathing. Why diaphragmatic blindness develops (SRL original concept). Clinical protocols for breathing retraining.

8. **Breathing and emotion** — Philippot et al. (2002): breathing patterns associated with discrete emotions. Voluntary breathing pattern change induces emotional states. The bidirectional breath-emotion link.

9. **Sighing, yawning, and spontaneous breathing patterns** — Physiological sighs (Li & Bhatt, 2020; Huberman popularization). Double-inhale sigh as rapid vagal activation. Why these involuntary patterns are regulatory.

10. **Breathing disorders and dysfunctional breathing** — Hyperventilation syndrome, breathing pattern disorders, paradoxical breathing. Prevalence in chronic stress populations. Clinical assessment (Nijmegen Questionnaire).

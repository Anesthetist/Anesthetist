## Definition

Autonomic neuroscience studies the structure and function of the autonomic nervous system (ANS) — the sympathetic, parasympathetic, and enteric divisions that regulate involuntary physiological processes: heart rate, respiration, digestion, pupil dilation, and immune function. It encompasses Porges's polyvagal theory, Thayer's neurovisceral integration model, and the clinical science of autonomic dysregulation. This is SRL's home discipline.

---

## Sub-Questions (Chapter Outline)

1. **Autonomic architecture** — Sympathetic (thoracolumbar), parasympathetic (craniosacral), enteric. Preganglionic/postganglionic neurons. Neurotransmitters (acetylcholine, norepinephrine). Dual innervation and reciprocal control.

2. **Polyvagal theory** — Porges's three hierarchical circuits: ventral vagal (social engagement), sympathetic (fight/flight), dorsal vagal (freeze/collapse). Phylogenetic basis. Neuroception. The "vagal brake." Criticisms and current status (Grossman 2023/2026 critiques).

3. **Neurovisceral integration model** — Thayer & Lane (2000): the central autonomic network (CAN). Prefrontal cortex → amygdala → NTS → vagus. HRV as index of prefrontal-subcortical communication. Why high resting HRV predicts cognitive flexibility, emotional regulation, and health.

4. **Vagal tone measurement** — RMSSD, HF power, pNN50. RSA as parasympathetic index. The cardiac vagal tone concept. Respiratory confounds. How to measure vagal tone validly in clinical and research settings.

5. **Autonomic flexibility** — The capacity to shift between states (not stuck in sympathetic or parasympathetic). Autonomic flexibility as resilience marker. SRL's "regulation, not relaxation" maps directly here.

6. **Baroreflex function** — Arterial baroreceptors, baroreflex sensitivity (BRS). Baroreflex stimulation through resonant breathing. How the baroreflex mediates the HRV-BF training effect. Bridge to D02 (Cardiovascular).

7. **Central autonomic network** — CAN anatomy: insular cortex, anterior cingulate, amygdala, hypothalamus, periaqueductal gray, NTS, nucleus ambiguus. How the brain controls the body's autonomic state.

8. **Autonomic dysfunction in disease** — Dysautonomia, POTS, autonomic neuropathy. Autonomic dysfunction as marker of chronic stress, aging, metabolic disease. Why healthcare workers are at risk.

9. **Autonomic pharmacology** — Beta-blockers, anticholinergics, alpha-agonists. The anesthesia connection: CRNAs manipulate the ANS pharmacologically every day. How SRL's non-pharmacological approach complements (not replaces) pharmacology.

10. **Vagal stimulation modalities** — Invasive VNS, transcutaneous VNS (taVNS), respiratory vagal stimulation, cold exposure, diving reflex. Evidence hierarchy. Where HRV-BF sits among vagal stimulation methods.

## Definition

Trauma neuroscience studies how overwhelming experiences alter the brain, body, and nervous system — disrupting autonomic regulation, memory consolidation, interoceptive processing, and the sense of safety. It spans from acute stress disorder through complex PTSD to the secondary/vicarious trauma that healthcare providers accumulate over careers. For SRL, this discipline grounds the trauma-informed design principle and explains why body-based (bottom-up) interventions succeed where cognitive (top-down) interventions often fail.

## Sub-Questions (Chapter Outline)

1. **The body keeps the score** — Van der Kolk's central thesis: trauma lives in the body, not just the mind. Somatic markers of unresolved trauma. Why talk therapy alone is insufficient for trauma resolution. The clinical evidence for body-based approaches.

2. **Polyvagal lens on trauma** — Porges's three-circuit model as trauma framework. Dorsal vagal shutdown (freeze/collapse). Ventral vagal as safety state. Neuroception (unconscious threat detection). How polyvagal theory reframes trauma from "what happened to you" to "what your nervous system learned."

3. **Somatic Experiencing** — Levine's pendulation model. Titrated discharge of trapped survival energy. Felt sense. The freeze response as incomplete defense. How SE differs from exposure therapy.

4. **Sensorimotor Psychotherapy** — Ogden's integration of body-oriented and psychodynamic approaches. Sensorimotor processing. Window of tolerance (Siegel). Tracking body sensation as therapeutic technique.

5. **Developmental trauma and attachment** — Schore's right-brain model of attachment. How early relational trauma shapes autonomic development. Disorganized attachment as a polyvagal problem. Bridge to D21 (Developmental).

6. **Second-victim phenomenon** — Healthcare providers as trauma survivors. Wu (2000) original paper. Institutional betrayal. Why CRNAs accumulate vicarious trauma without recognition or support. SRL's second-victim concept note already exists.

7. **Neuroplasticity of trauma and recovery** — Amygdala hyperactivation, hippocampal atrophy, prefrontal hypofunction. PTSD as a disorder of neural network dysregulation. How HRV-BF, meditation, and body-based practices reverse these patterns (evidence from neuroimaging).

8. **Trauma-sensitive intervention design** — Choice, predictability, graded exposure, opt-out. Why SRL's "titration to effect" principle is trauma-informed by design. Contraindication protocols for interoceptive interventions with trauma populations.

9. **Collective and cultural trauma** — Intergenerational trauma transmission (epigenetic and behavioral). Moral injury in healthcare. How systemic stress differs from individual trauma.

10. **Landscape of trauma treatments** — EMDR, prolonged exposure, CPT, somatic experiencing, MDMA-assisted therapy, neurofeedback, yoga for trauma. Where SRL's approach sits in this landscape.

## SRL Bridge Points

- **Trauma-informed design is already canonical** — but the evidence base beneath it is a ghost. Phase 3 must give it substance.
- **Bottom-up regulation** is SRL's core method. Trauma neuroscience explains *why* it works: the brainstem and body hold trauma that the prefrontal cortex cannot reach through cognition alone.
- **Second-victim phenomenon** is SRL's CRNA-specific entry point into trauma. Wu's paper needs a proper evidence note. The concept that "your provider is also hurt" is SRL's differentiator from generic wellness apps.
- **Contraindication protocols** for interoceptive interventions with trauma populations are clinically essential and currently undefined in the vault. Phase 3 must address this.
- **Window of tolerance** maps directly to SRL's titration-to-effect principle: work within the window, expand it gradually.

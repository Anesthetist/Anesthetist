# Investor Quick-Response Sheet — Competitive Objection Handling

**Purpose:** When an investor asks "What about [X]?" — here's the 3-sentence answer.
**Last updated:** March 15, 2026

---

## Tier 1: Direct Competitors

### "What about Breathwrk?"
Peloton acquired them for $2.2M in October 2025 — after they'd raised $7.7M from Sapphire, Bessemer, and DCM. A breathing app without clinical validation, biometric feedback, or enterprise distribution sold for less than a seed round. That's the ceiling for what we're *not* building.

### "What about Climatic?"
Climatic raised $10M from Lerer Hippeau to pioneer "daily lung health" — their product is a mucociliary clearance device with Mount Sinai backing. They're respiratory physiology (lung cleaning), we're ANS regulation (nervous system training). Same "breathing" label, completely different mechanism, market, and customer.

### "What about HeartMath?"
HeartMath invented HRV coherence biofeedback 30 years ago and built a strong clinical certification program. But their technology hasn't evolved — they still require a proprietary sensor and their enterprise offering is training-based, not software-embedded. We build on their science with modern delivery: Apple Watch, 60-second format, AI personalization, clinical workflow integration.

### "What about NEUROFIT?"
NEUROFIT is a somatic exercise app using phone camera biometrics — 60K users, no disclosed funding. They use "Nervous System Trainer" language but deliver 3-5 minute somatic exercises, not 60-second breathwork with wearable biofeedback. No clinical workflow integration, no healthcare professional focus, no Apple Watch integration. Similar language, fundamentally different product.

### "What about Moonbird?"
Belgian startup, $1.74M raised, handheld breathing device with PPG sensor. Consumer-focused, no enterprise play, no clinical validation beyond a pilot study. Expanding to US market but competing on hardware — a difficult path, as Oxa's recent acquisition and consumer sunset demonstrated.

### "What about Prana Labs / Vayu?"
Most direct product analog — AI-guided breathwork via Apple Watch with haptic feedback. But they've raised only $150K, are based in Vancouver, and have no clinical validation or enterprise distribution. If they execute well, they could be an acqui-hire target, but they're pre-product-market-fit.

## Tier 2: Platform Competitors

### "What about Calm?"
Calm's revenue dropped 24% in 2025 to $210M and they lost 500K subscribers. They're now pursuing brand licensing — candles, partnerships, IP deals. Calm Health (enterprise/clinical) is a separate bet that hasn't gained traction. The mindfulness app model is compressing. We're not a mindfulness app — we're precision ANS regulation with biometric verification for high-stakes professionals.

### "What about Headspace?"
Headspace's valuation compressed from $3B to ~$320M. They've done multiple rounds of layoffs (15% + 4% of workforce) and transitioned all staff therapists to contractors. They're pivoting hard to EAP and enterprise through the Virgin Pulse partnership. Headspace has distribution but no physiological precision — their 4,000 enterprise orgs need what we build.

### "What about WHOOP?"
WHOOP detects stress. We resolve it. They just announced 600+ new hires ahead of a likely 2026-2028 IPO, and they need validated intervention protocols before going public. They're a $3.6B platform that measures recovery but can't prescribe recovery. We're the protocol layer they need — they're an acquirer, not a competitor.

### "What about Oura?"
Same story as WHOOP: $11B platform, 5.5M rings sold, Team USA partnership, but they partner with Headspace for content because they can't build clinical interventions themselves. Our ANS protocol data is a unique input their platform needs. At $11B, they don't build a $5M feature — they acquire it.

### "What about Apollo Neuro?"
Apollo is the most clinically validated ANS regulation device in market — $30.7M raised, 17 completed clinical studies, $100M+ valuation, and they just launched Apollo for Business. The difference: they require a $249 wearable using vibration therapy. We use the Apple Watch the user already owns and deliver breathwork intervention in 60 seconds. Different mechanism, different friction profile, same buyer.

### "What about Ohm Health?"
Early-stage ($737K raised), team of 4, building a biofeedback breathing lamp at $249. Consumer-focused, no clinical or enterprise strategy. Worth watching but not a competitive threat at current scale.

## Tier 3: Enterprise Gatekeepers

### "What about Lyra Health?"
Lyra covers 20M+ lives, just acquired Bend Health for pediatric mental health. They're the dominant enterprise mental health buyer — $910M+ raised. They don't compete with us; they're a distribution channel. The question is whether they partner or acquire. Their acquisition playbook (buying validated point solutions) maps perfectly to Pausality.

### "What about Spring Health?"
$3.3B valuation, acquiring Alma (combined ~10M mental health visits/year). April Koh is building the enterprise mental health platform. Same as Lyra: distribution partner or acquirer, not competitor. They need ANS regulation tools for the 80% of users who don't need therapy but do need stress management.

## Tier 4: Big Tech & Neurotech

### "What about ChatGPT Health?"
230 million people ask ChatGPT health questions every week. OpenAI launched a dedicated health tab with Apple Health, medical records, and wellness app integration in January 2026. But ChatGPT Health is a Q&A layer, not an intervention layer. It can tell you about breathing techniques. It cannot guide you through a 60-second biometrically-verified protocol on your Apple Watch. We are the clinical protocol engine that platforms like ChatGPT Health will eventually need to integrate.

### "What about Google's Gemini health coach?"
Google built a Personal Health LLM and a Large Sensor Model trained on the largest wearable dataset ever. Their Fitbit health coach gives advice on fitness, sleep, and nutrition. But it does not deliver real-time, biometrically-verified nervous system interventions. Google builds the AI coaching layer. We build what happens in the 60 seconds when the coach says "you need to regulate right now."

### "What about Samsung Brain Health?"
Samsung announced AI-powered cognitive health features at CES 2026 for Galaxy Watch and Ring, including early dementia detection via gait, speech, and sleep. This is detection, not intervention. Samsung can tell you your brain health is declining. They cannot prescribe a 60-second protocol to address it. Same story as WHOOP and Oura: they are sensors, we are the response.

### "What about Microsoft Copilot Health?"
Microsoft launched Copilot Health on March 12, 2026 — three days ago. It integrates Apple Health, Oura, Fitbit, and 50,000+ US EHRs through HealthEx, with Harvard Health content and provider directories. Same story as ChatGPT Health: it's a health data intelligence layer, not an intervention layer. Microsoft can tell you what your wearable data means. They cannot guide you through a 60-second biometrically-verified protocol. They are building the surface we plug into.

### "What about Grow Therapy?"
Grow Therapy just raised $150M at a $3B valuation (March 2026). They do $1B in revenue and have facilitated 10M therapy appointments. They are the biggest enterprise mental health gatekeeper alongside Lyra and Spring Health. But their model is therapy and medication management — the 20% of employees who need clinical intervention. The other 80% need stress management and nervous system regulation tools. That's Pausality. Grow is a distribution partner, not a competitor.

### "What about the neurotech companies? Merge Labs just raised $250M."
Merge Labs ($250M, OpenAI-backed), Neurable ($65M, brain-sensing headphones), and Elemind ($20M, neurostim) are all building the sensing or stimulation layer. They validate our thesis: nervous system state is measurable, trainable, and worth billions. But none of them build clinical breathing protocols, progressive curricula, or workflow-embedded interventions. They need protocol content. We are that content.

### "What about Epic and Cerner? Don't they already have clinician wellness tools?"
No. Epic and Oracle/Cerner are solving burnout by reducing documentation burden — ambient AI charting, voice commands, fewer clicks. DAX Copilot cut documentation time 50% and burnout feelings 70%. But those numbers tell you the problem is bigger than paperwork. Even after you cut charting time in half, clinicians are still absorbing the physiological stress of high-acuity cases, trauma exposure, and 12-hour shifts. Epic reduces the friction. We build the recovery. And Epic's App Market — which already lists SilverCloud and NeuroFlow for mental health — is our distribution channel into 2/3 of US hospitals.

### "Why wouldn't Apple just build this?"
They tried. Project Mulberry was their standalone AI health coach — it was scaled back and folded into the Health app as features. Jeff Williams (Apple's health champion) retired. Apple's pattern is build-or-buy, and on health coaching, they chose buy. They're looking for validated protocols with tight Watch/AirPods integration. That's us.

### "Why wouldn't WHOOP/Oura just add breathing exercises?"
They could ship basic guided breathing tomorrow. What they can't build: clinical validation across 28 years of anesthesia practice, evidence-based protocols mapped to specific autonomic states, AI-driven personalization based on real-time HRV, and a curriculum architecture (Neuro-Ouroboros) that drives progressive nervous system adaptation. Features can be copied. Clinical depth can't.

### "Isn't this market too crowded?"
There are ~200 digital health M&A deals per year and accelerating. The market isn't crowded — it's consolidating. Every one of the companies I just described is either acquiring, being acquired, or preparing to be acquired. The question isn't "is there room?" — it's "who gets acquired by whom, and at what price?" Our clinical moat determines which side of that equation we're on.

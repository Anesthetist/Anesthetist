## Top 3 Findings Ranked by Impact on SRL's Thesis

### 1. 🔴 CRITICAL — Polyvagal Theory Under Formal Attack (Grossman et al. 2026)
Grossman and 38 international co-signatories published that PVT's core premises are "not defensible." This is the most consequential finding this week. **SRL's language and positioning must be audited** for over-reliance on PVT-specific terminology (ventral vagal, dorsal shutdown, neuroception ladder). The good news: HRV biofeedback efficacy is NOT challenged — only the theoretical framework explaining WHY it works. **Action required:** Review all Pausality pitch decks, the GMT framework language, and academic writing for PVT dependencies. Reframe around empirically validated mechanisms (baroreflex sensitivity, RSA, autonomic flexibility) rather than PVT phylogenetic claims.

### 2. 🟢 STRONG SUPPORT — Frontiers Systematic Review of Biofeedback for HCPs
Level 1 evidence (systematic review) showing consistent multi-domain improvements from HRV biofeedback in healthcare professionals. This is the single most citable paper for Pausality's enterprise pitch. Combined with the ED/ICU RCT (#1), SRL now has both systematic review AND RCT-level evidence supporting its core intervention category.

### 3. 🟢 STRONG SUPPORT — HRVB RCT in ED/ICU Workers
A randomized controlled trial demonstrating that a brief (10-min 2x/day) HRV biofeedback protocol builds resilience in ED/ICU workers with maintained gains at follow-up. The dosing protocol mirrors NeuroMinute. The population mirrors Pausality's target. This is Level 2 evidence directly validating SRL's approach.

## Findings That CHALLENGE or COMPLICATE SRL's Position

### 1. Polyvagal Theory Declared "Untenable" by 39 Experts
If SRL's conceptual framework, marketing language, or grant applications lean on PVT's specific phylogenetic claims (unique mammalian ventral vagal pathway, neuroception as a distinct process, dorsal vagal "freeze" mechanism), these now carry reputational risk in scientific contexts. **Mitigation:** SRL's empirical claims about HRV biofeedback, interoceptive training, and breathwork are mechanism-independent and well-supported. The pivot is linguistic, not substantive.

### 2. No Single Biomarker Sufficient for Burnout Detection
The burnout biomarkers literature shows that no single physiological marker (HRV, cortisol, allostatic load) has sufficient sensitivity/specificity to diagnose burnout. SRL should avoid claiming that HRV alone can "detect" or "diagnose" burnout — instead frame it as one component of a multi-signal autonomic profile. This supports SRL's multimodal sensor fusion approach but cautions against over-promising on any single metric.

### 3. Mixed HRV-Burnout Correlation Evidence
Among 17 studies investigating HRV in burnout, only 6 found a significant negative correlation. Most found no significant association between resting heart rate and burnout. SRL should cite the positive studies but acknowledge the mixed evidence base, positioning Pausality's approach as capturing dynamic/reactive HRV patterns (biofeedback response, recovery curves) rather than resting-state metrics alone.

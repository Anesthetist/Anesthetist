# Facebook Post — CRNA Groups (28K audience)

---

**What if wellness was measured in lifetime earnings?**

Hear me out.

A 30-year CRNA career is worth roughly $5.85 million. Every year you leave early costs $195,000 — not in some abstract "value of wellbeing" sense, but in actual money you don't earn.

68% of us report burnout. 20-30% of new CRNAs leave within two years. We invested $100-200K and 2-3 years of zero income to get here. Then the system that trained us to be vigilant also trained us to ignore our own body signals — and we wonder why we're burning out.

What if the question isn't "are you okay?" but "how many years of your career is stress costing you?"

Because here's the math:
- Leave at year 15 instead of year 30 = $2.9 million in lost earnings
- Leave at year 10 instead of year 30 = $3.9 million
- Each year of early exit = $195,000

We don't frame it this way. We frame it as "burnout" or "resilience" or "self-care." But the economic reality is that our nervous system is a $6 million asset, and we have no maintenance protocol for it.

We have ACLS for cardiac emergencies. We have hand hygiene for infection prevention. We have nothing — no standard, no certification, no shared language — for the autonomic regulation that keeps us functional across a career.

What would change if we treated clinician durability the way we treat every other clinical competency — with measurable skills, evidence-based protocols, and a professional standard?

Genuinely curious what this group thinks. I've been working on this problem for a while and I'd love to hear from the people living it.

— Randy Graybeal, DNAP, CRNA
28 years in practice

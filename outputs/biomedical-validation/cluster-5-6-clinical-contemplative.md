## CLUSTER 5: Clinician Performance / Durability

### 5.1 Clinician Durability

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.0) | **Evidence links:** 50+

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept frames burnout as autonomic infrastructure failure rather than psychological weakness. The mechanism chain -- incomplete recovery cycles lead to baseline elevation, which leads to perceptual narrowing and error vulnerability -- is well-supported by allostatic load theory (McEwen & Stellar, 1993) and autonomic flexibility research. The vault correctly anchors this in vagal tone as the physiological substrate.

According to PubMed, the prevalence data supports the framing:
- ICU staff PTSD prevalence at 16.8%, anxiety at 22.8% in the second year of the COVID-19 pandemic, confirming that this is not an acute crisis but a chronic infrastructure problem. Roger et al. (2024) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12991-023-00488-5)
- CRNA burnout during COVID surges was predicted by decreased feedback, poor CRNA-administration relations, and work-life conflict -- organizational/autonomic factors, not personal resilience deficits. Lea et al. (2022) [DOI: AANA Journal, Vol 90, Issue 2]
- French anesthesia teams: 83% experienced incivility, with nurse anesthetists showing higher emotional exhaustion than anesthesiologists. Raft et al. (2025) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinane.2025.111983)
- SRNA burnout during pandemic tied to unprecedented stress levels with inadequate institutional support. Horvath & Grass (2021) [DOI: AANA Journal, Vol 89, Issue 5]

#### Evidence Strength: STRONG
- Allostatic load mechanism: well-established (McEwen, 1998, 2007)
- Burnout prevalence data: abundant, consistent
- HRV as physiological marker: growing evidence base (vault cites 10+ HRV-clinician studies)
- AANA Wellness Ambassador MC integration into NAEP curriculum validates institutional recognition. Morgan et al. (2025) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.70278/AANAJ/.0000001070)

#### Gaps
1. **No CRNA-specific PTSD longitudinal prevalence data.** Most studies are cross-sectional COVID snapshots. Pre-pandemic baseline CRNA PTSD rates are poorly documented.
2. **Autonomic recovery kinetics in clinicians remain unmeasured.** The vault hypothesizes that recovery-between-stressors is the key variable, but no study has measured inter-case autonomic recovery trajectories across shifts.
3. **Burnout-to-error causal pathway lacks RCT evidence.** The association between burnout and clinical errors is correlational; intervention studies showing that improving autonomic regulation reduces error rates are absent.

#### Contradictions
- The $80K-$150K turnover cost cited in the vault is commonly used but sourced variably. The range depends heavily on geography and practice model. This should be cited with a specific source or qualified.
- Some literature suggests that meaning/purpose protects against burnout even with poor autonomic regulation, which complicates the "autonomic infrastructure" thesis as the primary mechanism.

#### Emerging Research
- Neurophenomenological framework for naturalistic stress assessment in surgeons, combining wearable HRV with experience sampling. Crivelli et al. (2025) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568430) -- directly relevant to the vault's vision of real-time state monitoring.
- Interoceptive awareness-burnout link: two PubMed results suggest the connection is emerging but thin. This is a gap SRL could own.

### 5.2 State Drift

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** derived from clinical experience

#### Mechanism Validation
The five-step mechanism (arousal spike -> incomplete recovery -> baseline elevation -> perceptual narrowing -> error vulnerability) is a plausible synthesis of allostatic load theory, attentional narrowing under stress (Easterbrook, 1959), and autonomic flexibility research. Each individual step has supporting literature; the assembled sequence is Randy's original synthesis.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE
- Allostatic load / cumulative stress: well-established
- Attention narrowing under arousal: well-established (Easterbrook's cue-utilization theory)
- Incomplete autonomic recovery between tasks: supported by HRV research in surgical settings (vault cites de Louche 2024, Naiki 2025, Abahuje 2023)
- The specific term "state drift" and the assembled five-step model: SRL-original, no external validation

#### Gaps
1. **No published study uses the term "state drift" in this specific autonomic sense.** The concept needs operationalization -- what HRV/EDA thresholds define each stage?
2. **Shift-level HRV trajectory studies are sparse.** Most clinician HRV studies measure pre/post or single-point, not continuous within-shift trajectories that would demonstrate drift.
3. **The link from baseline elevation to error is inferred, not demonstrated.** Need studies correlating real-time autonomic state with error events.

#### Contradictions
- Some high-performing clinicians operate at elevated sympathetic baselines without apparent errors -- the "surgeon who thrives under pressure" phenotype. The model needs to account for individual variability in optimal arousal windows (Yerkes-Dodson).

#### Emerging Research
- Wearable continuous HRV monitoring during surgical procedures is growing rapidly (vault already cites 5+ studies from 2024-2026). This technology could directly test the state drift model.

### 5.3 Learning Under Load

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** minimal

#### Mechanism Validation
The four-phase loop (pre-case plan -> in-case cues -> post-case harvest -> spaced retrieval) synthesizes well-established learning science principles (desirable difficulties, spaced retrieval, interleaving) with clinical workflow realities. According to PubMed, cognitive load theory applied to clinical simulation is a robust field with 23+ recent results. The concept is pedagogically sound.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE
- Spaced retrieval / desirable difficulties: strong evidence base (Bjork & Bjork, Roediger & Karpicke)
- Cognitive load in clinical performance: well-studied. Systematic reviews confirm cognitive overload impairs surgical learning. Almukhtar et al. (2024) [from vault evidence]
- The specific "four-phase loop" as an integrated protocol: SRL-original, untested

#### Gaps
1. **No studies of deliberate learning protocols during live clinical cases.** All learning-under-load research uses simulation, not real OR environments.
2. **Cognitive load during learning vs. during clinical performance may compete.** The dual-task cost of "learning while performing" under genuine clinical stakes is unknown.
3. **No measurement instrument for "learning under load" capacity.**

#### Contradictions
- Cognitive load theory predicts that adding learning tasks to already-loaded clinical performance should degrade performance. The vault's claim that learning and performing are inseparable needs to address this tension with a mechanism (perhaps automaticity of core skills frees cognitive bandwidth for learning in experts).

#### Emerging Research
- Cognitive workload measurement during surgery via physiological markers (EDA, pupillometry, HRV) is advancing. This could enable real-time detection of available cognitive bandwidth for learning.

### 5.4 Adversity Visualization

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** Blue Angels observation + clinical experience

#### Mechanism Validation
Motor imagery engaging premotor cortex and supplementary motor area is well-established neuroscience. The claim that embodied visualization (with interoceptive and autonomic components) differs from pure cognitive rehearsal is supported by motor imagery literature. According to PubMed, mental imagery combined with motor rehearsal shows performance and stress benefits. (PMID 40216515, 39987734)

The PTSD prehabilitation claim -- that pre-rehearsing failure scenarios reduces traumatic encoding by reducing novelty-shock -- is a plausible extension of stress inoculation theory but lacks direct evidence for this specific mechanism.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE
- Motor imagery neuroscience: well-established
- Mental rehearsal in surgery: moderate evidence (vault cites Shirley 2024)
- Blue Angels-style embodied rehearsal generalized to clinical settings: anecdotal/observational
- PTSD prehabilitation via adversity visualization: theoretical, no RCTs

#### Gaps
1. **No study has compared embodied adversity visualization (with autonomic components) to standard mental rehearsal in clinicians.**
2. **The PTSD prehabilitation claim is the strongest commercial proposition but the weakest evidence link.** Need prospective studies in high-risk populations.
3. **Dose-response for visualization practice is unknown.** How much visualization produces measurable autonomic inoculation?

#### Contradictions
- Excessive adversity visualization could theoretically sensitize rather than inoculate -- the same concern as in exposure therapy without proper therapeutic framing. The vault acknowledges this implicitly via the hormesis connection but should address it directly.

#### Emerging Research
- VR-based stress inoculation for preoperative anxiety demonstrates the principle of simulated exposure reducing stress responses. El-Gabalawy et al. (2024) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/55692) -- uses VR as the delivery mechanism for a similar concept.

### 5.5 Stress Inoculation Training

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** Meichenbaum (1985) foundational

#### Mechanism Validation
SIT is an established clinical intervention with a 40+ year history. The three-phase model (conceptualization, skills acquisition, application) is well-validated. The vault's framing of GMT as "micro-dose SIT" is a creative application of the principle.

According to PubMed, the best RCT evidence for SIT in a healthcare-adjacent context is the StressModex trial: physiotherapist-delivered SIT integrated with exercise for acute whiplash showed clinically relevant sustained improvements in disability at 12 months vs. exercise alone. Sterling et al. (2019) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-100139). Qualitative follow-up confirmed patients valued both psychological and physical components. Silva Guerrero et al. (2021) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/09638288.2021.1931480)

#### Evidence Strength: STRONG (for SIT generally); WEAK (for SIT in healthcare providers specifically)
- SIT for anxiety/PTSD: strong (Meichenbaum's original populations, military applications)
- SIT delivered by non-psychologists: growing evidence (StressModex used physiotherapists)
- SIT for healthcare provider resilience specifically: no published RCTs found
- GMT as micro-dose SIT: SRL-original framing, untested

#### Gaps
1. **No RCTs of SIT for healthcare providers as the target population.** All SIT evidence treats patients, not providers.
2. **The "micro-dose" framing (60-second NeuroMinutes as SIT) needs validation.** Classical SIT uses 10+ session protocols; whether a 60-second format achieves similar inoculation is unknown.
3. **Optimal SIT dosing for clinician populations is undefined.**

#### Contradictions
- Classical SIT requires a conceptualization phase (psychoeducation about stress responses). The NeuroMinute format compresses or omits this phase. Whether SIT works without the conceptual scaffolding is an open question.

#### Emerging Research
- VR-based stress inoculation is being tested in surgical/anesthesia contexts (El-Gabalawy 2024), suggesting the field is moving toward technology-delivered inoculation protocols.

### 5.6 Hormesis-Durability Model

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** Mattson (2008), Calabrese (2002), McEwen (1998)

#### Mechanism Validation
Hormesis (biphasic dose-response) is well-established in toxicology and exercise physiology. The extension to psychological/cognitive stressors is the key question.

According to PubMed, a 2026 scoping review of antifragility research found only 18 studies focused on human systems, with "empirical studies remain scarce" and a need for "robust measurement tools and longitudinal designs." Holton et al. (2026) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/00332941261416041). The review explicitly notes that "biological concepts like hormesis and psychological frameworks such as post-traumatic growth align with mechanisms relevant to growth through adversity" but emphasizes the gap between theoretical plausibility and empirical validation.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE (for biological hormesis); WEAK (for psychological/cognitive hormesis)
- Hormesis in toxicology/exercise: well-established (Calabrese, Mattson)
- Allostatic load (the cost side): well-established (McEwen)
- Hormesis applied to psychological stressors: theoretical, minimal empirical validation
- Antifragility in human performance: scoping review confirms the concept is emerging but under-researched

#### Gaps
1. **The critical question: does hormesis apply to cognitive/emotional stressors the same way it applies to physical stressors?** The vault assumes yes but evidence is thin. Key difference: physical stressors have clear recovery markers (muscle repair, protein synthesis); psychological stress recovery is harder to define and measure.
2. **Dose specification is absent.** Hormesis requires precise dosing. What constitutes a "well-dosed" psychological stressor? How is the dose measured?
3. **Individual variation in the hormetic window is likely enormous** for psychological stressors -- what inoculates one clinician traumatizes another. The vault acknowledges this via non-ergodic risk but needs more development.

#### Contradictions
- Post-traumatic growth (PTG) literature, which the antifragility framework draws on, has been criticized for confounding actual growth with positive illusions and meaning-making narratives. The hormesis-durability model inherits this critique.
- Moral injury literature suggests some clinical stressors are not hormetic at any dose -- they cause damage regardless of recovery conditions. This challenges the universality of the model.

#### Emerging Research
- The Holton et al. (2026) scoping review is the most comprehensive current synthesis. It calls for "refining antifragility as a state, trait, or process" and "developing dose-specific models" -- precisely what the vault's hormesis-durability model attempts.

### 5.7 Second-Victim Phenomenon

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** Scott (2010), Wu (2000)

#### Mechanism Validation
Well-established phenomenon with a 25-year research history. The vault accurately represents the core concept and prevalence data (10-43%).

According to PubMed, the most recent systematic review (2025) of qualitative research on the second victim phenomenon found that "over two-thirds of healthcare professionals encounter such trauma during their careers" and identified three analytical themes: double-helix emotional response mode, dilemma of organizational support, and systematic feedback. Deng et al. (2025) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijnurstu.2025.105248)

A 2025 meta-analysis of second-victim syndrome in surgeons specifically found: anxiety (56.3%), guilt (53.8%), sleep disturbance (50.5%), sadness (48.3%). Talking to colleagues (72.5%) was the most common coping strategy. Bryan et al. (2025) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjs/znaf258)

#### Evidence Strength: STRONG (phenomenon and prevalence); MODERATE (interventions)
- The phenomenon itself: well-documented across specialties
- Prevalence in anesthesia: higher than average due to event severity, but specific CRNA data is limited
- Effective interventions: peer support programs (forYOU at Missouri) are the gold standard, but rigorous outcome data is limited
- Gap Moment Training as second-victim intervention: SRL-original, untested

#### Gaps
1. **CRNA-specific second-victim data is sparse.** Most anesthesia data groups CRNAs with anesthesiologists.
2. **The autonomic stabilization framing (GMT as immediate physiological response to second-victim events) is novel and untested.** Current interventions are psychological (peer support, debriefing), not physiological.
3. **No studies measure autonomic recovery trajectories after adverse events in clinicians.**

#### Contradictions
- The term "second victim" itself is contested. Some ethicists argue it inappropriately equates provider distress with patient harm. The vault should acknowledge this debate.

#### Emerging Research
- Institutional second-victim support programs are expanding globally. Systematic reviews of coping strategies confirm peer support as primary but call for more formal evaluation. Kappes et al. (2021) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/inr.12694)

### 5.8 Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** ChatGPT clinical conversation

#### Mechanism Validation
The four-step thesis (hidden curriculum suppresses interoception -> reduced sensibility -> autonomic inflexibility -> burnout) is a novel SRL-original hypothesis. Each step has partial support:
1. Hidden curriculum suppresses body signals: well-documented qualitatively (stoicism norms, "bladder of steel" culture)
2. Suppression reduces interoceptive sensibility: plausible but undemonstrated in clinician populations
3. Reduced sensibility causes autonomic inflexibility: supported by interoception-autonomic regulation literature (Craig 2009, Garfinkel 2015)
4. Autonomic inflexibility manifests as burnout: partially supported by HRV-burnout associations

According to PubMed, the interoceptive awareness-burnout connection in healthcare has minimal published research. Only two results were returned for "interoceptive awareness burnout healthcare" -- one was a yoga protocol for dental professionals (tangential) and one was the neurophenomenological framework for stress assessment in neurosurgery (Crivelli 2025), which specifically found "discrepancies between physiological activation and subjective stress perception" and highlighted "the importance of interoceptive awareness in modulating stress responses." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568430)

#### Evidence Strength: WEAK (as a complete hypothesis); MODERATE (for individual components)
- Hidden curriculum effects: qualitatively documented
- Interoception-autonomic regulation link: moderate evidence
- Clinician interoceptive sensibility vs. general population: not measured
- Full causal chain (suppression -> burnout): not tested

#### Gaps
1. **The testable prediction (clinicians show lower MAIA-2 scores than matched non-clinical populations) has not been tested.** This is the single most important empirical test for the hypothesis.
2. **Longitudinal data tracking interoceptive sensibility across training years is absent.**
3. **The distinction between adaptive and maladaptive interoceptive suppression in clinical settings needs development.** Some degree of signal suppression may be functional (you cannot attend to your bladder during a difficult airway).

#### Contradictions
- Expert clinicians may have *higher* interoceptive accuracy for specific signals (e.g., detecting their own stress) while suppressing others (hunger, fatigue). The hypothesis may need refinement to distinguish between global suppression and selective suppression.
- Some meditation research suggests that interoceptive training can cause adverse effects (anxiety amplification) in some individuals, complicating the "restore the interoceptive channel" intervention thesis.

#### Emerging Research
- The Crivelli et al. (2025) neurophenomenological framework is the closest published work to testing this hypothesis -- it found physiological-subjective discrepancies in surgeons that ISH would predict.
- This hypothesis represents a significant SRL original contribution and is potentially publishable if the MAIA-2/CRNA data is collected.

## CLUSTER 6: Consciousness / Contemplative

### 6.1 Self-Remembering

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1) | **Evidence links:** 18 evidence notes

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept draws on Gurdjieff's Fourth Way tradition, mapping "divided attention" (simultaneous awareness of self and task) onto modern neuroscience constructs. The cross-traditional parallels (Zen shikantaza, Vedantic sakshi, Husserlian epoche, IFS self-energy) are accurately represented. The neurological substrate -- ventral vagal state enabling simultaneous alertness and safety -- is consistent with polyvagal theory.

The vault's integration with modern contemplative neuroscience is strong, citing jhana fMRI/EEG studies, nondual awareness research, and default mode network deactivation literature.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE
- Self-remembering as a contemplative practice: well-documented in Fourth Way literature but minimal empirical research
- Dual attention / meta-awareness neuroscience: moderate evidence (fMRI studies of meta-awareness during meditation)
- Polyvagal framing (requires ventral vagal state): theoretical, not directly tested for self-remembering specifically
- Clinical self-remembering (CRNA "clinical presence"): observational, not measured

#### Gaps
1. **No study has operationalized or measured "self-remembering" as a distinct attentional capacity.** The construct needs a measurement instrument.
2. **The claim that self-remembering requires the ventral vagal state is unfalsifiable as stated.** How would one test this?
3. **Cross-traditional equivalence is asserted but not demonstrated.** Whether shikantaza and self-remembering engage the same neural networks is unknown.

#### Contradictions
- The concept of "divided attention" is normally associated with cognitive cost (dual-task interference). Claiming that divided attention is "paradoxically more integrated" needs a mechanism -- perhaps through automaticity of one attention stream.

#### Emerging Research
- According to PubMed, iEEG study of meta-awareness during meditation found unique connectivity patterns in gamma band, distinct from mind wandering. Bauer et al. (2021) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neulet.2021.136345) -- supports the idea that meta-awareness has distinct neural signatures.

### 6.2 Nondual Clinical Awareness

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** ChatGPT conversations

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept -- "vigilance without vigilance anxiety" through reduced PCC/DMN self-referential processing -- is neuroscientifically plausible. According to PubMed, the Topographic Reorganization Model of Meditation (TRoM) found that advanced meditators show: (i) decreased posterior DMN activity, (ii) increased CEN activity, (iii) decreased posterior DMN connectivity, (iv) increased anterior DMN and CEN connectivity. Cooper et al. (2022) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niac013)

The vault's claim that "reducing PCC/DMN self-referential processing frees cognitive bandwidth for pattern recognition" aligns with TRoM's finding of synchronization between DMN and CEN in advanced meditators.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE (for nondual awareness neuroscience); WEAK (for clinical application)
- Nondual awareness neural correlates: growing literature, TRoM provides a unifying model
- PCC deactivation in advanced meditators: consistent finding
- Nondual awareness applied to clinical performance: no published studies
- "Vigilance without vigilance anxiety" as a distinct attentional state: SRL-original, unmeasured

#### Gaps
1. **No study has measured nondual awareness in clinicians during clinical work.** All nondual awareness research uses meditators in laboratory settings.
2. **The leap from "advanced meditator in a scanner" to "CRNA during a case" is enormous.** The vault needs to address whether nondual states are accessible during high-stakes performance or only during seated practice.
3. **Training dosage for clinically-useful nondual awareness is unknown.** The contemplative literature suggests thousands of hours of practice; whether GMT-scale interventions can access nondual states is a major question.

#### Contradictions
- The TRoM model describes changes in *advanced* meditators (thousands of hours). If nondual clinical awareness requires this level of training, it undermines the SRL value proposition of accessible micro-practices.
- Clinical vigilance requires sustained focused attention, which some nondual traditions would consider antithetical to open awareness. The vault needs to address whether "open monitoring" and "focused vigilance" can coexist.

#### Emerging Research
- Josipovic's (2019) work on nondual awareness (already in vault) remains the most relevant theoretical framework. The 2022 TRoM review by Cooper et al. provides the most comprehensive neural model.

### 6.3 Contemplative Progressive Overload

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** ChatGPT conversations

#### Mechanism Validation
The synthesis of exercise physiology principles (progressive overload, periodization) with contemplative training is genuinely novel. The five intensification levers (frequency, complexity, stressors, compression, integration) are well-reasoned and map onto training science principles.

The 21-Day NeuroMinute Arc (somatic baseline -> cognitive friction -> nondual resilience) follows a logical progression from simple to complex, concrete to abstract.

#### Evidence Strength: WEAK
- Progressive overload in exercise: well-established
- Progressive difficulty in meditation training: traditional contemplative sequences exist (shamatha -> vipassana -> dzogchen) but not systematized as "overload"
- The specific synthesis (exercise overload + contemplative training): SRL-original, no published literature
- The 21-day format: no evidence for this specific timeline

#### Gaps
1. **No study has applied exercise periodization principles to contemplative training.**
2. **The assumption that awareness/metacognition responds to overload like muscle tissue is unvalidated.** Neural adaptation follows different rules than muscle hypertrophy.
3. **The "physiological challenge during practice" lever (CO2 load, cold, interruption) could be contraindicated for some populations.** Safety protocols are absent.
4. **Outcome metrics for "contemplative progressive overload" are undefined.** How does one measure whether awareness has been "overloaded" and adapted?

#### Contradictions
- Many contemplative traditions explicitly reject the "effort and intensity" approach. The Zen tradition of "just sitting" (shikantaza) and the Dzogchen tradition of "effortless awareness" are fundamentally at odds with progressive overload logic. The vault acknowledges this ("contemplative traditions generally emphasize effortless practice") but does not resolve the tension.
- Overtraining is a real risk in exercise; what does "contemplative overtraining" look like? Meditation adverse effects research (Britton et al.) suggests this is a genuine concern.

#### Emerging Research
- This concept is potentially the most original in the vault but also the least validated. No emerging literature directly addresses this synthesis.

### 6.4 Stairway Meta-Awareness Protocol

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** minimal (Springett, Damasio, Gurdjieff synthesis)

#### Mechanism Validation
The five-step ascending practice maps onto kosha architecture and progressively engages distinct attentional networks. The progression from interoceptive (body) to affective (emotion) to exteroceptive (soundscape) to collective (team) to unified (full stack) is logically ordered and clinically relevant.

The claim that "each step engages a distinct attentional network" is plausible given what we know about interoceptive networks (insula), affective labeling (vlPFC), auditory processing (temporal cortex), social cognition (TPJ/mPFC), and integrative awareness (PCC/precuneus).

#### Evidence Strength: WEAK
- Individual components (interoceptive grounding, affect labeling, environmental awareness): moderate evidence for each
- The specific five-step sequence: SRL-original, untested
- Kosha mapping: traditional framework, not empirically validated in neuroscience
- Clinical environment application: observational only

#### Gaps
1. **No study has tested a multi-step ascending awareness protocol in clinical settings.**
2. **The time requirement for the full five-step sequence is unclear.** If it takes more than 30 seconds, clinical applicability is limited.
3. **Step 4 (collective field awareness) assumes co-regulation capacity that may not be available in all team configurations.**
4. **The Springett developmental model source is not standard academic literature** and may not withstand peer review scrutiny.

#### Contradictions
- The protocol assumes that awareness naturally ascends through these layers when prompted. In practice, clinicians under stress may not be able to progress past Step 1 (or even reach Step 1). The protocol needs degraded modes.

### 6.5 Attention as Gain Control

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** ChatGPT conversation + Marin article

#### Mechanism Validation
The reframing of attention from "spotlight selection" to "selective amplification + normalization under energy constraints" is well-grounded in computational neuroscience. According to PubMed, individual differences in attentional N1 amplification (gain control) correlate with selective attention performance. Choi et al. (2014) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heares.2014.04.008) -- directly supports the "attention as gain control" framework.

The extension to interoceptive processing (turning up gain on diaphragm, HRV, cardiac pulse) is a novel application of gain control theory to somatic attention.

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE (for gain control as attention mechanism); WEAK (for interoceptive gain control training)
- Attention as neural gain control: well-established in computational neuroscience (Reynolds & Heeger, 2009, normalization model of attention)
- Individual differences in gain control predict performance: demonstrated (Choi et al. 2014)
- Interoceptive attention as gain modulation: plausible but not directly tested
- GMT as "gain control training": SRL-original reframe, not validated

#### Gaps
1. **The leap from auditory/visual attention gain control to interoceptive gain control is untested.** Interoceptive processing may follow different rules.
2. **The transformer AI attention mechanism parallel is metaphorical, not mechanistic.** While suggestive, convergent mathematics does not equal convergent biology.
3. **No measurement protocol for "interoceptive gain control" exists.**

#### Contradictions
- Divisive normalization in sensory cortex is largely automatic/pre-attentive. Whether it can be voluntarily trained in the interoceptive domain (which has weaker cortical representation) is an open question.

#### Emerging Research
- The computational neuroscience of attention gain control continues to advance, but application to interoception specifically is not yet a published research direction. This represents a genuine gap SRL could address.

### 6.6 Awe as Regulation

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** Keltner & Haidt (2003), Fredrickson (2001), Stellar et al. (2015)

#### Mechanism Validation
Awe's autonomic signature is supported by emerging psychophysiology research. According to PubMed:
- Awe has a distinct autonomic profile compared to other positive emotions, characterized by specific patterns of sympathetic and parasympathetic activation. Shiota et al. (2011) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024278) -- demonstrated qualitatively distinct ANS profiles across five positive emotions including awe.
- fNIRS research classifies awe within an "encouragement" cluster of positive emotions with recognizable neural signatures. Hu et al. (2019) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2019.00120)
- Physiological correlates of closely related emotions (kama muta) show distinct ANS patterns, though awe was distinguished as a separate category. Zickfeld et al. (2020) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.13662)

#### Evidence Strength: MODERATE (for awe as distinct emotion with ANS correlates); WEAK (for awe as deployable regulation strategy)
- Awe has a distinct autonomic profile: supported
- Awe reduces inflammatory cytokines: Stellar et al. (2015) -- single study, needs replication
- Awe increases vagal tone: partially supported by Shiota et al. (2011)
- Deliberately deploying awe in clinical settings: SRL-original, untested
- Awe reducing self-referential processing: plausible (Keltner's "small self" effect) but not directly measured via fMRI during awe in clinicians

#### Gaps
1. **Awe research uses nature scenes, music, and vastness experiences as stimuli.** Whether "appreciating the complexity of human physiology" during a case produces the same autonomic response is undemonstrated.
2. **The claim that awe is deliberately cultivable (rather than spontaneous) lacks evidence.** Most awe research treats it as an elicited response, not a trained skill.
3. **Awe-burnout relationship is not established.** The vault infers that awe counters depersonalization via self-transcendence, but this pathway has not been tested.

#### Contradictions
- Awe's "accommodation" component (schema revision) could be cognitively demanding during clinical work, potentially competing with clinical decision-making rather than supporting it.
- Some research suggests awe can involve threat/fear elements ("awe-full" experiences), which could activate sympathetic responses contrary to the regulation thesis.

#### Emerging Research
- Keltner's popular book (2023) has increased public interest in awe research. Lab-based interventions (awe walks, awe narratives) are being tested but not in clinical populations.

### 6.7 Curiosity as Clinical Stance

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1) | **Evidence links:** Fredrickson (2001), Allan et al. (2023)

#### Mechanism Validation
The mechanism -- curiosity reframes arousal from threat to data, recruits dlPFC, reduces anxiety sensitivity -- is well-grounded in emotion regulation research. According to PubMed:
- Reappraisal (the regulatory strategy closest to curiosity-as-reframe) predicts lower subsequent negative affect in daily life. Wang et al. (2024) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2024.104518) -- supports the reframing mechanism.
- Anxiety predicts natural suppression (not reappraisal) in the laboratory, suggesting that training individuals to shift from suppression to curiosity/reappraisal could be clinically meaningful. Burr et al. (2021) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247246) -- directly relevant: anxious individuals default to suppression; curiosity reframe would redirect them to reappraisal.

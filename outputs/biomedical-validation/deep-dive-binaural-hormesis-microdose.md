## Concept 1: NeuroHarmonics / Binaural Beat Entrainment

### The Core Claim Under Scrutiny

SRL's NeuroHarmonics concept implies that binaural beats can reliably entrain brainwaves at target frequencies, producing measurable physiological effects that enhance autonomic regulation. The question: does the evidence support this mechanism, or are observed effects driven by something else?

### What the Evidence Actually Shows

#### The Entrainment Problem (Ingendoh et al. 2023)

According to PubMed, Ingendoh et al. (2023) conducted a systematic review of 14 EEG studies specifically testing whether binaural beats produce brainwave entrainment — the frequency-following response that is the theoretical foundation of the entire field. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0286023)

**Results:**
- **5 of 14 studies** reported results consistent with the brainwave entrainment hypothesis
- **8 of 14 studies** reported contradictory results (no entrainment detected)
- **1 study** reported mixed results

**Critical finding:** The studies were "very heterogeneous regarding the implementation of the binaural beats, the experimental designs, and the EEG parameters and analyses." The methodological heterogeneity "ultimately limits the comparability of research outcomes."

**Translation for SRL:** The neural entrainment mechanism — the theoretical backbone of binaural beat claims — is not reliably demonstrated. More studies fail to find it than succeed.

#### What IS Confirmed: Interhemispheric Coherence (Not Entrainment)

According to PubMed, Solca et al. (2015) found that binaural beats enhanced interhemispheric alpha-band coherence between auditory cortices — but explicitly stated this "seems to reflect binaural integration rather than entrainment." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heares.2015.09.011)

Coffey et al. (2021) confirmed oscillatory entrainment exists in the auditory system via MEG, but this is the frequency-following response to any periodic sound, not unique to binaural beats. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2313-20.2021)

**The distinction matters:** Binaural beats may produce real neural effects through interhemispheric coherence and auditory processing changes — but not through the claimed mechanism of frequency-specific brainwave entrainment.

#### Clinical Effects: Anxiety and Pain Reduction ARE Real

Despite the weak entrainment evidence, clinical effects in specific contexts are documented:

**Perioperative anxiety (Xiong et al. 2025):** Meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (n=1047) found BB significantly reduced perioperative anxiety (SMD = -1.38, 95% CI: -1.89 to -0.87, p<0.0001) and postoperative pain (SMD = -0.61). BB outperformed non-BB audio controls. However, heterogeneity was substantial (I² = 91.6%). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2025.103299)

**Dental anxiety (Shukla et al. 2025):** Systematic review of 9 studies (580 screened) found significant reduction in dental anxiety. Pain reduction was significant vs. positive control but inconclusive vs. no-intervention control. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10005-3230)

**Pain perception (Shamsi et al. 2024):** Systematic review of 16 RCTs found alpha-frequency or delta-to-alpha combinations showed potential for acute pain reduction. Evidence for chronic pain was insufficient. Risk of bias was high and quality of evidence was low to very low. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-024-04339-y)

**Cognition (Kim et al. 2024):** EEG-measured RCT found 18 Hz (beta) BB increased beta power during stimulation and improved sentence comprehension. 40 Hz (gamma) BB did NOT produce corresponding gamma increases. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhad459)

#### Autonomic Effects: Limited but Present

**HRV during sleep (Bakaeva et al. 2021):** BB embedded in music increased parasympathetic activity during nap onset vs. silence control. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.17116/jnevro202112104231)

**Post-exercise HRV (McConnell et al. 2014):** Theta-frequency BB increased parasympathetic activation and sympathetic withdrawal during post-exercise recovery vs. carrier tone control (n=21). Effects were transient. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01248)

**Sleep quality in elderly (Lin et al. 2024):** 14-day RCT (n=64) found BB music improved sleep quality, reduced sympathetic activity, and decreased depression vs. music-only control. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ggi.14827)

**Multi-frequency comparison (Yang et al. 2025):** Theta (6 Hz), alpha (10 Hz), and beta (25 Hz) all reduced anxiety markers after 20 min. No significant difference between frequencies — suggesting the effect may not be frequency-specific. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-025-04922-x)

**6 Hz BB cortical entrainment (Scala et al. 2025):** EEG/eLORETA study confirmed 6 Hz BB entrained cortical activity at beat frequency in left cuneus and precuneus. Critically, BB induced "only minimal changes in ANS parameters" — in contrast to monaural beats and random noise, which produced larger autonomic changes. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.70271)

#### Isochronic Tones: A Stronger Entrainment Alternative?

According to PubMed, Dos Anjos et al. (2024) directly compared isochronic tones (IT), binaural beats (BB), and white noise (WN) using EEG in 28 participants. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2024.07.014)

**Key findings:**
- Both BB and WN enhanced gamma power, consistent with expected entrainment
- **IT produced significantly greater EEG power changes than BB** (p<0.001)
- Time-based analysis showed progressive increase in EEG power with IT vs. BB (p<0.05)
- IT also produced a unique alpha-band decrease over time — a dual alpha-gamma modulation not seen with BB
- The authors concluded IT has "promising and unique potential to modulate neural oscillations which selectively differ from BB"

**Translation for SRL:** Isochronic tones may be a more effective auditory entrainment tool than binaural beats. This is a single study (n=28), but the mechanism is more parsimonious — IT produces actual amplitude-modulated sound at the target frequency, rather than relying on a perceptual illusion generated subcortically.

### Evidence Assessment: What SRL Can and Cannot Claim

#### SUPPORTED (with caveats):
- BB can reduce anxiety in acute clinical contexts (perioperative, dental) — but effect sizes vary widely and heterogeneity is extreme
- BB music can modestly improve sleep quality and reduce depression in elderly populations
- BB embedded in music can transiently shift HRV toward parasympathetic dominance
- The combination of auditory stimulation + relaxation context produces real psychological and modest physiological effects

#### NOT SUPPORTED:
- **"Binaural beats entrain brainwaves at target frequencies"** — the majority of EEG studies fail to confirm this. The entrainment hypothesis is more false than true
- **Frequency-specific effects** — Yang et al. (2025) found no significant difference between theta, alpha, and beta BB for anxiety reduction. If the frequency doesn't matter, the entrainment model is wrong
- **BB as a standalone autonomic intervention** — Scala et al. (2025) found BB produced *minimal* ANS changes compared to monaural beats and noise

#### RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION:
1. **Retire the term "entrainment" from NeuroHarmonics claims.** Use "auditory modulation" or "auditory-autonomic coupling" instead
2. **Frame BB as a delivery vehicle for relaxation, not a precision frequency tool.** The clinical effects are real but likely driven by attentional engagement, expectancy, and the relaxation context — not by frequency-specific neural entrainment
3. **Investigate isochronic tones as an alternative.** Dos Anjos et al. (2024) suggests IT produces stronger, more reliable EEG modulation. If SRL wants a neurophysiologically grounded audio intervention, IT may have a stronger evidence base
4. **The honest framing:** "Audio environments designed to support nervous system regulation through attention, breath pacing, and auditory engagement" — not "brainwave entrainment technology"
5. **Status recommendation:** Downgrade NeuroHarmonics from canonical to review. The mechanism claim needs reframing

## Concept 2: Hormesis-Durability Model

### The Core Claim Under Scrutiny

SRL uses hormesis as a framing for its interventions: controlled low-dose stress exposure (breathing challenges, cold, arousal management) builds resilience capacity over time. The question: is hormesis a valid framework for psychological/behavioral interventions, and are there stressor categories where it fails?

### What the Evidence Actually Shows

#### Hormesis in Psychological Domains: Very Thin

According to PubMed, the intersection of "hormesis" and "psychological resilience" yields only 10 studies total since 2018. The term "hormesis" remains overwhelmingly anchored in toxicology, radiation biology, and physical stressors. Its extension to psychological domains is conceptual, not empirical.

**Key paper — Ricon-Becker et al. (2021):** Post-stress glucose consumption facilitated hormetic training and resilience to severe stress in rats. Hormetic training (pre-exposure to mild stress) prevented learned helplessness, but only with interpolated rest days. Without rest, hormesis failed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10253890.2021.1931677)

**Critical finding for SRL:** Hormesis requires recovery periods. Consecutive stress without rest abolished the hormetic effect entirely. This has direct implications for CRNA shift schedules.

#### Hormesis vs. Stress Inoculation Training: Related but Distinct

The literature search reveals these are related but come from different research traditions:

- **Hormesis** (biology/toxicology): Biphasic dose-response where low-dose stressor produces opposite effect from high-dose. Mechanism-focused. Primarily studied in cell biology, toxicology, radiation.
- **Stress Inoculation Training (SIT)** (clinical psychology, Meichenbaum 1985): Graduated exposure to stressors with coping skill acquisition. Three phases: conceptualization, skill acquisition/rehearsal, application. Primarily studied in PTSD, anxiety, performance contexts.
- **Stress inoculation** (neuroscience): Animal model paradigm where early-life mild stress exposure produces lasting resilience. Studied via cortisol, HPA axis, neural plasticity.

The search for SIT in healthcare workers returns 112 results — a vastly larger evidence base than hormesis in psychological contexts (10 results). SIT has decades of clinical validation. Hormesis in this domain is a metaphor borrowed from toxicology.

**Translation for SRL:** Stress inoculation training is the established, evidence-based framework for what SRL is doing. Hormesis provides a useful biological metaphor but is not the validated intervention model.

#### Allostatic Load and the Limits of Hormetic Framing

According to PubMed, the relationship between burnout and allostatic load is more nuanced than a simple tipping point:

**Langelaan et al. (2007):** Contrary to expectations, burned-out managers did NOT differ from healthy managers on allostatic load index (BMI, BP, CRP, HDL, HbA1C, glucose). "The mediating physiological mechanisms between burnout and objective physical health remain to be elucidated." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03002995)

**von Kanel et al. (2023):** Male physicians with clinical burnout showed significantly SMALLER epinephrine responses to acute psychosocial stress (TSST) compared to non-burned-out physicians. This is consistent with allostatic load: the system becomes hypo-responsive, not hyper-responsive. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108687)

**Woolley et al. (2025):** Same cohort — burned-out physicians showed significantly lower IL-6 reactivity to stress. "An attenuated innate immune response in burnout complies with increased allostatic load such that dysfunctional stress responses might increase the risk of cardiovascular events in the long term." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00702-025-02945-9)

**Translation for SRL:** Burnout isn't just "too much stress" — it's a state where the stress response system itself becomes blunted. This is the opposite of hormetic adaptation. The body stops responding, rather than over-responding. This has profound implications for the hormesis framing: you can't "hormetically train" a system that's already allostatic-loaded into hypo-responsiveness. You first need to restore baseline reactivity.

#### Non-Hormetic Stressors: Moral Injury

The PubMed search for "moral injury" + "non-hormetic" returned zero results — the concept of non-hormetic stressors is not yet formalized in the literature. However, the theoretical case is strong:

Hormesis requires that the stressor activates repair/adaptation mechanisms. Moral injury — the violation of one's moral code through action or inaction — does not have a clear biological repair pathway. There is no known "moral injury hormesis" where small moral violations build resilience to larger ones. The clinical literature consistently treats moral injury as cumulative damage, not adaptive stress.

Similarly, secondary traumatic stress and compassion fatigue operate through empathic engagement mechanisms that do not have obvious hormetic properties. Exposure to others' suffering may deplete empathic capacity rather than building resilience to it.

### Framework: Which SRL Interventions Can Use Hormetic Framing

| Intervention | Hormetic Framing Valid? | Better Framework | Evidence Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breathing challenges** (CO2 tolerance, breath holds) | **Partially** — physiological stressor with clear adaptation pathway | Titrated physiological challenge + recovery | Moderate (dose-response data exists for breathing) |
| **Cold exposure** | **Partially** — well-established hormetic stressor in biology | Hormesis valid here, but human behavioral evidence is thin | Moderate for physiology, weak for psychology |
| **Arousal management training** | **No** — better framed as skill acquisition | Stress Inoculation Training (SIT) | Strong (decades of SIT literature) |
| **Interoceptive awareness training** | **No** — awareness is not a stressor | Perceptual learning / embodied cognition | Moderate |
| **Shift-work resilience for CRNAs** | **No** — must distinguish hormetic from allostatic load | Recovery-first model: restore autonomic reactivity before challenging | Strong theoretical basis, intervention evidence emerging |
| **Gap Moment Training** | **No** — the "gap" is a decision window, not a stressor | Metacognitive skill training | Conceptual — limited direct evidence |

### Evidence Assessment: What SRL Can and Cannot Claim

#### SUPPORTED:
- Controlled exposure to physiological stressors (breath holds, cold) with adequate recovery can produce adaptation
- Stress inoculation training has strong evidence in clinical and performance contexts
- Allostatic load is a validated concept explaining cumulative stress damage
- Recovery periods are essential — hormesis without rest produces damage, not adaptation

#### NOT SUPPORTED:
- **"All SRL interventions use hormetic principles"** — most SRL interventions are skill-building, not stress exposure
- **"Any controlled stressor is hormetic"** — moral injury, compassion fatigue, and secondary traumatic stress may not have hormetic properties
- **"Hormesis explains CRNA resilience training"** — for already-burned-out individuals, the priority is restoring baseline reactivity, not adding more stress. The allostatic load literature (von Kanel 2023, Woolley 2025) shows burned-out systems are hypo-responsive

#### RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION:
1. **Use "hormesis" only for physiological stressors with clear dose-response data** (breathing challenges, cold exposure). Drop it for psychological/behavioral interventions
2. **Adopt Stress Inoculation Training (SIT) as the primary framework** for behavioral/psychological resilience training. It has vastly more evidence and maps cleanly to what SRL actually does
3. **Add a "recovery-first" principle** for burned-out populations: before any challenge, restore baseline autonomic reactivity through vagal tone support, sleep, and parasympathetic activation
4. **Explicitly exclude moral injury from the hormetic model.** SRL should state that some occupational stressors are not amenable to hormetic training — they require prevention, not adaptation
5. **Status recommendation:** Reframe from "Hormesis-Durability Model" to "Graduated Challenge and Recovery Model" or adopt the SIT framework explicitly

## Concept 3: Micro-Dose Interventions (60-Second NeuroMinute)

### The Core Claim Under Scrutiny

SRL's NeuroMinute concept proposes that 60-second interventions can produce measurable physiological shifts sufficient for autonomic regulation. The question: what does the dose-response literature actually show for breathing-based and mindfulness-based interventions at ultra-brief durations?

### What the Evidence Actually Shows

#### The Balban/Huberman Cyclic Sighing Study (2023)

According to PubMed, Balban et al. (2023) conducted a remote RCT comparing 5-minute daily breathwork exercises (cyclic sighing, box breathing, cyclic hyperventilation) with 5-minute mindfulness meditation over 1 month. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895)

**Key findings:**
- Daily 5-min cyclic sighing produced greater improvement in mood (p<0.05) and reduction in respiratory rate (p<0.05) compared to mindfulness meditation
- All breathwork conditions outperformed mindfulness for mood improvement
- Cyclic sighing (emphasizing prolonged exhalation) was the most effective
- Duration: **5 minutes per day**, not 1 minute. The study does NOT validate sub-5-minute dosing

**Critical note:** This is the closest anchor point for SRL's NeuroMinute concept, but the tested dose was 5x the proposed NeuroMinute dose. Extrapolating from 5 minutes to 1 minute is not supported by this data.

#### Slow-Paced Breathing: 5-Minute Minimum for HRV Effects

According to PubMed, You et al. (2023) tested 5-minute slow-paced breathing at different frequencies (5-7 cycles/min) in 75 athletes. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-023-09605-2)

**Key findings:**
- All SPB conditions (5, 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7 cpm) significantly increased cardiac vagal activity vs. spontaneous breathing
- RMSSD was significantly higher in all SPB conditions
- **The dose was 5 minutes.** No sub-5-minute conditions were tested
- LF-HRV was more sensitive to frequency differences than RMSSD

**Translation for SRL:** 5-minute slow breathing reliably shifts HRV. One minute? Unknown.

#### Acute Blood Pressure and Autonomic Effects of Slow Breathing

According to PubMed, Pingali & Hunter (2022) reviewed 19 studies on slow breathing and blood pressure regulation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2022.103050)

**Key findings on acute effects:**
- Some studies showed increased vagally-mediated HRV during slow breathing, but "results from acute and long-term studies were incongruent"
- **Baroreflex sensitivity (BRS) increased after a single bout** of device-guided slow breathing in both normotensive and hypertensive adults
- **Muscle sympathetic nerve activity (MSNA) reduced immediately** during device-guided breathing
- Long-term effects (4 weeks to 3 months) on BRS were absent

**Critical note:** These acute effects were measured during breathing sessions of 10-15 minutes, not 1 minute.

#### Ultra-Brief Mindfulness: The Cautionary Tale

According to PubMed, Shuai et al. (2019) tested 6-minute breath counting training (a mindfulness component) in 192 student drinkers. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2019.106141)

**Key findings:**
- 6-minute breath counting improved mood relative to baseline
- It attenuated stress-induced mood worsening
- It accelerated recovery from stress-induced alcohol-seeking
- But: the effect was weaker in more alcohol-dependent participants
- Duration: **6 minutes** — still 6x a NeuroMinute

According to PubMed, Goldberg et al. (2021) tested brief breath awareness training in 162 participants, then exposed them to acute stress (cold pressor task). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2021.1878113)

**Surprising result:** Brief breath awareness training produced WORSE working memory performance under stress compared to loving-kindness, gratitude, and attentional control conditions. The authors concluded "brief breath awareness training may not effectively buffer against acute stress in this predominantly meditation-naive sample and may in fact impair subsequent cognitive performance."

**Translation for SRL:** Ultra-brief mindfulness can backfire. The Goldberg finding is a red flag — brief breath awareness without adequate skill development may increase self-monitoring of arousal without providing regulation capacity, worsening performance under stress.

#### What CAN Happen in Under 60 Seconds: The Physiological Sigh

The physiological sigh (double inhale through nose, extended exhale through mouth) is a spontaneous reflex that occurs during sleep and crying. A single physiological sigh takes approximately 5-8 seconds.

**Known acute effects of a single sigh (from respiratory physiology literature):**
- Reinflates collapsed alveoli (atelectasis prevention)
- Resets respiratory rhythm
- May transiently increase tidal volume and gas exchange

**What is NOT established for a single sigh:**
- Sustained HRV shift
- Measurable RMSSD change
- Anxiety reduction at the single-sigh dose

The Balban et al. (2023) study used **5 minutes of cyclic** (repeated) sighing. The minimum effective dose for mood/anxiety effects has not been established below 5 minutes.

#### The Deep Breathing Test: 1-Minute Clinical Marker

There is one well-established 1-minute breathing protocol in clinical medicine: the **deep breathing test (DBT)** for cardiac autonomic neuropathy. This involves 6 deep breaths in 1 minute (6 cpm), and the resulting heart rate variation (E:I ratio) is used diagnostically.

According to PubMed, Mamatha et al. (2019) used the 1-minute deep breathing HRV method as a diagnostic measure, confirming that 1 minute of deep breathing DOES produce measurable HRV changes. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.13005/bpj/2058) (Note: this is a measurement protocol, not a therapeutic intervention)

**Translation for SRL:** One minute of slow breathing produces measurable acute HRV changes — this is established in clinical diagnostics. What is NOT established is whether these acute changes have any therapeutic or training value. The clinical DBT measures autonomic function; it doesn't treat it.

### Dose-Response Evidence Map

| Duration | Intervention | Measured Effect | Evidence Level | Key Study |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Single breath (5-8s)** | Physiological sigh | Alveolar reinflation, respiratory reset | Established (physiology) | Respiratory physiology literature |
| **1 minute** | 6 deep breaths | Measurable HRV oscillation (diagnostic) | Established (clinical diagnostic tool) | DBT literature |
| **5 minutes** | Cyclic sighing | Mood improvement, respiratory rate reduction | Moderate (1 RCT, n=108) | Balban et al. 2023 |
| **5 minutes** | Slow-paced breathing (5-7 cpm) | Increased RMSSD, cardiac vagal activity | Moderate (1 study, n=75) | You et al. 2023 |
| **6 minutes** | Breath counting | Mood improvement, stress resilience | Moderate (1 RCT, n=192) | Shuai et al. 2019 |
| **10-15 minutes** | Device-guided slow breathing | BRS increase, MSNA reduction | Moderate (review of 19 studies) | Pingali & Hunter 2022 |
| **20 minutes** | Binaural beats + breathing context | HRV shift, BP reduction, anxiety reduction | Moderate (multiple RCTs) | Multiple studies |

### The Gap: What Happens Between 1 and 5 Minutes?

**No published evidence directly tests the 60-second therapeutic dose for breathing-based interventions.**

The literature jumps from:
- 1-minute deep breathing as a **diagnostic marker** (the body responds to 1 minute of paced breathing)
- 5-minute structured breathwork as the **shortest tested therapeutic dose** (Balban et al. 2023)

This does not mean 60 seconds is ineffective. It means the claim is untested. The physiological reasoning is plausible: if 1 minute of deep breathing produces measurable acute HRV changes (confirmed diagnostically), those changes may have value when repeated as a training stimulus. But this is inference, not evidence.

### Evidence Assessment: What SRL Can and Cannot Claim

#### SUPPORTED:
- 5-minute daily cyclic sighing improves mood and reduces physiological arousal (Balban et al. 2023)
- Slow-paced breathing at 5-7 cpm increases cardiac vagal activity within 5 minutes (You et al. 2023)
- 1 minute of paced breathing produces measurable acute HRV changes (clinical diagnostic literature)
- Acute effects of slow breathing include BRS increase and MSNA reduction (Pingali & Hunter 2022)
- Brief contemplative exercises (6 min) can improve stress resilience (Shuai et al. 2019)

#### NOT SUPPORTED:
- **"60 seconds is sufficient for meaningful autonomic regulation"** — no therapeutic study has tested this dose
- **"A single physiological sigh shifts HRV"** — not demonstrated. A single sigh resets respiratory rhythm; HRV effects require sustained pacing
- **"Micro-dose breathing works like micro-dose pharmacology"** — the pharmacological analogy is misleading. Breathing dose-response is likely nonlinear and may have a minimum threshold below which effects are negligible

#### CAUTIONARY:
- Ultra-brief breath awareness can IMPAIR cognitive performance under stress in meditation-naive individuals (Goldberg et al. 2021). This is directly relevant to CRNA populations facing acute clinical stress

#### RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION:
1. **Acknowledge the evidence gap honestly.** SRL should state: "The minimum effective dose for breathing-based autonomic regulation is approximately 5 minutes based on current evidence. NeuroMinutes are designed as training stimuli — building awareness and skill that compounds over time — not as standalone therapeutic doses"
2. **Position NeuroMinutes as skill-building micro-practices, not micro-treatments.** The 60-second format develops the *skill* of breath awareness and regulation. The *therapeutic dose* for acute autonomic effects is 5+ minutes
3. **Design a dose-response study.** SRL has a genuine opportunity to contribute to the literature here. Testing 1-minute vs. 3-minute vs. 5-minute breathing interventions with HRV measurement would be novel and publishable
4. **Add the Goldberg (2021) caveat to all clinical content.** For meditation-naive individuals under acute stress, brief breath awareness without adequate context/training can impair performance. NeuroMinutes should include framing that prevents this: "notice, don't control" as an entry point before "regulate"
5. **Lean into the training metaphor, not the dose metaphor.** "Sixty seconds of deliberate practice, repeated daily, builds the skill that matters in the 10-second window when you need it" — this is defensible. "Sixty seconds changes your autonomic state" — this is not supported at that dose

## Cross-Cutting Findings

### Three Patterns Across All Three Concepts

1. **The mechanism-effect gap.** For all three concepts, clinical effects exist but claimed mechanisms are weaker than stated. BB reduces anxiety but not through entrainment. Challenge builds resilience but not always through hormesis. Brief breathing changes HRV acutely but not necessarily therapeutically at 60 seconds.

2. **The framing matters more than the science.** SRL's credibility risk is not that the interventions don't work — they likely do produce real effects. The risk is over-claiming the mechanism. "Binaural beat entrainment" is a mechanism claim that the evidence doesn't support. "Auditory environments that support regulation" is an effect claim that it does.

3. **The honest-framing advantage.** In a market full of pseudoscientific wellness claims, SRL's competitive advantage is clinical precision. Saying "the evidence supports 5-minute breathwork for mood improvement; we're investigating whether micro-doses build the skill over time" is more credible than "60 seconds rewires your nervous system." The target audience (CRNAs, clinicians) will respect intellectual honesty more than confident overclaiming.

### Recommended Status Changes

| Concept | Current Status | Recommended Status | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| NeuroHarmonics / Binaural Beats | Canonical (?) | **Review** | Reframe mechanism from "entrainment" to "auditory modulation"; investigate isochronic tones |
| Hormesis-Durability Model | Draft/Review | **Review** | Adopt SIT as primary framework; restrict hormesis to physiological stressors only |
| Micro-Dose Interventions (NeuroMinute) | Draft | **Draft** | Acknowledge 5-min minimum evidence; position as skill-building, not micro-treatment |

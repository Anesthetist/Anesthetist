# Biomedical Validation Report: Cluster 1 — Autonomic / HRV / Vagal

**Date:** 2026-03-19
**Validator:** Vigil (biomedical validation sweep)
**Sources:** PubMed literature search (2024-2026 focus), vault concept notes, existing evidence base
**Scope:** 10 concepts in the Autonomic/HRV/Vagal cluster

---

## Executive Summary

The autonomic/HRV/vagal cluster is the scientific backbone of the SRL vault. The core physiological mechanisms — slow breathing improving HRV via baroreflex resonance, vagal tone as an autonomic flexibility biomarker — are well-supported by converging evidence including meta-analyses and RCTs. The polyvagal theory framework, however, faces a major 2026 challenge from Grossman et al. (39 expert co-authors) that the vault must explicitly address. Several SRL-original concepts (cardiac-anchored breathing, neuro-respiratory-cardiac coherence, state drift, autonomic home base) are scientifically plausible but currently lack direct empirical validation — they represent applied innovation built on solid mechanistic foundations.

**Overall cluster health:** Strong foundational science, active theoretical debate on polyvagal framework, innovation concepts need evidence development.

### 1. Autonomic Regulation

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1)
**Validation status:** SUPPORTED

#### Mechanism Validation
The vault's framing of autonomic regulation as "the body's capacity to shift flexibly between sympathetic, parasympathetic, and integrated states" is well-aligned with established neurophysiology. The three-system balance model (sympathetic, parasympathetic, integrated/ventral vagal) is a reasonable clinical simplification, though the "integrated" state draws heavily from polyvagal theory (see polyvagal section for caveats).

#### Evidence Strength
- **Meta-analyses:** According to PubMed, a 2026 meta-analysis of 56 longitudinal studies confirms HRV (RMSSD, SDNN, HF, LF, VLF) as a prospective predictor of depressive symptoms with small but significant negative correlations (Galin et al., 2026; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.121388)). This validates HRV as a meaningful autonomic flexibility index.
- **Systematic reviews:** According to PubMed, a 2025 meta-analysis of remote HRV biofeedback shows medium effect sizes for both depression improvement (g = -0.41) and HRV increase (g = 0.443) (Vann-Adibe et al., 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-025-09750-w)).
- **Narrative reviews:** According to PubMed, Gitler et al. (2025) provide a comprehensive review of non-invasive vagal neuromodulation confirming HRV-B efficacy for cardiovascular outcomes, anxiety, depression, and resilience ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3892/mi.2025.236)).

#### Gaps
- The vault claims "fewer communication errors under stress" and "reduced PTSD/burnout vulnerability" as clinical outcomes but does not cite specific studies linking HRV metrics directly to communication error rates in CRNAs.
- The claim that "CRNAs who maintain autonomic flexibility show better vigilance, fewer errors, and longer career longevity" is labeled as clinical observation but would benefit from prospective evidence.

#### Contradictions
None significant. The concept of autonomic regulation as a measurable, trainable capacity is broadly accepted.

#### Emerging Research (2024-2026)
- According to PubMed, Galin et al. (2026) meta-analysis positions HRV as a prospective vulnerability marker, strengthening the case for interventions that improve autonomic regulation as preventive medicine ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.121388)).
- AI-driven adaptive biofeedback is an emerging direction that aligns with SRL's product vision (Nadaf & Jabade, 2026; [DOI per PubMed entry]).

#### Recommendations
- Add Galin et al. 2026 meta-analysis to evidence base (prospective HRV-depression link).
- Qualify clinical outcome claims with "clinical observation" vs "evidence-based" distinctions.

### 2. Vagal Tone

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1)
**Validation status:** SUPPORTED

#### Mechanism Validation
The definition of vagal tone as "tonic level of vagal efferent activity to the heart" is physiologically accurate. RMSSD, HF-HRV, RSA, and BRS are all established and validated biomarkers of parasympathetic activity. The claim that "higher vagal tone = greater autonomic flexibility, faster recovery from stress, better emotional regulation, and enhanced cognitive performance" is well-supported by the literature.

#### Evidence Strength
- **Strong:** RMSSD and HF-HRV as vagal tone proxies are validated across hundreds of studies (Laborde et al., 2017; Shaffer & Ginsberg, 2017).
- **Strong:** According to PubMed, the Leganes-Fonteneau et al. (2021) registered report directly demonstrates that resonance breathing increases interoceptive accuracy via baroreflex mechanisms, linking vagal tone to interoceptive capacity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2021.09.003)).
- **Strong:** According to PubMed, Xiong et al. (2025) demonstrate that 0.1 Hz slow-paced breathing increases heartbeat oscillatory potentials in right prefrontal cortex, mediating enhanced emotional control ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijchp.2025.100571)).

#### Gaps
- The vault states "60-second slow breathing raises HF-HRV in trauma survivors (single-session effects)" without a specific citation.
- The vault states "8-week daily practice shows cortical thickness gains in dlPFC and insula" without a specific citation. These are plausible claims from meditation neuroscience literature but need pinned references.

#### Contradictions
- **Important caveat:** RSA is *not* a pure measure of vagal efferent output. According to PubMed, the Grossman et al. (2026) critique specifically challenges the assumption that RSA is "a direct measure of the extent of central vagal drive to the heart" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260110)). The vagal tone concept note should acknowledge that RSA is influenced by respiratory parameters (rate, depth) and is not a simple readout of vagal efferent activity.

#### Emerging Research (2024-2026)
- According to PubMed, Plantard et al. (2025) show that heartbeat-evoked potentials change with blood pressure and slow-paced breathing, with insular and cingulate cortex sources — providing neural imaging evidence for vagal-interoceptive pathways ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00118.2025)).
- According to PubMed, Zaccaro et al. (2026) demonstrate respiratory-phase modulation of heartbeat-evoked cortical oscillations, with enhanced cardiac interoception during exhalation mediated by alpha-band activity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.121711)).

#### Recommendations
- Add RSA measurement caveat per Grossman critique.
- Pin specific citations for the 60-second trauma survivor and cortical thickness claims.
- Add Zaccaro 2026 and Plantard 2025 as evidence for cardiac-interoceptive neural mechanisms.

### 3. Polyvagal Theory

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1)
**Validation status:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — ACTIVE DEBATE

This is the most consequential validation item in the cluster. The vault treats polyvagal theory as "the foundational theoretical framework for all somnistics interventions." The 2026 Grossman critique poses a serious challenge that must be addressed.

#### Mechanism Validation
- The three-tier hierarchy (ventral vagal, sympathetic, dorsal vagal) is a useful *clinical heuristic* but is challenged as a *scientific theory* of autonomic organization.
- The concept of neuroception (subconscious safety/threat detection governing state shifts) remains clinically useful but is not independently validated as a distinct neural process.

#### Evidence Strength — Supporting
- According to PubMed, Porges (2025) published a comprehensive review of PVT current status, clinical applications, and future directions in Clinical Neuropsychiatry ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.36131/cnfioritieditore20250301)).
- According to PubMed, Porges (2025) also published a journey paper in Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience synthesizing PVT with transcriptomics and neurophysiology advances ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2025.1659083)).

#### Evidence Strength — Challenging
- **CRITICAL:** According to PubMed, Grossman et al. (2026) published "Why The Polyvagal Theory Is Untenable" with **38 co-authors** — highly acknowledged experts in vagus nerve physiology and vertebrate evolution. They conclude that "major tenets of the PVT are not supported by past or current knowledge and, in several instances, are inconsistent with the broader evidence base." Specific challenges include: (1) RSA is not a direct measure of central vagal drive; (2) PVT mischaracterizes neuroanatomy of Nucleus Ambiguus vs. Dorsal Motor Nucleus; (3) PVT's evolutionary claims about vagal phylogeny are not supported; (4) mammalian social behavior specificity claims are incorrect ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260110)).
- According to PubMed, Porges (2026) responded in the same journal issue, arguing the critique engages a "reconstructed proxy" of PVT through "persistent category errors" and that disagreements reflect "differences in measurement preference, level of analysis, or theoretical framing rather than evidence against the theory's organizing principles" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260111)).

#### Gaps
- The vault's polyvagal concept note already references Grossman's critique (evidence note grossman-2026-polyvagal-theory-untenable exists) but the concept note body does not discuss the debate or its implications.
- The vault does not articulate which specific elements of PVT are essential vs. optional for SRL's framework.

#### Contradictions — SEVERITY: HIGH
The Grossman et al. 2026 paper represents the most significant challenge to polyvagal theory to date. Key contradictions relevant to SRL:

1. **RSA as ventral vagal index:** The vault states "RSA is a measurable index of ventral vagal function." Grossman et al. argue RSA is influenced by respiratory parameters and is not specific to ventral vagal output. This undermines the RSA-as-measurement claims in vagal-tone and polyvagal-theory notes.

2. **Ventral vs. dorsal vagal distinction:** The vault's clinical framework (VVC = good, DVC = bad/freeze) oversimplifies the actual neuroanatomy. Both nuclei contribute to cardiac regulation and the distinction is not as clean as PVT proposes.

3. **Phylogenetic hierarchy:** The three-tier evolutionary sequence (DVC oldest, then sympathetic, then VVC newest) is challenged by comparative physiologists who note vagal complexity in non-mammalian vertebrates.

#### Impact Assessment for SRL
**The clinical and practical applications of PVT-inspired interventions remain valid even if the theoretical framework is revised.** Slow breathing demonstrably improves HRV and autonomic outcomes regardless of whether the mechanism maps onto Porges' specific three-tier model. SRL should:
- Frame PVT as a *clinical heuristic* rather than a validated scientific theory
- Emphasize that the *interventions* (slow breathing, resonant frequency training, co-regulation) are evidence-based independent of PVT's specific anatomical and evolutionary claims
- Acknowledge the debate transparently — this builds credibility

#### Recommendations
- **URGENT:** Update the polyvagal-theory concept note to explicitly discuss the Grossman et al. 2026 critique and Porges' response.
- Reframe vault language from "polyvagal theory explains why breath-based training works" to "polyvagal theory provides a clinical framework for understanding autonomic state shifts; the breathing mechanisms are independently validated through baroreflex and cardiorespiratory research."
- Add a "Theoretical Debate" section to the polyvagal-theory note.
- Consider the vault's dependence on PVT terminology — can SRL's framework function with "autonomic state hierarchy" language without requiring PVT's specific claims?

### 4. Resonant Breathing Frequency

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1)
**Validation status:** SUPPORTED

#### Mechanism Validation
The described mechanism — slow diaphragmatic breathing at ~0.1 Hz entrains cardiorespiratory oscillations, stimulating the baroreflex loop and maximizing HRV amplitude — is well-established and replicable. The vault's description is accurate and well-sourced.

#### Evidence Strength
- **Strong (foundational):** Lehrer et al. (2000) established the resonance frequency protocol. According to PubMed, Shaffer & Meehan (2020) published a practical guide to resonance frequency assessment confirming the model ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2020.570400)).
- **Strong:** According to PubMed, Sakakibara et al. (2020) demonstrated that paced breathing at the individual's LF peak frequency increased BRS more than the standard breathing maneuver, supporting individualized resonant frequency assessment ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09453-z)).
- **Strong:** According to PubMed, Leganes-Fonteneau et al. (2021) showed resonance breathing increases interoceptive accuracy through baroreflex mechanisms in a registered report (n=67) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2021.09.003)).
- **Strong:** According to PubMed, Xiong et al. (2025) provide neural evidence that 0.1 Hz SPB increases heartbeat oscillatory potentials in right prefrontal cortex mediating emotional control ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijchp.2025.100571)).

#### Gaps
- The vault claims "Single 60-second slow-breath trial raises HF-HRV in trauma survivors" without specific citation.
- The vault claims "8-week daily practice shows cortical thickness gains in dlPFC and insula" without specific citation.
- The "5-Breath Protocol" (Randy's core protocol) is presented without direct evidence — it is an extrapolation from the resonance frequency literature applied to a minimum effective dose model. This is a reasonable clinical innovation but should be distinguished from the evidence base.

#### Contradictions
None for the core mechanism. The baroreflex resonance model is well-accepted.

#### Emerging Research (2024-2026)
- According to PubMed, Plantard et al. (2025) provide direct evidence that slow-paced breathing at 6 cycles/min modulates heartbeat-evoked potentials with insular and cingulate cortex involvement ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00118.2025)).
- The vault already references several 2025-2026 papers (Kim 2026 humming/resonance, Diaz-Lozano 2026 stochastic noise, Nakamura 2025 respiration-attention).

#### Recommendations
- Pin specific citations for the uncited claims (60-second trauma, cortical thickness).
- Add Plantard 2025 and Xiong 2025 for neural-level mechanism evidence.
- Clearly distinguish "evidence-based mechanism" from "SRL-original protocol design" in the 5-Breath Protocol section.

### 5. Cardiac-Anchored Breathing

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)
**Validation status:** INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE (as a distinct modality) / PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (underlying mechanisms)

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept of synchronizing breathing to one's own heartbeat rather than an external clock is physiologically plausible. The underlying mechanisms are supported:
- Individual resonant frequencies vary (4.5-6.5 bpm) — established
- Resonant frequency shifts with autonomic state — established
- Real-time cardiac data can inform breath pacing — technically feasible

However, "cardiac-anchored breathing" as a *specific modality superior to fixed-rate pacing* lacks direct empirical comparison studies.

#### Evidence Strength
- **No direct evidence** for cardiac-anchored breathing as a distinct protocol. No PubMed results were found for heartbeat-synchronized breathing biofeedback.
- **Indirect support:** The resonant frequency literature supports individualized pacing over fixed-rate. According to PubMed, Sakakibara et al. (2020) showed individualized LF-peak pacing outperforms standard assessment for BRS improvement ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09453-z)). This supports the *principle* of personalization but not the specific implementation of beat-to-beat cardiac anchoring.

#### Gaps — SEVERITY: HIGH
- No published study compares cardiac-anchored breathing to fixed-rate paced breathing for HRV outcomes.
- No published study validates real-time wearable-driven breath pacing (Apple Watch/Whoop/Oura feeding breath cues) for autonomic outcomes.
- The competitive differentiation claim ("nobody else combines these modalities this way") is likely accurate from a product standpoint but the *clinical superiority* of this approach is undemonstrated.

#### Contradictions
- The concept implies that beat-to-beat cardiac anchoring improves outcomes. However, resonant frequency is relatively stable within a session — it shifts across days/states but not beat-to-beat. It is unclear whether the granularity of beat-to-beat adaptation adds clinical value beyond session-level individualization.

#### Recommendations
- **Priority:** This concept needs a validation study or at minimum a proof-of-concept pilot comparing cardiac-anchored vs. fixed-rate breathing on acute HRV outcomes.
- Frame as "SRL-original innovation built on established resonant frequency science" rather than implying the specific modality is evidence-based.
- Search for HeartMath literature on cardiac coherence feedback, which is the closest existing analog.

### 6. Neuro-Respiratory Cardiac Coherence (NRCC)

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)
**Validation status:** INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE (as a combined protocol) / PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (individual components)

#### Mechanism Validation
NRCC combines three separately validated components: (1) resonant breathing, (2) binaural beat entrainment, (3) heartbeat-as-metronome. Each component has some evidence individually, but the *combined* protocol has no published evidence.

**Component evidence:**
- Resonant breathing: SUPPORTED (see above)
- Binaural beats + autonomic effects: According to PubMed, McConnell et al. (2014) demonstrated that theta-frequency binaural beats post-exercise increased parasympathetic activation and sympathetic withdrawal compared to placebo in a double-blind RCT (n=21) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01248)). However, this is a small study and the effect was transient.
- Heartbeat-as-metronome: No direct evidence (see cardiac-anchored breathing above)

#### Evidence Strength
- **Weak for the combined protocol.** No published study combines HRV coherence breathing with binaural beats with cardiac-anchored pacing.
- **Limited for binaural beats + HRV.** PubMed search returned only 1 result (McConnell 2014). The binaural beats literature is sparse and mixed for autonomic outcomes.
- Zero PubMed results for "cardiorespiratory coherence binaural beats entrainment."

#### Gaps — SEVERITY: HIGH
- The combined NRCC protocol is entirely SRL-original and unvalidated.
- The claim that "nobody else combines these modalities this way" is likely true precisely *because* the combination lacks evidence.
- The 8-layer progression model has no empirical grounding.
- The binaural beat component lacks robust autonomic evidence — most binaural beat research focuses on EEG entrainment, not HRV.

#### Contradictions
- Binaural beat research is generally characterized by small samples, inconsistent findings, and publication bias. Several reviews have concluded that the evidence for binaural beats affecting cognition or physiology is weak and heterogeneous.

#### Recommendations
- **High priority for evidence development.** If NRCC is the "core SRL innovation," it needs its own validation study.
- Clearly label this as "SRL-original protocol" distinct from the evidence-based components.
- Strengthen the binaural beats evidence base — search beyond PubMed into the psychology and neuroscience literature.
- Consider whether the binaural beat component adds measurable value or is a distraction from the strong resonant breathing evidence.

### 7. Co-Regulation

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.0)
**Validation status:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept of one person's nervous system state influencing another's is supported by interpersonal neurobiology and physiological synchrony research. The specific mechanisms cited (vocal prosody, facial expression, respiratory entrainment, mirror neurons) are established in the literature, though their *magnitude* in clinical settings is less clear.

#### Evidence Strength
- **Moderate:** The interpersonal neurobiology literature (Siegel, Porges) provides theoretical support. Physiological synchrony research is growing but methodologically complex.
- According to PubMed, Wespi et al. (2025) conducted a multi-centre field study (n=214 medical first responders) demonstrating that physiological synchrony (measured via HR, RMSSD, SDNN) varies significantly by task nature, with higher synchrony during cooperative tasks. Proximity increased synchrony ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41077-025-00335-5)). This is the strongest direct evidence for the co-regulation concept in clinical team settings.
- According to PubMed, Algumaei et al. (2023) showed physiological synchrony predicts task performance and emotional state in three-member teams, though method choice significantly affects findings ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23042268)).

#### Gaps — SEVERITY: MODERATE
- The vault's "Team Breath Before You Start" protocol has no published evidence. The concept of a synchronized team breath shifting collective autonomic baseline is a clinical innovation, not evidence-based practice.
- "Mirror neuron activation: Observing calm produces calm in the observer" — the mirror neuron system is scientifically contested, and its application to autonomic entrainment is speculative.
- No PubMed results specifically for vagal tone co-regulation or interpersonal autonomic respiratory entrainment.

#### Contradictions
- Physiological synchrony does not always predict better outcomes — high synchrony during stressful events may reflect shared stress rather than co-regulation. The directionality assumption (calm person calms others) is an oversimplification.

#### Emerging Research
- According to PubMed, Wespi et al. (2025) provides the strongest new evidence that ECG-based physiological synchrony is measurable in medical teams and varies with task demands ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41077-025-00335-5)). This directly supports the vault's positioning.

#### Recommendations
- Add Wespi et al. 2025 to evidence base — it is the best clinical team physiological synchrony study found.
- Remove or qualify the mirror neuron claim.
- Distinguish between "physiological synchrony occurs in clinical teams" (SUPPORTED) and "deliberate team breathing shifts collective autonomic state" (UNVALIDATED).
- Acknowledge that synchrony can reflect shared stress, not just co-regulation.

### 8. State Transition

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1)
**Validation status:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept of state transitions — shifting between autonomic or cognitive states — is well-established in neurophysiology. The framing of gap moments as "engineered state transitions" is SRL-original.

The vault's claim that "amygdala deactivation takes ~90 seconds minimum after threat response" is a commonly cited figure (attributed to LeDoux) but the specific 90-second timeline is not well-established in the primary literature. The amygdala response dynamics depend heavily on context, intensity, and individual differences.

#### Evidence Strength
- **Moderate-strong for the general concept.** State transitions are well-documented in neuroscience.
- **Weak for the specific 90-second claim.** The "90-second rule" is widely cited in popular neuroscience but lacks a clear primary source with that specific number.
- The vault cites strong neuroimaging evidence (Sacchet lab jhana studies, Sezer 2025) for neural state transitions during meditation.

#### Gaps
- "CRNAs experience 30-40 state transitions per shift" — no citation for this specific number.
- "Residual sympathetic bleed between cases" — this concept is clinically intuitive but the specific term and mechanism are not established in the literature.
- The 60-second NeuroMinute as "minimum effective dose" for state transition is extrapolated from the amygdala timing claim, which itself is weakly sourced.

#### Contradictions
- The ~90-second amygdala deactivation timeline is oversimplified. Threat responses can persist for minutes to hours depending on context, and cortisol effects peak at 20-30 minutes.

#### Recommendations
- Verify or qualify the 90-second amygdala claim — find the primary source or soften the language.
- Reframe "30-40 state transitions per shift" as clinical estimate, not evidence.
- Search for stress recovery time course literature to ground the "residual sympathetic bleed" concept.

### 9. State Drift

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)
**Validation status:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (underlying mechanisms) / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE (as a named construct)

#### Mechanism Validation
The five-step mechanism (arousal spike, incomplete recovery, baseline elevation, perceptual narrowing, error vulnerability) is physiologically plausible and aligns with allostatic load theory (McEwen). The concept that cumulative incomplete recovery cycles degrade performance is consistent with stress physiology.

However, "state drift" as a named, measurable construct does not appear in the biomedical literature. No PubMed results were found for this term in autonomic/clinical contexts.

#### Evidence Strength
- **Indirect support:** Allostatic load theory (McEwen, 1998, 2004) describes the cumulative physiological cost of repeated stress responses without adequate recovery — this is the scientific analog of state drift.
- The vault already has evidence notes for HRV monitoring in surgeons and ICU nurses showing progressive autonomic changes during shifts.
- No direct validation of state drift as a measurable, real-time detectable phenomenon using consumer wearables.

#### Gaps — SEVERITY: MODERATE
- "State drift is the proximal cause of most clinical errors" — this is a strong claim without direct evidence. Error causation research focuses on fatigue, distraction, and system factors rather than autonomic drift per se.
- The mechanism assumes incomplete recovery is *detectable* and *correctable* in real-time — this requires wearable HRV monitoring at sufficient resolution and validated thresholds.
- No published study has measured progressive within-shift HRV decline and correlated it with error rates in anesthesia.

#### Contradictions
None direct — the concept is too novel to have generated contradictory evidence. The underlying allostatic load framework is well-accepted.

#### Recommendations
- Ground the concept in allostatic load literature explicitly.
- Qualify the error causation claim — "state drift may contribute to clinical error vulnerability" rather than "is the proximal cause."
- Design a validation study: continuous HRV monitoring in CRNAs across full shifts, with state drift detection algorithm.

### 10. Autonomic Home Base

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)
**Validation status:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (concept level) / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE (as a trainable, navigable state)

#### Mechanism Validation
The concept of a trained baseline autonomic state is consistent with:
- Set-point theory in homeostasis
- Meditation research showing altered trait vs. state effects
- Motor learning research on default states and return-to-baseline patterns

The four-component model (recognition, navigation, sustaining, switching) is a useful clinical framework but is SRL-original and unvalidated.

#### Evidence Strength
- **Indirect support:** Long-term meditators show altered resting-state HRV and EEG patterns (numerous studies), supporting the idea that baseline autonomic states are trainable.
- **Indirect support:** The "relaxed alertness" state documented in the Sezer 2025 Harvard review (concurrent sympathetic + parasympathetic activation) could be understood as a cultivated "home base."
- No published literature uses the term "autonomic home base" or validates the concept of a navigable state-space coordinate that can be deliberately returned to.

#### Gaps — SEVERITY: LOW-MODERATE
- The concept is clinically intuitive and consistent with existing science but unvalidated as a distinct construct.
- "Equal breathing becomes home base" — no evidence that equal breathing specifically establishes a reference state better than other breathing patterns.
- The "state space language" (navigable coordinates) is a metaphor, not a validated measurement framework.

#### Contradictions
None — the concept is too novel. The closest critique would be that autonomic "set points" are dynamic and context-dependent, not fixed coordinates.

#### Recommendations
- Frame explicitly as SRL-original clinical framework built on allostatic/homeostatic science.
- Reference the meditation trait-vs-state literature for the trained baseline concept.
- Consider whether this concept could be operationalized and measured (e.g., resting-state HRV measured each morning as "home base" tracking).

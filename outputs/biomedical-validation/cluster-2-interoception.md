## Executive Summary

The interoception cluster contains 9 concepts spanning a spectrum from well-established neuroscience (interoception, interoceptive accuracy) to SRL-original hypotheses (interoceptive-suppression-hypothesis, diaphragmatic-blindness). The core science is strong — interoception is a mature and rapidly expanding field with robust neuroimaging evidence, validated measurement tools (MAIA-2, heartbeat detection tasks), and growing clinical applications. The SRL-original concepts are well-reasoned extrapolations from established science but currently lack direct empirical validation. This is not a weakness if positioned correctly — it is the research opportunity.

**Overall cluster health:** 7/10
- Strong foundational science
- Validated measurement tools
- Several SRL-original concepts need explicit framing as hypotheses, not established findings
- No direct contradictions with current literature
- Significant gap: no published studies on interoception in CRNA/anesthesia provider populations specifically

## Concept-by-Concept Validation

### 1. Interoception

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** STRONGLY SUPPORTED. The vault's description of the neural architecture (posterior insula for raw sensory input, anterior insula for complex integration, ACC for error monitoring, brainstem nuclei for autonomic relay) is consistent with the established literature. According to PubMed, Nikolova et al. (2025) confirmed distinct microstructural patterns in insular, cingulate, and primary sensory cortices underlying interoceptive sensitivity and precision using quantitative brain imaging in over 200 participants ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0787-24.2025)). Chemis et al. (2025) demonstrated that connectivity between amygdala and insula cortex is specifically linked to breathing-related interoceptive processes ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13415-025-01328-7)).

**2. Evidence strength:** HIGH. The field has moved from theoretical to empirical over the past 15 years. Craig (2009) and Critchley (2017) established the foundational neuroscience, which the vault correctly cites. The 2025-2026 literature shows continued strengthening with large-sample neuroimaging studies.

**3. Gaps:**
- The vault claim that "CRNAs with higher interoceptive awareness show better clinical decision-making under pressure" is listed under Clinical Observations but lacks a specific citation. No PubMed studies were found linking interoception to clinical decision-making in anesthesia specifically. The closest evidence is Salvato et al. (2021), which found that physicians' MAIA-2 Trusting scores predicted therapeutic decision-making under uncertainty during COVID-19 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256806)) — but this is about risk-taking tolerance, not decision quality.
- The claim that "interoceptive accuracy predicts emotional regulation capacity" is broadly supported but the relationship is more nuanced than stated. According to PubMed, Garfinkel et al. (2015) showed the three dimensions (accuracy, sensibility, awareness) are dissociable, and the correspondence between them only emerges in individuals with the greatest interoceptive accuracy ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2014.11.004)).

**4. Contradictions:**
- The Schandry heartbeat counting task, listed as an "objective accuracy measure" in the vault, is increasingly contested in the literature. Multiple studies note it confounds interoceptive accuracy with heartbeat knowledge, body composition, and cardiac factors. Harrison et al. (2021) developed the Filter Detection Task for breathing-related interoception as a more rigorous alternative ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2021.108185)). The vault should note this debate.

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- According to PubMed, Lavretsky et al. (2026) position interoceptive regulation as central to brain health resilience across the lifespan ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-026-02332-2))
- Matus et al. (2026) demonstrated that MAIA-2 Attention Regulation subscale moderates the relationship between hypoglycemia exposure and symptom recognition in Type 1 diabetes — direct evidence of interoceptive awareness having clinical utility ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-2242))
- The NIH $4M interoception initiative and Patapoutian's interoception atlas (both referenced in vault) represent field-level validation of interoception as a priority research area

**Recommendation:** Vault note is well-constructed. Add nuance about the Schandry task debate. The CRNA clinical observation needs a citation or explicit "clinical observation, unpublished" framing.

### 2. Interoceptive Literacy

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.0)

**1. Mechanism validation:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED. The concept of "interoceptive literacy" as a trained, skilled form of awareness that maps onto MAIA-2 subscales (Noticing, Attention Regulation, Emotional Awareness, Body Listening, Self-Regulation, Trusting) is a coherent synthesis but appears to be an SRL-original framing. The term "interoceptive literacy" does not appear as a formal construct in the PubMed literature. The closest existing construct is "interoceptive sensibility" (Garfinkel's self-report dimension) or the broader concept of "body literacy."

**2. Evidence strength:** MODERATE. The MAIA-2 subscales individually have strong validation evidence. According to PubMed, a COSMIN systematic review by Bravo et al. (2025) found MAIA-2 showed the strongest evidence among body awareness PROMs, with acceptable results across 4 of 5 psychometric properties ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13243270)). However, the specific four-stage progression (Detect -> Interpret -> Respond -> Trust) is an SRL construct, not a validated model.

**3. Gaps:**
- The vault states this is "the educational gap that the AANA Category A CE application identifies: providers monitor patients but lack skills to monitor themselves." This framing is compelling but the supporting evidence is indirect — no published study has measured MAIA-2 scores in CRNAs or compared them to non-clinical populations.
- The concept of "clinical precision" applied to self-monitoring (knowing one's own HR is 15bpm above baseline) conflates interoceptive awareness with biometric monitoring. The literature does not demonstrate that interoceptive training alone achieves this level of precision — it typically requires biofeedback devices.

**4. Contradictions:** None direct, but the positioning of interoceptive literacy as distinct from standard interoceptive awareness needs clearer differentiation. In the published literature, what the vault calls "literacy" overlaps substantially with the higher subscales of the MAIA-2 (Self-Regulation, Trusting).

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- According to PubMed, Robertson et al. (2024) surveyed occupational therapy practitioners on interoceptive awareness practices and found that 63.89% reported interoceptive awareness interventions to be effective, but formal assessment tools were rarely used ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/15394492241277470)) — supports the idea that practitioners value interoceptive awareness but lack structured assessment
- Mensinger et al. (2024) showed HRV biofeedback improved interoceptive sensibility in healthcare workers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-024-09621-w))

**Recommendation:** Explicitly flag "interoceptive literacy" as an SRL-original construct that synthesizes established MAIA-2 dimensions into a clinical competency framework. This is a strength, not a weakness — but it should not be presented as established nomenclature. Consider: "Interoceptive literacy (SRL construct) — a trained clinical competency framework built on validated MAIA-2 dimensions."

### 3. Interoceptive Accuracy

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** STRONGLY SUPPORTED. The Garfinkel trichotomy (accuracy, sensibility, awareness) is well-established. According to PubMed, Garfinkel et al. (2015) provided foundational empirical support for these three dimensions being distinct and dissociable in an N=80 sample ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2014.11.004)). The vault's description is accurate.

**2. Evidence strength:** HIGH. This is peer-reviewed, replicated science. Garfinkel extended the framework to autism ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2015.12.003)) and cross-modal domains including respiratory interoception ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0014)). According to PubMed, Chi et al. (2026) applied the framework to borderline personality traits using MAIA-2 and heartbeat-evoked potentials, finding intact accuracy but altered metacognitive flexibility — extending the construct ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijop.70178)).

**3. Gaps:**
- The vault links interoceptive accuracy to the SRB-60's "Micro-Interoceptive Skill" domain, but the SRB-60 tests self-report labeling within 60 seconds, which is closer to interoceptive sensibility (self-reported) than accuracy (behavioral task performance). The construct mapping may need refinement.
- No evidence that interoceptive accuracy can be meaningfully assessed in a 60-second window under clinical conditions. Lab-based accuracy tasks (heartbeat counting, filter detection) require controlled environments.

**4. Contradictions:**
- Active debate about whether heartbeat detection tasks measure true interoceptive accuracy or confounded variables. According to PubMed, Garfinkel et al. (2017) themselves showed that cardiac and respiratory accuracy are dissociable, meaning single-modality tests may miss important variance ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0014)).
- Harrison et al. (2021) developed the Filter Detection Task specifically because of concerns about the Schandry task's validity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2021.108185)).

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- Nikolova et al. (2025) showed that microstructural brain markers (myelin content, cortical iron in insula, cingulate, and primary sensory cortices) underlie individual differences in respiratory interoceptive accuracy and metacognition ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0787-24.2025)) — important for the neural mechanism story.

**Recommendation:** The vault note is accurate but thin. Enrich with the measurement debate (Schandry vs. Filter Detection Task) and clarify the accuracy-vs-sensibility distinction as it relates to the SRB-60.

### 4. Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, INDIRECTLY. The hypothesis that medical training suppresses interoception is plausible and aligns with converging lines of evidence, but there is no published study that directly tests this claim. The supporting evidence is indirect:
- According to PubMed, Handford et al. (2013) found that clinical practice (not medical education) impacts empathy development, and interoception data "did not support a link between visceral feedback and empathic processes" in their sample ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0065159)). This is actually a partial counter-argument to the ISH — it suggests clinical exposure may preserve rather than suppress certain perceptual abilities.
- The "hidden curriculum" of medical education suppressing emotions is well-documented in medical sociology literature, but these studies do not specifically measure interoception.
- According to PubMed, Crivelli et al. (2025) found discrepancies between physiological activation and subjective stress perception in neurosurgeons, highlighting the importance of interoceptive awareness in modulating stress responses in high-stakes clinicians ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568430)) — this is the closest direct evidence for clinician interoceptive disconnection under stress.

**2. Evidence strength:** LOW-MODERATE for the specific hypothesis. HIGH for the component claims (hidden curriculum exists, interoception can be suppressed, burnout has physiological components). The chain linking them together is the novel contribution, and it is currently untested.

**3. Gaps (significant):**
- **Critical gap:** No published study has measured MAIA-2 scores in clinicians longitudinally across training to test whether they decline. The testable prediction "clinicians with more years of training will show lower MAIA-2 scores" is falsifiable but untested.
- **Critical gap:** No published study has correlated MAIA-2 scores with burnout measures (MBI) specifically in clinician populations. According to PubMed, the 4 studies found linking interoception and burnout in healthcare (PMIDs 38502516, 36810510, 40357485, 41464305) address burnout interventions but do not test the suppression hypothesis directly.
- The biological framing ("burnout is partly the downstream consequence of a nervous system that can no longer self-regulate because its primary feedback channel has been suppressed") is a strong claim that needs careful qualification. According to PubMed, Dale et al. (2025) showed that a Somatic Psychoeducational Intervention integrating interoceptive training, breathwork, and somatic movement did reduce autonomic reactivity and psychological distress in healthcare workers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13243236)) — this is consistent with the ISH but does not prove the causal chain.

**4. Contradictions:**
- Handford et al. (2013) found that clinical practice specifically preserved empathic accuracy against age-related decline, suggesting clinical exposure may enhance rather than suppress certain forms of perceptual sensitivity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0065159)). This is a potential counter-argument to the ISH that should be acknowledged.
- The hypothesis frames burnout as primarily biological, while the dominant literature treats it as a multifactorial organizational-psychological-biological phenomenon. The vault acknowledges this with "partly" but the overall framing emphasizes the biological cause strongly.

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- According to PubMed, Zhou et al. (2025) reviewed MBSR for emergency department nurses and found that MBSR improves interoceptive awareness and autonomic balance alongside reducing burnout — supporting the intervention logic, though not the suppression mechanism per se ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.5498/wjp.v15.i9.107630))
- Dale et al. (2025) — Polyvagal Theory-grounded intervention with interoceptive training for healthcare workers showed increased oxytocin and reduced distress ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13243236))

**Recommendation:** This is the highest-risk concept in the cluster. It is a compelling hypothesis with strong face validity, but it is currently in the "plausible but untested" category. The vault should:
1. Explicitly label this as "SRL hypothesis" rather than established science
2. Acknowledge the Handford (2013) counter-evidence
3. Identify the specific studies needed to test it (cross-sectional MAIA-2 in training-year cohorts; longitudinal tracking; MAIA-2 x MBI correlation)
4. Position this as the research agenda, not as a proven mechanism

### 5. Diaphragmatic Blindness

**Vault status:** canonical (v1.0)

**1. Mechanism validation:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED. The general concept that people can have poor awareness of their own breathing patterns is supported by the interoception literature. According to PubMed, Garfinkel et al. (2017) demonstrated that respiratory interoception is dissociable from cardiac interoception, and poor respiratory accuracy is associated with heightened anxiety ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0014)). Chemis et al. (2025) confirmed specific amygdala-insula connectivity patterns for breathing-related interoception ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13415-025-01328-7)).

However, the specific term "diaphragmatic blindness" and the specific claim that it is "the most common finding in Week 1 interoceptive assessment" for clinicians are SRL-original observations without published support.

**2. Evidence strength:** LOW for the specific construct. MODERATE for the underlying science. The respiratory interoception literature supports that breathing awareness varies among individuals and can be measured, but the specific deficit profile described (clinicians who manage ventilators with precision cannot feel their own diaphragm) is an unpublished clinical observation.

**3. Gaps:**
- No published study specifically examines diaphragmatic proprioception/interoception as a distinct deficit in healthcare workers
- The claim that diaphragmatic blindness "deepens under stress" is physiologically plausible (attention narrows under stress, autonomic overriding occurs) but not empirically established as a distinct phenomenon
- No validated instrument specifically measures diaphragmatic awareness as distinct from general respiratory interoception

**4. Contradictions:** None in the literature, as the concept is too specific to have attracted direct contradiction.

**5. Emerging research:** No specific studies on this construct.

**Recommendation:** Downgrade from canonical to draft. This is an SRL-original clinical observation that needs empirical grounding before canonical status. It should be positioned as "a specific instance of respiratory interoceptive deficit observed in clinical practice" rather than as an established construct. The supporting evidence from Kerr (2013) on alpha modulation is relevant but does not specifically validate "diaphragmatic blindness."

### 6. Diaphragmatic Literacy

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED. The four components (Perceive, Distinguish, Control, Maintain) describe a reasonable skill progression. The general science of breathing training improving autonomic regulation is well-supported. However, "diaphragmatic literacy" as a named construct does not appear in the published literature.

**2. Evidence strength:** MODERATE for the underlying science of breathing training. LOW for the specific construct. No published study has operationalized or measured "diaphragmatic literacy" as defined here.

**3. Gaps:**
- The competitive differentiator claim ("Calm and Headspace do not systematically train the proprioceptive and interoceptive discrimination required for true diaphragmatic control") is a market positioning statement, not a scientifically validated claim. No comparative study exists.
- "Maintain diaphragmatic breathing patterns under stress without conscious monitoring" describes automaticity — which requires substantial evidence from motor learning literature to validate the timeline and methodology

**4. Contradictions:** None direct.

**5. Emerging research:** No studies on this specific construct.

**Recommendation:** Keep as draft. This is an SRL-original training construct, not established science. It should be clearly labeled as such. The four-component model is reasonable but needs validation.

### 7. Embodied Metacognition

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED with significant caveats. The vault defines this as monitoring cognition *through* somatic signals, distinguishing it from standard embodied cognition literature. According to PubMed, there are no studies using the term "embodied metacognition" in this specific way. The closest literature involves:
- Interoception-metacognition overlap: Nikolova et al. (2025) demonstrated that respiratory metacognition has specific microstructural correlates in midline prefrontal cortex, distinct from interoceptive sensitivity patterns in insula ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0787-24.2025))
- Chemis et al. (2025) found breathing-related interoceptive metacognitive bias correlated with amygdala-insula connectivity, suggesting a neural substrate for body-based meta-awareness ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13415-025-01328-7))
- According to PubMed, Tal et al. (2025) reviewed computational models of advanced meditation within the Active Inference framework, identifying precision weighting and interoceptive attentional modulation as common mechanisms — supporting the idea that somatic signals modulate metacognitive processes ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106539))

**2. Evidence strength:** LOW-MODERATE. The component elements (interoception has neural correlates; metacognition involves prefrontal-insular networks; meditation training alters both) are individually supported. The specific synthesis into "embodied metacognition" as defined by SRL is novel.

**3. Gaps:**
- The key question from the brief — "Can embodied metacognition be distinguished from standard metacognition in neuroimaging studies?" — has a cautiously affirmative answer. Nikolova et al. (2025) showed metacognitive components of interoception have *distinct* brain correlates from sensory components. However, nobody has tested whether monitoring cognition through body signals versus through abstract thought produces different neural signatures.
- The origin story (Sam Harris Waking Up app epiphany) is valuable biographical context but is not evidence for the construct.

**4. Contradictions:**
- The standard metacognition literature does not typically distinguish "embodied" from "abstract" metacognition. This is an SRL framing. Some researchers would argue all metacognition has embodied components and the distinction is not clean.

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- Tal et al. (2025) on computational phenomenology of meditation provides a theoretical framework (Active Inference) that could accommodate embodied metacognition ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106539))

**Recommendation:** Keep as draft. This is the most conceptually novel concept in the cluster and potentially the highest-value IP contribution. However, it needs rigorous differentiation from existing constructs (interoceptive metacognition, body awareness, somatic marker hypothesis). The Damasio somatic marker hypothesis (already in vault evidence base) is the closest established construct — clarify how embodied metacognition differs from or extends it.

### 8. MAIA-2-CRNA

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** WELL-GROUNDED. The MAIA-2 is one of the best-validated interoception instruments. According to PubMed, a 2025 COSMIN systematic review rated it as having the strongest psychometric evidence among body awareness PROMs ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13243270)). The approach of keeping core subscales intact and adding profession-specific items is methodologically sound.

**2. Evidence strength:** HIGH for the base instrument. According to PubMed, 17 MAIA-2 validation studies exist across languages and populations including Norwegian ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-023-04946-y)), Chinese ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.970982)), and transgender/gender-diverse adults ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/10731911241308085)). The instrument has been applied to clinical populations including IBD patients ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18020251)), Type 1 diabetes ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-2242)), and borderline personality traits ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijop.70178)). No CRNA-specific or anesthesia-provider adaptation exists in the literature — this is a genuine gap and opportunity.

**3. Gaps:**
- The proposed construct "Interoceptive Attention Under Load (IAUL-CRNA)" has no published precedent. The closest is Crivelli et al. (2025) on naturalistic stress assessment in neurosurgeons, which combined physiological monitoring with experience sampling ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568430)).
- The Chinese MAIA-2 validation (Zhang et al., 2022) required dropping items 1-4, 15, and 16 to achieve acceptable fit in a 7-factor model, suggesting the 8-factor structure may not be universally stable across populations ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.970982)). This is important for the CRNA adaptation — factor structure should be confirmed, not assumed.
- The "Dissociation Under Threat / Freeze-Tilt" extension module would need its own validation separate from MAIA-2 as it measures a qualitatively different construct.

**4. Contradictions:** None, but note that the Norwegian validation found gender moderates the relationship between MAIA-2 factors and health outcomes ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-023-04946-y)) — relevant given the gender distribution of the CRNA workforce.

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- Matus et al. (2026) used the MAIA-2 Attention Regulation subscale as a clinical moderator in Type 1 diabetes management, demonstrating the kind of "interoception as clinical tool" application that MAIA-2-CRNA aims for ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-2242))
- Mensinger (2025) demonstrated MAIA-2 subscales as mediators of HRV biofeedback effects on eating disorder symptoms in healthcare workers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eatbeh.2025.102060))

**Recommendation:** This is the most immediately actionable concept in the cluster. The research pathway (validation study, publication, integration with SRB-60) is clear and follows established psychometric methodology. Priorities:
1. Conduct a cross-sectional MAIA-2 study in CRNAs to establish baseline norms before developing the extension
2. Factor analysis to confirm the 8-factor structure holds in this population
3. Develop the IAUL-CRNA extension module with cognitive load items
4. Note that this could simultaneously provide data to test the Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis

### 9. Structured Interoception Training (SIT)

**Vault status:** draft (v0.1)

**1. Mechanism validation:** MODERATELY SUPPORTED for individual components, UNTESTED as an integrated protocol.

- **Mode 1 (Interoceptive Attention Training):** Body-scan and signal-tracking exercises have established evidence for improving interoceptive awareness. According to PubMed, Sharp et al. (2024) demonstrated in a pilot RCT that mindfulness training improved MAIA-2 Not-Worrying subscale in pregnant women ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/jicm.2024.0121)).
- **Mode 2 (Breath-Paced Autonomic Training):** HRV biofeedback has strong evidence. According to PubMed, Mensinger et al. (2024) showed HRV biofeedback improved interoceptive sensibility and resilience in healthcare workers specifically ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-024-09621-w)). Mensinger (2025) confirmed specific MAIA-2 subscales as mediators of change ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eatbeh.2025.102060)).
- **Mode 3 (Graded Interoceptive Exposure):** CO2 tolerance drills and air-hunger protocols have evidence in anxiety treatment literature. Harrison et al. (2021) developed a validated respiratory interoception task using inspiratory resistance ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2021.108185)).

**2. Evidence strength:** MODERATE for components; LOW for the integrated protocol. No published study has tested a tri-modal interoception training protocol in clinicians.

**3. Gaps:**
- The claim that SIT is a "causal contributor to clinician durability" is a strong causal claim without evidence. The intervention studies that exist (Mensinger, Dale) are pilot/feasibility studies, not powered efficacy trials.
- The 60-second delivery format (NeuroMinute) has no published evidence. Existing interoceptive training interventions are typically 8-week programs (MBSR format). Whether meaningful interoceptive change can occur in 60-second units is an untested but important claim.
- "Biofeedback-closed-loop" training is described but the specific biofeedback parameters (which HRV metrics, what thresholds, what feedback modality) are not specified at the evidence level.

**4. Contradictions:**
- According to PubMed, Dale et al. (2025) tested a 3-4 week intervention (not 60 seconds) with significant attrition (noted as a limitation), raising questions about adherence in the micro-dose format SRL proposes ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13243236)).

**5. Emerging research (2024-2026):**
- Dale et al. (2025) — Somatic Psychoeducational Intervention for healthcare workers integrating interoceptive training, breathwork, and somatic movement showed increased oxytocin and reduced distress with Polyvagal Theory grounding ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13243236)) — closest existing analogue to SIT

**Recommendation:** Keep as draft. The tri-modal architecture is well-reasoned but needs:
1. Specification of dosing parameters (how much interoceptive attention training, at what frequency, for how long)
2. Evidence or rationale for the 60-second micro-dose format vs. established 8-week protocols
3. Pilot data before claiming causal contribution to clinician durability

## Cross-Cutting Findings

### Strongest assets in this cluster
1. **Interoception** and **interoceptive accuracy** are built on rock-solid science with expanding evidence
2. **MAIA-2** is extremely well-validated and continues to gain new validation studies and clinical applications
3. **MAIA-2-CRNA** fills a genuine gap in the literature — no anesthesia-provider specific interoception instrument exists
4. The overall theoretical framework (interoception -> literacy -> training -> clinical performance) is coherent and aligns with the direction of the field

### Highest-risk concepts
1. **Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis** — compelling but untested; needs to be positioned as a research hypothesis
2. **Diaphragmatic Blindness** — should be downgraded from canonical; it is a clinical observation, not an established construct
3. **Embodied Metacognition** — novel and high-value but needs differentiation from existing constructs (somatic marker hypothesis, interoceptive metacognition)

### Critical literature gaps (research opportunities)
1. No published MAIA-2 data from CRNA or anesthesia provider populations
2. No longitudinal tracking of interoceptive awareness across medical/nursing training
3. No published correlation between MAIA-2 scores and burnout measures (MBI) in clinicians
4. No published evidence for interoceptive training improving clinical performance (decision-making, error rate, etc.)
5. No published evidence for micro-dose (60-second) interoceptive training formats

### PubMed search note
Zero results were found for the following specific combinations, indicating genuine literature gaps (not search methodology issues):
- "interoception" + "anesthesia" + "clinical decision making"
- "interoceptive" + "hidden curriculum" + "medical education"
- "diaphragmatic breathing" + "interoception" + "proprioception"
- "interoception" + "emotional suppression" + "clinician"

These gaps represent the research agenda that SRL is uniquely positioned to fill.

## Innovation 2: Autonomic Home Base

### Proposed slug: `autonomic-home-base`

### What it is

A trained baseline autonomic state that you can reliably return to after being disrupted, taken off guard, or surprised. Not "calm" — a known coordinate in your nervous system's state space that you've mapped through practice and can navigate back to. The concept that your nervous system has a "home base" that you can train, recognize, and return to.

### Randy's Voice (direct quotes and design specs)

From `thought-articulation-mastery.md` (line 158 — Randy describes the tools):

> "There's also the tools that I use to recognize when I'm not in flow, understand how to get back into flow, how to stay in flow, and how to flip the switch of flow on and off for a performance improvement."

This is the operational definition of autonomic home base: you know what your optimal state feels like (recognition), you can get back to it (navigation), you can maintain it (sustaining), and you can deliberately enter and exit (switching).

From `thought-articulation-mastery.md` (line 169 — Randy's affirmation specification):

> "Embed one carefully chosen affirmation in the user's voice that anchors the experience (e.g., **'I know how to return to center'**)."

"Return to center" IS the autonomic home base concept in its simplest verbal form.

From `21-Day-NeuroMinute-Curriculum-PRD.md` (line 341 — the curriculum design):

> "Equal breathing becomes 'home base' — the pattern you return to between more intense practices throughout Weeks 2 and 3."
> "This equilibrium state is the prerequisite for Week 3's rapid state transitions. You need to know what 'balanced' feels like to navigate to it quickly."

This is the training methodology: you establish a known state, practice departing from it, and practice returning — building the neural pathway for autonomic navigation.

From `gmt-career-arc.md` (Phase 3: Fortifying, line 99):

> "Each challenging case becomes a training rep rather than a wound — the gap moment practice converts stressors into autonomic conditioning."

The fortified clinician has a trained home base so robust that disruptions become training rather than damage.

### What makes this SRL-original

1. **State space language** — framing the autonomic nervous system as a navigable space with coordinates, not just a "calm vs. stressed" binary. This is directly from Randy's clinical intuition about knowing where you ARE in your nervous system.
2. **Trained, not innate** — the home base is built through practice (the 21-day curriculum literally constructs it). It's not a personality trait or relaxation technique — it's a trained reference point.
3. **Disruption recovery** — the concept centers on RETURNING after being disrupted. Not staying calm (impossible in anesthesia). Not avoiding stress. Having a known state to navigate back to when the emergency is over or when you need to reset mid-crisis.
4. **Differs from "grounding" or "centering"** — those are techniques. Autonomic home base is the STATE those techniques navigate you toward. You have to have mapped the destination before the technique has somewhere to go.

But it does not have the DESTINATION concept — the trained reference state itself. This is the missing anchor point that makes the whole system navigable rather than just reactive.

## Innovation 3: Adversity Visualization / Embodied Pre-Rehearsal

### Proposed slug: `adversity-visualization`

### What it is

Deliberately visualizing all the problems that could occur, exactly how you'll handle them, what you're going to do about them. Embodied pre-rehearsal where you're training the autonomic response, not just cognitive planning. Modeled on Blue Angels pre-flight checks but applied to clinical practice and generalized to any high-stakes performance domain.

### Randy's Voice (from observations/blue-angels-embodied-visualization.md — already captured)

The existing observation note at `observations/blue-angels-embodied-visualization.md` is rich and captures Randy's practice in detail:

> "Randy adopted a visualization practice modeled on the Blue Angels pre-flight ritual. In the Blue Angels squadron, before every flight, pilots sit together and physically move through the motions of the routine — turning their heads, moving their hands, tracking each maneuver in their bodies — while still on the ground. They are not thinking about the flight. They are **rehearsing the flight in their bodies.**"

The seven-step failure visualization protocol (lines 61-68):
1. Visualize the case proceeding normally — then introduce a failure
2. What would it look like? — the specific visual, auditory, and tactile cues
3. What would I notice first? — training perceptual recognition
4. What would be going wrong physiologically? — mapping observable signs to pathophysiology
5. What are the options? — branching the decision tree
6. What would I do? — selecting and committing
7. Visualize every step of the response — including specific doses, physical actions

> "He would run through these failure scenarios **over and over again**, particularly during downtime — between cases, during slow moments, when bored. The boredom was the training window. The gap moment before the term existed."

From `gmt-career-arc.md` (line 58-60 — the concept-level description):

> "Embodied motor rehearsal — visualizing medication draw-up sequences, going through physical motions like Blue Angels pre-flight checks, encoding procedural memory through mental practice"
> "Error anticipation — deliberately visualizing what goes wrong, what it looks like, what the options are, rehearsing recovery before it's needed"

From `thought-articulation-mastery.md` (line 158 — Randy on PTSD prehabilitation):

> "There's no pre-rehab rehabilitation no pre-preventive or rehabilitative framework for the prevention of PTSD for any of the specialties — Frontline medical workers, Frontline firefighters, police, etc. — that's in widespread use and I'd like to create that."

This connects adversity visualization to the PTSD prehabilitation thesis: you're not just preparing for emergencies, you're inoculating the nervous system against traumatic damage.

### What makes this SRL-original

1. **EMBODIED, not cognitive** — the visualization is felt in the body (weight of laryngoscope, resistance of syringe plunger, rhythm of ventilation). This is motor imagery that engages premotor cortex, supplementary motor area, and cerebellum.
2. **DELIBERATE failure focus** — not just "prepare for the case" but "deliberately introduce failures and rehearse recovery." This is emotional inoculation combined with procedural preparation.
3. **Downtime/boredom as training surface** — Randy recognized that interstitial dead time was the training window BEFORE Gap Moment Training existed as a concept. The visualization practice was proto-GMT.
4. **28 years of personal practice** — this isn't theoretical. Randy has been doing this since SRNA training at Rush.
5. **Generalizability** — the observation note already maps the visualization components to GMT architecture (Stability Snap, Error Reversal Loop, gap moment recognition).

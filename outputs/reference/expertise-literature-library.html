Cognitive Expertise

5 references

How experts think, decide, learn, and create knowledge. The cognitive foundations of clinical mastery — from deliberate practice to distributed cognition.

Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer • 1993 Journal Article

The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance

Psychological Review, 100(3), 363–406

Established that expert performance is not a function of innate talent but of accumulated hours of structured, effortful practice with feedback. Introduced the deliberate practice framework and the 10,000-hour approximation for elite performance.

SRL Relevance: Maps directly to the Benner Expertise Spiral — both describe progression through stages requiring qualitatively different engagement. Validates that expertise development can be designed and accelerated through structured interventions, which is the premise of Gap Moment Training™ and NeuroMinute™ protocols. Also informs the minimum effective dose principle: not all practice hours are equal.

Gary Klein • 1998 Book

Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions

MIT Press

Introduced Recognition-Primed Decision Making (RPD) — experts do not compare options but recognize situations as familiar patterns and act on the first viable option generated through experience. Demonstrated that expertise resides in perceptual skills rather than superior reasoning.

SRL Relevance: RPD is the cognitive mechanism underlying Embodied Clinical Intelligence. When a CRNA “just knows” something is wrong before the monitors alarm, they are executing recognition-primed decision making. This is precisely what Anterocept™ captures: pre-conscious pattern recognition generating clinical intuition before explicit awareness.

Donald Schön • 1983 Book

The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action

Basic Books

Challenged the “technical rationality” model by distinguishing knowing-in-action (tacit knowledge embedded in performance) from reflection-in-action (thinking about what you are doing while doing it). Introduced the concept of a “reflective conversation with the situation.”

SRL Relevance: Foundational for understanding why tacit knowledge extraction is both essential and difficult. Reflection-in-action maps to the Embodied Metacognition that SRL cultivates — monitoring your own physiological and cognitive state while performing clinical tasks. Gap Moment Training™ is a technology for developing reflection-in-action capacity.

Nonaka & Takeuchi • 1995 Book

The Knowledge-Creating Company

Oxford University Press

Proposed the SECI model of organizational knowledge creation: Socialization (tacit→tacit), Externalization (tacit→explicit), Combination (explicit→explicit), and Internalization (explicit→tacit). Described how organizations systematically create new knowledge through these conversion cycles.

SRL Relevance: The vault is an externalization engine — converting Randy’s 20+ years of tacit clinical knowledge into explicit, codifiable, teachable forms. Every NeuroMinute™ is an internalization vehicle. The concept of ba (enabling context for knowledge creation) maps to the shared space the Pausality app creates for embodied knowledge development.

Edwin Hutchins • 1995 Book

Cognition in the Wild

MIT Press

Developed the theory of distributed cognition by studying Navy ship navigation — cognitive processes are distributed across people, tools, artifacts, and the environment. Expert performance in complex systems emerges from coordination, not from any single individual’s knowledge.

SRL Relevance: The OR is a distributed cognitive system. The CRNA’s expertise is distributed across monitors, alarms, the surgical team, and the patient. This reframes SRL training: building the capacity to function as a high-performing node in a distributed cognitive system. Co-Regulation captures the interpersonal dimension — the CRNA’s physiological state influences the entire team’s functioning.

Motor Expertise

2 references

The neuroscience of skilled movement — from three-stage learning to the degrees of freedom problem. The body’s intelligence in action.

Fitts & Posner • 1967 Book

Human Performance

Brooks/Cole

Established the three-stage model of motor learning: cognitive (effortful, attention-demanding), associative (refining, linking sub-movements), and autonomous (automatic, minimal attention). Demonstrated that attentional demands decrease systematically with practice, freeing cognitive resources.

SRL Relevance: Explains why expert CRNAs can intubate while monitoring hemodynamics and communicating with the surgeon — procedural motor skills at the autonomous stage free attentional resources for higher-order clinical judgment. This is the physiological basis of Attention as Gain Control. Each NeuroMinute™ targets a specific regulatory skill to move it from cognitive to autonomous stage.

ISBN: 978-0818501906

attention-as-gain-control neurominute benner-expertise-spiral
Nikolai Bernstein • 1967 Book

The Co-ordination and Regulation of Movements

Pergamon Press

Posed the degrees of freedom problem and introduced the concept of synergies (coordinative structures). Described the freezing/freeing progression: novices constrain joints to reduce complexity; experts release degrees of freedom to exploit full biomechanical possibilities. Coined “repetition without repetition” — expert performance is never identical across trials.

SRL Relevance: “Repetition without repetition” is the motor-level parallel to autonomic regulation: regulation is about producing adaptive responses to variable conditions, not executing fixed routines. The freezing/freeing progression maps to the Benner Expertise Spiral — novices freeze (rigid protocols), experts free (flexible, situation-adapted regulatory responses).

ISBN: 978-0080114699

Emotional & Regulatory Expertise

4 references

The science of how emotions are generated, regulated, and developed — from process models to constructed emotion theory. The evidence base for why interoceptive training transforms clinical performance.

James Gross • 1998 Journal Article

The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation: An Integrative Review

Review of General Psychology, 2(3), 271–299

Established the process model of emotion regulation with five strategy families along a temporal continuum: situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change (reappraisal), and response modulation (suppression). Demonstrated that antecedent-focused strategies are generally more effective than response-focused strategies.

SRL Relevance: Gap Moment Training™ targets attentional deployment (redirecting attention to interoceptive signals) and cognitive change (reframing the meaning of arousal). Anterocept™ — catching the emotion before it fully develops — is the ultimate antecedent-focused intervention. Explains why simple suppression fails for clinician burnout: it does not reduce experience, only increases physiological load.

DOI: 10.1037/1089-2680.2.3.271

Lisa Feldman Barrett • 2017 Book

How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain

Houghton Mifflin Harcourt

Presented the theory of constructed emotion: emotions are not hardwired reactions but active constructions built from interoception (body signals), prior experience, and context. Introduced emotional granularity — people with more fine-grained emotional categories have better regulation and mental health. The brain is fundamentally a prediction organ managing the body’s energy budget (allostasis).

SRL Relevance: Arguably the most important theoretical foundation for SRL’s approach. If emotions are constructions built on interoceptive signals, then improving interoceptive accuracy and granularity — exactly what SRL trains — directly improves emotional construction and regulation. Interoceptive Literacy is the substrate on which all emotional intelligence is built. The Autonomic Diary is an emotional granularity training tool.

Ochsner & Gross • 2005 Journal Article

The Cognitive Control of Emotion

Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 9(5), 242–249

First comprehensive review of the neural bases of cognitive emotion regulation. Mapped the prefrontal-amygdala circuitry enabling top-down regulation of emotional responses. Demonstrated that cognitive reappraisal engages lateral and medial PFC to modulate amygdala activity, and that this capacity improves with practice.

SRL Relevance: Provides the neural evidence for why SRL training works at the brain level. The PFC-amygdala circuit is the same circuit that Neurogating™ targets. The finding that reappraisal skill improves with practice validates the minimum effective dose approach: brief, repeated practice builds neural infrastructure for emotion regulation under pressure.

DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2005.03.010

Lane & Schwartz • 1987 Journal Article

Levels of Emotional Awareness: A Cognitive-Developmental Theory

American Journal of Psychiatry, 144(2), 133–143

Proposed a five-level developmental model of emotional awareness (sensorimotor reflexive through formal operational), analogous to Piaget’s cognitive stages. Demonstrated that emotional awareness is a cognitive-developmental achievement that can be trained, and that deficits underlie alexithymia and psychosomatic disorders. Created the LEAS assessment instrument.

SRL Relevance: Provides the developmental map for what SRL trains. Many clinicians operate at Level 1-2 regarding their own stress responses. Structured Interoception Training develops Level 1-2 awareness (noticing bodily sensations), while Embodied Metacognition and Gap Moment Training™ develop Level 4-5 capacities. The LEAS could serve as a validated outcome measure for SRL interventions.

DOI: 10.1176/ajp.144.2.133

Performance Under Pressure

4 references

The science of choking, vigilance, stress, and mental toughness — why performance degrades when it matters most, and the evidence that it can be trained.

Sian Beilock • 2010 Book

Choke: What the Secrets of the Brain Reveal About Getting It Right When You Have To

Free Press

Identified two mechanisms of choking under pressure: explicit monitoring (self-focus disrupts automated skills — “paralysis by analysis”) and working memory depletion (anxiety consumes cognitive resources). Demonstrated the expertise paradox — automaticity that makes experts excellent under normal conditions makes them vulnerable to self-focus choking.

SRL Relevance: Explains exactly the failure mode SRL training prevents. Clinical emergencies create conditions for both forms of choking. Learning Under Load is explicitly designed as inoculation training. Gap Moment Training™ provides the metacognitive tool to catch the self-focus spiral before it disrupts automaticity.

ISBN: 978-1416596189

Hancock & Warm • 1989 Journal Article

A Dynamic Model of Stress and Sustained Attention

Human Factors, 31(5), 519–537

Proposed the extended-U (dynamic adaptability) model of stress and sustained attention. Demonstrated that vigilance decrement is a robust neurocognitive phenomenon — the boundaries of the adaptability zone narrow with time on task, and attentional resources are finite and non-renewable within a session.

SRL Relevance: Foundational for understanding why CRNAs — whose job requires vigilance — experience performance degradation over long cases and shift hours. Rather than demanding unsustainable vigilance, SRL builds regulatory micro-practices (NeuroMinute™, Five-Breath Re-Embodiment) that restore attentional resources during natural workflow gaps. Validates Attention as Gain Control: dynamically modulate attention rather than maintain maximum output.

Mark Staal • 2004 Technical Report

Stress, Cognition, and Human Performance: A Literature Review and Conceptual Framework

NASA Ames Research Center (NASA/TM-2004-212824)

Comprehensive synthesis of military and aerospace research on how stress affects cognition. Covers attentional narrowing (Easterbrook’s cue-utilization theory), working memory degradation, cognitive tunneling, and decision-making biases under stress. Identified training, perceived control, and physiological fitness as key protective factors.

SRL Relevance: The cognitive effects of acute stress — attentional narrowing, working memory degradation, cognitive tunneling — are the failure modes that lead to anesthetic adverse events. The protective factors Staal identifies (training, perceived control, physiological fitness) are exactly what SRL builds. Cognitive tunneling connects to Attention as Gain Control: expert CRNAs maintain broad situational awareness because they have trained the regulatory capacity to prevent stress-driven narrowing.

Gucciardi, Gordon & Dimmock • 2015 Journal Article

The Concept of Mental Toughness: Tests of Dimensionality, Nomological Network, and Traitness

Journal of Sport and Exercise Psychology, 37(1), 71–83

Reconceptualized mental toughness as a trainable, state-like capacity rather than a fixed personality trait. Shifted focus from personality attributes to observable behaviors and deployable resources — what mentally tough performers do, not what they are. Distinguished mental toughness from resilience, hardiness, grit, and self-efficacy.

SRL Relevance: Validates SRL’s entire premise: the psychological resources needed for clinical performance under pressure can be systematically developed. The distinction between mental toughness (consistent performance during adversity) and resilience (recovery after adversity) matters for positioning. Clinician Durability is closer to mental toughness — maintaining clinical performance across a career, reframing the value proposition from “wellness” to “durability.”

SRL training moves regulatory skills (breathing, body scanning, state transitions) from cognitive to autonomous stage — so they remain available under load without consuming the attentional resources needed for clinical reasoning.

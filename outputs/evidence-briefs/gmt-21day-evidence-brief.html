Evidence Brief: Gap Moment TrainingTM — 21-Day Protocol

Somnistics Research Labs | For Clinical Leadership Review

The Hypothesis
One 60-second NeuroMinuteTM per day for 21 days, progressively increasing in complexity and delivered via the Pausality app, improves autonomic regulation (HRV, respiratory sinus arrhythmia), interoceptive awareness (MAIA-2 subscales), and stress resilience (state recovery speed under clinical load) in Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetists.
Evidence Supports
Phase 1: Somatic Baseline (Days 1-7)
5 minutes of daily cyclic sighing significantly improves mood and reduces physiological arousal versus mindfulness meditation. Structured breathing outperforms unstructured attention training. The respiratory vagal stimulation model identifies breath as the primary mechanism of contemplative benefit.
Balban et al. 2023, Cell Reports Medicine Level 1b RCT
Gerritsen & Band 2018, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience Level 2a
Phase 2: Cognitive Friction (Days 8-14)
Resonance frequency breathing (~0.1 Hz) stimulates the baroreflex, producing large-amplitude HR oscillations that strengthen autonomic reflexes over sessions. Short-duration HRV biofeedback improves cognitive performance under laboratory-induced stress in a single session. Effects are mechanistic (reflex training), not merely state induction.
Lehrer & Gevirtz 2014, Frontiers in Psychology Level 1a Review
Prinsloo et al. 2011, Applied Psychophysiology & Biofeedback Level 2b
Phase 3: Graded Exposure (Days 15-21)
Eight weeks of progressive mindfulness training produces measurable increases in gray matter density in insula, hippocampus, and TPJ. The anterior insula integrates interoceptive signals into unified awareness — training interoception is training the neural substrate of clinical decision-making under pressure.
Hölzel et al. 2011, Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging Level 2b
Craig 2009, Nature Reviews Neuroscience Level 5 Theory
Evidence Challenges
Dose Below Evidence Floor
The strongest breathing RCT (Balban 2023) used a 5-minute daily dose. The strongest HRV biofeedback data is based on 10-20 minute sessions. A 60-second dose is below the floor of any published positive trial. The literature suggests rapid autonomic shifts are possible in brief windows (Prinsloo 2011), but no RCT has tested a 60-second standalone protocol.
Gap No published 60-second breathing RCT
21-Day vs. 8-Week Timeline
Structural neuroplastic changes (Hölzel 2011) were measured after 8 weeks of MBSR. The HRV biofeedback literature typically reports cumulative benefits over 4-10 weeks. Twenty-one days may produce functional shifts (autonomic tone, interoceptive attention) without structural reorganization. The timeline is plausible for skill acquisition but unvalidated for durability.
Gap Functional vs. structural change boundary unclear at 21 days
Ecological Validity
Laboratory stressors (Stroop tasks, mental arithmetic) do not replicate the cognitive-emotional demands of intraoperative anesthesia care — where the stakes are a patient's life and the stressors are unpredictable, concurrent, and sustained. Transfer from app-based practice to OR performance is assumed, not demonstrated.
Gap No transfer study from micro-training to clinical performance
No GMT-Specific RCT
Gap Moment Training as a named, integrated protocol — combining breath pacing, multi-anchor interoceptive attention, progressive complexity, and biometric feedback within a 60-second format — has not been studied in any published randomized controlled trial. Each component has evidence. The integration does not.
Level 5 Expert opinion / SRL protocol design
The Honest Assessment

Component evidence is strong. Slow breathing improves autonomic regulation (Level 1a-1b). HRV biofeedback strengthens baroreflex function (Level 1a). Interoceptive training reshapes insula connectivity (Level 2b). Progressive skill acquisition compounds neuroplastic effects (Level 2a). Each ingredient in the GMT protocol has independent, replicated support.

Integration evidence is absent. No study has tested whether these components, combined into a 60-second progressive protocol delivered via a mobile app, produce the claimed outcomes in CRNAs. The GMT 21-day protocol is currently classified as Level 5 / SRL-P (proprietary protocol, expert-designed, empirically untested as a bundle).

The gap between "each ingredient works" and "this recipe works" is precisely where the planned VCU collaboration lives. 83% of anesthesiology trainees report a stressful event in any 30-day window (Putnam 2025). The clinical need is not in question. The protocol's efficacy as an integrated system is.

Three Things We Are Doing About It
  1. MAIA-2-CRNA Validation Study (N=240)
    CRNA-specific adaptation of the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness. Keeps 8 core MAIA-2 subscales intact for literature comparability. Adds 5-10 items targeting interoceptive attention under clinical load. Produces a validated outcome instrument before the intervention trial begins.
  2. Continuous State-Drift Monitoring (N=60)
    Apple Watch-based longitudinal HRV tracking during clinical shifts. Establishes baseline autonomic variability patterns in CRNAs — the "before" picture. Maps gap moment frequency, state recovery time, and circadian autonomic rhythms without any intervention.
  3. GMT 21-Day Pre/Post Pilot (N=60)
    Randomized waitlist-controlled pilot. Primary outcomes: MAIA-2-CRNA delta, resting HRV change, and state recovery speed post-stressor. Secondary: ecological momentary assessment of perceived regulation capacity. This is the first direct test of the integrated GMT protocol.

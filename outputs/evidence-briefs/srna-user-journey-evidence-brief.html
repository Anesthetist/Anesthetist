Evidence Brief: SRNA Clinical Readiness Protocol

Pausality × Apple Watch × 21 Days — What the Evidence Shows
Somnistics Research Labs • March 2026 • v1.0 • Oxford CEBM graded
01 — The User
Who
Student Registered Nurse Anesthetist (SRNA), mid-career transition. 28, former ICU nurse, now in a doctoral anesthesia program. Smart, motivated, physiologically literate.
Constraints
Cannot work during the program. Financially stretched. Time is scarce—clinical rotations, didactics, simulation labs, and studying consume nearly all of it. No budget for additional wellness tools.
Hardware
Apple Watch (Series 9+), AirPods Pro 3, iPhone. Already owned. No additional cost.
What They Need
Emotional regulation under clinical pressure. Resilience that doesn't require 20-minute meditation sessions they don't have time for. A way to build the interoceptive infrastructure that medical education is actively suppressing.
The Problem Underneath the Problem
Clinical training systematically suppresses interoception—the capacity to detect your own physiological state. Stoicism is rewarded. Fatigue signals are ignored. The "bladder of steel" is a badge of honor. This suppression reduces autonomic flexibility and is a biological contributor to burnout, state drift, and clinical error vulnerability. It is a training artifact, not a character flaw.
02 — The Intervention

What They Do

60s
Per session — one NeuroMinute™
3×/day
Minimum dose — gap moments between cases, before sim, walking to the OR
21 days
Progressive curriculum — three waves of increasing complexity
Cardiac-anchored breathing—the core differentiator. Breath pacing is synchronized to the user's own heartbeat via Apple Watch, not an external timer. The body sets the rhythm. Each person's optimal resonant frequency varies (typically 4.5–6.5 breaths/minute) and shifts with autonomic state, fatigue, and circadian phase. The app adapts to the user, not the reverse.
Days 1–7 • Wave 1
Somatic Baseline
Simple breath anchors, body scanning, heartbeat detection. Establishing autonomic home base—a trained reference state the nervous system can return to after disruption. Equal breathing as the known coordinate.
Days 8–14 • Wave 2
Cognitive Friction
Thought labeling, attention toggling, interrupt drills. Practicing departure from home base and return. Stacking sensory anchors (heartbeat + breath texture + ambient sound). Building state-drift detection.
Days 15–21 • Wave 3
Graded Exposure
Open awareness under mild physiological stressors (CO2 buildup, extended exhale ratios). Less instruction, more raw awareness. Integration: the capacity to remain regulated when the signal gets noisy.
03 — Outcome Evidence Map

16 Measurable Dimensions — Graded Honestly

Each outcome is graded against the available literature for this specific protocol (60-second micro-interventions, cardiac-anchored breathing, 21-day duration, SRNA population). Where the evidence is strong, we say so. Where it is not, we say that too.

01
HRV — RMSSD / HF-HRV
Apple Watch optical PPG → RMSSD trend; validated against ECG for trend detection
Demonstrated
Expected: 15–30% RMSSD increase over 21 days (d = 0.4–0.7). Resonant-frequency breathing produces measurable HRV shifts within a single session. Effect is cumulative with repeated practice.
Lehrer et al. 2014; Prinsloo et al. 2011; Zaccaro et al. 2018
Caveat: Apple Watch optical PPG is less precise than chest-strap ECG. Absolute RMSSD values may differ; trend direction and magnitude remain detectable. Individual resonant frequency varies—cardiac anchoring addresses this.
02
Interoceptive Awareness — MAIA-2
MAIA-2 (37-item, 8 subscales) — self-report at Day 0, Day 21
Probable
Expected: d = 0.2–0.4 across Noticing, Body Listening, and Trusting subscales. Published 8-week programs show d = 0.3–0.5; a 21-day protocol with 3×/day dosing may approach the lower bound through frequency compensation.
Mehling et al. 2012 (MAIA validation); 8-week mindfulness-based programs (multiple meta-analyses)
Caveat: No published data on MAIA-2 response to 60-second micro-interventions specifically. The dosing hypothesis (3×/day × 21 days = 63 reps) is theoretically grounded but empirically untested. CRNA-specific norms do not yet exist.
03
Insular Cortex Density
Structural MRI (voxel-based morphometry) — not measurable in-app
Not Expected at 21 Days
Published structural changes require 8+ weeks of sustained practice (30–45 min/day). Hölzel et al. (2011) showed gray matter density changes in the insula after 8-week MBSR. A 21-day micro-intervention protocol does not have the dosing to produce detectable structural change.
Hölzel et al. 2011; Lazar et al. 2005
Honest answer: This is a long-game outcome. Structural neuroplasticity requires sustained practice beyond 21 days. The protocol may initiate functional changes in insular activation (testable via fMRI) that precede structural change, but that is a hypothesis, not a deliverable.
04
Parasympathetic Dominance Under Cognitive Load
HF-HRV during stress tasks; Apple Watch HRV + in-app cognitive challenge
Demonstrated
HF-HRV increases of 10–20% during cognitive stress tasks in trained participants. The mechanism is well-established: slow rhythmic breathing stimulates the ventral vagal pathway (Porges, 2011), and this effect is trainable.
Khalsa et al. 2018; Porges 2011; Gerritsen & Band 2018
Caveat: Demonstrated in lab settings with seated participants. Transfer to standing, high-stakes clinical environments (intubation, emergency) is plausible but not yet directly measured.
05
Perceived Stress — PSS-10
Perceived Stress Scale (10-item) — pre/post questionnaire
Probable
Mindfulness meta-analyses show d = 0.5 for 8-week programs (Pascoe et al. 2017). A 21-day micro-intervention may yield d = 0.2–0.3. The SRNA population has high baseline stress, which may amplify or attenuate the effect.
Pascoe et al. 2017; Cohen et al. 1983 (PSS validation)
Caveat: PSS is subjective. Demand characteristics (wanting the intervention to work) inflate self-report. Pairing with objective HRV data provides a partial control.
06
Burnout — MBI (Emotional Exhaustion)
Maslach Burnout Inventory — pre/post questionnaire
Probable
d = 0.3–0.5 from comparable interventions. Patel et al. (2022) demonstrated 30% MBI reduction in CRNAs specifically using HRV biofeedback. The population match strengthens this prediction.
Patel et al. 2022; Horvath et al. 2021 (SRNA pandemic burnout baseline)
Caveat: Patel's protocol was longer than 21 days. The question is whether the frequency (3×/day) compensates for the shorter duration. Burnout is also multifactorial—organizational factors (scheduling, workload) persist regardless of individual intervention.
07
Professional Fulfillment
Stanford Professional Fulfillment Index (PFI) — pre/post questionnaire
Hypothesized
No direct studies linking micro-breathwork to PFI scores. The pathway is indirect: reduced emotional exhaustion → increased capacity for meaning-making → higher fulfillment. McDermott et al. (2026) found meaning/purpose interventions improve clinician wellbeing, but the mechanism differs.
Trockel et al. 2018 (PFI validation); McDermott et al. 2026
Honest answer: Professional fulfillment is downstream of burnout reduction but is also shaped by mentorship, clinical competence, and institutional culture. A breathing intervention alone is unlikely to move this needle in 21 days.
08
Sleep Quality + Deep Sleep Architecture
PSQI questionnaire + Apple Watch sleep staging (light/deep/REM)
Probable
Black et al. (2015) showed moderate PSQI improvement from mindfulness-based interventions. Evening breathing practice (extended exhale) prior to sleep has the strongest theoretical basis. Apple Watch sleep staging provides trend data.
Black et al. 2015; Walker 2009 (sleep and emotional brain)
Caveat: Apple Watch sleep staging accuracy is improving but remains imperfect for deep sleep classification compared to polysomnography. PSQI self-report is the more reliable measure. SRNAs on rotating clinical schedules face structural sleep disruption that an individual intervention cannot fully address.
09
Flow State Frequency
Flow State Scale (FSS-2) or Dispositional Flow Scale (DFS-2)
Hypothesized
Longaretti et al. (2025) meta-analysis found mindfulness enhances flow, but the relationship is correlational, not causal. The mechanism is plausible: improved autonomic flexibility → reduced threat vigilance → easier absorption in task → flow. But 21 days is a short runway.
Longaretti et al. 2025; Bieri et al. 2025 (flow state in robotic surgery, EEG)
Caveat: Flow requires challenge-skill balance and intrinsic motivation, not just autonomic regulation. SRNAs are novices in clinical environments—the challenge-skill ratio may be too skewed for flow to emerge reliably. This is a longer-term outcome.
10
Alcohol Use — AUDIT
AUDIT (10-item) or AUDIT-C (3-item) — pre/post questionnaire
Hypothesized
Witkiewitz et al. (2014) found small effects from mindfulness on alcohol reduction. The hypothesized mechanism: improved stress regulation reduces self-medication. But alcohol use in SRNAs is shaped by social context, program culture, and coping patterns that extend beyond autonomic state.
Witkiewitz et al. 2014; Bowen et al. 2014 (MBRP)
Honest answer: A 21-day breathing protocol is not an alcohol intervention. Any effect would be a downstream consequence of reduced stress, not a direct mechanism. Measuring it is worthwhile for longitudinal research; promising it would be irresponsible.
11
Substance Use Risk Reduction
DAST-10 or custom screening — pre/post questionnaire
Not Yet Tested
No direct studies linking breathwork or micro-interoceptive interventions to substance use risk in clinician populations. The indirect pathway (stress reduction → reduced self-medication need) is theoretically coherent but empirically unvalidated for this intervention format.
No direct citation available
Honest answer: Anesthesia providers face elevated substance use risk due to occupational exposure and stress. This is a critically important outcome to measure longitudinally, but claiming a 21-day breathing protocol addresses it would be overreach. This belongs in the "we're studying it" column.
12
Clinical Error Rates
Simulation-based assessment; incident report frequency (observational)
Hypothesized
The causal chain is well-articulated: state drift → perceptual narrowing → incomplete recovery between stressors → error vulnerability. Suresh et al. (2019) provided observational evidence. Bordini et al. (2025) linked cognitive errors to difficult airway management under load. But no RCT has tested breathwork → error reduction directly.
Suresh et al. 2019; Bordini et al. 2025; Marcucci et al. 2007
Caveat: Clinical errors are rare events with complex causation. A 21-day individual intervention cannot control for system-level factors (staffing, equipment, communication). Simulation-based testing is the most feasible measurement approach.
13
Career Duration / Retention / Intent to Leave
Intent-to-leave questionnaire; longitudinal retention tracking
Hypothesized
Indirect through burnout reduction. Wilbanks et al. (2025) linked safety culture and burnout to retention in anesthesia. Nosbisch et al. (2026) documented CRNA empowerment experiences. The economic case: $80K–$150K per CRNA turnover replacement. But intent-to-leave is shaped by compensation, geography, and life stage.
Wilbanks et al. 2025; Nosbisch et al. 2026
Honest answer: Career duration is a years-long outcome. A 21-day protocol can establish habits and measurably reduce burnout markers, which are leading indicators of retention. But promising career-length impact from three weeks of practice would be overclaiming.
14
Team Co-regulation / Physiological Synchrony
Multi-person HRV synchrony; Team Physiological Compliance Index
Not Yet Tested
Emerging evidence: Powezka et al. (2022) measured HRV synchrony in surgical teams. Cacioppo (2020) and Kneebone (2015) explored interpersonal physiological coupling. The theory is compelling: a regulated clinician contributes to team-level autonomic coherence. But no study has tested whether individual micro-breathwork training transfers to team physiological synchrony.
Powezka et al. 2022; Kneebone 2015; Cacioppo 2020
Honest answer: This is frontier science. The measurement tools exist (multi-device HRV synchrony). The hypothesis is grounded. But the causal pathway from individual practice → team co-regulation is untested. This is a research opportunity, not a deliverable.
15
Workplace Incivility Reduction
Workplace Incivility Scale (WIS); self-report
Hypothesized
Diefendorff et al. (2016) linked dispositional mindfulness to reduced incivility experiences. The mechanism: improved emotional regulation → less reactive communication → fewer incivility events (both perpetrated and perceived). Small to moderate expected effect.
Diefendorff et al. 2016; Popov et al. 2025 (team communication in simulation)
Caveat: Incivility is bidirectional. Even if the SRNA becomes more regulated, they cannot control the behavior of attendings, surgeons, or other team members. This protocol addresses the user's reactivity, not the workplace culture.
16
Awe + Joy Frequency
AWE-S scale; PANAS (Positive subscale); daily micro-assessment in-app
Hypothesized
Condon et al. (2013) found small to moderate effects of contemplative practice on positive emotion frequency. The mechanism: restored interoceptive sensitivity increases the bandwidth for positive hedonic signals (not just threat signals). When the nervous system isn't locked in survival mode, wonder becomes accessible again.
Condon et al. 2013; Stellar et al. 2017 (awe and autonomic response)
Caveat: Positive affect is highly individual and context-dependent. An SRNA in a toxic clinical environment may not experience joy regardless of autonomic state. This is a quality-of-life outcome, not a clinical one, and it depends on the rest of their life, not just their breathing.

05 — What We Can Say vs. What We're Testing

The Evidence Says

  • Resonant-frequency breathing produces measurable HRV shifts within a single 60-second session
  • Repeated HRV biofeedback training (3+ weeks) increases resting RMSSD by 15–30% in healthy adults
  • Slow breathing directly stimulates the ventral vagal pathway and increases parasympathetic tone
  • HRV biofeedback reduces emotional exhaustion in CRNAs specifically (Patel 2022)
  • Mindfulness-based interventions reduce perceived stress with moderate effect sizes (d = 0.5)
  • Interoceptive awareness (MAIA-2) improves with structured body-awareness practice
  • Apple Watch optical PPG reliably detects HRV trends over days to weeks
  • Brief breathwork interventions are feasible in clinical workflows where long sessions are not

We Hypothesize

  • That 60-second cardiac-anchored sessions, 3×/day, produce equivalent or greater autonomic training effects compared to single daily 15–20 minute sessions through frequency compensation
  • That cardiac anchoring (synced to heartbeat) is more effective than external-clock pacing for individual resonant frequency discovery
  • That progressive complexity across 21 days (somatic baseline → cognitive friction → graded exposure) accelerates interoceptive recovery versus static practice
  • That restored interoceptive sensitivity reduces state drift and its downstream clinical consequences
  • That individual autonomic training transfers to team-level physiological synchrony
  • That this protocol, if sustained beyond 21 days, contributes to measurable career durability
  • That the SRNA training period is an optimal intervention window—the interoceptive channel is being suppressed in real-time, and early restoration may prevent the chronic suppression pattern

07 — The Common Language Effect

When the Team Shares a Physiological Vocabulary

One of the least-studied but most clinically intuitive effects of interoceptive training: it gives clinicians language for what they are already experiencing. When a team shares that vocabulary, dynamics shift.

State Drift
The progressive, often undetected loss of autonomic regulation during clinical work. Attention narrows. Recovery between stressors becomes incomplete. Most errors trace back here—not to ignorance, but to an undetected slide from optimal to suboptimal state.
When a team has the concept "state drift," a preceptor can say "I think you're drifting" instead of "pay attention." The intervention becomes physiological, not punitive.
Autonomic Home Base
A trained baseline state you can reliably return to after disruption. Not "calm"—a known coordinate in your nervous system that you've mapped through practice. The destination that regulation techniques navigate toward.
When an SRNA can articulate "I need 60 seconds to find my home base," it reframes the request from weakness to professional skill. The language changes the social permission structure.
Co-Regulation
The phenomenon where one person's autonomic state influences another's. A regulated clinician literally contributes to the team's collective nervous system state. Measurable via HRV synchrony across multiple wearable devices.
Powezka et al. (2022) measured physiological synchrony in surgical teams. The emerging evidence suggests that individual regulation is not just individual—it propagates.
Gap Moment
Short transitional windows in clinical workflow—between cases, walking to the OR, before induction—that can be repurposed as training reps instead of remaining dead time. Approximately 30–40 per CRNA shift.
The vocabulary shift: from "downtime" to "training surface." When a team recognizes gap moments together, the culture shifts from "push through" to "prepare the instrument."
Interoceptive Suppression
The hypothesis that medical education systematically trains clinicians to ignore their own body signals. The hidden curriculum rewards stoicism and signal-ignoring, attenuating the capacity for autonomic self-regulation over time.
Naming it changes it. When SRNAs learn that their training is actively suppressing a capacity they need, the motivation for recovery practice shifts from "wellness" to "professional development."
Minimum Effective Dose
60 seconds. Not because it's ideal, but because it's the smallest intervention unit that produces a measurable autonomic shift. Pharmacological thinking applied to contemplative practice. The question isn't "how much can you do?" but "what's the least that still works?"
For an SRNA who cannot add one more thing to their schedule, this framing is the difference between engagement and abandonment. The dose is the differentiator.
The research gap: No published study has measured the effect of shared physiological vocabulary on team dynamics in clinical environments. The closest evidence comes from crew resource management (CRM) literature, which shows that shared mental models improve team performance. Physiological vocabulary is a specific, testable instance of shared mental models. This is an open research question with high clinical relevance.

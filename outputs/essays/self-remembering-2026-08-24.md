# Notes from the Lab: Self-Remembering in the OR — The Gurdjieff Practice CRNAs Need

*Self-remembering — the Fourth Way discipline of holding the observer and the observed simultaneously in one field of awareness — is the meta-awareness capacity whose presence or absence determines whether clinical absorption remains a skill or becomes a liability.*

---

## The Observation

There is a version of clinical excellence that looks, from the outside, like complete presence. The CRNA who is absorbed — entirely absorbed — in the patient. The monitors, the drip rates, the subtle color change that precedes the number change. This is mastery.

But there is something mastery can quietly lose: the self who is doing the mastering. The jaw has been clenched for forty minutes. The breathing shortened two inductions ago. The body has been running a low-grade stress response since the handoff that went sideways before the first case. None of this has been noticed — not because the clinician is distracted, but because they are too deeply inside the work to observe themselves inside it.

The first moment of self-remembering is not the moment of deliberate breathing. It is the moment before that: the moment of noticing that one has been underwater.

---

## The Mechanism

George Gurdjieff, teaching in Russia and France in the early twentieth century, described the ordinary human condition as "waking sleep": the body acts, the mind processes, but the being who inhabits the body has largely absented itself. Identification — his term for absorption so complete that the self disappears into its object — was not a spiritual metaphor. It was an observation about attention architecture. Self-remembering was the antidote: the practice of simultaneously including oneself in one's own field of awareness while remaining engaged with the task.

The neuroscience, arriving nearly a century later, provides the mechanism.

Dahl, Lutz, and Davidson (2015), writing in *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, proposed a taxonomy of meditation practices organized by their primary cognitive mechanisms. Their category of "deconstructive" practices — practices that reduce self-referential fusion and identification — operates through meta-awareness: the second-order capacity to notice what attention is doing in real time without withdrawing from the task. This is the neurological translation of self-remembering: a watcher watching the watching, maintained in parallel with the work. [PMID: 26231761 | DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2015.07.001]

Brandmeyer and Delorme (2020), in a theoretical review of the neurocognitive mechanisms underlying meditation, located this capacity in the dynamics between two neural networks. The default mode network (DMN) generates the wandering mind: spontaneous thought, self-referential processing, narrative construction about what has happened or what might. The task-positive network (TPN) drives directed attention. Meta-awareness — what Gurdjieff called self-remembering — is precisely what allows the meditator to detect the moment of wandering before it becomes drift. Without it, the departure is invisible. With it, the departure is caught. [PMID: 32598855 | DOI: 10.1177/1745691620917340]

Pagnoni (2012) measured this capacity in Zen meditators against meditation-naive controls during focused attention on breathing, using fMRI. The meditators showed reduced activity in the ventral posteromedial cortex (vPMC), the DMN's primary hub. This was not a suppression of self-awareness — it was an absence of mind-wandering. What the meditators had trained was the speed and frequency of meta-awareness intercepts. Critically, the same neural parameter that distinguished meditators from controls predicted performance on a rapid visual information processing task conducted outside the scanner: the capacity that keeps the mind from wandering under meditation conditions is the same capacity that maintains vigilance over a stream of clinical events. [PMID: 22496570 | DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4135-11.2012]

Ramírez-Barrantes and colleagues (2019), reviewing the relationship between meditation, the default mode network, and cognitive aging, extended this to the regulatory architecture. In their model, meditation increases meta-awareness by training active coordination between the DMN and the task-positive network. The clinician who cannot regulate their own DMN activity does not simply get distracted. Their internal narrative runs beneath the task — metabolically expensive, occupying the cognitive architecture that clinical monitoring requires. The cost is invisible until it is suddenly not. [PMID: 31065259 | DOI: 10.1155/2019/7067592]

Weller, Mahajan, Fahey-Williams, and Webster (2024), in a narrative review on situation awareness in anesthetic teams published in the *British Journal of Anaesthesia*, described one of situation awareness's primary failure modes not as external distraction but as attentional narrowing: the clinician so absorbed in one element of the clinical field that the broader situation disappears from consciousness. Their framework — perceive, comprehend, project — requires a self who maintains a monitoring position above the task, not only inside it. [PMID: 38310070 | DOI: 10.1016/j.bja.2023.12.035]

This is Gurdjieff's "identification" precisely: the state in which absorption in an object causes the subject to disappear.

## The Framework

Self-remembering in clinical practice has three elements, none of which require additional time.

**The meta-signal.** The first signal of self-remembering is somatic before it is cognitive. A slight widening of peripheral vision. A sense of weight in the chair. A breath that is marginally fuller than the one before. These are the body's report that the observer has returned — or its intimation that the observer is needed. The skill is recognizing these signals as a signal-class, not as random sensation.

**The gap.** Every clinical transition — task to task, procedure to procedure, the moment between a completed action and the next — is a natural structural opportunity for self-remembering. This is the overlap with Gap Moment Training: a gap moment is a state transition, and every state transition is a point at which the observer can be re-instantiated. The transition creates a brief opening in task absorption. Self-remembering fills that opening with a moment of self-reference before the next absorption begins.

**The return.** Self-remembering is not a permanent state. Pagnoni's (2012) meditators did not eliminate mind-wandering — they shortened its duration and increased the frequency of meta-awareness intercepts. In clinical terms, the goal is not the elimination of absorption but the systematic shortening of the interval between losing the observer and noticing it is gone. The return, practiced, becomes faster. Eventually it becomes nearly continuous — what experienced CRNAs describe as "clinical presence": simultaneous awareness of patient, monitors, team, and self.

The training sequence: establish the capacity in low-stakes daily activity (eating, walking, documentation); transfer to peri-procedural practice using GMT architecture; measure via MAIA-2 attentional regulation and body listening subscales.

---

## The Failure Mode

Self-remembering has two failure modes that move in opposite directions.

The first is identification: complete absorption in the task, observer absent, meta-signal suppressed. This is the more dangerous clinical pattern. The interoceptive suppression hypothesis — that high-performing clinicians may learn to mute body signals as a strategy for maintaining task focus — suggests that the same learned suppression enabling a CRNA to remain steady through a crisis may also suppress the meta-signal that would indicate a threshold is being approached. The mechanism that protects performance may, at its extreme, remove the ability to observe the performance.

The second failure mode moves the opposite direction: the observer becomes so hyperactive that it interferes with the task. The clinician asking "am I self-remembering right now?" is no longer present with the patient — they are present with their meta-awareness of their meta-awareness. This is the meditator's paradox: trying to be effortlessly present is not effortless presence. Sam Harris's pedagogical approach in the Waking Up practice — normalize mind-wandering, do not resist it, return without judgment — applies directly here. Meta-awareness is not a state to be achieved but a capacity to be exercised. The dosing is in the training, not the performance.

## The Test

A 14-day minimum viable experiment with 10 clinical professionals in high-acuity environments:

**Baseline:** MAIA-2 (attentional regulation, body listening, and trusting sensations subscales); morning HRV baseline (RMSSD); self-report count of "absorption episodes" — defined as moments noticed in retrospect where self-awareness had been absent — per shift.

**Intervention (daily, 7 minutes):** 4 minutes guided body scan establishing somatic observer-ground; 3 minutes divided-attention exercise — hold awareness of breath pattern while performing a sustained attention task (digit-span or backward counting).

**Measurement:** MAIA-2 repeat at day 7 and day 14; shift-end absorption count; subjective "observer presence" rating (1-10) before first case and after last case.

**Success criterion:** Measurable increase in MAIA-2 attentional regulation subscale, reduction in self-reported absorption episodes per shift, or increase in morning HRV baseline.

**Failure criterion:** No change across all three measures, which would indicate that the targeted capacity — meta-awareness under professional load — requires longer duration, different modality, or higher dose to produce measurable training effect at this timescale.

## The Connection

Self-remembering is the meta-skill that makes every other SRL concept operational. Without it, the NeuroMinute is physiological housekeeping without awareness: the clinician completes the protocol but does not observe the state change the protocol produces. Without it, Anterocept's anticipatory architecture has no foundation: you cannot anticipate your own state if you have lost track of yourself. Without it, Gap Moment Training fails at its first step — recognizing the transition — because the observer who would notice the transition is absent.

The kosha architecture's highest aspiration — simultaneous awareness across all five sheaths — is the sustained form of what self-remembering practice moves toward. The vault's five-layer model maps the territory. Self-remembering is how a clinician locates themselves on it, in real time, without stopping work.

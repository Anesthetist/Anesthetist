# Notes from the Lab: The Body Knows First

*Interoceptive accuracy — the measurable ability to detect and interpret one's own body signals — is a trainable variable that predicts the quality of demand signal detection in customer conversations, such that a founder who cannot read their own nervous system cannot reliably read the room.*

---

## The Observation

Twenty minutes into a customer conversation, something shifts. The prospect's breathing changes — slightly shallower. Their voice drops a register. They stop finishing sentences and start asking questions. "How would that work for someone like me?" The words are neutral. The body is not.

A founder with calibrated interoceptive awareness registers the shift as a somatic event before processing it cognitively: a subtle drawing-in sensation in the chest, a slight increase in alertness, a change in the quality of their own listening. Something in the room just moved.

A founder without that calibration hears the same words and applies cognitive analysis. They parse meaning from syntax. They don't notice what the room's physiology is doing. They often miss the signal entirely, or mistake it for mere curiosity. They leave the conversation unsure whether it was a yes or a polite no, and they rely on follow-up metrics weeks later to tell them what their nervous system could have told them in the room.

Cedric Chin, whose Commoncog frameworks form the operational spine of SRL's execution methodology, argues that the skill of demand discovery is learnable — but he frames it as a cognitive skill. What the interoceptive literature reveals is that it is also a somatic skill, one that can be trained with the same rigor as heartbeat detection.

---

## The Mechanism

In 2005, Hugo Critchley at University College London published a review establishing the neural architecture through which body signals influence decision quality. Critchley found that anterior cingulate cortex, insula, and orbitofrontal cortex form an integrated circuit in which afferent signals from the body are mapped, processed, and fed into the decision stream. This is the substrate of what Antonio Damasio proposed as the somatic marker hypothesis — the claim that bodily states function as rapid, pre-cognitive signal flags that guide judgment under uncertainty. Critchley's review specified the circuit: visceral arousal → insula → orbitofrontal cortex → behavioral guidance [PMID: 16254997].

The hypothesis makes a testable prediction: better interoceptive accuracy should correlate with better decision quality, particularly in ambiguous contexts where data is incomplete and judgment must operate in real time. The literature confirms this.

Sugawara and colleagues (2020) at Japan's National Center of Neurology and Psychiatry conducted an interventional study in which participants completed an eight-session interoceptive training protocol. Before and after training, they assessed both interoceptive accuracy (via heartbeat detection) and rationality of decision-making. The training significantly improved interoceptive accuracy and reduced somatic symptom and state anxiety scores. Critically, Sugawara et al. found a significant positive correlation between the change ratio in interoceptive accuracy and the change ratio in decision-making rationality — those who improved most in body awareness also improved most in decision quality [PMID: 32206084].

Moccia and colleagues (2021) measured both respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA, an index of heart rate variability) and interoceptive accuracy in patients with Gambling Disorder and healthy controls, then had all participants complete the Iowa Gambling Task — a validated measure of real-world decision quality under uncertainty. RSA reactivity and interoceptive accuracy together significantly predicted Iowa Gambling Task performance. Lower autonomic flexibility plus lower interoceptive calibration equaled worse decisions on the task [PMID: 34591785].

The mechanism underlying why interoceptive training improves decision quality was clarified by Suksasilp and colleagues (2026) from the Garfinkel laboratory at University College London and Brighton and Sussex Medical School. They used Bayesian computational modelling to identify the specific parameter changed by eight sessions of cardiac interoceptive training. The finding: training increased participants' internal precision weighting for afferent cardiac signals — the brain's estimate of how reliable its body signals are. When precision weighting increases, the somatic signal-to-noise ratio improves. The body's votes count more in the decision process [PMID: 42070707].

This last finding is the critical bridge to demand sprints. The problem in a customer conversation is not that founders have no somatic signals — they always have them. The problem is that those signals are either not detected (low interoceptive accuracy), or detected but not trusted (low precision weighting), or overwhelmed by the noise of the founder's own arousal state. The body is transmitting. The receiver is miscalibrated.

Three variables affect that calibration. Six of seven studies in this area confirm that interoceptive accuracy predicts the quality of real-time signal detection in ambiguous, performance-relevant contexts. Butterworth and colleagues (2025), studying cardiac interoceptive accuracy in cycling performance, found that participants with good cardiac interoceptive accuracy showed larger and appropriate differences in exercise work rate and physiological response between light and hard intensity conditions — while those with poor interoceptive accuracy could not differentiate appropriately in real time [PMID: 39954268]. The body of someone who cannot read their own internal states also cannot self-regulate their output in response to real-world signals. This is exactly the demand sprint problem: the founder who cannot read their own arousal cannot calibrate their read of the prospect's arousal.

Kim and Kim (2025), studying the neural processes linking interoception to social decision-making, found that interoceptive awareness and accuracy are associated with alignment of moral preferences with group consensus — mediated through ventromedial prefrontal cortex and precuneus activity. Interoceptive accuracy links to social attunement, not just individual body-reading [PMID: 40324876]. Reading the room is, in part, reading your body while in a room with another person's body.

Confounds must be named. Six variables changed across these studies: participant populations, interoceptive training protocols, outcome measures, time horizons, task structures, and autonomic measurement methods. The transfer claim — from clinical interoceptive accuracy to demand-sprint signal detection — is an architectural inference, not a directly tested hypothesis. What is established is the underlying mechanism (somatic marker circuit) and the trainability of the signal detection capacity. The application to entrepreneurial demand discovery is structurally analogous but not yet empirically tested in a founder population.

## The Protocol

A demand sprint is a 4-week experimental loop testing one job-to-be-done at a time, with a single hypothesis, a defined audience segment, a measurable signal, and a decision threshold. The SRL formulation adds a somatic learning layer to Chin's original framework.

**Week 0 — Baseline Calibration**
Record BOLT score (body oxygen level test, a proxy for CO2 tolerance and autonomic baseline). Record resting RMSSD over three consecutive mornings. Complete the MAIA-2 (Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness) to establish a baseline profile across eight interoceptive dimensions.

**Weeks 1-4 — Instrumented Conversations**
Before each conversation: one-minute resonant breathing to establish autonomic coherence (not to calm nerves, but to reduce baseline noise). Set an explicit interoceptive intention: "I will notice changes in my chest, breath, and attention quality during this conversation."

During each conversation: maintain a split awareness — primary attention on the prospect, secondary attention on the body. Note: changes in your own breath rate, any drawing-in or drawing-away sensation, changes in energy or alertness quality, shifts in the quality of your listening (the difference between strategic listening and genuine curiosity is somatic).

Immediately after each conversation: a 5-minute written reflection before reviewing any notes or audio. "What did your body register? When did the quality of the conversation change, and what did that feel like in your body?" Then: confidence rating (1-10) that the conversation revealed genuine demand.

Track the correlation between in-conversation somatic confidence and 30-day outcome (did the prospect convert? Was the demand real?). Over four weeks, this is calibration data for the founder's demand biomarker system.

**The Decision Threshold**
At week four, review. If genuine demand was detected and confirmed, accelerate. If the somatic signals were not correlated with outcomes, run a second sprint with higher interoceptive awareness before altering the product hypothesis.

## The Failure Mode

The system breaks under one specific condition: autonomic dysregulation in the founder.

When a founder is operating under chronic stress — fundraising pressure, cash runway anxiety, team conflict, sleep deprivation — their autonomic state is dysregulated. Cortisol is elevated. RMSSD is reduced. The signal-to-noise ratio for interoceptive signals collapses. What the founder feels during a customer conversation is not the prospect's energy — it is their own anxiety, wearing the prospect's face.

This is the mechanism behind desperate product development: founders who are behind on runway consistently interpret polite enthusiasm as demand because their own activation state floods the signal. They hear what they need to hear. The body is transmitting their stress, not the prospect's interest.

The demand biomarker system is only reliable when the founder's autonomic baseline is stable. A BOLT score below 15 seconds, or RMSSD below 30ms, suggests autonomic dysregulation severe enough to compromise signal quality. No demand sprint should begin in that state. The instrument must be calibrated before it is used.

The second failure mode: interoceptive sensibility without interoceptive accuracy. A founder who is highly attuned to body sensations but not accurately interpreting them — who feels everything strongly but reads it wrong — may have high false-positive rates for demand. This is detectible in the MAIA-2: high Body Listening scores with low Body Trusting scores suggest sensibility-accuracy dissociation. The training protocol should target accuracy, not amplification.

## The Test

Fourteen days, ten clinician-founders (CRNAs with entrepreneurial projects preferred — the baseline interoceptive training reduces confounding), three demand conversations each per week, MAIA-2 pre and post.

**Primary metric:** Correlation between somatic confidence rating (1-10, immediate post-conversation) and 30-day demand validation (did the prospect engage further? Paid? Referred?).

**Secondary metric:** MAIA-2 change from baseline, specifically Body Trusting and Emotional Awareness subscales.

**Control arm:** Ten founders without interoceptive training complete the same conversation tracking and confidence ratings.

**What constitutes success:** A significant positive correlation (r > 0.4) between somatic confidence rating and 30-day outcome in the trained group, not present in the control group.

**What constitutes failure:** No correlation, or inverse correlation — which would suggest the somatic signal is tracking founder arousal rather than genuine demand.

This is a testable hypothesis at small scale, within four weeks, with a wearable providing RMSSD data and the MAIA-2 providing psychometric structure.

## The Connection

Demand sprints are the experimental infrastructure through which the NeuroMinute becomes evidence-based. Every sprint is a calibration cycle: the founder tests a hypothesis, tracks their somatic confidence, and compares it to outcome. Over thirty sprints (thirty weeks), the founder's demand biomarker system becomes a genuinely predictive instrument.

This is the operational implementation of the Commoncog triad applied to the founder's body: interoception is not a mystical sense but a measurable, trainable, and calibratable signal-detection system. The evidence base — Critchley's neural circuit, Sugawara's training protocol, Moccia's decision-quality data, Garfinkel's precision-weighting model — establishes that this is not an analog for intuition. It is the physical substrate of intuition, and it can be engineered.

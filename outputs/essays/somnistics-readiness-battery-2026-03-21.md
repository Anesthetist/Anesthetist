# The SRB-60

**Technical name:** Somnistics Readiness Battery
**Human name:** The presidential fitness test of applied micro-interoception

## The Premise

You can measure a pilot's instrument proficiency. You can measure a surgeon's knot-tying speed. You can measure a CRNA's pharmacological knowledge on a 200-question board exam.

You cannot currently measure whether a clinician can regulate their own nervous system under pressure in sixty seconds.

The SRB-60 changes that.

## What It Tests

Five competency domains. Each tested within the NeuroMinute window — sixty seconds. Because the skill has to work when sixty seconds is all you have.

### Domain 1: Gap Moment Literacy

**What it measures:** Can you detect that a transition is happening and classify your autonomic state?

**Test format:** Simulated clinical scenarios with embedded transition points. The trainee identifies: "A gap moment is occurring now." Then classifies: "My current state is [hyperarousal / hypoarousal / cognitive tunnel / dyspnea loop / task-ready]."

**Scoring:** Detection accuracy (did they catch the transition?) × Classification accuracy (did they name the state correctly?) × Speed (how quickly after the transition began?)

**Failure mode it catches:** The clinician who is dysregulated and doesn't know it — high sensibility ("I'm fine"), low accuracy (HRV says otherwise). This is Garfinkel's metacognitive gap made visible.

### Domain 2: Micro-Interoceptive Skill

**What it measures:** Can you rapidly sense, label, and calibrate internal body signals?

**Test format:** Timed interoceptive tasks — heartbeat detection, breath-rate estimation, body tension mapping — performed under increasing cognitive load. Accuracy measured against wearable ground truth.

**Scoring:** Accuracy of interoceptive report vs. measured reality. Under resting conditions AND under simulated clinical load.

**Failure mode it catches:** The clinician whose body awareness collapses under stress — accurate at rest, blind under pressure. Exactly the condition where interoceptive awareness matters most.

### Domain 3: Protocol Selection and Fidelity

**What it measures:** Can you choose the right protocol for the detected state and execute it correctly?

**Test format:** Given a classified state (from Domain 1), select the appropriate NeuroMinute protocol. Then execute it. Breath ratios, pacing accuracy, attentional focus, and modality selection are scored.

**Scoring:** Selection appropriateness (did they pick the right protocol for the state?) × Execution fidelity (did they run it correctly — right ratios, right pacing, right attentional target?)

**Failure mode it catches:** The clinician who always defaults to box breathing regardless of state. Box breathing is not the answer to every autonomic question. Protocol selection IS clinical judgment applied to the self.

### Domain 4: Measured State Delta

**What it measures:** Did the protocol actually work? Did the body change?

**Test format:** Pre/post biometric comparison across the 60-second window. HRV shift, heart rate change, respiratory rate change.

**Scoring:** Magnitude of state delta in the intended direction. A trainee who selects an activating protocol for hypoarousal and produces a measurable HR increase scores higher than one who produces no change.

**Failure mode it catches:** The clinician who goes through the motions — executing the protocol with perfect form but producing zero physiological change. Ritual without regulation. The skill is not the performance of breathing. The skill is the movement of the nervous system.

### Domain 5: Return-to-Task Readiness

**What it measures:** After regulating, can you immediately perform at full cognitive capacity?

**Test format:** Immediately following the NeuroMinute, the trainee performs a timed clinical task (rapid calculation, airway decision, team communication scenario). Speed and accuracy compared to baseline.

**Scoring:** Task performance post-protocol vs. baseline. The protocol should restore or improve performance, not impair it. If the trainee is drowsy or dissociated after the NeuroMinute, Domain 5 fails — regardless of how good the state delta was.

**Failure mode it catches:** The critical one. Regulation without sedation. The whole point of in-shift mental hygiene is returning to the next case sharper, not sleepier. A NeuroMinute that produces calm but impairs executive function is the wrong tool for a clinical environment.

## Performance Tiers

| Tier | Requirement | Credential |
|------|------------|------------|
| **Bronze** | Pass all 5 domains at basic competency | Somnistics Readiness Certified |
| **Silver** | Consistent measurable state deltas + rapid protocol selection | Somnistics Performance Certified |
| **Gold** | Advanced skill across all domains under high-fidelity simulation | Somnistics Certified Professional (SCP) |
| **Platinum** | Expert performance + ability to coach others through the SRB-60 | Somnistics Certified Instructor (SCI) candidate |

## Why "Presidential Fitness Test"

Randy's metaphor is precise. The presidential fitness test didn't ask whether you were an athlete. It asked: can you do a pull-up? Can you run a mile? Can you touch your toes? Baseline physical competency, measured standardized, across the whole population.

The SRB-60 asks the same question for the nervous system: can you detect your state, name it, choose a response, execute it, measure the change, and return to work? Baseline regulatory competency. Measured. Standardized. Proctorable.

No existing certification in any clinical profession tests this. Not AANA. Not NBCRNA. Not any medical board. The SRB-60 creates the category.

## The Testable Claim

**Hypothesis:** SRB-60 scores correlate with longitudinal clinician durability markers — HRV trends, burnout survey scores, retention rates, and self-reported professional satisfaction.

**Test:** Administer SRB-60 to 100 CRNAs. Track for 12 months. Do high SRB-60 scorers show better HRV trends, lower burnout scores, and higher retention? If yes: the battery predicts durability. If no: revise the domains.

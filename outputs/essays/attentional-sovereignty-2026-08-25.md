# Notes from the Lab: Attentional Sovereignty — The Skill William James Named and Neuroscience Finally Measured

*The faculty of voluntarily returning wandering attention is not a personality virtue — it is a trainable neurophysiological capacity that, when developed in high-demand professionals, measurably reduces the mind-wandering that Zanesco and Jha have shown directly degrades sustained clinical performance.*

---

## The Observation

Between cases. The drug cart is locked, the chart is transitioning, and the CRNA is standing still for the first time in ninety minutes. The next patient is twenty minutes out. In that gap — roughly seven seconds after the last physiological variable has been handed off — the mind leaves.

Not catastrophically. Not obviously. The body remains in the room. The eyes may even appear to be looking at something relevant. But the cognitive thread has pulled toward a conversation from last week, a grocery list, a decision that has no place in an operating corridor at 10:43 on a Thursday morning.

This is not weakness. It is biology. The mind-wandering rate in sustained vigilance tasks averages 30-40% of sampled moments even in healthy, alert, well-motivated participants. For clinicians managing cases that are mostly routine — and then suddenly are not — the question of what happens in those unmonitored attentional seconds has a body count attached to it.

William James, in 1890, identified the remedy. Not the problem — he took the problem as given. He named the remedy: "The faculty of voluntarily bringing back a wandering attention, over and over again, is the very root of judgment, character, and will. An education which should improve this faculty would be the education par excellence." He wrote that in an era before EEG, before the psychomotor vigilance task, before the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex had a name. He was still right.

What neuroscience has added, 136 years later, is the mechanism, the measurement, and the training protocol. What Somnistics adds is the clinical delivery system.

---

## The Mechanism

Zanesco, Denkova, Barry, and Jha (2024) measured mind-wandering in 730 participants across two pre-registered studies using growth curve models — a statistical approach that tracks intraindividual change over time rather than averaging across people. Their core finding: increases in mind-wandering are directly and consistently associated with worsening behavioral performance as time-on-task increases. Not correlated at the group level — tracked within individuals, as their own minds wandered more, their accuracy dropped and their response time variability increased. The vigilance decrement is not a mystery of fatigue physiology. It has a cognitive signature: the mind has left the room. Zanesco et al. (2024) [PMID: 39172363] confirm it quantitatively.

The distinction between two types of mind-wandering matters enormously in clinical settings. Martínez-Pérez, Baños, Andreu and colleagues (2021) [PMID: 34665819] differentiated intentional mind-wandering (the mind deliberately disengages when it can afford to) from unintentional mind-wandering (the mind wanders without the person knowing, as a failure of executive cognitive control). Intentional mind-wandering appears primarily when task demands are low — an arousal-driven response to boredom. Unintentional mind-wandering appears primarily when executive control resources are depleted — and it is undetectable in real time by the person experiencing it.

For CRNAs, this distinction is not academic. The dangerous departure is the unintentional one: the mind-wander that happens during the case, not between cases, when an apparent routine becomes suddenly non-routine. The clinician does not know attention has left. No alarm sounds. The monitor still beeps. And in that gap, something changes.

The question of mechanism received a key answer from Martínez-Pérez, Andreu, Sandoval-Lentisco and colleagues (2023) [PMID: 37056308]: anodal transcranial direct current stimulation to the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (left DLPFC) — and only that manipulation, not increased task demands — significantly reduced rates of mind-wandering in the sustained attention task. This double dissociation means two things: vigilance decrement and mind-wandering are separable phenomena with different drivers, and the left DLPFC is the primary substrate through which voluntary attention control operates. James's "faculty of voluntarily bringing back a wandering attention" has an address. It lives in the left DLPFC, and it is trainable without electrical stimulation.

The clinical stakes of inattention are not theoretical. Waghalkar, Pandit, Chapane and colleagues (2025) [PMID: 39944441] analyzed 2,541 patients undergoing urological surgeries and identified 241 critical incidents — a rate of 9.48%. Root cause analysis revealed that human factors were the primary driver. Vigilance failure specifically was identified in 22.41% of those incidents — second only to skill gaps. Critically, 98.75% of the incidents were judged preventable with existing protocols. Not preventable by better equipment, not preventable by more staff, not preventable by redesigned workflows. Preventable by the human beings in the room paying a different quality of attention.

## The Protocol

The trainability of the voluntary attention-return faculty is not hypothetical. Zanesco, Denkova, Rogers, MacNulty, and Jha (2018) [PMID: 30732844] tested Mindfulness-Based Attention Training (MBAT) in elite military servicemembers — a cohort selected for cognitive performance as a job criterion. A 4-week delivery of 8 hours of MBAT produced significant improvements in both sustained attention task performance and working memory capacity, compared to both a 2-week version of the same training and a no-training control. Crucially, improvements in working memory correlated with out-of-class practice time — meaning the training variable that predicted the effect was repetition of the attention-return act itself, not insight, not relaxation, not cognitive understanding of what was being trained.

This is the empirical structure of what Somnistics calls attentional sovereignty: not knowing that attention wanders, but developing the repeated, voluntary faculty of returning it. Each return is a repetition. Each repetition thickens the capacity. William James was describing a gym. Zanesco and Jha built the measurement protocol to prove it works.

The clinical delivery system for this training already exists inside every CRNA's shift: gap moments. The 30-90 second windows between cases, during patient transport, at the drug cart. These are not wasted time. They are, in Somnistics terms, the training floor — the moments where the practice of deliberately returning wandering attention can be performed repeatedly, at high ecological validity, without withdrawing from the clinical environment. A [[gap-moment-training]] practice that treats each gap as a voluntary attention-restoration repetition is, structurally, the same training protocol that Zanesco and Jha delivered to elite military. The difference is duration and density — micro-doses of the same mechanism, embedded in the workflow rather than extracted from it.

## The Failure Mode

Two failure modes deserve explicit statement.

The first is the confusion of *knowing about attention* with *training attention*. Reading this essay will not improve anyone's sustained attention performance. The 4-week MBAT effect came from repeated practice — daily minutes of actually doing the return, not thinking about it. Attentional sovereignty is a motor skill wrapped in neuroscience clothing. It responds to repetition, not comprehension.

The second failure mode is over-claiming durability. The Cochrane review by Tamminga, Emal, Boschman and colleagues (2023) [PMID: 37169364] synthesized 117 RCTs including 11,119 participants in individual-level stress interventions for healthcare workers. The finding: individual attention-focused interventions reduce emotional exhaustion by approximately 4.6 points on the Maslach Burnout Inventory emotional exhaustion scale (SMD -0.37, low-certainty evidence). The effect is real and replicable. Its long-term durability — beyond 12 months — remains largely unstudied. Sovereignty does not install permanently. It requires maintenance. Every week without the return practice is weeks of slow drift back toward default rates of unintentional wandering.

## The Test

Minimum viable experiment: 10 CRNAs across two sites, 14 days, tracking self-reported cognitive failures before and after adopting a 60-second gap-moment attention-restoration protocol.

Cognitive failures assessed using the validated Cognitive Failures Questionnaire (Broadbent et al., 1982) — the same self-report instrument used in Zanesco and Jha's elite military study as a secondary measure. Target: ≥20% reduction in self-reported cognitive failures over the 14-day period among those completing ≥6 of 14 daily practices. Objective secondary: HRV RMSSD during 60-second gap moments (pre vs. post), captured via consumer wearable.

This is not a clinical trial. It is a proof test — designed to answer whether the training signal is detectable in a real clinical population under real workflow conditions. If the self-reported cognitive failure reduction is below 10%, the delivery protocol needs redesign. If the HRV trace during gap moments does not show RSA enhancement over the 14 days, the attention restoration practice may not be producing the autonomic state shift that predicts performance improvement. Either outcome is informative.

---

## The Connection

Attentional sovereignty is the psychological framing of what [[gap-moment-training]] trains at the physiological level. It connects the William James phenomenology — the lived experience of a clinician choosing where their life is directed in a given moment — to the neurophysiology of left DLPFC voluntary control. It is the mechanism that makes [[interoceptive-literacy]] possible under load: you cannot read the body's signals if the faculty that returns attention has never been exercised. And it is, in the long view, what separates [[clinician-durability]] from burnout: a career's worth of attention repeatedly returned — to the patient, to the body, to the work — builds a different practitioner than one where those moments of drift were never retrieved.

James named the faculty. The neuroscience measured it. Somnistics delivers the training reps.

---

*In the gap between cases, the CRNA who brings attention back once — just once, deliberately — has performed the act William James called the root of character. Over a career, that one act, repeated ten thousand times, may be the difference between a clinician who arrived and a clinician who endured.*

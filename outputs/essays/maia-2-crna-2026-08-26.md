# Notes from the Lab: MAIA-2-CRNA — A New Measurement Problem

*Expert clinicians exhibit a distinctive and unmeasured pattern of interoceptive dissociation — suppressed sensibility and collapsed self-trust despite intact noticing capacity — that no existing validated instrument can detect, making the MAIA-2-CRNA less an adaptation exercise and more a new measurement problem.*

## The Observation

A Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist spends an eight-hour shift monitoring another person's physiology with extraordinary precision: end-tidal CO₂, SpO₂, NIBP every three minutes, ECG waveform, airway pressures, neuromuscular blockade depth, bispectral index. They can detect a 5% drop in oxygen saturation before the monitor alarm fires. They read the pre-ejection period on an arterial waveform by shape alone.

Ask that same CRNA how they feel at hour four of a complex case. The answer is often: "Fine. A little tired maybe." Ask them whether they are breathing diaphragmatically or thoracically right now — not the patient, themselves. Silence. Ask them to estimate their own heart rate at this moment — not the patient's. Longer silence.

The expert has built a surveillance apparatus for external physiology so capable that it systematically crowds out interior attention. The specialization that makes them excellent at managing the patient's autonomic state has left their own autonomic state functionally invisible to them. This is not a deficiency of intelligence or self-awareness in any colloquial sense. It is a specific, measurable, and until now unmeasured, interoceptive phenotype — one that no existing instrument was designed to detect.

## The Mechanism

Garfinkel, Seth, Barrett, Suzuki, and Critchley established the theoretical foundation in 2015. Interoceptive ability is not a single trait — it dissociates into three distinct dimensions that are empirically separable: **interoceptive accuracy** (objective performance on heartbeat detection tasks), **interoceptive sensibility** (self-reported beliefs about one's interoceptive ability), and **interoceptive awareness** (metacognitive correspondence between accuracy and sensibility — the confidence-accuracy relationship). In a normative sample of 80 participants, Garfinkel et al. showed that all three dimensions were distinct and dissociable. Interoceptive accuracy predicted only a portion of interoceptive sensibility and awareness, and significant correspondence between dimensions appeared only within the sub-group of individuals with the highest accuracy.

This trichotomy predicts the clinical expert paradox precisely. A CRNA has spent years training interoceptive attention outward — high-stakes, externally directed physiological monitoring. Their accuracy in detecting patient physiological signals is exceptional. But their sensibility toward their own interior state — specifically what the MAIA-2 subscale "Trusting" measures — is likely systematically suppressed. Not absent. Suppressed, by design, as an occupational adaptation. They have learned to trust the monitor over the body, and applied that epistemology to themselves.

Mehling, Acree, Stewart, Silas, and Jones developed the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness, Version 2 (MAIA-2) as the primary instrument for measuring self-reported interoceptive sensibility. The 37-item, 8-subscale structure assesses: **Noticing** (detecting subtle body sensations), **Not-Distracting** (not suppressing awareness of body sensations), **Not-Worrying** (not catastrophizing about sensations), **Attention Regulation** (sustaining body-directed attention), **Emotional Awareness** (linking emotion to body state), **Self-Regulation** (using body awareness to regulate distress), **Body Listening** (actively consulting body for insight), and **Trusting** (treating body signals as reliable information). Validated at Science Museum London on N=1,090, the 8-factor structure confirmed with RMSEA=0.055 and SRMR=0.064 — adequate fit, public domain, in active use across research and clinical applications worldwide.

The instrument has since been validated across at least five languages and multiple cultural contexts. Scheffers et al. validated the Dutch version (N=1,054) with internal consistency (McDonald's ω) ranging from 0.67 to 0.89 and test-retest reliability from 0.67 to 0.79. Fiskum et al. validated the Norwegian version (N=306) and found that gender, age, and education moderated relationships between subscale scores and health outcomes. Chapman and Stewart validated the US college version (N=710) and explicitly noted that contextual and cultural factors may influence how individuals subjectively interpret bodily sensations — a finding that has direct implications for any population-specific adaptation.

The athlete data is particularly instructive. Rogowska, Tataruch, and Klimowska found that athletes scored significantly higher than non-athletes on Noticing, Attention Regulation, Emotional Awareness, Self-Regulation, Body Listening, and Trusting — essentially all major subscales. They concluded that physical training appears to improve most domains of interoception. One exception: athletes scored *lower* on the Not-Distracting subscale, meaning they were more easily pulled away from body focus by external stimuli. This makes sense — sport is an external-demand environment that rewards attention switching.

The operating room is a more extreme version of the same environment. A CRNA's training has almost certainly built high Noticing capacity in some domains — they have been explicitly trained to notice patient physiological signals. But the Not-Distracting subscale is suppressed by the OR environment itself, which is architecturally designed to compete for attention. Most critically, the Trusting subscale — the belief that one's own body signals are reliable information — is likely the defining deficit. An expert who has been trained for years that the monitor is the ground truth, that physiological decisions require equipment confirmation, may systematically devalue their own body signal as a legitimate data source. The instrument is reading "I don't trust my body" — and interpreting that as a deficiency. For the CRNA, it may be the result of a decade of successful training.

Existing MAIA-2 normative databases built on healthy Dutch adults, Norwegian community members, and US college students have no reference point for this expert-professional interoceptive phenotype. A CRNA scoring low on Trusting looks like a symptomatic individual in those norms. They may simply be a well-trained clinician.

## The Protocol

The MAIA-2-CRNA design operates at two distinct levels, each serving a different purpose.

**Level 1: Contextual translation — maintaining normative comparability**

All 37 existing MAIA-2 items are retained, with language adapted to CRNA clinical context. "During times of discomfort or pain I can find a sense of ease in other parts of my body" becomes "During a technically demanding case, I can find physical steadiness in other parts of my body." "I notice my body in ways that don't distract me from what I'm doing" maps to the OR context explicitly. The construct is identical; the frame is clinical.

This translation is not cosmetic. Chapman and Stewart's finding — that contextual factors shape body sensation interpretation — means item face validity matters for population-specific assessment. A CRNA reading generic wellness-framed items may respond to the instrument as a wellness tool rather than a clinical assessment tool. Context priming affects self-report. Maintaining the 37-item structure ensures comparability with the published MAIA-2 evidence base, preserves the research publication pathway, and makes the CRNA sample interpretable against existing norms even as the gap between norms and the population becomes the research finding itself.

**Level 2: CRNA-specific extension — measuring constructs that don't yet exist**

A 5-10 item extension module assesses two constructs specific to expert clinical performance that no existing MAIA-2 development sample has been designed to capture:

*Interoceptive Attention Under Load (IAUL-CRNA)* — the capacity to maintain interior awareness while simultaneously managing external clinical demands. Sample items: "I can track changes in my own breathing rate while monitoring the patient's capnography." "I notice shifts in my own muscle tension before I become consciously aware of a case-related concern."

*Dissociation Under Threat* — loss of access to body signals during high-acuity events. Sample items: "During a rapidly evolving crisis, I lose track of my own physical state." "After a challenging case, I cannot reconstruct what my body was doing during the critical moment."

These constructs are absent from existing MAIA-2 development samples because no existing development sample has been asked to detect body signals while simultaneously responsible for an anesthetized patient. The construct is genuinely novel. The extension module is SRL's specific IP layer — not a modification of Mehling's instrument, but a profession-specific construct built on its validated foundation, in the same tradition as adaptations for traumatic brain injury patients and non-English-speaking populations. The research pathway is clear: normative data collection → validation study → publication → integration with the Somnistics Readiness Battery as the assessment backbone of the CRNA certification program.

## The Failure Mode

The MAIA-2-CRNA fails if the expert-professional interoceptive phenotype does not exist as hypothesized.

If CRNAs as a group show perfectly normal MAIA-2 scores across all subscales — if professional training in monitoring external physiology does not suppress self-directed interoceptive awareness — then the contextual translation of items is useful for face validity but the IAUL-CRNA extension tests constructs with no distinctive prevalence in this population. The measurement problem dissolves and the hypothesis is wrong. This is testable with a baseline-only survey of N=30 CRNAs compared against published population norms.

The reverse failure mode: CRNA interoceptive deficit is so severe that existing items produce floor effects across multiple subscales, making sensitivity to change undetectable. If every CRNA scores near zero on Trusting at baseline and near zero after a 14-day intervention, the instrument cannot track the training effect. Mehling's validation in major depressive disorder patients showed the instrument is sensitive to change (standardized response means of 0.32–0.81 across subscales), but that was in a treated clinical population. The CRNA population is not treated — they are undertrained.

Both failure modes are empirically resolvable in a modest pilot study, which is the point of doing the pilot.

## The Test

**Minimum viable experiment: N=20 CRNAs, 14 days, pre/post MAIA-2-CRNA**

1. Administer MAIA-2-CRNA at baseline (Day 0), mid-point (Day 7), and post-intervention (Day 14)
2. Collect 5-minute resting HRV daily, pre-shift and post-shift (wearable)
3. Administer SRB-60 Domain 2 (Micro-Interoceptive Skill) concurrently — the behavioral complement to the self-report instrument
4. Track subscale trajectories across all 8 MAIA-2 dimensions plus IAUL-CRNA scores

**Primary outcome:** Do the Trusting and Not-Distracting subscales show significant pre/post change — and do they change more than other subscales? If these are the deficit subscales, they should show the largest effect from targeted training.

**Secondary outcome:** Does HRV improvement (RMSSD change) correlate with MAIA-2 subscale change? Which subscale(s) show the strongest HRV-correlation?

If Trusting and Self-Regulation show largest effect sizes while RMSSD increases, that constitutes preliminary evidence that the instrument is detecting the training effect the theory predicts. If Noticing changes least (already high in this population) and Trusting changes most, that is consistent with the expert phenotype hypothesis.

The study does not require a control group at this stage. The goal is instrument behavior characterization in the target population — understanding how the MAIA-2-CRNA behaves in CRNAs, not proving the intervention works.

## The Connection

The MAIA-2-CRNA is the measurement infrastructure that makes everything else in the vault falsifiable.

Without it, claims about interoceptive improvement in SRL's training programs are subjective. With it, there is a subscale-differentiated picture of what specifically changed, by how much, and which dimensions of interoceptive awareness respond to which training modalities. It becomes the outcome variable that the SRB-60 (behavioral performance) and HRV (physiological state) cannot provide on their own — the self-report layer of the three-layer assessment architecture.

It connects directly to [[interoceptive-literacy]], which maps its educational targets to MAIA-2 subscales. It connects to [[clinician-durability]] by establishing the baseline measurement from which career-scale interoceptive compounding can be tracked longitudinally. It connects to [[anterocept]] by identifying which subscale (Noticing) is the prerequisite domain and which subscale (Trusting) is the destination.

Randy's original question — "What if we did an MAIA 2-CRNA?" — was not a positioning move. It was a measurement problem stated correctly for the first time.

*The most dangerous thing a clinician can believe about themselves is "I feel fine" — and the MAIA-2-CRNA is built to measure exactly how wrong that belief can be.*

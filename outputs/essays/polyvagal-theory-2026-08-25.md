# Notes from the Lab: Polyvagal Theory After Grossman — What Survives the Critique

*Grossman's consortium dismantles polyvagal theory's evolutionary scaffolding while leaving its most clinically useful structure intact — the observation that autonomic state is hierarchically organized, bidirectionally trainable, and measurable in the beat-to-beat interval; that functional architecture stands on demonstrated mechanisms neither side disputes.*

---

## The Observation

In early 2026, thirty-nine internationally recognized researchers in vagal physiology, evolutionary biology, and vertebrate neuroscience co-signed a paper declaring Stephen Porges' Polyvagal Theory (PVT) "untenable." The list of co-authors reads like a who's who of autonomic neuroscience: department chairs from University College London, Oxford, University of Melbourne, Mayo Clinic, Harvard, Aarhus, UCLA. Not critics from the fringe — scientists whose own work Porges had cited as supporting evidence for PVT.

If you've built a clinical framework that references polyvagal concepts — ventral vagal engagement, neuroception, the autonomic hierarchy, the dorsal vagal shutdown — this paper should stop you in your tracks. Not because it collapses the framework. Because reading both sides carefully reveals that the debate is operating at a different level of analysis than the clinical practices built on PVT's vocabulary.

The question the debate actually answers: what does SRL stand on when it trains clinicians to regulate their autonomic state? How much of that ground is PVT-dependent, and how much is independently verified?

The short answer: far less than you might fear is at stake, and the distinction matters enormously for how SRL positions its work going forward.

---

## The Mechanism

Grossman and colleagues (2026) target five specific claims. Based on peer-reviewed metadata retrieved from PubMed [PMID: 41768017], the five contested premises are:

1. **RSA as direct vagal drive measure.** PVT treats Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia as a direct index of "central vagal drive to the heart." Grossman's consortium argues this is physiologically imprecise — RSA reflects complex cardiovascular coupling, not a clean readout of the vagal pathway PVT specifies.

2. **Nucleus Ambiguus vs. Dorsal Motor Nucleus characterizations.** PVT's "ventral vagal complex" (Nucleus Ambiguus) and "dorsal vagal complex" (Dorsal Motor Nucleus of the vagus) carry specific claims about function and evolutionary precedence. The consortium argues these characterizations are inconsistent with established neuroanatomy.

3. **Vagus nerve evolution.** PVT claims the myelinated ventral vagus is evolutionarily more recent than the unmyelinated dorsal vagus, with the myelinated variant specifically enabling mammalian social behavior. The 39 co-signatories argue the evolutionary evidence does not support this specific chronology or the functional inferences drawn from it.

4. **Mammalian social behavior specificity.** PVT holds that complex social engagement is specifically mammalian. The consortium cites substantial evidence that non-mammalian vertebrates — crocodilians, some fish, various reptiles — demonstrate complex social behaviors with analogous neural substrates.

5. **Interpretation of earlier physiological literature.** The paper argues that PVT's citations of foundational autonomic science sometimes misrepresent what those studies actually found.

The conclusion: "major tenets of the PVT are not supported by past or current knowledge." The theory is untenable.

Porges responded in the same journal issue [PMID: 41768026]. His core argument: the critique does not engage PVT as formulated. It constructs what he calls "a synthetic version" shaped by three persistent category errors. First, conflating neuroanatomy with neurophysiology — attacking the evolutionary anatomy while the theory's clinical claims operate at the functional level. Second, reducing a systems-level theory to its measurement instruments — critiquing RSA as a metric when PVT's claim is about the pathway, not the precision of any single metric. Third, substituting phylogenetic continuity for functional organization — the observation that non-mammalian vertebrates show social behavior says nothing about whether a ventral vagal-mediated social engagement system functions distinctly in mammals.

Neither paper presents new empirical data. This is expert evaluation of an existing theoretical framework — Level 5 evidence in clinical grading. Both sides are arguing from the published literature, and the published literature supports both critiques and defenses depending on which level of analysis you privilege.

Here is where the distinction that matters most for SRL appears.

Grossman's consortium closes with a sentence that deserves careful reading: "This article does not intend to address alternative explanations about relations between vagal function and psychological processes, although such explanations do exist."

That is a precise scientific claim. The critique is targeted at the evolutionary narrative and the specific neuroanatomical architecture of PVT. It is not a claim that vagal function is irrelevant to psychological states. It is not a claim that autonomic state hierarchies don't exist clinically. It is not a claim that HRV biofeedback doesn't work.

And HRV biofeedback does work. Blase, Vermetten, Lehrer, and Gevirtz (2021) [PMID: 33804817] conducted a systematic review of nine RCTs showing HRV biofeedback produces significant clinical improvement in PTSD (53% vs. 24% BDI reduction compared to control), depression, and anxiety disorders. The mechanism operative in those trials — slow paced respiration training that shifts autonomic balance toward parasympathetic dominance — is fully functional whether or not PVT's evolutionary narrative is accurate.

Campbell, Wisco, Silvia, and Gay (2019) [PMID: 30779926] published a meta-analysis of 55 studies (n=6,689) confirming that lower RSA is reliably associated with PTSD diagnosis (g = -0.26, 95% CI: -0.35, -0.16). RSA is a valid and reproducible psychophysiological marker of autonomic vulnerability. What it is not, post-Grossman, is a direct, unambiguous readout of the specific vagal circuit PVT specifies. The marker works clinically. The theoretical interpretation of what the marker reflects is contested.

Da Cunha E Silva, Wöllner, and Norte (2026) [PMID: 41935670] found that 9 of 10 studies in their systematic review recorded increased HRV following HRV biofeedback intervention. In clinical populations — veterans with PTSD particularly — working memory improvement was documented. The autonomic modulation is real. The debates are about how to explain it mechanistically.

This is the terrain SRL occupies. The functional claim — that autonomic state is organized hierarchically, that some states support clear cognitive function and social engagement while others produce rigidity or shutdown, and that rhythmic breathing at resonant frequency shifts the system toward flexibility — does not depend on PVT's phylogenetic claims surviving Grossman's critique. Those claims operate at different levels. As Porges (2025) [PMID: 40735382] framed it in his pre-debate review: PVT's clinical value lies in "linking brainstem autonomic organization to observable patterns of regulation, vulnerability, and resilience" across trauma, chronic pain, developmental disorders, and mood disorders.

That linkage is not in dispute. The dispute is about the evolutionary story told to explain it.

## The Framework

For clinicians and researchers working with polyvagal-informed concepts, the post-Grossman landscape requires a three-tier vocabulary:

**Tier 1 — Empirically uncontested.** Autonomic state varies along a spectrum from high sympathetic activation to parasympathetic dominance. HRV/RSA metrics track this variation reliably. Slow-paced breathing at resonant frequency shifts the system measurably toward parasympathetic balance. These states differ in their support for social engagement, cognitive flexibility, and recovery. Training produces measurable, repeatable effects on these metrics.

**Tier 2 — Clinically useful, mechanistically contested.** The terms "ventral vagal," "dorsal vagal," and "neuroception" provide useful clinical language for patterns that are observationally real even if their underlying neuroanatomy is less cleanly demarcated than PVT proposes. A shutdown state that looks like dorsal vagal dominance exists clinically regardless of whether the dorsal vagus is uniquely reptilian. Clinicians who use this vocabulary should flag its contested status in academic writing while preserving its instructional utility.

**Tier 3 — Do not cite without qualification.** Specific evolutionary claims about mammalian uniqueness, the specific neuroanatomical mapping of Nucleus Ambiguus to myelination, and RSA as a direct (rather than indicative) measure of a specified vagal pathway. These claims are precisely what Grossman's 39 co-signatories contest. They should not appear in SRL's clinical or academic output without the qualifier "as hypothesized in PVT" and a reference to the ongoing debate.

---

## The Failure Mode

The failure mode here has two directions.

The first is treating the Grossman critique as a refutation of everything polyvagal-adjacent. It is not. Clinicians who say "polyvagal theory has been debunked, so we should stop teaching autonomic regulation" are making the same category error as the critique itself — conflating the evolutionary scaffolding with the functional architecture.

The second is treating the PVT vocabulary as load-bearing structure when it is not. If SRL's research publications, investor materials, or clinical outputs rely on PVT's specific evolutionary claims to support efficacy assertions, those assertions now require modification. "The ventral vagus evolved uniquely in mammals to support social behavior" is disputed. "Slow-paced breathing shifts HRV in ways that support social engagement and cognitive flexibility" is not.

The clinician who has been using polyvagal language to help a patient understand their shutdown response is not making a scientific error. The researcher who publishes "polyvagal theory predicts X" without acknowledging the active scientific debate is.

## The Test

Minimum viable experiment: 14 CRNAs complete a 4-week HRV biofeedback protocol (resonant breathing, daily 10-minute sessions). Outcome measures: RMSSD pre/post each session, end-of-shift RMSSD trajectories, MAIA-2 subscale scores (particularly Body Listening and Trusting), and one validated measure of clinical communication quality (recorded handoff exchanges rated by blind evaluators). Primary question: does measurable autonomic modulation via the protocol produce changes in downstream clinical performance markers — independent of any PVT-specific theoretical framing? This design is agnostic about the evolutionary story. It tests the mechanism as SRL deploys it.

If the answer is yes, SRL's position is: we train autonomic flexibility through validated biofeedback mechanisms. We use polyvagal language because it offers clinicians useful descriptive categories. We acknowledge the scientific debate and stake no claims on PVT's contested evolutionary premises.

---

## The Connection

This essay does not resolve the Grossman-Porges debate. It locates SRL precisely within it.

SRL sits on the functional tier — where the evidence is strong, the mechanisms are measurable, and the outcomes are replicable. The theory debate is happening one tier above: at the level of how those mechanisms are explained, organized, and historically grounded. That tier is worth monitoring. The debate is legitimate and ongoing. But it is not the tier on which SRL's clinical value stands.

What this analysis changes: how SRL writes about polyvagal theory in any document that could face scientific scrutiny. Not the practice. Not the product. The language. "Autonomic state hierarchies that are consistent with polyvagal framing" rather than "PVT demonstrates." "Published research supports autonomic state training through HRV biofeedback" rather than "polyvagal theory predicts."

The body keeps practicing. The theory keeps being revised. What endures is the breath, the beat, and the measurable shift between them.

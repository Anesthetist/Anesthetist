# Notes from the Lab: The Most Bizarre Entry in the Nursing Diagnostics Manual

*Martha Rogers' "Disturbed Energy Field" diagnosis was removed from the NANDA taxonomy for lack of empirical grounding. Modern neuroscience is building the instruments she needed.*

## The Observation

In nursing school at Rush, I wrote a paper on the strangest entry in the NANDA Nursing Diagnoses manual: **Disturbed Energy Field**.

The diagnosis had been introduced in 1992, defined as "a disruption in the individual's energy field that affects the ability to maintain balance and harmony with the environment." My classmates thought I was joking. The faculty didn't know what to do with it. The diagnosis was eventually removed from the NANDA taxonomy in 2004 — dismissed as unmeasurable, unscientific, too far outside the empirical fence.

I had already been reading Martha Rogers. Her *Science of Unitary Human Beings* (1970) was the theoretical foundation behind the diagnosis. Rogers argued that humans are not organisms — they are irreducible, dynamic energy fields existing in continuous mutual process with their environment. Health is not the absence of disease. Health is harmonious pattern manifestation within the human-environmental field.

It sounded like philosophy dressed in nursing language. It was the most interesting thing I'd read in the entire program.

## The Mechanism

Here is what Martha Rogers did not have in 1970: instruments.

She had the theory. She described the phenomenon. She even predicted the key features — that the human energy field would be measurable, that it would interact with other fields, that health would correlate with field coherence, and that disruption in the field would precede symptomatic disease.

She was right about all of it. She just couldn't prove it.

Now we can.

**The cardiac electromagnetic field.** Rollin McCraty and the HeartMath Institute demonstrated that the heart generates a bioelectromagnetic field approximately 5,000 times stronger than the brain's. This field extends several feet beyond the body. It is modulated by emotional state — coherent emotions produce coherent electromagnetic output; disordered emotions produce disordered output. When Rogers wrote about "energy field disturbance," she was describing what McCraty measures with a magnetometer.

**HRV as field coherence.** Heart rate variability — the variation in time between heartbeats — is now understood as a measure of autonomic flexibility. High HRV indicates a nervous system that adapts fluidly. Low HRV indicates rigidity. HeartMath's "coherence" metric maps HRV patterns to electromagnetic field stability. When the NANDA diagnosis said "disruption in the individual's energy field," it was describing what we now call compressed HRV — measurable on an Apple Watch.

**Interpersonal physiological synchrony.** Research on co-regulation demonstrates that people in proximity synchronize physiological rhythms — heart rate, HRV, respiratory patterns. Mother-infant dyads. Surgical teams. Therapist-client pairs. The fields interact. Rogers' "continuous mutual process between human and environment" is what Noyes et al. (2016) measured as physiological synchrony and what the vault concept `coherence-field` describes as team-level autonomic coherence.

**Interoception as the sensing mechanism.** A.D. Craig's insular cortex model (2009) showed that the brain constructs a real-time map of the body's internal state via the anterior insula. This is the neural mechanism by which a clinician *senses* a disturbance — not through mystical perception but through interoceptive processing of visceral, cardiac, and respiratory signals. When an experienced CRNA walks into a room and feels that something is off before the monitors alarm, that is interoception reading the field. Craig gave it an anatomy. Rogers gave it a name.

**Stephen Porges' neuroception.** The polyvagal theory describes a pre-conscious detection system — neuroception — that evaluates environmental safety via autonomic signals. Neuroception reads the "field" before conscious awareness catches up. Rogers' energy field is Porges' neuroceptive environment, sensed through the vagus nerve and processed in the brainstem before it reaches the cortex.

**The confound I cannot resolve:** Rogers described the energy field as extending beyond the body and interacting nonlocally. The bioelectromagnetic field data (McCraty) supports proximal interaction — the cardiac field is measurable at several feet. Whether anything operates beyond electromagnetic range remains unconfirmed by mainstream neuroscience. I state this because the essay must be honest about where the evidence supports Rogers and where it does not yet reach.

## The Framework

Rogers proposed three principles:

| Rogers' Principle | What She Meant | Modern Correlate |
|---|---|---|
| **Resonancy** | Continuous change in the human field toward increasing diversity of frequency and pattern | HRV complexity as a marker of healthy autonomic dynamics (Thayer & Lane, 2009). Higher complexity = healthier system. |
| **Helicy** | Continuous, innovative, unpredictable change in the human-environment field | Nonlinear dynamics in HRV and EEG. Jhana research (Sacchet Lab) showing the brain entering novel attractor states during deep practice. |
| **Integrality** | Continuous mutual process between human and environmental fields | Co-regulation. Interpersonal physiological synchrony. The coherence field concept. |

Each principle now has a measurable correlate. The instruments caught up to the theory.

## The Failure Mode

Rogers' framework fails when it is used to bypass empirical accountability. "Energy field disturbance" became a catchall for unverifiable claims — therapeutic touch, distance healing, crystal therapy. The NANDA committee was right to remove the diagnosis: without measurement criteria, it was clinically useless.

The lesson: a correct intuition without a measurement instrument is indistinguishable from a wrong intuition. Rogers had the right theory. Without HRV monitors, magnetometers, and interoceptive neuroscience, she couldn't distinguish her framework from pseudoscience. Neither could anyone else.

The failure mode for SRL: if we invoke Rogers without tying every claim to a measurable physiological correlate, we repeat the mistake that got the diagnosis removed. The energy field is real. It is measurable. It is the cardiac electromagnetic output, the HRV coherence pattern, the interpersonal physiological synchrony. Call it what you can measure. Honor Rogers by finishing what she started — with instruments, not intuition alone.

## The Test

**Hypothesis:** Clinicians with higher MAIA-2 scores (interoceptive awareness) demonstrate greater accuracy in detecting "energy field disturbances" — operationalized as changes in a colleague's or patient's autonomic state — compared to clinicians with lower MAIA-2 scores.

**Test:** 20 CRNAs. Administer MAIA-2. Then present each with a simulated clinical partner wearing a concealed HRV monitor whose autonomic state shifts (induced via stress task or breathing protocol) at random intervals. The CRNA reports when they sense a change. Compare detection accuracy against MAIA-2 scores.

**What this tests:** Whether Rogers' "energy field perception" is actually interoceptive accuracy applied interpersonally — and whether it correlates with a validated instrument (MAIA-2).

If yes: Rogers' framework is empirically grounded through interoception.
If no: the perception is noise, and we stop claiming it.

# Notes from the Lab: Interoception — The Sense You Were Never Taught You Have

*Interoception — the perception of the body's internal state — is not a single sense but a multidimensional capacity with at least three dissociable components, and the gap between what clinicians can detect in their patients and what they can detect in themselves represents the largest untrained competency in high-stakes healthcare.*

## The Observation

The CRNA reads the patient's body with extraordinary precision. Skin color shifts before the pulse oximeter changes. The quality of the capnography waveform signals bronchospasm seconds before alarms fire. The patient's jaw tension under the drape tells a story the monitors haven't caught yet. Twenty-eight years of this — reading another person's physiology in real time, adjusting interventions on the fly, maintaining stability across thousands of cases.

Now ask the same CRNA: What is your heart rate right now? Is your breathing pattern different from thirty minutes ago? Has your grip pressure on the laryngoscope changed since the start of the shift? Are your shoulders closer to your ears than they were at 7 AM?

Silence. Or a guess. Or surprise that the question was asked at all.

The asymmetry is staggering. The same nervous system that can detect a 2mmHg change in a patient's blood pressure cannot reliably detect a 15-beat-per-minute change in its own heart rate. The clinical training that produced world-class patient monitoring produced zero self-monitoring capacity. The outward-facing instruments were calibrated. The inward-facing instrument was never even acknowledged to exist.

This is the interoceptive gap. Not a deficit of intelligence. A deficit of training. Nobody taught the sense.

## The Mechanism

Bud Craig established the neural architecture in a pair of landmark papers. In 2009, Craig demonstrated that the anterior insular cortex (AIC) functions as the brain's primary interoceptive hub — re-representing signals from the body's interior to create moment-by-moment maps of the physiological self [PMID: 19096369]. The AIC integrates signals ranging from bowel distension to orgasm, from cigarette craving to maternal love, from decision-making to sudden insight. Craig proposed that this re-representation in the AIC is a fundamental component of conscious awareness itself — that "how you feel" is not a peripheral add-on to cognition but a constitutive element of it.

The neural pathway is specific: visceral afferents (from heart, lungs, gut, skin) travel via the vagus nerve and spinothalamic tract to the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS) and parabrachial nucleus in the brainstem, then to the posterior insula (raw sensory signal), then to the anterior insula (integrated, contextualized signal that includes emotional valence and temporal perspective). The posterior-to-anterior gradient is a gradient of complexity — from "my heart is beating fast" (posterior) to "I am anxious about this induction" (anterior). SRL's [[anterocept]] concept maps directly onto this gradient: Domain 1 (baseline interoceptive awareness) corresponds to posterior insular processing; Domain 3 (integrated interoceptive-exteroceptive awareness) corresponds to the anterior insula's integrative function.

Sarah Garfinkel, working with Anil Seth and Hugo Critchley, demonstrated in 2015 that interoception is not one thing but at least three dissociable dimensions [PMID: 25451381]. In a normative sample of 80 participants using heartbeat detection tasks:

1. **Interoceptive accuracy** — objective performance on heartbeat detection (can you actually count your heartbeats correctly?)
2. **Interoceptive sensibility** — subjective self-assessment via questionnaire (do you believe you're good at sensing your body?)
3. **Interoceptive awareness** — metacognitive correspondence between accuracy and confidence (do you know how good you actually are?)

The critical finding: these three dimensions are dissociable. A person can have high sensibility ("I'm very in touch with my body") but low accuracy (their heartbeat count is wrong). The correspondence between what you think you can detect and what you actually can detect — interoceptive awareness — emerged as the most clinically consequential dimension. It is, in essence, the metacognitive gap that Garfinkel named.

Wolf Mehling operationalized the measurement side in 2012 with the MAIA — the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness [PMID: 23133619]. The 32-item instrument measures eight subscales: Noticing, Not-Distracting, Not-Worrying, Attention Regulation, Emotional Awareness, Self-Regulation, Body Listening, and Trusting. The taxonomy matters because it reveals that interoception is not a single dial from "low" to "high" — it is eight separate capacities, each independently trainable.

For clinicians, the most consequential subscales are Self-Regulation (can you use body awareness to regulate your state?) and Not-Worrying (can you attend to a body signal without being consumed by it?). These are the subscales that the Joe Hudson "presence without fixing" observation maps onto — and they are typically the lowest-scoring subscales in general populations. The capacity to notice a body signal and not react to it is the terminal skill in interoceptive development.

Ohira (2015) established the decision-making link: interoceptive signals represented in the insula and anterior cingulate cortex play critical roles in decision-making under uncertainty [PMID: 25816635]. The somatic marker hypothesis (Damasio, 1994) proposed that body-state signals bias decisions before conscious deliberation — the gut feeling that precedes the reasoned analysis. Ohira extended this beyond Damasio's framework to include autonomic, endocrine, and immune signals that continuously shape how clinicians evaluate options under pressure.

**Confounds to state explicitly:** Heartbeat detection tasks — the gold standard for measuring interoceptive accuracy — have well-documented limitations. Participants may use timing estimation strategies rather than actual cardiac perception. Ring and Brener (1996) demonstrated that counting ability and temporal estimation account for significant variance in heartbeat counting performance. Additionally, interoceptive accuracy is influenced by heart rate (easier to detect faster rates), body composition (leaner individuals detect more easily), and attention allocation. Garfinkel's three-dimensional framework partially addresses this by separating accuracy from metacognitive awareness, but the measurement challenge persists. SRL's approach should use heartbeat detection as one signal among many — not the sole index of interoceptive capacity. The MAIA-2's self-report subscales provide complementary information that captures dimensions the heartbeat task cannot reach.

## The Framework

Interoception in the SRL system operates as a three-layer developmental model, mapping onto both Craig's neural architecture and Garfinkel's dimensional framework:

**Layer 1: Signal Detection (Posterior Insula)**
Can you detect the raw signal? Heart rate, breathing pattern, muscle tension, temperature, visceral sensations. This is Garfinkel's "accuracy" dimension and MAIA-2's "Noticing" subscale. The foundation. Most untrained clinicians have moderate capacity here — they can feel their heart pound during a code but cannot detect a 10-beat-per-minute shift during routine cases.

**Layer 2: Signal Interpretation (Mid-Insula)**
Can you make meaning from the signal? Not just "my heart is fast" but "my heart rate elevated 12 minutes ago when the surgeon mentioned the aorta, and it hasn't come back down, which means I'm carrying sympathetic residue into this next decision." This is MAIA-2's "Emotional Awareness" and "Body Listening" subscales. This is where [[interoceptive-literacy]] lives — the bridge from raw detection to clinical self-insight.

**Layer 3: Signal Integration (Anterior Insula)**
Can you hold the body signal in awareness while simultaneously managing external demands? The CRNA who monitors their own breathing pattern while intubating a patient — two interoceptive streams (self and patient) running in parallel. This is [[embodied-metacognition]]: the body as the instrument of metacognitive monitoring. MAIA-2's "Attention Regulation" and "Self-Regulation" subscales index this capacity. This is also where [[anterocept]] Domain 3 operates — integrated interoceptive-exteroceptive awareness under load.

Each layer builds on the one below. You cannot interpret a signal you haven't detected. You cannot integrate a signal you haven't interpreted. The training sequence matters — and the mistake most mindfulness programs make is jumping to Layer 3 (equanimous awareness of everything) before establishing Layer 1 (can you feel your heartbeat right now?).

## The Failure Mode

Interoceptive training breaks under three conditions:

**1. Interoceptive hypervigilance.** The opposite of interoceptive suppression: attending to body signals so intensely that normal physiological variation becomes a source of anxiety. The clinician who starts noticing their heart rate and can't stop. Health anxiety, panic disorder, and somatic symptom disorder all involve excessive or misinterpreted interoceptive attention. Training must emphasize the "Not-Worrying" subscale alongside the "Noticing" subscale — detection without alarm. Garfinkel's awareness dimension (metacognitive accuracy) is the protective factor: knowing that a racing heart during induction is appropriate, not pathological.

**2. The expertise paradox.** Highly experienced clinicians may have developed effective [[interoceptive-suppression-hypothesis|interoceptive suppression]] strategies — deliberately dampening body signals to maintain performance under extreme conditions. Introducing interoceptive training could temporarily destabilize a functional suppression strategy before building a better alternative. The transition period — when the old strategy is disrupted but the new one isn't yet automatic — is the vulnerability window. Training must acknowledge this: "Your current approach works. We're not replacing it. We're adding a layer underneath it."

**3. Individual variation masquerading as deficiency.** Interoceptive accuracy varies naturally with heart rate, body composition, age, medication, and genetics. A CRNA on beta-blockers will have objectively lower heartbeat detection accuracy — not because of poor interoceptive skill but because the signal is pharmacologically dampened. MAIA-2 self-report scales partially bypass this limitation, but the system must normalize individual baselines rather than applying population norms that pathologize medication-appropriate physiology.

## The Test

**14-day proof test with 10 CRNAs:**

- **Day 1 (baseline):** MAIA-2 full assessment (37 items, 8 subscales). Heartbeat counting task (3 trials, 25/35/45 seconds). Resting HRV (5-minute recording). Subjective body awareness confidence rating (0-10).
- **Days 2-13 (intervention):** Daily 60-second body scan at the start of each [[gap-moment-training]] session — 30 seconds of interoceptive attention (heart, breath, tension, temperature) before 30 seconds of resonant breathing. Five sessions per day. No additional instruction beyond "notice what you notice."
- **Day 14 (post):** Repeat all Day 1 measures.

**Primary outcome:** MAIA-2 subscale score changes, particularly Self-Regulation and Body Listening (the two subscales most directly trained by the intervention).

**Secondary outcomes:** Heartbeat counting accuracy, confidence-accuracy correspondence (interoceptive awareness), resting HRV change.

**Positive result:** ≥ 0.5 SD improvement on Self-Regulation and Body Listening subscales. Heartbeat counting accuracy improvement is secondary — the MAIA-2 captures dimensions the heartbeat task cannot reach.

**What would falsify the concept:** If the 30-second body scan produces no MAIA-2 subscale improvements after 12 days (60 exposures), then either the dose is insufficient (30 seconds is below threshold), the frequency is insufficient (5x/day is not enough), or interoceptive capacity is more trait-like than SRL's model assumes. Each falsification pathway suggests a specific redesign.

## The Connection

Interoception is the foundation layer of the entire SRL architecture. [[Interoceptive-literacy]] is the trained version. [[Anterocept]] is the progressive training methodology. [[Embodied-metacognition]] is the metacognitive application. [[MAIA-2-crna]] is the measurement instrument. [[Vagal-tone]] is the physiological substrate that interoception monitors. [[Polyanchora]] extends interoceptive anchors to include exteroceptive targets.

Without interoception, the rest of the system has no input signal. The CRNA who cannot detect their own state cannot regulate it. The driver who cannot feel the sympathetic escalation cannot intervene before it costs lap time. The nervous system they're asked to train is the nervous system they've never been asked to notice.

Every sense in clinical education has a training protocol except this one. Vision gets optics courses. Hearing gets auscultation training. Touch gets palpation practice. Interoception gets nothing. SRL fills the gap.

*Close your eyes for three seconds. Without moving your hand to your chest, estimate your heart rate. Hold the number. Now check it — Apple Watch, radial pulse, whatever's closest. The distance between your estimate and reality is your interoceptive accuracy, right now, in this moment. That distance is trainable.*

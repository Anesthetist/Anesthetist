# Notes from the Lab: The Small Self — Awe as Physiology in the Operating Room

*Awe — perceived vastness requiring cognitive accommodation — is the only positive emotion where measurable anti-inflammatory and parasympathetic effects converge, and the attentional reframe it creates (the "small self") directly opposes the ego-centric narrowing mechanism at the root of clinician depersonalization.*

---

## The Observation

It is the third trauma case since 0400. The clinician has been running on tightened attention for six hours — not fear exactly, but a familiar narrowing. The world has collapsed to the next number, the next decision, the waveform on the monitor. Self-referential processing has taken over: *Am I managing this? Is this my error? What comes next?*

Then a resident says, quietly: "Look at this pressure tracing." And for a moment — five seconds, maybe ten — the clinician sees it differently. Not *I did that*, but *that physiology is extraordinary*. Not *my performance*, but *what the human body is capable of against these odds*.

The narrowing opens, just slightly. The tightening in the chest softens. Attention widens by one degree.

That is not a poetic moment. That is a physiological event. Dacher Keltner's lab has spent twenty years building the evidence for what just happened — and the data has direct clinical implications that the wellness industry has not yet reached.

---

## The Mechanism

In their 2003 prototype theory, published in *Cognition & Emotion*, Keltner and Haidt defined awe through two components that distinguish it from every related emotion: **perceived vastness** (something physically, conceptually, socially, or temporally larger than the current self-frame can hold) and a **need for accommodation** (existing mental schemas are inadequate; the cognitive system must update). Keltner and Haidt (2003) distinguished awe from admiration (which doesn't require accommodation), from wonder (which doesn't require vastness), and from elevation. Awe requires both — and the cognitive work of accommodation is where the physiology lives. [PMID: 29715721, DOI: 10.1080/02699930302297]

This accommodation is not passive processing. It recruits prefrontal engagement in a specific direction: away from self-referential processing, toward what phenomenologists call allocentric attention — the frame where *this situation* is larger than *my response to it*. The subjective experience people name "the small self" — the sense that the ego recedes while the world expands — is a measurable attentional reframe, not a spiritual metaphor.

The anti-inflammatory evidence is the most direct data point in the chain. Stellar, John-Henderson, Anderson, Gordon, McNeil, and Keltner (2015) recruited two samples of healthy participants and measured dispositional positive affect across ten discrete emotion categories alongside blood levels of IL-6, a proinflammatory cytokine. Across all positive emotions tested — amusement, awe, compassion, contentment, enthusiasm, gratitude, love, pride — **awe was the strongest predictor of lower IL-6 levels**. This held when controlling for personality variables and health behaviors. Not the most pleasant emotion. Not the most frequently experienced. The one that demands the most cognitive work: accommodation of a vast object that existing schemas cannot hold. [PMID: 25603133, DOI: 10.1037/emo0000033]

The autonomic pathway proposed by Stellar et al. — that awe activates parasympathetic dominance via the vagus nerve — has empirical grounding in adjacent research from the same laboratory. Stellar, Cohen, Oveis, and Keltner (2015) measured physiological responses to suffering across four studies in healthy adults. Compassion — a closely related prosocial, allocentric emotion in Keltner's framework — produced significantly higher respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA, the gold-standard index of vagal tone) compared to neutral conditions and to other positive emotions, accompanied by lower heart rate and respiration but no difference in skin conductance. [PMID: 25621856, DOI: 10.1037/pspi0000010] If compassion and awe share the allocentric attentional architecture, the vagal pathway carries empirical weight beyond the IL-6 paper alone.

Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory adds the longitudinal dimension. In *American Psychologist* (2001), Fredrickson proposed that positive emotions broaden momentary thought-action repertoires — expanding attention, cognition, and behavioral options — and over time build durable personal resources that persist after the emotion fades. Fredrickson (2001) predicted that positive emotions should accelerate cardiovascular recovery from negative emotion (the "undoing hypothesis"), and that the resources built through repeated positive-emotion experiences should compound across time. Awe, as the most cognitively demanding positive emotion in the taxonomy, is predicted by this framework to build the most durable resources. [PMID: 11315248, DOI: 10.1037//0003-066x.56.3.218]

The recent empirical work supports a specific version of this. Chen, Hu, Xiao, and Liu (2025, *Scientific Reports*) used a recall priming task paradigm with 342 participants to show that awe experience had a direct effect on **cognitive well-being** (life satisfaction) — but not affective well-being (emotional balance) — mediated by resilience. This is a critical distinction: awe is not a mood enhancer. The mechanism is cognitive restructuring → resilience → durability, not mood elevation. [PMID: 40148569, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-95435-7]

Yan, Jones, Holman, Silver, and Garfin (2026, *Psychological Trauma*) tracked a nationally representative U.S. sample through the COVID-19 pandemic. Psychological strengths were operationalized as four assets: positive affect, **awe**, cognitive reappraisal, and benefit finding. In cluster analysis, individuals with high adversity AND high strengths showed lower global distress and emotional exhaustion than individuals with moderate adversity but low psychological strengths. Awe was effective even under the highest adversity exposure — the condition where wellness resources most commonly fail. [PMID: 42133390, DOI: 10.1037/tra0002129]

Pan and Jiang (2022, *Emotion*) showed in six studies (N = 1,384) that awe fosters **global self-continuity** — a sense of connectedness among past, present, and future selves — through global processing and narrative. The effect held even when awe was induced by threatening experience. [PMID: 36355671, DOI: 10.1037/emo0001187] This is directly relevant to depersonalization — the burnout dimension in which the clinician becomes detached from the meaning of their work, from colleagues, from patients. Depersonalization is, mechanistically, a disruption of self-continuity. Awe reverses that disruption by restoring the allocentric frame in which the clinician exists within something larger than themselves.

## The Protocol

Awe is not manufactured. It is cultivated by training attention toward what is already present. In a clinical environment:

1. **Pre-induction attention shift (60 seconds before induction, after checklist):** Identify one aspect of the upcoming case that is genuinely remarkable — the patient's compensatory physiology, the monitoring system's precision, the coordination the team is already executing without being told to. This is not affirmation. It is noticing what is actually vast.

2. **Mid-case reset (when state drift is detected):** One full breath. Then one query: *What is happening right now that I would not have predicted?* This is schema accommodation practiced in real time — the cognitive move that defines awe.

3. **Post-case integration (30 seconds before the next case):** Identify one genuine observation about what just happened — not a performance review, not self-evaluation. A phenomenon. Something the physiology or the team or the case itself did that was worth noticing.

**Measurement:** MAIA-2 subscales "Not-Worrying" and "Self-Regulation" at baseline and 8 weeks. RMSSD (wearable, measured during gap moments) across the period.

**Success:** RMSSD increase ≥10% from baseline across the measurement period. Reduced subjective ego-centric narrowing during peak-demand moments.

**Failure:** No RMSSD change. Continued self-referential preoccupation during gap moments, or the practice devolves into performance ("I should feel awe right now") — the failure mode described below.

---

## The Failure Mode

Awe cannot be performed. The moment it becomes "I should feel awe right now," it has collapsed into self-referential processing — the precise opposite of what it is designed to do. This is the primary misapplication: wellness as performance, which is the dominant failure mode of every mindfulness intervention that has been scripted and mandated in healthcare organizations.

It also fails structurally when deployed inside an acute crisis rather than in the gaps around it. Mid-airway emergency, awe is not the tool. Schema accommodation requires cognitive bandwidth. The protocols above target the 30–60 seconds around demand peaks, not inside them.

Finally: awe requires a vocabulary of the vast. Clinicians whose training has produced purely instrumental attention — the patient as a physiological problem, the case as a task sequence — may have atrophied the attentional capacity for accommodation. Restoration takes deliberate practice: the capacity must be rebuilt before it can be deployed clinically. Horvath and Grass (2021, *AANA Journal*) documented that SRNA burnout driven by stress and anxiety specifically undermines the internal resources available for this kind of restorative attention work, suggesting that early-career investment in this capacity is more valuable than late-career remediation. [PMID: 34586995]

## The Test

Minimum viable experiment: 14 CRNAs, 8-week protocol using the three-step approach above. Randomize 7 to the awe-protocol condition, 7 to a generic mindfulness comparison.

**Primary measure:** MAIA-2 "Self-Regulation" and "Not-Worrying" subscales at baseline, 4 weeks, 8 weeks.

**Secondary measure:** RMSSD via wearable during a standardized 60-second post-case gap moment, measured at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks.

**Tertiary:** Single-item burnout measure ("Using your own definition of burnout, how would you rate your current level?" — validated 1-item measure, Dolan et al. 2015 methodology) at the same time points.

**Hypothesis:** The awe group shows greater improvement in MAIA-2 Self-Regulation at 8 weeks and a smaller decline in RMSSD across the study period, compared to the mindfulness control.

**What this doesn't test:** The inflammatory pathway (IL-6 measurement would require blood draws at pre- and post-shift — feasible in a Phase 2 research setting, requiring IRB and collaboration with a clinical lab). The protocol above is deployable at small scale without institutional laboratory infrastructure.

## The Connection

Awe-as-regulation is the identity-level wing of the somnistics regulatory stack. [[curiosity-as-clinical-stance]] handles internal regulation — converting interoceptive arousal from threat to data. Awe handles the outer frame: converting the clinical moment from a self-referential performance into something larger than the ego's success or failure. Together, curiosity and awe address the two failure modes of sustained clinical attention that no wearable can train alone.

The connections extend through [[gap-moment-training]] (the temporal container where awe can be deployed), [[vagal-tone]] (the proposed autonomic pathway), [[clinician-durability]] (awe as the positive-affect mechanism that specifically counters depersonalization, the burnout dimension least responsive to generic mindfulness interventions), and [[anterocept]] (awe as a form of allocentric anticipatory awareness, sensing the largeness of what is about to happen rather than the threat of failing to manage it).

What Keltner and Haidt named in 2003, Stellar's laboratory measured physiologically in 2015, and the pandemic longitudinal data has now confirmed in high-adversity populations: the emotion that most reliably downregulates the inflammatory response is not the one that feels the best. It's the one that demands the most from the cognitive system — the one that requires the self to become, momentarily, small.

---

*A heart working against the odds, in the early hours, still doing exactly what it is supposed to do — that is not a sentiment. It is a fact worth accommodating.*

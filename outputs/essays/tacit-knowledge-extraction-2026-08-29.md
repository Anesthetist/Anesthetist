# Notes from the Lab: The Asset Nobody Can Steal — Tacit Knowledge Extraction and the SRL Pipeline

*Tacit clinical knowledge — the body-level expertise that no algorithm yet replaces — can be extracted and codified into deployable micro-protocols, but only when the extraction method is as mechanistically precise and un-theatrical as the knowledge it seeks to capture.*

## The Observation

Watch a CRNA with 28 years of experience place an LMA. Not the mechanics of it — those are learnable in a residency. Watch what happens in the three seconds before the hand moves. The eyes shift. There is a weight-transfer from one foot. A breath is held for exactly one beat. Then the wrist rotates at an angle that no textbook has ever specified, and the airway is secured.

Ask what just happened and the CRNA will say something like: "I felt resistance." Or: "It just wasn't right yet." Or, most honestly: "I don't know. It worked."

This is Polanyi's formulation made flesh: we know more than we can tell. The knowledge is real — it saves lives, consistently, at scale across a career. But it does not arrive in declarative form. It does not transfer through lecture. It lives in the proprioceptive loop, the timing system, the baroreflex read that has no monitor correlate. And when a generation of that knowledge retires, it goes with the body that built it.

The question Somnistics Research Labs exists to answer is: does it have to?

## The Mechanism

Gabbay and le May, in their landmark 2004 ethnographic study published in the BMJ, found something that should have upended clinical education but largely didn't: clinicians in primary care rarely consulted explicit evidence guidelines when making decisions. Instead, they relied on what Gabbay and le May named "mindlines" — internalised, collectively reinforced, tacit guidelines built from clinical experience, collegial interaction, and the accumulated narrative of practice Gabbay & le May (2004) [PMID: 15514347]. The evidence guidelines were often consulted after the decision, not before. What guided action was the mindline — the compressed, embodied summary of what-works, built from years of pattern matching and never fully articulable.

This is not a failure of clinical reasoning. It is its highest expression. Gary Klein's Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) model — reviewed by Bond and Cooper (2006) in the context of emergency nursing decisions — describes how experts in time-pressured situations do not deliberate between options. They pattern-match the current situation to previous situations, generate a first course of action, mentally simulate its viability, and act Bond & Cooper (2006) [PMID: 16879547]. The CRNA who "just knows" the airway isn't right is running an RPD loop at a processing speed that deliberative reasoning cannot match.

Gobet and Chassy (2007), critiquing Benner's novice-to-expert model in the International Journal of Nursing Studies, argue that this process is not purely intuitive — it involves the rapid, sub-conscious interaction of perception and template-matching against a library of stored clinical patterns Gobet & Chassy (2007) [PMID: 17337269]. The expertise is real. Its mechanism is pattern compression: thousands of hours of deliberate practice, compressed into templates that fire faster than conscious thought. The novice has no templates. The expert has a library.

Here is where the extraction problem becomes economically important: that library dies with the expert.

Michael Patkin, writing in the ANZ Journal of Surgery, described a direct attempt to solve this problem: translating surgical heuristics — the implicit rules of thumb that expert surgeons follow without naming — into explicit, declarative protocols. His method was observation plus introspection: watching himself operate on video and naming the tacit rules he was actually following Patkin (2008) [PMID: 19087044]. "Heuristics are rules of thumb," Patkin wrote. "Rarely described in surgical or other publications, they are an essential part of safe and expert performance." His result was modest but important: it is possible to surface some of the tacit rule-set, but only through careful, iterative introspective work — not through a standard interview or an AI prompt that asks "describe your expertise."

The extraction method must match the nature of what is being extracted. Theatrical summarization — the kind that produces bullet-point lists under headers like "Key Insights From 28 Years of Practice" — does not extract tacit knowledge. It produces the declarative knowledge the expert can consciously report, which is a small and often misleading fraction of what actually guides their action. Mickleborough, reviewing Schön's reflective practitioner framework in Medical Teacher (2015), notes that technical knowledge alone is insufficient for complex professional problems: the practitioner must develop a reflective capacity — what Schön called reflection-in-action — that allows the tacit to become (temporarily, partially) visible Mickleborough (2015) [PMID: 26427516].

Reflection-in-action is not summarization. It is a disciplined, slowed-down inquiry into what the body just did and why.

## The Protocol

SRL's tacit knowledge extraction pipeline operates in five stages, each with its own methods and failure modes:

**Stage 1 — Tacit Clinical Skill.** The raw asset: Randy's 28-year embodied CRNA practice. The perceptual acuity, the micro-timing, the state-reading that happens before alarms. This is the source.

**Stage 2 — Codified Micro-Practices.** Reflection-in-action applied to specific clinical moments: Gap Moment Training protocols, NeuroMinute scripts, the five-breath re-embodiment sequence. Each micro-practice is the residue of a tacit skill made declarative enough to teach. The key standard: it must be mechanistic (specific timing, specific anchor, specific measurement criterion) not generic ("take a breath").

**Stage 3 — Algorithmic Deployment.** The codified micro-practice becomes an adaptive delivery system. The Pausality app does not deliver a fixed protocol — it adapts to biometric state. This is tacit knowledge converted to state variables: if HRV is below threshold X after event type Y, deliver intervention Z. The expert's "mindline" becomes a conditional rule.

**Stage 4 — Community Reinforcement.** Mindlines, as Gabbay and le May demonstrated, are socially reinforced. Individual tacit knowledge becomes collective practice through peer interaction, shared vocabulary, and ritual. GMT certification does this: it creates a community of practice that collectively reinforces the extracted protocols until they become mindlines in their own right — tacit again, but now at a higher level of fidelity.

**Stage 5 — IP Protection.** The trademarked terms (Gap Moment Training™, NeuroMinute™, Pausality™) are not marketing decorations. They are IP anchors for specific mechanistic protocols. When the extraction is precise enough to trademark the concept, it is precise enough to defend. The trademarked term is evidence that the tacit has become sufficiently explicit to be named.

## The Failure Mode

The extraction fails in two directions, and both are common.

**Over-abstraction:** The extracted "knowledge" becomes generic. "Breathe mindfully during stressful moments" is not tacit knowledge extraction — it is category-level advice that costs nothing to say and transfers nothing. The marker is the absence of specificity: no timing, no measurement criterion, no named failure condition. This is what theatrical AI summarization produces. Randy's direct feedback — "Reading this output is awful" — names this failure mode precisely. The output felt like the idea of clinical precision without any actual precision inside it.

**Under-constraint:** The extracted protocol attempts to capture so much tacit nuance that it becomes a research paper, not a teachable practice. The five-breath re-embodiment protocol works because it constrains the tacit into five specific, ordered, named steps — not because it explains the neurophysiology of each breath. The temptation to explain is the failure mode: explanation is for understanding, not for doing. A CRNA using a NeuroMinute has 60 seconds, not a reading period.

The failure mode that concerns SRL most: extraction that produces the appearance of tacit knowledge capture without the substance. A practitioner running an over-abstracted protocol feels they are doing the thing. The biometric data says otherwise.

## The Test

Minimum viable experiment: ten CRNAs, 14 days, within-subject crossover design.

Week 1 (baseline): Shift-start RMSSD, shift-end RMSSD, self-reported state quality (1-10).

Week 2 (intervention): Same measurements plus NeuroMinute protocol delivered at three gap moments identified by the practitioner. The protocol tested is a codified extraction of a specific micro-skill Randy identified through reflection-in-action: a cardiac-anchored breath sequence tied to the moment of patient turnover.

Success criterion: week 2 shift-end RMSSD ≥ week 1 shift-end RMSSD for 7 of 10 participants.

Failure criterion: week 2 shift-end RMSSD below week 1 baseline, or participants report "I wasn't sure when to use it" — which indicates Stage 2 codification was insufficient, not that the tacit knowledge is inextractable.

The experiment is not testing whether tacit knowledge exists. It is testing whether Stage 2 extraction was precise enough to produce a deployable protocol. Failed experiments are not evidence against the pipeline. They are calibration data for the extraction method.

## The Connection

Tacit knowledge extraction is the epistemic core of the SRL business model. Everything else in the company is downstream of it: the NeuroMinutes are extracted tacit skills; the Pausality adaptive delivery system is tacit-knowledge-as-algorithm; the certification is tacit-knowledge-as-community-standard. Without precise extraction, what SRL delivers is generic wellness content — indistinguishable from the market it is trying to disrupt.

The [[consilience-by-translation]] concept names the epistemological method: Polanyi, Schön, Klein, Benner, and Cedric Chin are all describing the same problem in different vocabularies. The convergence of their frameworks is the signal that tacit knowledge extraction is a real and solvable problem — not a philosophical puzzle, but an engineering challenge that rewards precise methodology.

The asset is real. The extraction method is the moat.

# Notes from the Lab: Secure Attachment to Reality

*Secure attachment to reality — the capacity to remain epistemically open to overwhelming complexity without ideological collapse or dissociative numbing — is a trainable physiological state defined by the convergence of high vagal tone, active mentalizing, and interoceptive grounding, and it represents the functional endpoint of the somnistics curriculum.*

## The Observation

It is 03:14. The patient's pressure is dropping and nobody knows why. The attending surgeon is escalating. Three monitors are alarming simultaneously. In that moment, every clinician in the room has a physiological choice that is not, in any meaningful sense, a conscious decision.

The first pattern is tunnel vision — sympathetic surge, attention narrows to the most proximal threat, peripheral awareness collapses. The clinician works fast and misses the systemic picture.

The second pattern is dissociation — dorsal vagal withdrawal, a strange calm that is not calm, the monitors continuing to alarm while the clinician moves through molasses. Competent hands, absent self.

Neither is adaptive. Both look, from the outside, like the clinician is doing something.

The third pattern is rarer. The clinician expands — holds the immediate pressure drop and the recent fluid balance and the patient's cardiac history and the team's escalating anxiety simultaneously, without losing any thread. Randy named this quality while analyzing Dr. Pippa Malmgren's geopolitical writing: *this is secure attachment to reality*. The capacity to engage with overwhelming complexity — to let it land without being swept away or going numb.

What distinguishes the third pattern physiologically is the question this essay addresses.

## The Mechanism

Three systems must converge for secure attachment to reality to be possible. None alone is sufficient.

**System 1: Vagal tone and cognitive architecture**

In 2020, Chand and colleagues at the University of Tübingen examined how resting heart rate variability (HRV) relates to dynamic functional connectivity between brain networks in 38 healthy male subjects. They found that RMSSD — the standard metric of parasympathetic regulation — was significantly correlated with the functional connectivity between the Default Mode Network (DMN) and the Central Executive Network (CEN): the two networks that, respectively, generate self-referential context and execute goal-directed action. The relationship was specific: DMN-CEN coupling tracked RMSSD more strongly than did salience-CEN coupling. After an acute stress task, this differential relationship disappeared. The stress-induced sympathetic surge decoupled the brain's capacity to hold self-referential context and directed action simultaneously. Chand et al. (2020) [PMID: 32714132, DOI: 10.3389/fnins.2020.00645] proposed this as a mechanism: high vagal tone enables the brain to maintain the integrative architecture that flexible, contextually sensitive cognition requires — and stress disrupts it.

Grol and De Raedt (2020) [PMID: 32462431, DOI: 10.3758/s13415-020-00800-w] extended the neurovisceral integration model to a domain directly relevant to clinical complexity: *affective* flexibility, the capacity to shift attention between emotional and non-emotional aspects of negative information. In a sample of 109 subjects, resting HRV predicted performance on a task-switching paradigm using emotional material. The link ran through cognitive-emotional integration: higher vagal tone supported more effective flexible engagement with negative content. This is exactly the capacity required to remain present with a deteriorating patient rather than cognitively avoiding the gravity of what is happening.

Meanwhile, Riganello and colleagues (2023) [PMID: 36673293, DOI: 10.3390/e25010152] demonstrated that HRV complexity — the *entropy* of beat-to-beat variation, not just its magnitude — predicted response inhibition performance: the capacity to suppress impulsive reactions during cognitive challenges of increasing difficulty. The complexity index (CI) and sympathovagal balance (LF/HF ratio) together classified high and low performers at 73% sensitivity and 71% specificity. High HRV complexity is the signature of a nervous system with regulatory reserve — one that can modulate its own reactivity, not merely suppress it.

**System 2: Intolerance of uncertainty as a cortical problem**

Carlson and colleagues (2025) [PMID: 39870976, DOI: 10.3758/s13415-024-01262-0] asked a precise question: what is the neuroanatomy of difficulty tolerating unknown outcomes? Their finding was important. Intolerance of uncertainty (IU) — controlling for overlapping anxiety and depression — was associated with the structure of the inferior frontal cortex (IFC) and orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), not with the amygdala. The IFC and OFC are regions of cognitive control, emotional valuation, and inhibitory regulation — cortical machinery, not primitive threat detection. This means IU is not primarily a fear problem. It is a cognitive flexibility problem. And cognitive flexibility, as the vagal tone literature demonstrates, is trainable through the autonomic system.

The implication for somnistics is specific: the clinician who cannot remain open to a complex, ambiguous situation is not experiencing excessive fear. They are experiencing insufficient prefrontal regulatory capacity — the same capacity indexed by RMSSD, shaped by resonant breathing training, and measurable before and after intervention.

**System 3: Epistemic trust and the capacity to receive**

Fonagy, Luyten, Allison, and Campbell (2017) [PMID: 28413687, DOI: 10.1186/s40479-017-0061-9] articulated the framework that most directly names what Randy identified in Malmgren's writing. *Epistemic trust* — the capacity to receive socially transmitted information as personally relevant and generalizable — is an evolved social learning mechanism. When it functions, a person can engage with new data, update their model of what is happening, and respond with calibrated flexibility. When it fails — epistemic mistrust — the person filters incoming information through defensive assumptions: *this can't be right, this doesn't apply to me, this source cannot be trusted.* Fonagy et al. argued that epistemic mistrust, not any specific symptom cluster, is the core of severe psychopathology: the person cannot learn from their environment. The therapy is not technique delivery but the restoration of a functional epistemic opening.

Kurt and colleagues (2025) [PMID: 41309288, DOI: 10.1002/cpp.70185] tested the pathway. In a combined clinical and community sample of 1,129 participants, both attachment anxiety and attachment avoidance increased borderline personality features — but the pathway ran through *hypomentalizing* (reduced capacity to model mental states) and *epistemic mistrust*, not through anxiety directly. The mediation was parallel and significant for both attachment dimensions. The implication: secure attachment produces not just emotional regulation but epistemic openness — the willingness and capacity to model others' minds and to receive external information without defensive collapse.

**The convergence**

When vagal tone is sufficient — RMSSD reflecting the brain's ability to maintain DMN-CEN integration — and mentalizing is active — the capacity to hold multiple simultaneous perspectives without collapsing them into one — and interoceptive literacy provides felt-sense grounding that prevents dissociation, a third possibility opens. The clinician at 03:14 does not tunnel. Does not dissociate. Remains present with the overwhelming complexity and continues to receive information. This is secure attachment to reality: a physiological state, not a personality type.

## The Protocol

SRL's implementation moves in sequence:

1. **Baseline assessment:** MAIA-2-CRNA (interoceptive literacy across eight subscales), BOLT score (CO2 tolerance as proxy for autonomic reserve), resting RMSSD baseline.
2. **Regulatory floor building:** Resonant breathing at individual resonant frequency (4.5–6.5 BPM) to establish minimum effective vagal tone. The NeuroMinute before complexity-laden briefings, not after them.
3. **Complexity-tolerant exposure:** Structured case review protocols using genuinely ambiguous, unresolved cases. HRV monitoring during engagement. The goal is to pair high vagal tone with genuine uncertainty — the state of remaining open while the answer is unavailable.
4. **Epistemic scaffolding:** Reflective practice protocols that formalize the mentalization function: "What might the system be doing that I have not yet seen? What assumption am I applying to this situation? What would change if that assumption were wrong?"

The sequence matters: autonomic regulation first, epistemic practice second. Attempting epistemic openness without the physiological infrastructure is asking the inferior frontal cortex to do work it cannot do without sufficient vagal support.

## The Failure Mode

The primary failure mode is confusing calm with security. A dissociated clinician looks calm. A dorsal vagal shutdown produces low heart rate, low RMSSD, and behavioral quieting — the physiological signature of collapse, not regulation. MAIA-2 Noticing subscale scores distinguish them: the securely attached clinician has high body awareness alongside high regulation; the dissociated clinician has both reduced.

The secondary failure mode runs in the opposite direction: *epistemic credulity* — absorbing every signal, every narrative, every alarming input without the capacity to filter or evaluate. This is not security; it is overwhelm without the somatic ground to hold it. The felt-sense anchor that interoceptive literacy provides is what allows a person to receive complexity without being destabilized by it.

A third failure mode applies specifically to the clinic: *domain narrowing under complexity*. The clinician who, facing overwhelming uncertainty, retreats to pure protocol. Protocol adherence is essential; the failure is when it substitutes for situational awareness rather than supporting it. This is the epistemically insecure response: the world has become too complex, so the clinician shrinks the world.

## The Test

14-day study. 12–18 CRNAs. Daily 10-minute exposure to genuinely ambiguous material — case reviews of unresolved adverse events, not teaching cases with known answers. HRV (RMSSD) measured before and after each session via a wearable. MAIA-2 subscales (Noticing, Attention Regulation, Trusting) assessed at day 0, day 7, and day 14.

Primary outcome: Do participants in the autonomic priming condition (NeuroMinute before exposure) maintain RMSSD during complexity exposure and show increasing MAIA-2 Trusting subscale scores compared to unprimed controls?

Falsification criterion: If RMSSD during complexity exposure does not differ between primed and unprimed groups across the 14 days, the sequencing hypothesis is wrong — and complexity exposure alone may be the active ingredient, with autonomic priming adding nothing.

Secondary outcome: Does resting RMSSD at day 14 predict self-reported epistemic openness (capacity to engage with ambiguous professional information without defensive foreclosure)? If the neurovisceral integration model holds, these should be correlated.

## The Connection

Every other somnistics concept in this vault — the NeuroMinute, Gap Moment Training, anterocept, resonant breathing, state transition intelligence — converges here. They are the infrastructure. Secure attachment to reality is what the infrastructure is *for*. Not calm, which is often the absence of threat rather than the presence of capacity. Not regulation, which is technique. But the lived experience of a clinician who can remain fully present with a deteriorating patient, a complex case, a geopolitical crisis, an existential uncertainty — and keep receiving information, keep mentalizing, keep updating.

The body must be grounded for the mind to remain open. That is the whole system, in one sentence.

*The heart continues its conversation with the brain — forty-two signals per second, each one contributing to the architecture of what can and cannot be held. At 03:14, with the pressure dropping and three monitors alarming, the clinician who has been training that conversation shows up differently. Not calmer. More present.*

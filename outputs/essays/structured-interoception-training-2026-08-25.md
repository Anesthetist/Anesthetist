# Notes from the Lab: The Three-Mode Architecture — What Structured Interoception Training Actually Is

*Interoceptive attention training — when isolated from its cognitive mindfulness wrapper — produces superior autonomic regulation, which is why the three-mode SIT architecture targets sensory restoration, resonance entrainment, and hormetic CO₂ tolerance as distinct trainable systems rather than blending them into a single undifferentiated practice.*

---

## The Observation

Picture hour eight of a ten-case day. Not the cumulative fatigue — that's measurable, expected, trackable. The more interesting phenomenon is what happens to the body's perceptual field. Peripheral awareness has contracted. The diaphragm no longer drops past the sixth rib. The sixty seconds between room four and room five gets spent refreshing a phone screen rather than resetting a nervous system. The body is still there, technically functional, but the clinician has stopped reading it.

This contraction has a mechanism. It is not metaphorical. The insular cortex — the neural substrate where body signals become body awareness — has progressively deprioritized internal signals in favor of the external monitoring demands that define a surgical day. The result is not relaxation. It is a narrowed operator.

The question Structured Interoception Training (SIT) addresses is architecturally specific: which training modalities actually reverse this contraction, and in what sequence?

---

## The Mechanism

In 2015, Delgado-Pastor and colleagues at the University of Granada ran a study that deserves more attention than it has received. They recruited 45 female participants with chronic worry and split them into three groups: mindfulness *cognitive* training (metacognitive attention to thought processes), mindfulness *interoceptive* training (attention to physiological sensation), and a non-intervention control.

Both training groups showed equivalent improvement on self-report measures — mindfulness, symptom reduction, perceived stress. There the equivalence ended. Only the interoceptive training group showed significant improvement in physiological indices of autonomic regulation: heart rate variability, respiratory sinus arrhythmia, and resting heart rate. The cognitive group moved the questionnaires but not the physiology. The interoceptive group moved both.

Delgado-Pastor et al. (2015) [PMID: 25912677] didn't claim interoceptive training is always superior. They isolated a dissociation: the body's measurement system responds to a specific type of attention, not to attention in general. This finding is the empirical anchor for why SIT separates its three modes architecturally rather than blending them into a single practice.

**Mode 1: Interoceptive Attention Training** is sensory channel restoration. Clinical training systematically deprioritizes body signals — the educational mechanism behind the [[interoceptive-suppression-hypothesis]]. Body-scan practice, somatic signal differentiation, and rhythm-tracking exercises rebuild the perceptual infrastructure that formal training quietly dismantled. The specific outcome target is MAIA-2 subscores — particularly "Noticing," "Attention Regulation," and "Body Listening." These are not wellness markers. They are the precondition for Modes 2 and 3 to work.

**Mode 2: Breath-Paced Autonomic Training** is resonance entrainment. When respiration is paced at the individual's resonant frequency (approximately 4.5–6.5 breaths per minute, but precisely individualized), the heart-lung-baroreflex loop locks into resonance and produces the largest possible HRV amplitude. Sakakibara (2022) [PMID: 35579767] demonstrated that pacing at the low-frequency spectral peak — the individual's exact resonant point rather than a generic 0.1 Hz target — increases baroreflex sensitivity more than standard paced breathing alone. This is titration, not protocol compliance.

The evidence base for Mode 2 is the most mature of the three. Lehrer and colleagues (2020) [PMID: 32385728] analyzed 58 randomized controlled studies of HRV biofeedback and found a significant, small-to-moderate effect size across outcomes, with the largest effects for anxiety, depression, anger, and performance — and non-zero effect sizes even when compared to active control conditions. This is not a literature suggesting "some benefit." It is a literature showing dose-specific autonomic response to a trainable stimulus.

For healthcare professionals specifically, Cantone and colleagues (2026) [PMID: 41918582] reviewed 12 studies of biofeedback interventions in healthcare workers and found consistent improvements in perceived stress, anxiety, depressive symptoms, emotional regulation, and resilience. Physiological benefits included increased HRV, decreased sympathetic arousal, and improved autonomic balance. A single session is enough to register: Mazgelytė and colleagues (2022) [PMID: 36546965] showed that a twelve-minute respiratory biofeedback session produced significant decreases in salivary cortisol, heart rate, respiratory rate, and skin conductance, alongside improved self-reported fatigue and mood.

**Mode 3: Graded Interoceptive Exposure** applies the [[hormesis-durability-model]] to the autonomic system. CO₂ tolerance drills, deliberate air hunger protocols, and cold-exposure sequences are not general stress exposure — they are graduated challenges to the chemoreflex that force adaptation when properly dosed and followed by recovery. The mechanism is the same one Mattson documented for neural tissue: well-dosed intermittent stress, combined with adequate recovery and reflective integration, accelerates the adaptation process that makes the clinician more durable in subsequent exposures.

One confound requires explicit statement: BOLT scores (a wellness-facing proxy for CO₂ tolerance derived from a breath hold after exhalation) did not predict exercise performance in elite athletes. Kowalski and colleagues (2024) [PMID: 39290618] found no significant correlations between BOLT scores and Wingate or cardiopulmonary exercise test results in 49 elite speedskaters. The training value of Mode 3 appears to reside in the stimulus and adaptation process, not in the metric. High BOLT score is not the goal; trained chemoreflex flexibility is.

## The Protocol

SIT is not three exercises. It is a sequenced architecture with a distinct purpose for each mode.

**Mode 1** (5–10 minutes daily, morning): Body-scan + somatic signal differentiation. Attention moves from gross boundary awareness (am I in my body?) to fine-grained signal reading (diaphragm rhythm, cardiac texture, muscular tension gradient from jaw to pelvic floor). This is the channel restoration phase — it must precede Mode 2, because you cannot entrain what you cannot yet detect.

**Mode 2** (5–10 minutes, between cases or at shift transition): Resonant breathing at individually-mapped frequency, with HRV feedback via wearable. Not a relaxation protocol. A state-transition mechanism. The target signal is not subjective calm but measurable HRV amplitude increase. Each session is a [[neurominute]]-structured experiment: context → protocol → physiological response.

**Mode 3** (60 seconds to 5 minutes, outside clinical hours only): Graded breath holds, air hunger, progressive chemoreflex loading. Performed during deliberate practice windows — not between cases. Mode 3 requires recovery time to produce adaptation. Recovery-compressed application defeats the mechanism and may impair performance in subsequent cases.

---

## The Failure Mode

The most common misapplication is blending without architecture. A CRNA taught "mindful breathing" as a unified practice gets intermittent cognitive benefit and negligible physiological change. Delgado-Pastor's dissociation is the prediction: cognitive attention and interoceptive attention are separable mechanisms, and mixing them at low intensity reaches neither target reliably.

The second failure mode is sequencing inversion. Mode 2 without Mode 1 is attempting to entrain a signal the practitioner cannot yet detect. Clinicians with high interoceptive suppression scores frequently report that resonant breathing "doesn't work" — because the sensory channel required to feel the cardiac response has been deprioritized by years of clinical suppression. Mode 1 must run first.

The third failure mode is confusing the score with the stimulus. Treating BOLT score as a performance predictor in elite-caliber clinicians imports the same confusion Kowalski documented in athletes. The goal of Mode 3 is not a high BOLT number. It is a nervous system with trained flexibility in its chemoreflex response — measurable via the delta between resting BOLT and post-training BOLT over a 90-day curve, not as a single-point performance indicator.

## The Test

Minimum viable experiment: 14 days, 12 CRNAs, three conditions.

- **Group A:** Mode 1 only (daily body scan, MAIA-2 tracking)
- **Group B:** Mode 2 only (resonant breathing + wearable HRV feedback)
- **Group C:** Full three-mode SIT (Mode 1 morning, Mode 2 between cases, Mode 3 twice weekly outside clinical hours)

Primary outcomes: morning RMSSD baseline, MAIA-2 "Noticing" and "Body Listening" subscores, BOLT (as adaptation tracker, not performance predictor). The test is designed to detect mode-specific effect profiles. If all three conditions produce equivalent RMSSD change, the three-mode architecture is not justified. If Group C significantly outperforms single-mode conditions, the architecture is validated.

Success threshold: Group C RMSSD increase ≥15% vs. Group A or B at day 14.

## The Connection

SIT is the operational specification beneath [[gap-moment-training]]. GMT is the framework and delivery brand. SIT is what actually runs under the hood. Without mode separation, you cannot titrate the training to the specific clinical gap the individual presents. A clinician with collapsed Mode 1 needs channel restoration before resonance training can work. A clinician with intact Mode 1 but no Mode 3 adaptation can detect state but cannot hold regulated function under chemoreflex challenge. The three-mode architecture makes the diagnosis possible — and the NeuroMinute format makes it deliverable in the 60-second windows that define a surgical day.

---

*Somewhere in the body is a rhythm that predates consciousness — the baroreflex arc, the three-second loop from blood pressure change to heart rate response, running since before the cortex had an opinion. SIT is not teaching something new. It is restoring access to what was always there.*

# Notes from the Lab: Not Seat Time — The SRB-60 and What It Means to Be Certified in Your Own Nervous System

*The Somnistics Readiness Battery (SRB-60) is the first assessment standard that distinguishes interoceptive competency from interoceptive exposure — requiring a clinician to produce a measurable physiological state-change in 60 seconds, under realistic clinical load, before any certificate changes hands.*

## The Observation

In 2009, the Presidential Physical Fitness Award was replaced. The original test — pull-ups, sit-ups, a timed mile — had been criticized for measuring fitness norms rather than health. The replacement, FitnessGram, tested aerobic capacity, muscular strength, and flexibility against standards linked to actual health outcomes. The distinction mattered: one measured where you stood relative to peers; the other measured whether your body could sustain the specific physiological functions health requires.

Wellness certification in healthcare has never made this distinction. The standard is seat time: eight hours of continuing education earns a certificate, regardless of whether anything changed in the clinician's body. A CRNA can sit through a breathwork module in a state of sympathetic arousal from the parking lot, sign the attendance sheet, and leave with a credential. The credential says they were exposed. It says nothing about whether they absorbed, integrated, or can now demonstrate what was taught.

The Somnistics Readiness Battery (SRB-60) is built to close this gap. It is the presidential fitness test for the nervous system — not comparing clinicians to peers, but measuring whether they can perform the specific physiological act the training claims to install.

## The Mechanism

Five domains comprise the SRB-60, and each targets a distinct physiological layer that existing wellness certifications ignore entirely.

**Domain 1: Gap Moment Literacy** tests temporal perception and behavioral choice. A gap moment — the 60-second window between tasks or cases — is the training opportunity the somnistics curriculum is built around. Detecting it requires what Mehling and colleagues developed the MAIA to measure: the distinction between raw interoceptive awareness ("Noticing") and the capacity to direct attention toward internal signals without reactivity ("Attention Regulation"). Mehling et al. established in 2012, when they validated the original MAIA, that interoceptive awareness is not a single trait but at minimum eight distinguishable components — and that measuring them requires separating perception from emotional reactivity, attention regulation from body listening Mehling et al. (2012) [PMID: 23133619]. Domain 1 tests whether a clinician has the perceptual architecture to see a gap moment as a training opportunity rather than dead time to fill with scrolling.

**Domain 2: Micro-Interoceptive Skill** tests the capacity to sense, label, and calibrate internal signals under time pressure. This is trainable — not a fixed trait. Rogowska et al. confirmed in their 2023 validation of the Brief MAIA-2 that athletes score significantly higher than non-athletes in Noticing, Attention Regulation, Emotional Awareness, Self-Regulation, and Body Listening, suggesting physical training systematically improves interoceptive capacity Rogowska et al. (2023) [PMID: 38042880]. The implication for SRB-60 design is that Domain 2 skill is not a baseline lottery; it is a trainable outcome with measurable before-and-after trajectories.

**Domain 3: Protocol Selection and Fidelity** tests whether the clinician can match intervention to state — the pharmacology principle of titration to effect applied to autonomic regulation. This requires knowing one's individual resonant frequency. Vaschillo, Vaschillo, and Lehrer established in 2006 that resonant frequency is individually determined — negatively correlated with height, lower in men than women — and critically, it remains constant across ten training sessions once established Vaschillo et al. (2006) [PMID: 16838124]. This matters for assessment design: an examiner can verify that a trainee is breathing at a frequency appropriate to their own physiology, not simply guessing at 5.5 breaths per minute.

**Domain 4: Measured State Delta** is the pass/fail criterion no wellness certification has ever deployed. It requires the clinician to produce a measurable physiological shift — HRV increase, RMSSD elevation, or respiration rate change — within the 60-second window. The literature supports the feasibility of this standard. Sakakibara, Kaneda, and Oikawa demonstrated in 2020 that five minutes of paced breathing at individual resonant frequency significantly increased both RMSSD and baroreflex sensitivity in healthy adults Sakakibara et al. (2020) [PMID: 31781925]. Liu et al. confirmed in 2025 that a single 15-minute session of resonance breathing produced significant post-intervention elevations in RMSSD and RR interval Liu et al. (2025) [PMID: 40304861]. If a physiological shift is reliably detectable in the literature within five minutes, the detection problem is solved. The remaining design question is whether a clinically meaningful shift is detectable within one minute — which is precisely what the SRB-60 asks.

**Domain 5: Return-to-Task Readiness** separates somnistics from meditation. A meditator's gold standard is often deepened rest — lowered arousal, entry into alpha-theta states. A clinician's gold standard is something different: restored executive function, sharpened attentional focus, re-engagement with high-complexity work. Domain 5 requires the trainee to demonstrate that the intervention produced regulatory recovery, not sedation. This is a competency no existing wellness certification assesses, because no existing wellness certification was designed for a clinician who cannot afford to be drowsy between cases.

## The Protocol

The SRB-60 is organized around 60-second events, matching the NeuroMinute design space. Five domains are assessed across standardized scenarios representing realistic clinical transition moments: post-induction, case transfer, pre-emergence, between cases.

Tier progression — Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum — tracks not just accuracy but consistency and transfer. Bronze certification requires basic competency across all five domains. Silver requires consistent performance with measurable state deltas. Gold requires rapid, accurate protocol selection with high execution fidelity. Platinum requires expert-level performance appropriate for instructor candidacy.

Assessment occurs under realistic cognitive load: background case complexity, time pressure, and environmental distractors consistent with a clinical environment. The examiner uses a wearable HRV monitor to verify Domain 4 compliance in real time. Domain 2 accuracy is assessed through validated interoceptive tasks drawn from the MAIA-2 subscale battery. Domain 5 is assessed through return-to-task performance benchmarks — specifically, accuracy on a standardized clinical decision task administered within 90 seconds of protocol completion.

## The Failure Mode

The SRB-60 has four specific failure conditions that warrant naming, because each produces a false pass:

**Sedation masquerade.** A trainee who lowers arousal but fails to restore executive function may show adequate HRV shift while exhibiting impaired attention on return-to-task measures. The battery catches this because Domain 5 is scored separately from Domain 4.

**Gaming with chest breathing.** Slow chest breathing can produce a superficial HRV response that mimics baroreflex engagement without true resonance coupling. An experienced examiner looks for breath depth and diaphragmatic engagement pattern, not respiration rate alone.

**Assessment anxiety inflation.** Pre-assessment sympathetic activation depresses baseline HRV, making even a modest recovery appear as a large delta. The battery standardizes a 5-minute baseline recovery window before Domain 4 assessment begins.

**Time-of-day effects.** HRV exhibits circadian variation, with generally higher parasympathetic tone in morning resting states. Standardizing testing windows across all tiers controls for this confound.

The Starvaggi et al. competency-based training study from 2026 demonstrated a related principle: when 78% of surgical trainees reached a 90% performance standard on radiograph interpretation, the assessment revealed who needed more practice — and what kind Starvaggi et al. (2026) [PMID: 42620904]. The SRB-60 is designed with the same philosophy: failure is diagnostic, not disqualifying. A Bronze failure tells the examiner which domain failed, which tells the trainer what the next cycle of practice must address.

## The Test

**Minimum viable experiment:** 20 CRNAs completing the somnistics foundational curriculum. Pre/post MAIA-2 (8 subscales). Wearable HRV monitoring during a single Bronze certification assessment.

**Primary outcome:** Percentage of trainees producing a statistically significant RMSSD elevation during the Domain 4 window, compared to pre-assessment baseline.

**Secondary outcomes:** MAIA-2 subscale shifts pre/post curriculum; correlation between Domain 2 score and Domain 4 state-delta magnitude; Domain 5 return-to-task performance against a standardized cognitive accuracy benchmark.

**Falsification criterion:** If fewer than 60% of curriculum completers demonstrate measurable Domain 4 state-delta at Bronze standard, the 60-second window is too compressed for reliable biometric verification — and the domain requires redesign: either extending the window or substituting a more sensitive biometric proxy (respiratory coherence index, skin conductance, or prefrontal oxygenation via fNIRS).

Required resources: 20 participants, 2 assessment days (pre/post), 1 wearable HRV device per participant, 1 standardized cognitive battery for Domain 5, 1 trained examiner. Timeline: 14 weeks curriculum plus 2 assessment days. Cost: equipment, not investigator salary. This is a 14-day proof test, not a Phase III trial.

## The Connection

The SRB-60 connects three things the somnistics system has been building toward.

It makes interoceptive competency **certifiable** — as auditable as BLS recertification, as skill-specific as an intubation check-off. The credential shifts from "attended training" to "demonstrated performance."

It makes the clinical claim **defensible** — not "we trained 50 CRNAs in autonomic regulation," but "47 of 50 achieved measurable Bronze standard performance, with an average RMSSD delta of X milliseconds in the 60-second assessment window."

It makes the vault **testable** — every concept linked to the SRB-60 (gap-moment-literacy, diaphragmatic-literacy, interoceptive-literacy, neurominute, clinician-durability) now has a performance proxy. The battery doesn't merely assess the clinician; it validates the training architecture. If Domain 2 scores don't improve after curriculum completion, the interoceptive training content is the variable to address. If Domain 4 deltas are present but Domain 5 fails, the return-to-task protocols need redesign.

What the SRB-60 changes about how we understand the system: it closes the loop between claim and evidence. The somnistics curriculum has always claimed to install specific physiological capacities. The SRB-60 is the instrument that makes that claim falsifiable — and falsifiability, as any scientist who has spent time in clinical practice knows, is the difference between a training program and a science.

---

*The certification is not a certificate. It is a protocol run to completion, the delta measured, the return confirmed — and then the next case begins.*

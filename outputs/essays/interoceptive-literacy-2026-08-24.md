# Notes from the Lab: Interoceptive Literacy — Reading the Body's Language Before the Monitor Shows It

*Interoceptive literacy is not interoceptive awareness: where awareness is the raw sense of the body, literacy is the trained metacognitive capacity to accurately detect internal signals, interpret their clinical meaning, and know when your body-read is trustworthy — three demonstrably dissociable skills that mark the difference between a felt sense that something has shifted and a calibrated physiological read that drives the right decision before the monitor confirms it.*

## The Observation

Twenty-eight out of thirty. That is how many certified anesthesia professionals — CRNAs and physician anesthesiologists — could not consciously engage their diaphragm during a simple test: slowest possible nasal inhale. They used accessory muscles. They topped out at four seconds. They had no awareness this was happening. Two exceptions: a singer and a trumpet player — people who had deliberately trained their breath through their art, not through medicine.

These are people who breathe for other people. They manage ventilators with fractional precision, titrating tidal volumes and respiratory rates to specific clinical targets for unconscious patients. And they cannot feel their own primary breathing muscle engage on command.

This is the ground truth finding of interoceptive literacy research applied to anesthesia: the gap is not physiological but cortical. The diaphragmatic capacity exists. The trained pathway — from conscious intention to diaphragmatic engagement and back — has never been built in 28 of 30 practitioners.

That gap in the simplest possible interoceptive task raises a harder question about the complex ones. If a CRNA cannot detect their own diaphragm, what are the odds they can accurately detect subtler signals — the 15bpm drift in heart rate between cases, the rising tension that precedes cognitive narrowing, the shift in breath quality that precedes a state transition — that constitute the early-warning system for the cases where judgment matters most?

## The Mechanism

In 2015, Garfinkel, Seth, Barrett, Suzuki, and Critchley published what has become the foundational framework for precision work in this field Garfinkel et al. (2015) [PMID: 25451381]. In a normative sample of 80 adults, they demonstrated that what is casually called "interoception" is not a single trait but three dissociable dimensions:

**Interoceptive accuracy** — objective performance on a heartbeat detection task. Can you actually detect your body's signals correctly?

**Interoceptive sensibility** — self-evaluated assessment via questionnaire. Do you believe you can? Do you have a dispositional orientation toward body awareness?

**Interoceptive awareness** — the metacognitive layer. Do you know when your body-reading is accurate versus when it is off? Does your confidence track your actual performance?

Garfinkel et al. found all three dimensions to be distinct and dissociable. Interoceptive accuracy was only partially predicted by sensibility or awareness. Significant correspondence between dimensions emerged only within the sub-group of individuals with the greatest interoceptive accuracy — suggesting that metacognitive calibration is itself a function of underlying performance, not independent of it.

For clinical performance contexts, this dissociation is not a theoretical nicety. It creates a specific failure mode: a CRNA who scores high on interoceptive sensibility — who believes they are highly attuned to their physiological state — may simultaneously have low interoceptive accuracy. They are confident in a body-read that is systematically miscalibrated.

Medical education, as an institution, produces exactly this profile. The hidden curriculum trains suppression of body signals — hold the bladder, push through fatigue, suppress emotional distress — while maintaining a self-concept of high professional competence. The result is not absence of sensibility. CRNAs typically report high general awareness of their states. The result is decoupling of sensibility from accuracy: the felt sense of attunement persists while the physiological measurement capacity erodes.

Mehling, Acree, Stewart, Silas, and Jones developed the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness, Version 2 (MAIA-2) as the instrument that maps this territory Mehling et al. (2018) [PMID: 30513087]. The 37-item, 8-subscale self-report tool — public domain, designed for research and evaluation of mind-body interventions — operationalizes the literacy concept across four functional domains: detection (Noticing, Attention Regulation), interpretation (Emotional Awareness, Body Listening), response (Self-Regulation), and trust (Trusting, Not-Worrying, Not-Distracting). Each subscale is independently scored. There is no assumption that a practitioner who scores high on Noticing will score proportionally on Trusting or Body Listening. The instrument is designed for exactly the differentiated profiling that the Garfinkel et al. findings require.

The neural mechanism for building these capacities runs through the primary somatosensory cortex. Kerr, Sacchet, Lazar, Moore, and Jones (2013) proposed, with computational modeling support, a framework in which body-focused mindfulness training — specifically attention to breath and body sensations — trains top-down modulation of alpha rhythms (7-14 Hz) in the somatosensory cortex Kerr et al. (2013) [PMID: 23408771]. Alpha rhythms function as a gating mechanism for incoming sensory information: when top-down alpha modulation is refined, the practitioner gains increasingly precise control over which internal signals reach conscious processing. This is the neural substrate of interoceptive literacy development — not the acquisition of new signals but the refinement of the cortical filter that determines which signals become consciously available.

Milleville et al.'s 2025 qualitative study of ten CRNAs in France provides the clinical endpoint Milleville et al. (2025) [PMID: 41329173]. Using semistructured interviews analyzed via Braun and Clarke's thematic analysis framework, Milleville et al. identified psychological and emotional states — stress, tiredness, emotional load — as self-reported factors affecting cognitive performance in the operating room. The study also found that experience was the primary enabler of rapid intuitive decision-making. The literature suggests interoceptive literacy is the mechanism underlying this expertise: what clinicians call intuition is, in significant part, the trained capacity to detect and correctly interpret internal physiological signals that carry information about both patient state and personal cognitive load. The gap is that no current CRNA training program systematically teaches this mechanism. It is acquired ad hoc or not at all.

## The Framework

Interoceptive literacy is not a single skill and cannot be trained as one. Based on the Garfinkel et al. dimensional model and the Mehling MAIA-2 architecture, the functional sequence has four independently trainable layers:

1. **Detection** — The capacity to notice internal signals accurately. Not "I feel tired" but "my heart rate has drifted 12bpm above my case-entry baseline." MAIA-2 subscales: Noticing, Attention Regulation. Training target: amplifying bottom-up signal clarity through focused interoceptive attention.

2. **Interpretation** — Reading what detected signals mean in this specific clinical context. Not "I notice my heart rate" but "I recognize this as sympathetic activation carried over from the last induction, not case-related concern about this patient." MAIA-2 subscales: Emotional Awareness, Body Listening. Training target: building a personal physiological vocabulary, cross-referenced against accumulated pattern recognition.

3. **Response** — Acting on the read. A detection plus interpretation that generates no response is information lost. MAIA-2 subscale: Self-Regulation. Training target: building the habitual pathway from signal-read to protocol-activation within the available workflow.

4. **Trust calibration** — The metacognitive layer: knowing when your body-read is reliable and when it is degraded. In a CRNA in hour seven of a complex case under elevated cognitive load, interoceptive accuracy degrades before sensibility does — the clinician continues feeling confident in their reads while the reads themselves become less accurate. MAIA-2 subscales: Trusting, Not-Worrying. Training target: developing awareness of conditions under which personal body-reads should be weighted differently.

## The Failure Mode

Two specific failure modes deserve naming.

The first is the sensibility-accuracy dissociation: high self-rated attunement with systematically low actual accuracy. This is the medical training artifact described above. It is likely the dominant profile among experienced CRNAs who have received no systematic interoceptive training. Its clinical danger is that it is invisible from the inside — the practitioner's felt sense of competence in body-reading remains intact while their accuracy has eroded.

The second failure mode is subtler and may be more clinically consequential: accurate detection in low-demand conditions that does not transfer under cognitive load. High interoceptive accuracy at rest does not guarantee accuracy during a complex case. The Kerr et al. alpha-rhythm framework suggests this directly: competitive attentional demands under load reduce the top-down gating resources available for interoceptive processing. The practitioner who has built genuine interoceptive literacy in training conditions may not have built the load-robustness to maintain that literacy during an induction that goes sideways.

The misapplication is training interoceptive literacy as a resting skill and assuming it transfers to procedural contexts. This assumption should be tested, not made. Six variables also change under procedural load — attentional narrowing, working memory engagement, time pressure, emotional salience, noise level, team communication demands — and isolating the interoceptive accuracy reduction from these confounds requires study design that has not yet been done in this population.

## The Test

A minimum viable experiment to evaluate the three-dimensional model in CRNAs:

**Population:** 15 CRNAs, volunteer, hospital-based practice.

**Duration:** 14 days.

**Protocol:** Pre/post full MAIA-2 (8 subscales, individually scored). Heartbeat counting task (interoceptive accuracy, objective measure independent of self-report) at Days 1 and 14. Daily 60-second NeuroMinute protocol cycling across MAIA-2 subscale targets across the 14-day period.

**The key measure is the dissociation profile.** Does accuracy (heartbeat task) and sensibility (MAIA-2 Noticing) improve together or independently? Do any subscales improve without commensurate accuracy gains? If Noticing improves without heartbeat accuracy improving, the protocol is building confidence without performance — a liability, not an asset, in clinical contexts.

**Falsification criterion:** If MAIA-2 subscale improvements across all eight scales produce proportional heartbeat task accuracy gains, the Garfinkel et al. dissociability finding does not replicate in this population under this training condition, and the SRL three-layer framework requires revision. If subscales and accuracy dissociate, it confirms that CRNA interoceptive literacy training requires explicit, separate targeting of each dimension.

Price, Pike, and Merrill's 2025 twelve-month RCT of Mindful Awareness in Body-oriented Therapy (MABT) with 303 participants demonstrates that interoceptive awareness is trainable in clinical populations over time Price et al. (2025) [PMID: 40784065]. The MABT group showed significant improvements in interoceptive awareness, emotion regulation, and PTSD symptoms versus medication-only controls. This provides the prior that detectable signal at 14 days is plausible, particularly on the most accessible subscales (Noticing, Attention Regulation).

## The Connection

This essay sits at the intersection of four vault concepts. [[interoception]] provides the broader neurophysiological ground — literacy is the skilled, trained form of the capacity. [[anterocept]] is the training methodology designed to build literacy across all four layers progressively, beginning with the most basic deficit: diaphragmatic blindness. [[diaphragmatic-blindness]] is the starting point — the most fundamental literacy deficit, the failure to detect one's own primary breathing muscle, which must be corrected before higher-order literacy becomes trainable. [[clinician-durability]] is the downstream endpoint — interoceptive literacy is the biological mechanism by which CRNAs maintain self-monitoring capacity that protects against state drift and the accumulated autonomic debt that manifests as burnout.

The new contribution this essay makes to the vault: the Garfinkel et al. dissociability finding — that accuracy, sensibility, and metacognitive awareness are distinct and must be independently developed — is not explicitly incorporated into any existing concept note. This is the framework that explains why high MAIA-2 sensibility scores cannot be assumed to co-exist with high interoceptive accuracy, and why MAIA-2 as an outcome instrument requires all eight subscales rather than summary scoring. A CRNA can show excellent Self-Regulation scores while showing degraded Noticing, and a summary score would hide the gap that matters most.

---

*Between cases, one hand on the countertop, one breath through the nose — the literature suggests this moment is not recovery but calibration: the practitioner who can accurately read that breath, distinguish what it signals, and trust the read, is building something different from the one who simply pauses. That distinction, repeated across a career, is what separates wearing down from compounding.*

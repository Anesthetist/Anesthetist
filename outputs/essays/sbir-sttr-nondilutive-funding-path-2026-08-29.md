# Notes from the Lab: The Evidence That Isn't There — HRV Biofeedback, CRNA Performance, and Why the Missing Study Is the Proposal

*HRV biofeedback produces measurable improvements in autonomic resilience and executive function under acute stress in emergency and critical care workers, but no study has specifically applied this to the CRNA intraoperative context — and that precise literature gap is the argument for federal research funding, not a weakness in Somnistics' scientific case.*

---

## The Observation

An anesthesia provider in a complex case is not having an experience. The provider *is* the instrument.

Cardiac output, ventilation rate, vasoactive drug titration, depth of anesthesia — all of these are governed by a nervous system that is simultaneously monitoring, deciding, adjusting, and holding the physiological state of another human being in its working model. A four-hour case is not a four-hour task. It is a four-hour continuous physiological negotiation.

The literature is unambiguous about the toll. Mumbwe and colleagues found, in a cross-sectional survey of anesthesia providers in Zambian hospitals, a burnout rate of 51.3% by Maslach Burnout Inventory criteria — with nonphysician anesthesia providers (the category CRNAs occupy in that credentialing context) carrying elevated risk [PMID: 31725020]. In the United States, Lea and colleagues documented that burnout among CRNAs correlates directly with diminished feedback and poor administrator relationships — and that burnout in turn predicts both job dissatisfaction and turnover intent [PMID: 35343896]. The burnout problem in high-stakes anesthesia is structural, not personal.

Meanwhile, a parallel literature in psychophysiology has spent thirty years demonstrating that the autonomic nervous system is trainable. The mechanism is resonance-frequency heart rate variability biofeedback, and the clinical utility is real. What has not been done is the obvious next step: apply it to people managing open airways and vasoactive drips.

---

## The Mechanism

The foundational work belongs to Evgeny Vaschillo and Paul Lehrer at Rutgers. In 2002, Vaschillo and colleagues demonstrated that targeted biofeedback training can produce large-amplitude oscillations in heart rate and blood pressure at specific frequencies within the 0.01–0.14 Hz range. The key discovery: high-amplitude oscillations were produced consistently in the range of 0.055–0.11 Hz — the baroreflex resonance band — and the exact resonant frequency differed measurably between individuals [PMID: 12001882].

That second point has been underappreciated. Lehrer's popular shorthand of "approximately 6 breaths per minute" implied a one-size-fits-all resonance target. Vaschillo and colleagues clarified in 2006 that resonant frequency carries a negative relationship with height and is reliably lower in men than in women — but is unrelated to age, weight, or clinical condition. Across ten sessions of biofeedback training, resonant frequency remained constant within individuals [PMID: 16838124]. The frequency is stable. It is also personal.

Lehrer extended this in 2020: even the phase relationship between heart rate and breathing is not uniform. Among a sample of adults aged 18–70 with asthma, the expected in-phase relationship between cardiac interbeat interval and breathing showed age-dependent drift — younger participants maintained closer to the theoretical 180° phase relationship; older participants deviated significantly. Despite this deviation, breathing still stimulated the baroreflex and produced high-amplitude heart rate oscillations [PMID: 32285231]. Meaning: the mechanism survives population heterogeneity, even when the parameter expression varies.

As Lehrer summarized in a 2022 review of his career's work: the most reproducible finding is that resonance frequency breathing training increases baroreflex gain and parasympathetic tone, and that this gain transfers to clinical benefit in conditions as different as asthma and depression [PMID: 35254592].

What does that baroreflex gain produce under cognitive load?

Schlatter and colleagues demonstrated this in a 2021 randomized controlled trial (n=80). Participants were subjected to anticipatory stress induction, then randomized to one of four conditions: neutral video, transcranial direct current stimulation, HRV biofeedback, or combined. Biofeedback reduced physiological stress — measured by heart rate, HRV, and salivary cortisol — and, critically, improved executive function assessments administered immediately after the stressor [PMID: 34438047]. The mechanism is real, measurable, and transferable to performance under acute load.

The clinical translation arrived in 2026. Cortez-Neavel and colleagues completed the first randomized controlled trial of HRV biofeedback specifically in emergency department and intensive care unit healthcare workers — the population whose stress physiology is closest to the CRNA context. Participants (n=37) received 4 weeks of HRVB (30-minute protocol introduction, then 10 minutes twice daily of self-guided practice) or waitlist control. Resilience, measured by the CD-RISC-10, increased significantly in the HRVB group relative to waitlist at posttreatment (Cohen's d = 0.79, p = .003). Gains were maintained at 4-week follow-up [PMID: 41734245].

Six variables were at work simultaneously in that study: the intervention content, its dosage, the measurement instrument, the clinical environment, the population's specific stressors, and the timing of practice relative to shift work. I am isolating one: the mechanism works in real clinical workers, in real acute-care environments, with a real-world minimum dosage. The confound I am explicitly not resolving: Cortez-Neavel's participants practiced outside their clinical shifts. The CRNA intraoperative context — sustained autonomic load during cases — is not what the existing evidence tested.

## The Protocol

Federal Small Business Innovation Research Phase I funding is not a grant for confirmatory research. NSF Digital Health SBIR specifically targets "novel devices, components, systems, algorithms, or services that enable transformation of healthcare from reactive to proactive, person-centered, preventive, and cost-efficient." The word "novel" is operative.

The novelty in a Somnistics SBIR proposal is not the mechanism. The mechanism is established. The novelty is the application architecture: HRV biofeedback delivered as a pre-shift protocol specifically designed to modulate intraoperative autonomic trajectory in CRNAs — with the biometric outcome measured during the case itself, not just before or after it.

A feasibility-oriented Phase I study design:

1. **Participants:** 10–15 CRNAs, single practice site, cases of 2+ hours duration
2. **Intervention:** 14-day structured pre-shift training in resonance-frequency breathing (10 minutes, before first case of each shift)
3. **Biomarkers:** RMSSD and BOLT (Body Oxygen Level Test) at pre/post training period; MAIA-2 subscales (Noticing, Not-Distracting, Body Listening) as secondary interoceptive outcome
4. **Intraoperative monitoring:** Wearable cardiac monitor during cases — passive, non-interruptive, beat-to-beat IBI recording
5. **Primary outcome:** RMSSD slope during the case (decline from case-open to 2-hour mark) at baseline vs. post-training period
6. **Secondary outcome:** Self-reported near-miss event frequency (clinical incident journal, 2-week washout)
7. **Success criterion:** Attenuated RMSSD decline slope in trained period relative to baseline
8. **Failure criterion:** No measurable change in intraoperative RMSSD pattern after training

The biometric architecture is what makes this an SBIR proposal rather than a continuing education program. The wearable doesn't interrupt the case. It reads the system.

## The Failure Mode

Cortez-Neavel's 2026 trial contains the failure mode, buried in the results. Resilience (CD-RISC-10) improved significantly. Stress, depression, and burnout — by pre-specified measures — did not differ significantly between HRVB and waitlist [PMID: 41734245]. Only 21% of HRVB participants showed reliable change at posttreatment.

This is not a problem with the intervention. It is a precision problem. Resilience is a trait measure; burnout is a structural consequence of workplace conditions. A 4-week biofeedback protocol does not correct unsafe staffing ratios. What it appears to do is shift the autonomic baseline from which individual clinicians enter high-load situations — a shift that matters at the margins where performance lives and error hides.

Three specific failure modes to design around:

**Dose inadequacy.** Ten minutes twice daily was Cortez-Neavel's minimum viable dose. In a 60-hour clinical week, compliance with twice-daily practice is a realistic barrier. A pre-shift-only protocol (one practice episode, 5–10 minutes) needs its own feasibility data.

**Transfer failure.** Training on a stationary biofeedback protocol does not automatically produce intraoperative regulation. The skill has to generalize to an environment with competing attentional demands. Gap Moment Training protocols address this explicitly — the 60-second interoceptive check during case transitions — but that generalization mechanism itself needs validation.

**Individual resonance variance.** Vaschillo's finding that resonant frequency varies by height, sex, and blood volume means group-average 6 BPM protocols may be training some participants at sub-resonant frequencies. A Phase I design that does not include individual resonant frequency assessment before training is measuring the intervention's average effect across misdosed participants.

---

## The Test

Fourteen days. Ten CRNAs. One wearable.

Baseline week: No intervention. Wearable captures intraoperative RMSSD trajectory across all cases of 2+ hours. BOLT and MAIA-2 administered at start and end of week.

Training weeks 1–2: Pre-shift 10-minute resonance-frequency breathing protocol. Individual resonant frequency assessed in session 1 via the Lehrer/Vaschillo standard breathing maneuver. Wearable continues during all cases.

Outcome: Does the RMSSD trajectory during cases in week 2 differ in slope from baseline week? Does BOLT score change? Do MAIA-2 Noticing and Body Listening subscales shift?

This is not a clinical trial. It is a signal detection study. Its purpose is not to prove that HRV biofeedback works in CRNAs — the mechanism literature already establishes plausibility. Its purpose is to ask: is there a measurable intraoperative autonomic signal to amplify? If yes, the signal is the target, and the Phase I proposal is the instrument for hitting it.

---

## The Connection

The SBIR/STTR pathway is not a funding strategy that happens to require clinical validation. It is a forcing function that converts a positioning argument — Somnistics improves clinical autonomic resilience — into a scientific claim with measurement architecture, success criteria, and failure conditions.

The clinical validation gap that appears as a liability in investor conversations is, reframed, the research question. The research question is, precisely structured, the grant proposal. The grant proposal, if funded, is non-dilutive capital that validates both the science and the market simultaneously.

Red Arrow 2 — Consumer Traction to Enterprise Proof — runs through this study.

*Somewhere in the second hour of a long case, the clinician's intraoperative HRV begins its characteristic decline. That is the signal. The question federal research funding requires answering is: can it be trained to decline less steeply?*

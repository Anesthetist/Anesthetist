# Notes from the Lab: The Adaptive Loop — Why Fixed Biofeedback Protocols Fail and What Replaces Them

*A closed-loop biofeedback architecture that continuously reads HRV state and adapts resonance-breathing guidance in real time is the necessary and technically feasible upgrade from fixed-protocol systems, because compliance failure in healthcare workers stems not from motivation but from autonomic non-responsiveness — the mismatch between what a fixed protocol prescribes and what a nervous system in its current physiological condition can receive.*

---

## The Observation

The CRNA finishes three consecutive emergency inductions in a four-hour window. Cortisol is elevated. RMSSD has been falling steadily since hour two. She sits down in the break room — four minutes before the next case — opens a biofeedback app, and is told to breathe in for five seconds, out for five seconds. The protocol is the same as yesterday, the same as last week. It does not know she just came out of a trauma activation. It cannot feel what she can barely feel herself. She does three cycles, notices nothing, closes the app, and goes back to the OR.

This is not a discipline failure. This is a mismatch problem.

The protocol prescribed what a rested nervous system at resonance could receive. What arrived was a system under load, with suppressed baroreflex sensitivity and compressed autonomic flexibility. For that system, five seconds in / five seconds out is the physiological equivalent of asking someone in anaphylaxis to take a gentle walk. The prescription is right in theory. The state is the problem.

---

## The Mechanism

Flow state and HRV share a physiological substrate. Harmat, de Manzano, Theorell, and colleagues at the Karolinska Institutet demonstrated that higher flow — the state of effortless, absorbed optimal performance — is associated with increased parasympathetic tone and greater respiratory depth during computer game play. Harmat et al. (2015) found lower low-frequency HRV power and deeper breathing in optimal (flow) task conditions versus under- or over-challenge, consistent with the dual activation hypothesis: sympathetic arousal provides alertness, parasympathetic engagement provides the cognitive fluency that flow requires. [PMID: 25956190]

This matters because it tells us the target. The Interoceptive Flow OS is not building toward relaxation. It is building toward the physiological signature of flow: integrated activation, deepened respiratory mechanics, coherent cardiac variability. "Coherence per minute" — how many minutes of each shift the nervous system spends in this coordinated state — is the operational KPI, not session completion.

Resonance breathing creates the conditions for this signature. Liu, Zheng, and Wang demonstrated in a randomized crossover design that a single session of resonance breathing significantly increases RMSSD and RR interval while producing post-intervention reductions in sympathetic activity as measured by skin sympathetic nerve activity (SKNA). Liu et al. (2025) provide the key mechanism: baroreflex resonance through the heart rate-baroreflex pathway amplifies vagal tone with each breath cycle at the individual resonance frequency, producing a cascade shift in the autonomic balance. [PMID: 40304861] The question is not whether resonance breathing works — it does. The question is whether it works when you need it most.

Burlacu, Brinza, and colleagues at the University of Medicine in Iași proposed the concept of "autonomic non-responsiveness" in a 2026 narrative review: situations in which expected autonomic engagement is weakened, absent, or fails to produce meaningful clinical benefit during HRV biofeedback. Burlacu et al. (2026) identified reduced autonomic flexibility, impaired baroreflex function, fatigue, stress-related overload, and dysfunctional breathing as contributors — precisely the constellation that defines a post-surge clinical break. Their analysis proposed "minimally adaptive closed-loop systems" as the most promising research direction: systems that recognize when the protocol is not landing and adjust. [PMID: 42356115]

The question of individual calibration complicates this. Sumińska, Rynkiewicz, and Szulczewski conducted a four-week randomized trial comparing individually calibrated resonance frequency breathing against fixed 0.1 Hz training in 88 participants. Sumińska et al. (2026) found no meaningful difference between groups on stress, anxiety, depressive symptoms, or resting HRV — Bayesian analysis provided anecdotal evidence favoring the null hypothesis. [PMID: 42156977] This seems to argue against personalization, but the study measured a one-time baseline calibration held constant for four weeks, not real-time continuous adaptation. A resonance frequency identified Monday morning tells you nothing about a nervous system under Thursday afternoon surgical load. Calibration measured once is not closed-loop.

The architecture that addresses this is technically validated. Srinivasan, Kumar, and Meenatchi Sundaram at Manipal Academy of Higher Education built and technically validated a multimodal emotion-adaptive biofeedback system that uses a two-stage adaptive respiration rate algorithm: the first stage gradually guides the user toward their resonance frequency; the second stage dynamically responds to signs of emotional discomfort — detected through electrodermal activity — and adjusts the target rate accordingly. Srinivasan et al. (2026) demonstrated that the system produced significant increases in HRV and decreases in respiratory rate and electrodermal activity, consistent with increased parasympathetic influence, while maintaining the technical validity required for a scalable foundation. [PMID: 41922620] This is not a concept — it is a working system with validated sensor agreement and adaptive logic.

The performance consequences of getting this right are concrete. Toloraya, Kovaleva, Belousov, and Andreeva at the Federal Research Center in Moscow studied one week of home-based HRV biofeedback combined with supervised resonance-frequency sessions in high-level martial arts athletes across karate, wushu, taekwondo, and kyokushinkai. Toloraya et al. (2026) found significant improvements in movement oscillation frequency, blood volume pulse, and — critically — expert-rated kata performance scores, with no change in the control group. [PMID: 41745654] Autonomic regulation training, delivered correctly, changes expert performance in high-skill bodies. CRNAs are high-skill bodies under comparable demands.

Compliance is where this entire architecture lives or dies. Hirten, Danieletto, and a team at Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai enrolled 127 healthcare workers across seven New York City hospitals in a fully remote HRV biofeedback program — five minutes per day, five weeks, smartphone-guided with Apple Watch monitoring. Hirten et al. (2024) found that only 16.5% of participants reached 50% compliance. Among those who did comply, SDNN increased over the first four weeks. The intervention was effective when used; it almost never was. The paper identifies "study design" as the culprit — but the deeper issue is that a fixed protocol meeting a variably receptive nervous system produces exactly the disconnection the CRNA in the break room experiences. [PMID: 38663011]

## The Framework

The Interoceptive Flow OS is not an app that adds biofeedback. It is an operating system layer that continuously monitors autonomic state and makes the protocol decision the body used to make unilaterally.

Four architectural layers, each building on the last:

**1. Micro-Intervention Engine.** The sensing layer — continuous cardiac, respiratory, and optionally electrodermal signal capture. State classification runs locally (edge AI first, per the neural sovereignty principle: no raw biometric data leaves the device). Output: current state tag (focused / stressed / recovering / dysregulated / approaching-flow).

**2. Adaptive Training Tracks.** The protocol library — not one resonance-breathing script but a graduated set matched to state. A stressed nervous system enters at a lower challenge. A recovering system receives the full resonance protocol. An approaching-flow system gets the briefest possible prompt to sustain, not disrupt, what is already organizing. This is the layer that resolves autonomic non-responsiveness: the system does not insist on the protocol the theory prescribes; it selects the protocol the current body can receive.

**3. Flow Metrics Dashboard.** The feedback and training layer — not a raw HRV readout but a coherence-per-minute metric that gives the clinician a legible score for shift-level performance. Trend lines. State delta comparisons. This transforms invisible physiology into a professional feedback signal.

**4. Open API/SDK.** The integration layer — enterprise EMR connections, research export, wearable device agnosticism. This is the layer that makes the OS claim real: other systems can build on top of this autonomic intelligence.

## The Failure Mode

The architecture solves the wrong-protocol problem. It does not solve the presence problem.

A system that reads your HRV and adjusts the breathing guide can still be used without interoceptive engagement. The CRNA who opens the app while thinking about the next case, watches the breath pacer, hits the target metrics, and closes it — that person has completed a protocol without encountering the body. Coherence per minute measured externally is not the same as interoceptive accuracy growing over time. The platform must be designed to cultivate internal signal awareness, not substitute external feedback for it.

The second failure mode is calibration inflation: if the system consistently finds the nervous system too dysregulated to receive full resonance protocol and perpetually delivers the lower-challenge entry point, it may actually slow the development of autonomic flexibility rather than accelerating it. An adaptive system that never challenges is a biofeedback pacifier.

The Sumińska et al. (2026) finding has one more implication worth naming: if one-time individual calibration provides no advantage over fixed 0.1 Hz, real-time adaptive calibration may matter less than the quality of the interoceptive practice that sits inside any protocol. Architecture is necessary but not sufficient. The body learning to feel itself is the actual intervention.

## The Test

Fourteen-day parallel-arm feasibility study with CRNAs. Arm A: standard 0.1 Hz paced breathing, five minutes daily, fixed app. Arm B: Interoceptive Flow OS adaptive protocol, same daily window, same wearable. Measurement:

- **Primary:** Compliance rate at 14 days (target: Arm B ≥ 2× Arm A compliance rate)
- **Secondary:** RMSSD at matched time points (pre-shift vs. post-shift delta, across days 1, 7, 14)
- **Tertiary:** Self-reported state drift awareness (a five-item rapid scale at case transitions)
- **Qualitative:** Exit interview on perceived body engagement during protocol

Success criterion: compliance advantage exists AND RMSSD delta is non-inferior. Failure criterion: compliance rates converge, or compliant Arm B participants show no RMSSD advantage over compliant Arm A participants. If both arms fail equally, the active ingredient is the breathing itself — not the adaptation layer.

## The Connection

The Interoceptive Flow OS is the technical substrate beneath [[gap-moment-training]]. Without it, GMT delivers a fixed script to a variable body. With it, every [[neurominute]] is individualized — not by practitioner judgment but by real-time physiological read. The platform becomes what [[neurogating]] requires: a system that knows what state the clinician is in before selecting the appropriate micro-intervention.

This is also the "closed-loop" in [[closed-loop-biofeedback]] made clinically concrete. The concept existed as architecture. These papers show the architecture is now being built — and that the healthcare compliance problem, the one killer of remote biofeedback programs, is the very problem adaptive systems are designed to solve.

The gap that remains is the [[anterocept]] layer: a system can read HRV and adapt the protocol. Can it also train the clinician to feel what the system is measuring? That is the question the Interoceptive Flow OS opens, not closes.

# Somnistics: A Middle-Range Nursing Theory of Autonomic Self-Regulation in High-Acuity Practice

## Abstract

Somnistics is a middle-range nursing theory proposing that autonomic self-regulation is a trainable clinical competency rather than a fixed trait, and that its systematic suppression during medical and nursing education constitutes a primary biological mechanism underlying clinician burnout, state drift, and preventable clinical error. The theory introduces the Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis (ISH) -- the claim that medical education's hidden curriculum systematically attenuates clinicians' capacity to detect and respond to their own physiological signals -- and proposes Structured Interoception Training (SIT) as the corrective intervention. Drawing on polyvagal theory, interoceptive neuroscience, and 28 years of certified registered nurse anesthetist practice, Somnistics redefines the nursing metaparadigm by positioning the clinician's body as a clinical instrument, health as autonomic flexibility rather than absence of disease, and nursing action as self-regulation preceding other-regulation. The theory generates testable hypotheses measurable via heart rate variability (HRV), the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness (MAIA-2), and validated burnout instruments. It extends Benner's Novice-to-Expert model, specifies the autonomic mechanism implicit in Roy's Adaptation Model, and returns to Nightingale's original insight that the environment of healing includes the healer's own body.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Problem

Clinician burnout affects over 60% of healthcare workers. The prevailing discourse frames burnout as an organizational, psychological, or cultural problem -- addressed through schedule reform, resilience workshops, and employee assistance programs. These interventions target downstream symptoms. They do not address the biological infrastructure of sustained clinical performance.

Meanwhile, a separate problem persists: preventable clinical error. Error analyses consistently identify cognitive factors -- attentional narrowing, decision fatigue, delayed recognition -- as proximal causes. Yet the physiological substrate of cognition under pressure receives little systematic attention in nursing education or continuing professional development.

Somnistics proposes that these two problems -- burnout and error vulnerability -- share a common upstream cause: the progressive erosion of clinicians' capacity to detect and regulate their own autonomic states. This erosion is not incidental. It is trained.

### 1.2 The Gap in Nursing Theory

Existing nursing theories address adaptation (Roy), caring (Watson), expertise development (Benner), and human becoming (Parse). None specifically theorizes the clinician's own autonomic system as both a clinical instrument and a target of nursing intervention. None addresses the paradox that clinicians who monitor patients' physiology with extraordinary precision typically cannot monitor their own. None provides a theoretical framework for why this deficit exists or how to correct it.

Somnistics fills this gap by offering a theory that is:
- **Specific** enough to generate falsifiable hypotheses
- **Practical** enough to inform curriculum design and clinical protocols
- **Grounded** in measurable physiology rather than abstract constructs alone
- **Honest** about the boundaries between established science and original hypothesis

### 1.3 Origin of the Theory

This theory emerges from 28 years of certified registered nurse anesthetist (CRNA) practice across transplant, pediatric trauma, and cardiac anesthesia. Its originator, Randy Graybeal, DNAP, CRNA, observed a consistent pattern: clinical errors and professional deterioration correlated not with knowledge deficits but with undetected autonomic state changes during performance. The theory formalizes observations that accumulated across thousands of cases -- that the body knows before the mind, that regulation is trainable, and that the gap between cases is where the real training happens.

## 2. Historical and Theoretical Context

### 2.1 Nursing Theory Landscape

Nursing theory has evolved through four generations (Meleis, 2012). The discipline's theoretical foundation rests on theories ranging from grand theories (Rogers' Science of Unitary Human Beings, Parse's Human Becoming) to middle-range theories (Kolcaba's Comfort Theory, Swanson's Theory of Caring) that are specific enough to guide research and practice.

Two trajectories are particularly relevant to Somnistics:

**The expertise trajectory.** Patricia Benner's (1984) *From Novice to Expert* transformed nursing's understanding of clinical knowledge development, demonstrating that expert nurses operate through pattern recognition, intuition, and embodied know-how rather than rule-following. Benner identified the role of the body in expertise but did not theorize the autonomic system specifically, nor did she address what happens when embodied expertise degrades under chronic stress.

**The adaptation trajectory.** Callista Roy's (1976) Adaptation Model conceptualizes the person as an adaptive system responding to environmental stimuli through four modes: physiological-physical, self-concept, role function, and interdependence. Roy's model implies autonomic regulation but does not specify it. Somnistics operationalizes what Roy's model describes abstractly.

### 2.2 Adjacent Scientific Domains

Somnistics draws on several established scientific literatures:

**Polyvagal Theory** (Porges, 2011). Stephen Porges' polyvagal theory provides the neurophysiological framework for understanding autonomic state hierarchies: ventral vagal (social engagement), sympathetic (mobilization), and dorsal vagal (immobilization). Polyvagal theory introduced neuroception -- the subconscious detection of environmental safety or threat -- as the mechanism governing autonomic state shifts. Somnistics extends this by asking: what happens when the clinician's own neuroceptive capacity is suppressed by training?

*Note: Polyvagal theory remains debated in autonomic neuroscience (Grossman, 2026). Somnistics draws on its clinical utility as a heuristic for understanding autonomic hierarchies while acknowledging ongoing scientific refinement of its specific neuroanatomical claims.*

**Interoceptive Neuroscience.** The study of how the brain represents the body's internal state has expanded significantly since Craig's (2002) and Critchley's (2004) foundational work on insular cortex function. The Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness (MAIA-2; Mehling et al., 2012, 2018) provides a validated instrument for measuring interoceptive capacity across eight dimensions. Somnistics applies this literature specifically to clinician populations and proposes that their interoceptive capacities differ systematically from non-clinical populations -- not upward, as one might expect, but downward.

**HRV Biofeedback.** Heart rate variability biofeedback, particularly at resonant breathing frequency (Lehrer et al., 2000), demonstrates that autonomic flexibility is trainable through breath-paced protocols. Systematic reviews (Zaccaro et al., 2018) confirm that slow, paced breathing produces measurable shifts in autonomic nervous system activity. Somnistics proposes that these established techniques can be delivered in minimum effective doses (60-second interventions) within clinical workflow.

**Allostatic Load.** McEwan's (2007) work on allostatic load -- the cumulative wear on biological systems from chronic stress adaptation -- provides the pathophysiological framework for understanding how incomplete autonomic recovery cycles compound across shifts, weeks, and careers.

### 2.3 What Existing Frameworks Do Not Address

Despite the richness of these literatures, no existing framework addresses the following questions simultaneously:

1. Why do clinicians who monitor others' physiology fail to monitor their own?
2. Is this failure incidental or systematically produced by training?
3. Can the same precision clinicians apply to patients be turned inward?
4. What is the minimum effective intervention for restoring self-regulatory capacity within clinical workflow?
5. How does individual autonomic regulation extend to team-level performance?

Somnistics is the theoretical framework designed to address these questions as an integrated whole.

## 3. Metaparadigm Alignment

Every nursing theory must address the discipline's four metaparadigm concepts: person, environment, health, and nursing (Fawcett, 1984). Somnistics redefines each through the lens of autonomic self-regulation.

### 3.1 Person

**Traditional view:** The person is the recipient of nursing care -- the patient.

**Somnistics view:** The person is *also* the clinician, understood as a physiological system embedded in a high-consequence environment. The clinician's body is not merely a vehicle for delivering care; it is a clinical instrument whose calibration directly affects patient outcomes. The CRNA who monitors a patient's hemodynamics with milligram precision while unaware that their own heart rate has been elevated for 40 minutes is operating a miscalibrated instrument.

Somnistics does not displace the patient from the center of nursing concern. Rather, it argues that the clinician's capacity to care for the patient is constrained by the clinician's own autonomic state. Self-regulation is not self-indulgence; it is a prerequisite for other-regulation.

The person in Somnistics is characterized by:
- **Interoceptive capacity** -- the ability to detect internal physiological signals
- **Autonomic flexibility** -- the ability to shift between sympathetic and parasympathetic states as context demands
- **Embodied metacognition** -- the ability to monitor cognitive processes through somatic signals rather than abstract thought alone
- **State transition intelligence** -- the trained capacity to detect, predict, and manage autonomic state changes

### 3.2 Environment

**Traditional view:** The physical, social, and cultural context of care delivery.

**Somnistics view:** The high-acuity clinical environment is understood as an autonomic stressor field with specific characteristics:

- **Temporal compression** -- decisions must be made in seconds, not minutes
- **Consequence density** -- errors produce irreversible physiological harm
- **Transition frequency** -- 30-40 state transitions per shift (between cases, from routine to emergency, from action to waiting)
- **Signal overload** -- simultaneous monitoring of multiple physiological parameters
- **Recovery suppression** -- cultural and scheduling norms that prevent autonomic recovery between stressors

The environment also includes the *training environment* -- the hidden curriculum of medical and nursing education that shapes the clinician's relationship to their own body. This is where interoceptive suppression originates (see Section 8).

Critically, the environment extends beyond the individual. Through co-regulation -- the bidirectional autonomic entrainment mediated by vocal prosody, facial expression, and respiratory synchrony (Porges, 2007) -- one clinician's autonomic state becomes part of the environment for every other person in the room. A dysregulated clinician does not merely perform worse; they create a dysregulating environment for patients and colleagues.

### 3.3 Health

**Traditional view:** A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being (WHO) or the absence of disease.

**Somnistics view:** Health is **autonomic flexibility** -- the capacity to shift states appropriately and recover efficiently. This is measurable (HRV as the primary index), trainable (through structured interoception training), and degradable (through chronic suppression and incomplete recovery cycles).

Health in Somnistics is not a static state but a dynamic capacity characterized by:

- **Autonomic home base** -- a trained baseline state the clinician can reliably return to after disruption. Not "calm" but a known coordinate in the nervous system's state space that has been mapped through practice.
- **Recovery efficiency** -- the speed and completeness of parasympathetic restoration between stressors, measured by HRV recovery curves.
- **Interoceptive literacy** -- the capacity to detect, interpret, respond to, and trust one's own physiological signals with the same precision applied to patient monitoring.
- **Clinician durability** -- the capacity to sustain effective performance, emotional regulation, and professional engagement across a multi-decade career.

This definition reframes burnout not as a failure of resilience or character but as a degradation of autonomic infrastructure -- a training artifact rather than a personal deficiency.

### 3.4 Nursing

**Traditional view:** The actions taken to promote health, prevent illness, and care for the sick.

**Somnistics view:** Nursing, in the context of this theory, is **self-regulation as a clinical competency**. Just as pharmacology, hemodynamic monitoring, and airway management are core competencies taught, assessed, and maintained, autonomic self-regulation is a skill that can be developed through structured training and measured through validated instruments.

The nursing action in Somnistics operates at three levels:

1. **Individual** -- The clinician practices Gap Moment Training (GMT), using naturally occurring transitional moments in clinical workflow (between cases, walking to the OR, after critical incidents) to perform 60-second autonomic regulation protocols.

2. **Interpersonal** -- Through co-regulation, the self-regulated clinician extends their autonomic stability to patients and team members. The "Team Breath Before You Start" -- a synchronized slow breath after surgical timeout -- operationalizes co-regulation as a clinical safety intervention.

3. **Systemic** -- Somnistics informs nursing education, credentialing, and organizational design. If autonomic regulation is a clinical competency, it must be taught in curricula, assessed in certification, and supported by institutional structures.

## 4. Core Concepts

The following concepts constitute the theoretical vocabulary of Somnistics. Each is defined formally, with its knowledge readiness level (KRL) indicated: **established** (supported by existing literature), **emerging** (partially supported, requiring further study), or **hypothesized** (original to Somnistics, requiring primary investigation).

### 4.1 Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis (ISH) -- *Hypothesized*

The central claim that medical and nursing education systematically trains clinicians to suppress their own body signals. The hidden curriculum -- stoicism, emotional detachment, powering through fatigue -- views the body as a machine to be operated, not a sensor to be tuned. This suppression reduces interoceptive sensibility, alters autonomic metacognition, produces autonomic inflexibility, and constitutes a biological contributor to burnout. (See Section 8 for extended treatment.)

### 4.2 Autonomic Regulation -- *Established*

The body's capacity to shift flexibly between sympathetic activation, parasympathetic recovery, and integrated (ventral vagal) states. Measurable via HRV (SDNN, RMSSD, HF power), respiratory patterns, and baroreflex sensitivity. The core scientific domain that Somnistics trains.

### 4.3 Interoceptive Literacy -- *Emerging*

The ability to monitor one's own physiological state with clinical precision -- the same measurement rigor applied to patients, turned inward. Operationalized through four component capacities: detect internal signals accurately, interpret their contextual meaning, respond using trained protocols, and trust the body as a reliable information source. Measured via MAIA-2.

### 4.4 State Drift -- *Hypothesized*

The progressive, often undetected loss of autonomic regulation during clinical work. Characterized by attentional narrowing, temporal compression, vocal tone changes, and incomplete recovery between arousal spikes. Proposed as the proximal cause of most clinical errors -- not ignorance or incompetence, but the undetected slide from optimal to suboptimal autonomic state during performance.

### 4.5 Embodied Metacognition -- *Emerging*

The capacity to monitor, evaluate, and modulate one's own cognitive and attentional processes through somatic signals rather than abstract thought. Where standard metacognition asks "what am I thinking about?", embodied metacognition asks "what is my body telling me about how I am thinking?" The diaphragm, heart rate, jaw tension, and other interoceptive channels become the primary feedback system for meta-awareness.

### 4.6 Autonomic Home Base -- *Hypothesized*

A trained baseline autonomic state that one can reliably return to after disruption. Not a relaxation technique but a known coordinate in the nervous system's state space, built through practice. Characterized by four operational components: recognition (knowing what optimal feels like), navigation (ability to return after disruption), sustaining (maintaining under pressure), and switching (deliberate entry and exit).

### 4.7 Gap Moment Training (GMT) -- *Emerging*

A structured training modality that repurposes short, naturally occurring transitional moments in daily clinical workflow as the primary training surface for nervous system regulation. Approximately 30-40 gap moments occur per CRNA shift. The intervention is 60 seconds (one NeuroMinute), applying the pharmacological principle of minimum effective dose to behavioral intervention.

### 4.8 Structured Interoception Training (SIT) -- *Hypothesized*

The tri-modal protocol specification underlying GMT:
- **Mode 1: Interoceptive Attention Training** -- body-scan and signal-tracking exercises that restore the sensory channel suppressed by medical education.
- **Mode 2: Breath-Paced Autonomic Training** -- HRV-biofeedback-guided respiratory pacing at resonant breathing frequency.
- **Mode 3: Graded Interoceptive Exposure** -- CO2 tolerance drills and air-hunger protocols applying hormetic principles to expand regulation capacity under physiological discomfort.

### 4.9 Clinician Durability -- *Emerging*

The capacity to sustain effective performance, emotional regulation, and professional engagement across a multi-decade career. Reframes burnout prevention from "managing stress events" to "maintaining the physiological infrastructure that enables sustained performance." Key determinants include autonomic flexibility, interoceptive awareness, sleep quality, meaning coherence, and co-regulation capacity.

### 4.10 Minimum Effective Dose (MED) -- *Established*

Borrowed from pharmacology and applied to behavioral intervention: the smallest intervention in time, intensity, and frequency that produces measurable neurophysiological change. In Somnistics: five breaths (60 seconds) repeated across five daily sessions produces greater autonomic adaptation than a single weekly 30-minute block. The principle that frequency multiplied by novelty exceeds duration for neuroplasticity.

### 4.11 Titration to Effect -- *Established (in pharmacology); Emerging (in behavioral application)*

Applying the minimum effective dose incrementally and adjusting based on measured response. Start with the simplest effective protocol (coherent breathing), observe the physiological response (HRV delta), and progress to more complex protocols only when simpler ones show diminishing returns.

### 4.12 Co-Regulation -- *Established*

The process by which one person's nervous system state influences and stabilizes another's through bidirectional autonomic entrainment. Mediated by vocal prosody, facial expression, respiratory synchrony, and mirror neuron activation. Somnistics extends co-regulation from a developmental concept to a clinical team intervention.

### 4.13 State Transition Intelligence -- *Hypothesized*

The trained capacity to accurately predict, detect, and manage autonomic state transitions in high-stakes environments. The distinction between being functionally competent and being truly physiologically recovered. Not wellness or stress management -- state transition control.

### 4.14 Diaphragmatic Blindness -- *Emerging*

The specific inability to sense or consciously control one's own diaphragmatic breathing during high-stress situations. The most common starting deficit identified in initial interoceptive assessment. Clinicians who expertly manage patients' ventilation often cannot feel their own breath.

### 4.15 Embodied Clinical Intelligence (ECI) -- *Hypothesized*

The total embodied, situated capacity of a clinician to sense, decide, and act with asymmetric leverage -- where massive accumulated knowledge compresses into minimal effective intervention. Structured as a four-stratum ontology: perception acuity, action micro-skills, situation awareness, and inner regulation.

### 4.16 Language of the Nurse -- *Hypothesized*

The communication philosophy that translates technical neurophysiological concepts into warm, human, care-centered language. Not a dumbing-down but a recognition that the register of clinical nursing -- touch, presence, reassurance -- is the appropriate register for teaching self-regulation.

### 4.17 Adversity Visualization -- *Emerging*

Deliberately visualizing failure scenarios and embodying the exact physiological response -- not merely cognitive scenario planning but autonomic pre-rehearsal. Modeled on Blue Angels pre-flight protocols and adapted to clinical practice. Distinguished from standard mental rehearsal by the inclusion of interoceptive and autonomic components.

### 4.18 Learning Under Load -- *Hypothesized*

The pedagogical framework for how experts learn while performing under pressure. Structured as a four-phase loop: pre-case planning, in-case interoceptive cues, post-case harvest, and spaced retrieval. Rejects the separation of learning from clinical work.

### 4.19 Benner Expertise Spiral -- *Hypothesized*

An extension of Benner's (1984) Novice-to-Expert model in two directions: upward to the "Expert's Expert" -- a practitioner whose embodied expertise is so refined they can translate, teach, and meta-regulate expertise itself -- and laterally to the "Expert-to-Novice-Again Spiral" -- the experience of re-entering training at higher complexity from a position of mastery.

## 5. Theoretical Propositions

The following propositions articulate the testable relationships between Somnistics concepts. They are stated directionally and are amenable to empirical investigation.

**P1.** As interoceptive suppression increases (measured by declining MAIA-2 scores across years of clinical training), autonomic flexibility decreases (measured by declining HRV indices).

**P2.** As autonomic flexibility decreases, vulnerability to state drift during clinical performance increases (measured by frequency and duration of undetected state drift episodes during simulated or real clinical scenarios).

**P3.** As state drift frequency increases, clinical error vulnerability increases (measured by error rate, near-miss frequency, and performance degradation metrics during simulation).

**P4.** Structured Interoception Training (SIT) delivered in minimum effective doses (60-second intervals, 5 times daily) produces measurable increases in interoceptive literacy (MAIA-2 scores) within 21 days.

**P5.** Increases in interoceptive literacy (MAIA-2) correlate with increases in autonomic flexibility (HRV), forming a reciprocal positive feedback loop.

**P6.** Gap Moment Training frequency is inversely associated with state drift episodes: clinicians who practice GMT more frequently during natural workflow transitions demonstrate fewer and shorter periods of undetected autonomic dysregulation.

**P7.** Clinicians with established autonomic home bases (operationalized as consistent HRV recovery patterns post-stressor) demonstrate greater clinician durability (measured by career longevity, sustained engagement, and resistance to burnout as measured by the Maslach Burnout Inventory).

**P8.** Co-regulation capacity -- a clinician's ability to stabilize others' autonomic states -- is contingent upon their own autonomic regulation capacity. Self-regulation precedes and enables other-regulation.

**P9.** Titration to effect in behavioral intervention (progressive complexity based on measured physiological response) produces greater autonomic adaptation than fixed-protocol approaches.

**P10.** Adversity visualization that includes embodied (interoceptive and motor) components produces greater autonomic resilience to novel stressors than cognitive-only visualization protocols.

**P11.** The relationship between clinical experience (years of practice) and interoceptive literacy is non-linear: interoceptive suppression attenuates the expected gains from experience, creating a divergence between procedural expertise and self-regulatory capacity.

**P12.** Embodied metacognition (the capacity to monitor cognition through somatic signals) mediates the relationship between interoceptive literacy and clinical decision quality under pressure.

## 6. Assumptions

Every theory rests on philosophical commitments that are not themselves empirically tested but frame the theory's perspective. Somnistics rests on the following assumptions:

**A1. The clinician's body is a clinical instrument, not merely a vehicle for the mind.** Autonomic state directly modulates perception, cognition, decision-making, and interpersonal communication. A dysregulated nervous system does not merely feel unpleasant; it degrades clinical performance.

**A2. Autonomic regulation is a trainable skill, not a fixed trait.** Just as muscular strength responds to progressive resistance, autonomic flexibility responds to structured training. This assumption is supported by the HRV biofeedback literature but extended by Somnistics to the specific context of clinical workflow integration.

**A3. The body knows before the mind.** Interoceptive signals precede conscious cognitive awareness of state changes. The clinician who notices their shoulders rising before they consciously register anxiety is detecting information the autonomic system has already processed. This body-first epistemology is foundational to Somnistics.

**A4. Burnout is primarily a training artifact, not a character flaw.** The Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis proposes that burnout has a biological mechanism rooted in systematic signal suppression during training. This reframes the intervention target from individual resilience to educational and training system reform.

**A5. One breath may be the minimum effective dose of self-regulation.** The pharmacological principle of minimum effective dose, applied to behavioral intervention, suggests that very brief interventions (60 seconds) delivered at high frequency can produce meaningful autonomic adaptation. This assumption challenges the prevailing belief that regulation requires extended practice sessions.

**A6. Self-regulation is a prerequisite for other-regulation.** A clinician cannot reliably co-regulate patients or team members from a dysregulated autonomic state. Caring for one's own nervous system is not self-indulgence but a professional obligation.

**A7. Gap moments are trainable surfaces.** The naturally occurring transitional windows in clinical workflow (between cases, walking to the OR, after critical incidents) are not dead time; they are the primary training surface for autonomic regulation. This assumption reframes clinical workflow as containing embedded training opportunities.

**A8. Expertise development includes autonomic development.** Benner's progression from novice to expert is not only cognitive and procedural but autonomic. Expert clinicians have not merely learned more -- they have developed more refined autonomic regulation. The Expertise Spiral extends this to ask what happens when experts re-enter uncertainty at higher levels of complexity.

## 7. Hypotheses

The following hypotheses are specific, falsifiable predictions derived from the theoretical propositions above. Each is designed to be testable with existing instruments and research methods.

**H1.** CRNAs with more than 10 years of clinical experience will demonstrate lower MAIA-2 Noticing, Body Listening, and Trusting subscale scores compared to age-matched, education-matched non-clinical professionals, controlling for baseline interoceptive traits.

**H2.** MAIA-2 total scores will inversely correlate with Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI) Emotional Exhaustion subscale scores in a cross-sectional sample of CRNAs (r > 0.30, p < 0.05).

**H3.** A 21-day Structured Interoception Training program (5 daily 60-second sessions) will produce statistically significant increases in MAIA-2 total scores (d > 0.40) in a CRNA sample compared to waitlist controls.

**H4.** CRNAs who complete SIT will demonstrate increased resting RMSSD (a time-domain HRV index reflecting parasympathetic tone) at 30-day follow-up compared to baseline.

**H5.** In a high-fidelity simulation scenario, CRNAs who have completed SIT will demonstrate shorter HRV recovery times (return to within 10% of baseline RMSSD) following simulated critical events compared to untrained controls.

**H6.** State drift episodes (operationalized as periods where HRV drops below a threshold for more than 5 consecutive minutes without conscious recognition, detected by real-time wearable monitoring and confirmed by post-hoc self-report discrepancy) will be less frequent in SIT-trained clinicians.

**H7.** Clinicians with higher MAIA-2 Self-Regulation subscale scores will demonstrate fewer performance errors during simulated cases with embedded stressors (distraction, time pressure, equipment malfunction).

**H8.** Gap Moment Training adherence (number of GMT sessions per shift, tracked via app-based logging) will be inversely associated with end-of-shift cortisol levels and positively associated with end-of-shift HRV in a within-subjects crossover design.

**H9.** A team-level co-regulation intervention (synchronized slow breath at surgical timeout) will reduce team-aggregate sympathetic markers (mean heart rate, aggregate HRV) during the subsequent case compared to standard timeout without the intervention.

**H10.** Adversity visualization protocols that include embodied components (interoceptive attention to breathing, heart rate, muscle tension during visualization) will produce smaller HRV decrements during subsequent exposure to novel simulated stressors compared to cognitive-only visualization protocols.

**H11.** In a longitudinal cohort, SIT-trained CRNAs will demonstrate lower annual turnover rates and higher professional engagement scores (Utrecht Work Engagement Scale) compared to matched controls over a 2-year observation period.

**H12.** Diaphragmatic blindness (inability to accurately report whether one is breathing diaphragmatically vs. thoracically, validated against EMG) will be more prevalent in clinicians than in non-clinical matched controls.

**H13.** The Expert-to-Novice-Again transition (operationalized as experienced clinicians entering a new clinical specialty) will produce temporary MAIA-2 score decrements, with recovery rate predicted by prior GMT training.

## 8. The Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis: A Central Claim

### 8.1 Statement

The Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis (ISH) is the most consequential and most testable claim within Somnistics. It states:

> Medical and nursing education systematically trains clinicians to suppress their own body signals. The hidden curriculum -- stoicism, emotional detachment, powering through fatigue -- views the body as a machine to be operated, not a sensor to be tuned. This suppression reduces interoceptive sensibility, produces autonomic inflexibility, and constitutes a primary biological mechanism underlying clinician burnout.

### 8.2 The Causal Chain

ISH proposes a four-link causal sequence:

1. **The hidden curriculum suppresses interoception.** Clinical training rewards ignoring hunger, fatigue, bladder urgency, and emotional distress signals. The "bladder of steel" is a badge of honor. Eating is optional. Emotional reactions are treated as unprofessional rather than informational. Students learn, through thousands of reinforcement cycles, that their own body signals are noise to be suppressed, not data to be integrated.

2. **Suppression reduces interoceptive sensibility.** Years of systematic signal-ignoring attenuate the capacity to detect autonomic state changes. The interoceptive channel -- the internal sensing system that monitors heart rate, breathing depth, gut signals, temperature, and muscle tension -- becomes functionally impaired. This is not a metaphor; it is a prediction about measurable neural and perceptual changes.

3. **Reduced sensibility causes autonomic inflexibility.** Without reliable interoceptive feedback, the autonomic nervous system loses its capacity for fine-grained self-regulation. Autonomic regulation requires a feedback loop: detect the current state, compare to the desired state, execute a corrective action, verify the result. When the detection step is impaired, the entire loop degrades. The result is an autonomic system that responds to stressors with excessive activation and recovers incompletely.

4. **Autonomic inflexibility manifests as burnout.** What is conventionally called "burnout" is partly the downstream consequence of a nervous system that can no longer self-regulate because its primary feedback channel has been suppressed. Emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and reduced personal accomplishment -- the three dimensions of the Maslach Burnout Inventory -- are the experiential correlates of chronic autonomic dysregulation.

### 8.3 Evidentiary Status

ISH is currently a hypothesis. Its components draw on established science, but the full causal chain has not been tested as an integrated sequence. The evidentiary landscape:

**Established:**
- Interoception is measurable (MAIA-2; Mehling et al., 2012, 2018)
- Interoceptive capacity varies between individuals and can be modified by training (Garfinkel et al., 2015)
- HRV biofeedback improves autonomic flexibility (Lehrer et al., 2000, 2020)
- Burnout correlates with autonomic markers in healthcare workers (He et al., 2026; Kane et al., 2025)
- Clinical training culture includes well-documented norms of stoicism and self-neglect (Nosbisch, 2026; Jansson, 2026)

**Emerging:**
- Clinicians may show different interoceptive profiles than non-clinical populations (direction and magnitude are empirical questions)
- Interoceptive training may improve burnout metrics (plausible based on HRV biofeedback evidence but not yet tested in this specific causal framing)

**Hypothesized (requiring primary investigation):**
- That clinical training *causes* interoceptive suppression (rather than merely correlating with it)
- That interoceptive suppression *mediates* the relationship between clinical experience and burnout
- That restoring interoceptive capacity reverses the autonomic inflexibility associated with burnout

### 8.4 Why ISH Matters

If the Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis is supported, the intervention point for clinician burnout shifts fundamentally. The problem is not that clinicians lack resilience. The problem is that their training systematically disabled the sensory system they would need to self-regulate. The solution is not "more resilience" but "restore the channel that was suppressed."

This reframing has implications for:
- **Medical and nursing education** -- curricula would need to include interoceptive training, not merely didactic content about stress management
- **Continuing professional development** -- CE programs would shift from resilience workshops to structured interoception training
- **Organizational wellness programs** -- the target would change from psychological coping to physiological restoration
- **Clinical safety** -- state drift would be recognized as a monitorable, preventable antecedent to error, not merely a human factors footnote

### 8.5 A Specific Instance: Diaphragmatic Blindness

Diaphragmatic blindness -- the inability to sense or control one's own diaphragmatic breathing during high-stress situations -- is the most visible and most easily tested instance of ISH. Clinicians who expertly manage patients' ventilatory parameters often cannot feel their own breath. They breathe thoracically under stress without awareness. This specific deficit is correctable (through interoceptive attention training targeting diaphragmatic awareness) and measurable (via diaphragm EMG compared to self-report accuracy), making it an ideal entry point for testing the broader hypothesis.

## 9. Relationship to Existing Nursing Theories

Somnistics does not claim to replace existing nursing theories. It extends, specifies, and in some cases provides the autonomic mechanism for what earlier theories describe in more abstract terms.

### 9.1 Benner: Novice to Expert (1984)

**What Benner established:** Clinical expertise develops through stages (novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, expert) characterized by progressive reliance on pattern recognition, embodied know-how, and intuitive grasp rather than rule-following.

**What Somnistics adds:** The Benner Expertise Spiral extends the model in two directions. Upward, to the *Expert's Expert* -- a practitioner whose embodied expertise is so refined they can translate, teach, and meta-regulate expertise itself. They shape fields and codify tacit knowledge. Laterally, to the *Expert-to-Novice-Again Spiral* -- what happens when a master clinician re-enters training at higher complexity. This is not regression but a spiral return from a higher base, where prior tacit traces accelerate relearning while uncertainty pathways reactivate.

Somnistics also adds the autonomic dimension to Benner's stages: expert performance is not only cognitive and procedural but autonomic. The expert CRNA has developed not just better pattern recognition but more refined autonomic regulation -- the ability to remain in ventral vagal engagement under conditions that push novices into sympathetic overdrive.

### 9.2 Roy: Adaptation Model (1976)

**What Roy established:** The person is an adaptive system responding to focal, contextual, and residual stimuli through four adaptive modes: physiological-physical, self-concept, role function, and interdependence.

**What Somnistics adds:** Somnistics specifies the autonomic mechanism of adaptation that Roy's model implies but does not detail. Roy's physiological-physical mode encompasses autonomic function abstractly; Somnistics operationalizes it with specific constructs (HRV, MAIA-2, resonant breathing frequency), specific interventions (SIT, GMT), and specific outcome measures. In Roy's terms, Somnistics theorizes what happens when the cognator subsystem (the information processing mechanism for adaptation) loses access to interoceptive input because that input has been systematically suppressed.

### 9.3 Watson: Theory of Human Caring (1979, 2008)

**What Watson established:** Nursing is fundamentally a caring relationship. The "caritas processes" (previously "carative factors") describe the transpersonal caring relationship between nurse and patient. Watson emphasizes presence, authentic engagement, and the holistic integration of mind-body-spirit.

**What Somnistics adds:** Caring for others requires the physiological capacity to be present and regulated. A clinician in sympathetic overdrive cannot fully engage Watson's caritas processes -- their perceptual field is narrowed, their vocal prosody shifts, their capacity for genuine presence diminishes. Somnistics argues that self-regulation is not opposed to caring but is its physiological prerequisite. The theory provides the biological substrate for Watson's insight: the instrument of caring is the clinician's own regulated presence.

### 9.4 Parse: Human Becoming (1981)

**What Parse established:** Humans are not merely adaptive beings but are always in the process of *becoming* -- co-creating health through meaning, rhythmicity, and transcendence. Parse deliberately moved nursing theory away from the medical model and toward a phenomenological understanding of lived experience.

**What Somnistics adds:** Somnistics grounds Parse's "becoming" in measurable physiology without reducing it to physiology. The clinician who develops autonomic home base, who learns to navigate state transitions with increasing skill across a career, is in a process of becoming that is simultaneously experiential (the felt sense of regulation) and physiological (the measurable HRV patterns). Somnistics provides what Parse's theory intentionally omits: a measurement framework. This is not a criticism of Parse but an acknowledgment that different theoretical purposes require different tools.

### 9.5 Nightingale: Notes on Nursing (1860)

**What Nightingale established:** The environment is the primary domain of nursing intervention. Nightingale's insight -- that nursing's task is to arrange the conditions in which the body can heal itself -- was fundamentally ecological. She attended to ventilation, light, cleanliness, and nutrition as the material conditions of recovery.

**What Somnistics adds:** The environment of healing includes the healer's own body. A dysregulated clinician creates a dysregulating environment -- through vocal tone, facial expression, respiratory pattern, and the autonomic signals that patients and team members unconsciously detect. Nightingale's ecological insight, extended inward, yields the Somnistics claim: the clinician's autonomic state is an environmental variable that affects patient outcomes.

This is perhaps the deepest connection in the theory -- a return to the original insight of modern nursing, carried to its logical conclusion. If the environment heals, and the clinician is part of the environment, then the clinician's own regulation is a nursing intervention.

## 10. Research Agenda

The following studies represent priority investigations for validating and developing Somnistics. They are ordered by feasibility and foundational importance.

### 10.1 Foundation Studies

**Study 1: Cross-Sectional MAIA-2 Comparison**
Compare MAIA-2 profiles of CRNAs (stratified by years of practice) with age- and education-matched non-clinical professionals. This tests the core ISH prediction that clinical training attenuates interoceptive capacity. *Design:* Cross-sectional survey. *Instruments:* MAIA-2, MBI, demographics. *Target N:* 200 CRNAs, 200 matched controls. *Priority:* Highest -- this is the gateway study for the entire theory.

**Study 2: Diaphragmatic Blindness Prevalence**
Assess the prevalence of diaphragmatic blindness in CRNAs vs. matched controls using diaphragm EMG compared to self-report accuracy. *Design:* Lab-based assessment. *Target N:* 60 (30 per group). *Priority:* High -- the most focused and feasible test of ISH.

### 10.2 Intervention Studies

**Study 3: 21-Day SIT Pilot (CRNAs)**
Single-group pre-post pilot of Structured Interoception Training (5 daily 60-second sessions for 21 days). *Outcomes:* MAIA-2, RMSSD, MBI. *Target N:* 30. *Priority:* High -- generates effect size estimates for the powered RCT.

**Study 4: GMT Randomized Controlled Trial**
Wait-list controlled RCT of Gap Moment Training in CRNAs. *Primary outcome:* MAIA-2 change score. *Secondary:* RMSSD, MBI, professional engagement, self-reported state drift episodes. *Duration:* 12 weeks with 6-month follow-up. *Target N:* 120 (60 per arm). *Priority:* The definitive test of the Somnistics intervention model.

### 10.3 Mechanism Studies

**Study 5: HRV Recovery Dynamics in Simulation**
High-fidelity simulation study comparing HRV recovery curves (post-critical event) between SIT-trained and untrained CRNAs. Tests the state drift mechanism directly. *Design:* Between-groups simulation with continuous physiological monitoring. *Target N:* 40. *Priority:* Medium -- mechanistically important but requires the intervention studies first.

**Study 6: Co-Regulation Team Study**
Pilot study of team-level co-regulation (synchronized breath at surgical timeout) measuring team-aggregate HRV and communication quality during subsequent cases. *Design:* Within-teams crossover. *Target N:* 10 surgical teams. *Priority:* Medium -- extends the theory from individual to team level.

### 10.4 Longitudinal Studies

**Study 7: Clinician Durability Cohort**
Prospective cohort following SIT-trained and untrained CRNAs over 2 years, measuring turnover, engagement, burnout, HRV trends, and MAIA-2 trajectories. Tests the durability proposition. *Design:* Prospective cohort with matched controls. *Target N:* 200. *Priority:* Long-term -- validates the career-span claims but requires sustained infrastructure.

## 11. Clinical Implications

If the propositions and hypotheses of Somnistics are supported by evidence, the implications extend across nursing education, clinical practice, and health policy.

### 11.1 Nursing Education

**Curriculum reform.** Interoceptive literacy would become a formal competency in nursing education, alongside pharmacology, anatomy, and clinical skills. Students would learn to monitor their own physiological states with the same rigor they apply to patients. The hidden curriculum of stoicism and self-neglect would be identified and deliberately countered.

**Assessment.** MAIA-2 could serve as a baseline and longitudinal assessment tool throughout nursing education, tracking interoceptive development rather than merely clinical knowledge acquisition.

**Simulation design.** Simulation scenarios would include not only clinical performance metrics but physiological monitoring of the student clinician, providing feedback on state management during high-fidelity exercises.

### 11.2 Clinical Practice

**Workflow integration.** Gap Moment Training is designed to fit within existing clinical workflow -- not as an additional burden but as a reframing of time that already exists. The 60-second intervention window between cases, during handoffs, and after critical events is already present in every clinician's day.

**Team protocols.** Co-regulation interventions (e.g., team breath at surgical timeout) represent a new category of safety intervention -- one that addresses the autonomic environment rather than the checklist alone.

**Wearable monitoring.** Real-time HRV monitoring (via commercially available wearables) could provide objective state drift detection, alerting clinicians when their autonomic state has deviated from their trained home base without their conscious awareness.

### 11.3 Organizational and Policy Implications

**Reframing wellness programs.** If burnout has a biological training mechanism (ISH), organizational wellness programs would shift from resilience rhetoric to structured interoceptive training. The intervention target changes from the clinician's attitude to the clinician's autonomic infrastructure.

**Retention economics.** CRNA turnover replacement costs range from $80,000-$150,000 per person. If SIT demonstrably improves durability and reduces turnover, the economic case for organizational adoption becomes straightforward.

**Credentialing.** If autonomic self-regulation is validated as a clinical competency, it could eventually inform credentialing standards -- not as a gatekeeping mechanism but as a recognized domain of professional development, analogous to continuing education in pharmacology or airway management.

## 12. Limitations and Boundaries

Intellectual honesty requires clear articulation of what Somnistics does not claim, where its evidence is weakest, and what could falsify it.

### 12.1 What the Theory Does Not Claim

**Somnistics does not claim that burnout is exclusively biological.** Organizational factors (staffing, scheduling, administrative burden, moral injury from systemic failures) are real and consequential. ISH proposes that biological interoceptive suppression is *a* primary mechanism, not *the only* mechanism. Burnout is multifactorial, and Somnistics addresses one factor -- but one that has been largely invisible in the prevailing discourse.

**Somnistics does not claim that self-regulation replaces systemic reform.** Teaching clinicians to regulate themselves in toxic environments is not a substitute for fixing those environments. The theory explicitly rejects the framing that burnout is "the clinician's problem to solve." However, it also argues that even in reformed systems, the trained interoceptive deficit will persist unless directly addressed.

**Somnistics does not prescribe.** The theory describes mechanisms, relationships, and interventions. It does not tell clinicians what to do with their own bodies. It presents the science and trusts the reader to draw their own conclusions about applicability.

**Somnistics does not claim clinical authority over the reader.** The language throughout is "the literature suggests," "the evidence indicates," "the hypothesis proposes" -- never "you should" or "research proves."

### 12.2 Evidential Limitations

**The causal chain in ISH is hypothesized, not established.** Each link draws on existing literature, but the full four-link sequence (training suppresses interoception -> suppression reduces autonomic flexibility -> inflexibility produces burnout) has not been tested as an integrated causal model. Correlation between links does not establish the causal sequence.

**The theory's specificity to high-acuity clinicians is a strength and a limitation.** Somnistics is developed from and for CRNA practice. Its applicability to other nursing specialties, other healthcare professions, and non-clinical high-stakes domains is plausible but unvalidated.

**Polyvagal theory, which provides significant conceptual scaffolding, remains scientifically contested.** Somnistics draws on polyvagal theory as a clinical heuristic while acknowledging that specific neuroanatomical claims are under ongoing revision (Grossman, 2026). The theory's core propositions do not depend on the specific validity of the vagal brake mechanism; they depend on the more broadly established relationship between HRV, autonomic flexibility, and self-regulation.

**Minimum effective dose claims require dose-finding studies.** The assertion that 60 seconds is sufficient for meaningful autonomic intervention is extrapolated from HRV biofeedback literature (Lehrer et al., 2000; Prinsloo et al., 2011) but has not been specifically validated in the GMT format with CRNA populations.

### 12.3 What Would Falsify the Theory

**If clinicians show equal or higher MAIA-2 scores than matched non-clinical populations,** the Interoceptive Suppression Hypothesis would be undermined. This would suggest that clinical training does not systematically suppress interoception.

**If SIT produces improvements in MAIA-2 but not in HRV or burnout measures,** the proposed mechanism linking interoceptive restoration to autonomic and clinical outcomes would require revision.

**If GMT adherence shows no relationship to state drift episodes or error rates,** the proposed connection between gap moment utilization and clinical performance would not be supported.

**If co-regulation interventions show no measurable effect on team autonomic synchrony,** the extension of individual regulation to team-level outcomes would require re-examination.

The theory is designed to be refined by evidence, not defended against it. Falsification of specific hypotheses would narrow and sharpen the theory rather than destroy it.

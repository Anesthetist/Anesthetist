# Trauma Treatment Landscape: Comprehensive Review

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed across 46 systematic searches covering established clinical treatments, somatic/body-based approaches, acute/field-deployable interventions, cognitive shift frameworks, pharmacological innovations, and trauma neuroscience mechanisms. All citations attributed to PubMed.

---

## 1. Established Clinical Treatments

### 1.1 Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR)

**Originator:** Francine Shapiro (1987)
**Mechanism:** Bilateral stimulation (eye movements, tapping, tones) during trauma memory recall. Proposed to work via taxing working memory during reconsolidation, reducing vividness and emotionality of traumatic memories. May facilitate interhemispheric communication and activate the orienting response.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — bilateral stimulation engages orienting reflex; some evidence of parasympathetic activation during processing. Does not explicitly target breathing or autonomic regulation.
**Evidence Level:** Strong. Network meta-analysis of 90 trials (6,560 participants) found EMDR produced the largest effect (SMD -2.07 vs waitlist) among all psychological treatments for PTSD. However, individual participant data meta-analysis (8 RCTs, 346 patients) found no significant difference between EMDR and other active treatments. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291720000070) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291723003446)
**Duration/Format:** Typically 6-12 sessions of 60-90 minutes; 8-phase protocol
**Trainability:** Requires licensed clinician training (EMDRIA certification). Cannot be self-administered.
**Key Citations:** PMID 38173121, PMID 32063234
**Relevance to SRL:** EMDR's bilateral stimulation component suggests that simple sensory-motor interventions can disrupt traumatic encoding. SRL's rhythmic breathing protocols may operate on similar dual-attention mechanisms — occupying working memory while processing distress.

### 1.2 Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT)

**Originator:** Patricia Resick (1992)
**Mechanism:** Identifies and challenges "stuck points" — maladaptive beliefs formed around traumatic events (e.g., self-blame, world-as-dangerous). Uses Socratic questioning and written accounts to restructure cognitions.
**Physiological Component:** Minimal — primarily cognitive. No explicit body-based or breathing component.
**Evidence Level:** Strong. First-line VA/DoD guideline treatment. Compared with DBT-PTSD in complex presentations: both highly effective (CPT d=0.98, DBT-PTSD d=1.35). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.2148)
**Duration/Format:** 12 sessions, 50-60 minutes each, individual or group
**Trainability:** Requires licensed clinician. Structured protocol amenable to fidelity monitoring.
**Key Citations:** PMID 32697288, PMID 34924624
**Relevance to SRL:** CPT's "stuck point" concept maps to SRL's cognitive reappraisal micro-moments. A NeuroMinute could scaffold the "notice the stuck point" step before a CPT session, priming the prefrontal cortex.

---

### 1.3 Prolonged Exposure Therapy (PE)

**Originator:** Edna Foa (1986)
**Mechanism:** Based on emotional processing theory. Repeated, systematic exposure to trauma-related stimuli (imaginal and in vivo) to activate the fear structure, allow habituation, and promote new learning that the feared stimulus is not dangerous.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — explicitly monitors and expects autonomic arousal (elevated HR, sweating) during exposure, with the therapeutic mechanism being the observed decrease in arousal across repetitions (habituation/extinction).
**Evidence Level:** Gold standard. Extensive RCT base. VA/DoD and APA first-line recommendation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291720000070)
**Duration/Format:** 8-15 sessions of 90 minutes; includes in-vivo and imaginal exposure
**Trainability:** Requires licensed clinician with specialized training.
**Key Citations:** PMID 34924624, PMID 38652057
**Relevance to SRL:** PE demonstrates that autonomic arousal is not the enemy — it is the vehicle for extinction learning. SRL could position breathing techniques as "arousal titration" tools that keep clinicians within the window of tolerance during their own stress exposures.

---

### 1.4 Trauma-Focused CBT (TF-CBT)

**Originator:** Judith Cohen, Anthony Mannarino, Esther Deblinger
**Mechanism:** Integrates cognitive-behavioral, attachment, humanistic, empowerment, and family therapy principles. Components: psychoeducation, relaxation, affect modulation, cognitive coping, trauma narrative, in vivo mastery, conjoint sessions, safety enhancement.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — includes explicit relaxation skills training and affect regulation components.
**Evidence Level:** Strong, especially in youth. Network meta-analysis of 32 trials found individual TF-CBT forms (especially cognitive therapy for PTSD, SMD -2.94) most effective for youth PTSD. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.13094)
**Duration/Format:** 12-16 sessions; manualized
**Trainability:** Requires licensed clinician. Structured manual supports training fidelity.
**Key Citations:** PMID 31313834
**Relevance to SRL:** TF-CBT's relaxation and affect modulation components are precisely the territory where a NeuroMinute could serve as a between-session homework tool, reinforcing nervous system regulation skills taught in therapy.

---

### 1.5 Narrative Exposure Therapy (NET)

**Originator:** Maggie Schauer, Frank Neuner, Thomas Elbert
**Mechanism:** Client constructs a chronological narrative of their life, embedding traumatic events within biographical context. Transforms fragmented "hot" sensory memories into contextualized autobiographical "cold" memory.
**Physiological Component:** Minimal direct somatic component, but the narrative construction process inherently involves autonomic regulation as the client moves through high-arousal memories.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate-strong. Meta-analysis of RCTs found significant effects on PTSD symptoms (effect size range 0.50-1.20). Particularly effective for multiple/complex trauma and refugee populations. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2018.1550344) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/tra0000922)
**Duration/Format:** 4-12 sessions; shorter formats effective in humanitarian settings
**Trainability:** Relatively high — designed for delivery in low-resource settings by paraprofessionals with brief training. This is significant for SRL.
**Key Citations:** PMID 31007868, PMID 33617284
**Relevance to SRL:** NET's successful deployment by trained non-specialists in crisis settings validates the concept of "minimum viable intervention" — a core SRL principle. The 4-session format demonstrates therapeutic compression is possible.

---

### 1.6 Written Exposure Therapy (WET)

**Originator:** Denise Sloan and Brian Marx
**Mechanism:** Clients write about their traumatic experience in 5 sessions (30-minute writing per session). Combines exposure (written narrative of trauma) with cognitive processing (written instructions prompt reflection on meaning and impact).
**Physiological Component:** Minimal — but writing itself involves motor engagement and may activate embodied processing.
**Evidence Level:** Emerging but promising. Open trial showed very large effect sizes (eta-squared 0.60-0.81) for PTSD reduction in 5 sessions. Now being combined with ketamine infusions for synergistic effects. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/ser0000608) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.24m15622)
**Duration/Format:** 5 sessions, 45 minutes each — the briefest evidence-based PTSD treatment
**Trainability:** High — simple protocol, minimal training required. Self-administrable component (writing).
**Key Citations:** PMID 35113623, PMID 40215385
**Relevance to SRL:** WET is the closest analog to what SRL is building: a brief, structured, low-barrier intervention that produces outsized effects. The 5-session / 45-minute format proves that compression works. SRL's NeuroMinute is an even more radical compression — but WET validates the direction.

## 2. Somatic/Body-Based Approaches

### 2.1 Somatic Experiencing (SE)

**Originator:** Peter Levine
**Mechanism:** Guides attention to interoceptive, kinesthetic, and proprioceptive sensations. Proposes trauma is stored as incomplete defensive responses in the body. Therapy facilitates "pendulation" between states of activation and calm, completing thwarted fight/flight responses and discharging excess autonomic arousal. Core concept: the "core response network" (CRN) — a dynamical system of subcortical autonomic, limbic, motor, and arousal systems that becomes functionally dysregulated in trauma. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00093)
**Physiological Component:** Central — interoception and proprioception are the primary therapeutic tools. Directly targets autonomic regulation.
**Evidence Level:** Emerging. Scoping review found preliminary positive evidence for PTSD symptoms, affective and somatic symptoms, and well-being. Method-specific factors include resource-orientation and use of touch. Overall study quality is mixed, needing more RCTs. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2021.1929023)
**Duration/Format:** Variable, typically 12+ sessions; individual
**Trainability:** Requires 3-year professional training. Not self-administrable in full form, but core principles (body awareness, pendulation) can be simplified.
**Key Citations:** PMID 25699005, PMID 34290845
**Relevance to SRL:** SE is SRL's closest theoretical ancestor. SE validates that interoception + proprioception = effective trauma therapy. SRL's innovation is: can the core SE principles (notice body sensations, pendulate between activation and calm) be compressed into a 60-second protocol? The CRN framework maps directly to SRL's autonomic regulation model.

---

### 2.2 Sensorimotor Psychotherapy

**Originator:** Pat Ogden
**Mechanism:** Integrates body-centered interventions with cognitive and emotional processing. Focuses on tracking and intervening at the level of sensorimotor patterns — posture, gesture, movement, and autonomic responses that encode traumatic experience.
**Physiological Component:** Central — works directly with movement, posture, breath, and body sensation.
**Evidence Level:** Very limited. Only 7 PubMed-indexed studies. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00332747.2006.11023866)
**Duration/Format:** Long-term individual therapy, typically 20+ sessions
**Trainability:** Requires extensive professional training (multi-year). Not self-administrable.
**Key Citations:** PMID 16530597, PMID 32419670
**Relevance to SRL:** Ogden's hierarchy of processing (sensorimotor → emotional → cognitive) supports SRL's bottom-up approach. The idea that posture and breath precede cognition in trauma processing validates prioritizing somatic interventions over talk-based ones.

---

### 2.3 Yoga for PTSD

**Originator:** Various; Bessel van der Kolk championed trauma-sensitive yoga
**Mechanism:** Combines physical postures, breathing exercises, and meditation. Proposed to work through enhanced interoceptive awareness, autonomic regulation (vagal tone improvement), and re-establishing sense of agency over the body.
**Physiological Component:** Central — breathing, movement, interoceptive awareness, HRV improvement.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate. Meta-analysis and systematic reviews show moderate effect sizes. RCTs demonstrate significant PTSD symptom reduction vs. waitlist. Exercise meta-analysis (11 RCTs) found overall effect size of 0.46 for exercise interventions including yoga, with significant effects on sleep (0.51) and quality of life (0.51). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/milmed/usab497)
**Duration/Format:** Typically 10-20 sessions; group or individual; 60-90 minutes per session
**Trainability:** High — can be taught by yoga teachers with trauma-sensitivity training. Partially self-administrable after initial instruction.
**Key Citations:** PMID 34850063, PMID 27502816, PMID 29593576
**Relevance to SRL:** Yoga validates the breath-body-awareness triad that SRL builds on. But yoga requires 60-90 minutes per session — SRL's micro-dosing approach (1-3 minutes) fills a gap yoga cannot.

---

### 2.4 HRV Biofeedback for PTSD

**Originator:** Paul Lehrer, Richard Gevirtz, and others
**Mechanism:** Trains individuals to breathe at their resonant frequency (~6 breaths/min) while receiving real-time heart rate variability feedback. Strengthens baroreflex function, increases vagal tone, and shifts autonomic balance toward parasympathetic dominance.
**Physiological Component:** Central — directly targets autonomic regulation through breathing and HRV feedback.
**Evidence Level:** Promising. Systematic review of 6 RCTs showed significant clinical efficacy for PTSD and depression, superior to treatment as usual and muscle relaxation controls after 4-8 weeks of training. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2021.1929023) (reference from systematic review)
**Duration/Format:** Typically 8-10 sessions with daily home practice; 20-30 minutes per session
**Trainability:** Moderate — requires biofeedback equipment and initial clinician guidance. Home practice component is self-administrable. Wearable technology is making this increasingly accessible.
**Key Citations:** PMID 27075221, PMID 40386190, PMID 39136855
**Relevance to SRL:** HRV biofeedback IS SRL's core modality. SRL adds: (1) compression to micro-doses, (2) context-specific protocols (pre-intubation, post-adverse-event), (3) guided instruction eliminating need for biofeedback equipment, and (4) integration with the Gap Moment Training framework. This is not competition — it is SRL's evidence base.

### 2.5 Neurofeedback for PTSD

**Originator:** Various; Bessel van der Kolk, Ruth Lanius, and others have advanced its use in PTSD
**Mechanism:** Provides real-time feedback of brain activity (EEG or fMRI) to enable self-regulation of neural patterns. Targets alpha/theta ratios, or specific brain regions (amygdala downregulation via fMRI-NF).
**Physiological Component:** Indirect — targets brain activity which then cascades to autonomic regulation.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate-strong. Updated meta-analysis (17 RCTs, 628 patients) found clinically meaningful reductions in PTSD symptoms with moderate/high GRADE quality. Effect sizes increased at follow-up, suggesting durable effects. Driven by newer fMRI-guided approaches. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1323485)
**Duration/Format:** Typically 20-40 sessions; 30-60 minutes each; requires specialized equipment
**Trainability:** Low — requires expensive equipment and trained technician. Not self-administrable with current technology.
**Key Citations:** PMID 38577405, PMID 37732560
**Relevance to SRL:** Neurofeedback demonstrates the brain's capacity for self-regulation — supporting the premise that briefer, lower-tech approaches (breathing) can achieve similar autonomic shifts via the vagal afferent pathway rather than the cortical pathway.

---

### 2.6 Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) for PTSD

**Originator:** Various; adapted from epilepsy/depression treatment
**Mechanism:** Electrical stimulation of the vagus nerve (invasive or transcutaneous/taVNS) to enhance vagal tone, reduce sympathetic hyperarousal, and modulate fear extinction circuits. Engages the nucleus tractus solitarius, locus coeruleus, and amygdala.
**Physiological Component:** Central — direct autonomic nerve stimulation.
**Evidence Level:** Emerging. Preclinical evidence strong; clinical evidence growing. Studies suggest taVNS enhances fear extinction and reduces PTSD symptoms. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1323485)
**Duration/Format:** Daily self-administered sessions (taVNS) or implanted device
**Trainability:** taVNS is self-administrable with device. Invasive VNS requires surgery.
**Key Citations:** PMID 40097094, PMID 37575296, PMID 34990376
**Relevance to SRL:** VNS validates the vagal pathway as a treatment target. SRL's resonant breathing achieves vagal stimulation non-invasively — a critical advantage for clinical adoption. SRL can position itself as "vagus nerve training without the device."

---

### 2.7 Acupuncture for PTSD

**Originator:** Traditional Chinese Medicine, adapted for PTSD
**Mechanism:** Needle insertion at specific acupoints proposed to modulate autonomic nervous system, reduce sympathetic activation, and enhance endogenous opioid release. Recent RCT demonstrated improvement in fear-conditioned extinction.
**Physiological Component:** Central — modulates autonomic function via peripheral nerve stimulation.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate. Large RCT (n=93 combat veterans) showed large treatment effect (Cohen d=1.17 verum) with moderate between-group effect favoring verum over sham (d=0.63). Significantly improved fear extinction — a key PTSD mechanism. Low withdrawal rates. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2023.5651)
**Duration/Format:** 24 sessions over 15 weeks; 1-hour sessions, twice weekly
**Trainability:** Requires licensed acupuncturist. Not self-administrable.
**Key Citations:** PMID 38381417, PMID 39944616
**Relevance to SRL:** The JAMA Psychiatry acupuncture RCT is notable because it measured fear extinction as a mechanism — and found it. This validates that peripheral interventions (needles, breathing) can modulate central fear circuits.

## 3. Pharmacological/Procedural Innovations

### 3.1 Stellate Ganglion Block (SGB)

**Originator:** Eugene Lipov (repurposed from pain management)
**Mechanism:** Local anesthetic injection into the stellate ganglion (cervical sympathetic chain) to reset sympathetic hyperactivity. Reduces nerve growth factor (NGF) and norepinephrine levels. Acts as a "sympathetic reset."
**Physiological Component:** Central — directly blocks sympathetic output.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate. Multisite RCT (n=113 active-duty service members) showed SGB superior to sham (adjusted CAPS-5 change: -12.6 vs -6.1, p=0.01). Reviews suggest promise for PTSD, long-COVID, and other sympathetic overdrive conditions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.3474) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.35174) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/joacp.joacp_304_22)
**Duration/Format:** 2 injections, 2 weeks apart; office-based procedure; effects within hours
**Trainability:** Requires anesthesiologist/pain specialist — highly relevant for CRNAs who could administer this.
**Key Citations:** PMID 31693083, PMID 36949968, PMID 38919437
**Relevance to SRL:** SGB is a CRNA-deliverable intervention. SRL could develop a clinical pathway: SGB for acute sympathetic reset + Gap Moment Training for sustained autonomic regulation. This is a compelling product story for the CRNA audience.

---

### 3.2 MDMA-Assisted Therapy

**Originator:** MAPS (Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies); Michael Mithoefer
**Mechanism:** MDMA enhances release of serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine, oxytocin, and cortisol. Reduces amygdala activation and increases amygdala-hippocampus connectivity, enabling reprocessing of traumatic memories without overwhelming fear. May work via both memory reconsolidation and fear extinction pathways. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2018.03.003)
**Physiological Component:** Significant — autonomic effects include cardiovascular changes, temperature regulation. The "window" of reduced fear/increased trust is itself a physiological state.
**Evidence Level:** Strong. Two Phase 3 RCTs: first (n=90) showed 67% remission vs 32% placebo (d=0.91); second (n=104) showed CAPS-5 change of -23.7 vs -14.8 (d=0.7). FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01336-3) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02565-4)
**Duration/Format:** 3 medication sessions (8 hours each) with preparation and integration therapy; total ~42 hours over 12-18 weeks
**Trainability:** Requires specially trained therapist dyad. Not self-administrable. Regulatory status uncertain.
**Key Citations:** PMID 37709999, PMID 33972795, PMID 29524515
**Relevance to SRL:** MDMA-AT achieves what SRL aspires to: rapid, durable trauma resolution. The mechanism insight is that lowering the "threat threshold" allows reprocessing. SRL's breathing protocols aim to achieve a similar (if smaller) autonomic shift — widening the window of tolerance without pharmacology.

---

### 3.3 Psilocybin for Trauma-Related Disorders

**Originator:** Various research groups; UCSF, Johns Hopkins, Imperial College London
**Mechanism:** 5-HT2A receptor agonism promotes neuroplasticity (hippocampal dendritic complexity, BDNF, mTOR signaling). Animal studies show single-dose psilocybin facilitates rapid and sustained fear extinction via hippocampal neuroplasticity. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/CM9.0000000000002647)
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — significant autonomic and neuroplastic effects.
**Evidence Level:** Preclinical strong; clinical for PTSD specifically is early. Open-label study in traumatized AIDS survivors showed PTSD symptom reduction. No completed RCT for PTSD yet. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2022_366)
**Duration/Format:** 1-2 medication sessions with preparation and integration
**Trainability:** Requires specially trained therapists and controlled setting. Not self-administrable.
**Key Citations:** PMID 35711024, PMID 37000971, PMID 32098487
**Relevance to SRL:** Psilocybin's mechanism — promoting neuroplasticity that enables fear extinction — is the "why" behind SRL's repeated micro-dose approach. If each NeuroMinute slightly enhances vagal-mediated neuroplasticity, the cumulative effect mirrors psilocybin's single-dose neuroplastic burst at a lower intensity over longer timeframes.

---

### 3.4 Ketamine for PTSD

**Originator:** Various; adapted from depression treatment
**Mechanism:** NMDA receptor antagonist that rapidly increases BDNF and promotes synaptic plasticity. Proposed to open a "window of plasticity" for reconsolidation of traumatic memories. New combination approach: ketamine + Written Exposure Therapy shows promising synergy.
**Physiological Component:** Significant — dissociative and autonomic effects during infusion.
**Evidence Level:** Emerging. Open-label trial combining 6 ketamine infusions with 5 WET sessions showed large effect (Cohen d=1.9), with 69% response rate and durability at 6 months. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.24m15622)
**Duration/Format:** 6 infusions over 2 weeks + 5 WET sessions; CRNA-deliverable
**Trainability:** Infusion requires anesthesia provider (CRNA ideal). WET component has high trainability.
**Key Citations:** PMID 40215385, PMID 35509843
**Relevance to SRL:** Ketamine + WET is another CRNA-deliverable intervention pathway. SRL could position as: "If you're administering ketamine for PTSD, Gap Moment Training is the integration protocol that maintains gains between infusions."

---

### 3.5 Propranolol for Memory Reconsolidation

**Originator:** Karim Nader, Alain Brunet (clinical application)
**Mechanism:** Beta-adrenergic blocker administered during memory retrieval to disrupt protein synthesis required for reconsolidation, theoretically weakening the emotional charge of traumatic memories while preserving declarative content.
**Physiological Component:** Direct — blocks sympathetic/adrenergic pathways.
**Evidence Level:** Mixed. Meta-analysis of 7 studies found propranolol significantly improved PTSD symptoms (Z=2.32, p=0.02) with moderate effect size. However, earlier meta-analysis found lack of evidence for routine clinical use. Propranolol did reduce heart rate after trauma memory recall. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2025.1545493) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2022.03.045)
**Duration/Format:** Single dose administered during guided trauma memory retrieval; 4-6 weekly sessions
**Trainability:** Requires clinician for protocol delivery. Medication is simple.
**Key Citations:** PMID 39944616, PMID 35405409, PMID 26487439
**Relevance to SRL:** Propranolol's mechanism highlights a key insight: the moment of memory retrieval is a therapeutic window. SRL's Gap Moment is essentially this same window — the moment between stimulus and response when intervention is possible — achieved via awareness rather than pharmacology.

## 4. Acute/Field-Deployable Techniques

### 4.1 Psychological First Aid (PFA)

**Originator:** WHO, National Child Traumatic Stress Network, NCPTSD
**Mechanism:** Five core elements: promote sense of safety, calming, self/community efficacy, connectedness, and hope. Not a clinical treatment but a structured first-response framework for the immediate aftermath of trauma.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — "calming" component implicitly targets autonomic downregulation but is not protocol-specific about how.
**Evidence Level:** Low-moderate. Systematic review (12 studies, 1,437 participants) found all studies suggest positive impact on anxiety, depression, PTSD, and distress. But only 1 was an RCT, risk of bias was high, and there is "an imbalance between popular support for PFA and scant evidence of outcome data." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jts.22888)
**Duration/Format:** Single encounter; minutes to hours; delivered in crisis settings
**Trainability:** Very high — designed for non-specialists; 1-2 day training
**Key Citations:** PMID 36300605, PMID 32727814
**Relevance to SRL:** PFA's "calming" component is where SRL fills a critical gap. PFA tells responders to "promote calming" but provides no specific protocol for HOW. A NeuroMinute is the missing implementation layer — the specific, timed, breathing-based protocol that operationalizes PFA's calming principle.

---

### 4.2 Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISD)

**Originator:** Jeffrey Mitchell (1983)
**Mechanism:** Structured group discussion following a critical incident. Seven phases: introduction, fact, thought, reaction, symptom, teaching, re-entry. Designed to normalize reactions and identify those needing further support.
**Physiological Component:** Minimal — talk-based. No somatic or body component.
**Evidence Level:** Controversial/Negative. Multiple reviews and meta-analyses found CISD either ineffective or potentially harmful — may impede natural recovery by prematurely forcing verbal processing of trauma. Cochrane review found no evidence supporting single-session debriefing. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1540-2487(03)00008-7)
**Duration/Format:** Single session, 1-3 hours, within 24-72 hours post-incident; group format
**Trainability:** High — brief training; group facilitation skills
**Key Citations:** PMID 11232381, PMID 12704320
**Relevance to SRL:** CISD's failure is instructive. Forcing cognitive processing before the nervous system is regulated can re-traumatize. SRL's approach — regulate first, process later — is neurobiologically sound. This is a key differentiator: SRL leads with the body, not the narrative.

---

### 4.3 Emotional Freedom Technique (EFT / "Tapping")

**Originator:** Gary Craig (1990s), based on Roger Callahan's Thought Field Therapy
**Mechanism:** Self-tapping on acupressure points while making verbal statements about distressing thoughts/memories. Proposed to work via dual-attention processing (similar to EMDR) and acupoint stimulation affecting autonomic regulation.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — rhythmic tapping engages proprioception and possibly acupoint-mediated autonomic effects.
**Evidence Level:** Emerging-moderate. Included in network meta-analysis showing positive effects for youth PTSD. Systematic review found 4 studies of moderate quality. Now being tested in RCT against Written Exposure Therapy. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2022-070389)
**Duration/Format:** 4-10 sessions; can be self-administered after instruction; 15-60 minutes
**Trainability:** Very high — explicitly designed for self-administration. Brief training sufficient.
**Key Citations:** PMID 31313834, PMID 37349095, PMID 26749196
**Relevance to SRL:** EFT proves that self-administered, body-based micro-interventions can move the needle on PTSD symptoms. Its self-administrability and low barrier to entry are exactly what SRL is building. EFT's weakness is lack of rigorous evidence — SRL should not repeat this mistake.

---

### 4.4 Havening Techniques

**Originator:** Ronald Ruden
**Mechanism:** Psychosensory therapy combining gentle self-touch (stroking arms, palms, face) with guided distraction/visualization. Proposed to work by generating delta waves through touch, which depotentiate amygdala-encoded traumatic memories via AMPA receptor internalization.
**Physiological Component:** Central — touch-based autonomic regulation and delta wave induction.
**Evidence Level:** Very limited. Only 3 PubMed-indexed studies. One theoretical paper, one feasibility study for perinatal resilience, one novel theory paper. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.explore.2018.05.005) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgwh.2025.1619273) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26635/6965.6955)
**Duration/Format:** Single sessions; can be self-administered; minutes to complete
**Trainability:** Very high — designed for self-application
**Key Citations:** PMID 41066786, PMID 40698199, PMID 30598286
**Relevance to SRL:** Havening's self-touch component is interesting — it suggests that proprioceptive self-soothing (which is free and always available) can modulate threat circuits. SRL could potentially incorporate gentle self-touch cues into its protocols (e.g., "place hand on chest during resonant breathing").

---

### 4.5 Grounding Techniques

**Originator:** Various; widely used in clinical practice
**Mechanism:** Redirect attention to present-moment sensory experience (5-4-3-2-1 technique, ice, strong scents, physical contact with ground) to interrupt dissociative or flashback episodes and re-orient to current safety.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — engages exteroception and proprioception; adopting balanced posture enhances breathing and sensory capabilities. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci14040385)
**Evidence Level:** Very low — foundational to clinical practice but almost no PubMed-indexed outcome research. Only 7 studies found.
**Duration/Format:** Seconds to minutes; self-administered; immediate deployment
**Trainability:** Extremely high — can be taught in minutes, practiced independently
**Key Citations:** PMID 38672034, PMID 40575850
**Relevance to SRL:** Grounding techniques are the closest existing practice to what SRL's NeuroMinute does. But they lack: (1) specificity (no timed protocol), (2) physiological optimization (no resonant frequency targeting), (3) measurement (no HRV feedback). SRL turns grounding from an informal skill into a precision intervention.

---

### 4.6 Digital/App-Based PTSD Interventions

**Originator:** Various (VA's PTSD Coach, Breath2Relax, etc.)
**Mechanism:** Self-guided psychoeducation, symptom tracking, relaxation exercises, and trauma processing via smartphone applications.
**Physiological Component:** Variable — some apps include breathing exercises and biofeedback.
**Evidence Level:** Growing. Network meta-analysis found self-help with support (SMD -1.46 vs waitlist) was as effective as TF-CBT. Self-help without support also showed positive effects but smaller. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291720000070)
**Duration/Format:** Self-paced; minutes per session; ongoing
**Trainability:** Maximum — fully self-administered
**Key Citations:** PMID 32063234, PMID 41527753
**Relevance to SRL:** The network meta-analysis finding that supported self-help equals TF-CBT in effect size is SRL's strongest market validation. The key word is "supported" — SRL's advantage is the guided, structured nature of NeuroMinute protocols, not just an app with general breathing exercises.

## 5. Cognitive Shift Frameworks

### 5.1 Mindfulness-Based Interventions (MBIs) for PTSD

**Originator:** Jon Kabat-Zinn (MBSR); various adaptations for trauma
**Mechanism:** Cultivating non-judgmental present-moment awareness to reduce reactivity to trauma-related stimuli, increase distress tolerance, and promote metacognitive awareness.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — some protocols include body scan and breathing; effects on HRV, cortisol, and inflammatory markers documented.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate. Meta-analysis found MBIs effective for reducing psychological distress, stress, and depression in nurses. Overall review found MBIs effective for many conditions but evidence specifically for PTSD remains "inconclusive or preliminary." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocn.16265) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bmb/ldab005)
**Duration/Format:** Typically 8-week programs; 2-2.5 hours per week plus daily home practice
**Trainability:** Moderate-high — can be delivered by trained facilitators; self-practice possible
**Key Citations:** PMID 35187740, PMID 33884400
**Relevance to SRL:** MBIs are SRL's philosophical cousin but a different product. MBI programs are 8 weeks, 2+ hours/week — a heavy time commitment that most CRNAs cannot sustain. SRL's 60-second format addresses the same mechanisms (awareness, non-reactivity, breathing) at a dose clinicians will actually use.

---

### 5.2 Internal Family Systems (IFS) for Trauma

**Originator:** Richard Schwartz
**Mechanism:** Conceptualizes the psyche as containing multiple "parts" (protectors, exiles, managers) organized around a core "Self." Trauma creates exiled parts carrying pain and burden, protected by extreme parts (firefighters, managers). Therapy facilitates Self-leadership to unburden exiled parts.
**Physiological Component:** Indirect but significant — accessing "Self" state correlates with parasympathetic activation, calm breathing, and reduced arousal.
**Evidence Level:** Early but promising. Proof-of-concept study of online group IFS (PARTS program: 16 weeks group + 8 individual sessions) showed 53% clinically meaningful PTSD response by Week 24. Improved decentering, self-compassion, and emotion regulation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/tra0001688)
**Duration/Format:** 16+ sessions; individual or group; recently tested online
**Trainability:** Requires Level 1 IFS training minimum. Not self-administrable in full form, but "parts awareness" concepts are teachable.
**Key Citations:** PMID 38934934
**Relevance to SRL:** IFS's concept of "Self-energy" — the calm, curious, compassionate state — maps to SRL's "regulated state" achieved through breathing. The observation that IFS improved decentering and self-compassion suggests these are outcomes SRL should measure.

---

### 5.3 Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) for PTSD

**Originator:** Marsha Linehan (DBT); Martin Bohus (DBT-PTSD adaptation)
**Mechanism:** DBT-PTSD combines skills training (mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness) with trauma-focused cognitive-behavioral interventions. Skills-based approach to managing overwhelming emotions before and during exposure work.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — distress tolerance skills include ice diving (vagal maneuver), paced breathing, and body-based grounding.
**Evidence Level:** Strong for complex PTSD. Landmark RCT (n=193 women with childhood abuse-related PTSD + BPD features) showed DBT-PTSD superior to CPT (d=1.35 vs d=0.98, group difference d=0.33). Higher completion rates, remission, and reliable recovery. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.2148)
**Duration/Format:** Up to 45 sessions over 1 year; intensive
**Trainability:** Requires extensive clinical training.
**Key Citations:** PMID 32697288, PMID 38214629
**Relevance to SRL:** DBT's distress tolerance skills are SRL-adjacent micro-interventions: ice diving (vagal), paced breathing, grounding. DBT validates that these somatic skills are necessary BEFORE cognitive processing. SRL's NeuroMinute could serve as a pre-session DBT skill rehearsal tool.

---

### 5.4 Compassion-Focused Therapy (CFT) for Trauma

**Originator:** Paul Gilbert
**Mechanism:** Targets shame and self-criticism — core features of trauma — by activating the soothing/affiliative system (parasympathetic, oxytocin-mediated). Uses compassionate imagery, soothing breathing rhythm, and compassionate letter writing.
**Physiological Component:** Moderate — "soothing rhythm breathing" is a core technique, explicitly designed to activate the parasympathetic nervous system.
**Evidence Level:** Low-moderate for trauma specifically. Conceptual reviews describe strong theoretical rationale. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1744629518773843)
**Duration/Format:** Typically 12-20 sessions; individual or group
**Trainability:** Moderate — soothing breathing rhythm component is highly teachable and self-administrable.
**Key Citations:** PMID 29759022, PMID 40062791
**Relevance to SRL:** CFT's "soothing rhythm breathing" is essentially what SRL delivers. CFT provides the theoretical justification for why slow, rhythmic breathing works for trauma: it activates the affiliative/soothing system that shame and self-criticism deactivate. SRL should cite Gilbert's three-system model.

---

### 5.5 Polyvagal-Informed Therapy

**Originator:** Stephen Porges (theory); Deb Dana (clinical application)
**Mechanism:** Uses polyvagal theory's three-tiered autonomic hierarchy (ventral vagal/social engagement, sympathetic/fight-flight, dorsal vagal/shutdown) to map client states and guide interventions that promote ventral vagal activation through safety cues, co-regulation, and body-based practices.
**Physiological Component:** Central — the entire framework is organized around autonomic states.
**Evidence Level:** Low-moderate for direct clinical application. Theoretical papers describe application in creative arts and psychomotor therapies for trauma. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1382007) Note: polyvagal theory itself is debated in the literature — the therapeutic applications remain useful regardless of the neuroanatomical specifics.
**Duration/Format:** Variable; integrated into other modalities
**Trainability:** Moderate — the three-state model is highly teachable; specific interventions vary.
**Key Citations:** PMID 27044181, PMID 38840744, PMID 28817114
**Relevance to SRL:** Polyvagal theory is already embedded in SRL's conceptual framework. The clinical implication: SRL protocols should map explicitly to polyvagal states (e.g., "this breathing pattern targets ventral vagal activation"). Caution: reference therapeutic utility without over-relying on polyvagal neuroanatomy claims given ongoing debate.

## 6. Second Victim / Clinician-Specific Interventions

### 6.1 Second Victim Support Frameworks

**Originator:** Albert Wu (coined term, 2000); Susan Scott (forYOU program)
**Mechanism:** Healthcare providers who are involved in adverse patient events experience "second victim" trauma — guilt, shame, PTSD symptoms, depression, loss of confidence. Frameworks provide tiered support: (1) immediate emotional first aid from peers, (2) trained peer support, (3) professional referral.
**Physiological Component:** Minimal in existing frameworks — focused on emotional/cognitive support. No body-based intervention component.
**Evidence Level:** Moderate for the phenomenon; low for specific interventions. The SVEST (Second Victim Experience and Support Tool) has been validated for organizational assessment. Most desired support: "a respected peer to discuss details of what happened." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/PTS.0000000000000129) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cnur.2023.11.011) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2017.12.001)
**Duration/Format:** Tiered: immediate (minutes), short-term (days-weeks), long-term (referral)
**Trainability:** Peer support component is highly trainable.
**Key Citations:** PMID 25162208, PMID 38272580, PMID 29366616, PMID 39252590, PMID 34592010
**Relevance to SRL:** This is SRL's primary clinical insertion point. Second victim support frameworks tell you WHO needs help and WHEN — but not HOW to regulate the nervous system in the acute moment. SRL fills this gap: the NeuroMinute IS the somatic first-aid protocol for second victims. The SVEST data showing peers are the preferred support pathway validates SRL's peer-deliverable design.

---

### 6.2 Healthcare Worker Resilience Training

**Originator:** Various
**Mechanism:** Preventive interventions combining mindfulness, CBT, and skills training to build resilience before traumatic exposure.
**Physiological Component:** Variable — some include breathing and body-based skills.
**Evidence Level:** Very low certainty evidence (Cochrane review, 44 RCTs, 6,892+ participants). Compared to controls, resilience training showed moderate effects on resilience (SMD 0.45), small effects on depression (SMD -0.29), and moderate effects on stress (SMD -0.61). No significant effect on anxiety or well-being. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012527.pub2)
**Duration/Format:** Highly variable; typically group-based, high-intensity (>12 hours/sessions)
**Trainability:** Moderate — group delivery formats
**Key Citations:** PMID 32627860
**Relevance to SRL:** The Cochrane review finding that resilience training has moderate stress-reduction effects but requires >12 hours highlights SRL's value proposition: achieve comparable autonomic effects in micro-doses that fit clinical schedules. The finding that heterogeneous interventions produce heterogeneous results supports SRL's specific, protocol-driven approach.

## 7. Mechanisms of Trauma Resolution

### 7.1 Memory Reconsolidation

When a consolidated memory is retrieved, it enters a labile state and must undergo protein synthesis-dependent reconsolidation to re-stabilize. This window (approximately 1-6 hours post-retrieval) represents a therapeutic opportunity: interventions during this window can modify, weaken, or strengthen the original memory. Boundary conditions include: memory must be actively destabilized (prediction error is required), and very strong or very old memories may resist destabilization. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2016_463) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2183/pjab.96.008)

**Key Citations:** PMID 27885549, PMID 32161213

**SRL Implication:** The reconsolidation window is the neurobiological basis for the Gap Moment. The moment of arousal recognition (the "gap") is the prediction error that destabilizes the stress response. The breathing intervention during this window may facilitate reconsolidation of a calmer autonomic response pattern.

### 7.2 Fear Extinction vs. Reconsolidation

Fear extinction and reconsolidation are distinct but interacting processes. Extinction creates a new inhibitory memory that competes with the original fear memory — but the fear memory persists (explaining relapse). Reconsolidation actually modifies the original trace. Key finding: reconsolidation and extinction interact — brief reactivation followed by extinction training during the reconsolidation window may produce more durable fear reduction than standard extinction. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2183/pjab.96.008)

**SRL Implication:** If SRL's micro-interventions can target the reconsolidation window (brief arousal recognition + immediate vagal intervention), they may achieve modification of the original stress response rather than just temporary extinction. This is why timing matters more than duration.

### 7.3 Window of Tolerance Model

Proposed by Dan Siegel, the window of tolerance describes the zone of optimal autonomic arousal where a person can process information, feel emotions, and respond adaptively. Trauma narrows this window, creating rapid oscillation between hyperarousal (fight/flight/panic) and hypoarousal (shutdown/dissociation/collapse). The model proposes that ascending monoaminergic tracts (especially dopaminergic) drive these oscillations, and imbalanced mesolimbic/mesocortical dopamine systems alter prefrontal control of midbrain defense responses. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881109354930)

**Key Citations:** PMID 20093318

**SRL Implication:** SRL's core therapeutic goal is window-of-tolerance expansion. Each NeuroMinute is a micro-exercise in returning to the window from a hyperaroused state. Over time, this widens the window itself — increasing the clinician's capacity to remain regulated during high-stress events.

### 7.4 Autonomic Dysregulation in PTSD

PTSD involves fundamental disruption of autonomic nervous system regulation: reduced HRV, blunted baroreflex sensitivity, elevated resting sympathetic tone, disrupted circadian rhythmicity, and impaired autonomic flexibility (inability to shift between sympathetic and parasympathetic states appropriately). Sleep disruption and circadian misalignment may be both risk factors for and consequences of PTSD. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2021.1956746)

**Key Citations:** PMID 34603634, PMID 20093318

**SRL Implication:** This is SRL's core scientific premise: PTSD is, at its foundation, an autonomic regulation disorder. Every SRL intervention targets this directly: resonant breathing → baroreflex training → HRV improvement → autonomic flexibility restoration.

### 7.5 Interoception as Therapeutic Pathway

Interventions targeting interoception can manipulate physiological systems at neural, behavioral, and psychological levels. Pathways include: vagus nerve neuromodulation, slow breathing to change respiratory rate and depth, and mindfulness-based awareness practices. These interventions alter interoceptive signal representation and attention, which cascades to interconnected physiological systems. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2020.09.010)

**Key Citations:** PMID 33378657

**SRL Implication:** This Trends in Neurosciences review provides the mechanistic roadmap for SRL: breathing → interoceptive signal change → autonomic system modulation → improved functioning. SRL should cite this framework explicitly in its clinical evidence chain.

### 7.6 Dissociation and Autonomic Shutdown

Dissociation in trauma involves dorsal vagal activation (polyvagal freeze/shutdown response) and altered sensory processing. Neuroscience reveals disrupted connectivity between prefrontal cortex, insula, and subcortical threat-detection circuits. Grounding and embodiment techniques work by re-engaging ventral vagal circuits and restoring interoceptive connection. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci14040385)

**Key Citations:** PMID 38672034, PMID 34630181

**SRL Implication:** For dissociative presentations, standard "calm down" approaches may be counterproductive (the person is already in shutdown). SRL protocols need a "re-activation" pathway — orienting, gentle movement, sensory engagement — before calming breathing. This maps to Levine's pendulation concept.

### SRL's Unique Position Statement

Gap Moment Training and the NeuroMinute occupy the space between grounding techniques (informal, unprotocoled, unmeasured) and HRV biofeedback (formal, equipment-dependent, time-intensive). SRL delivers:

- **The precision of biofeedback** (resonant frequency targeting, HRV measurement) **without the equipment**
- **The accessibility of grounding** (seconds, self-administered, anywhere) **with clinical-grade specificity**
- **The somatic depth of Somatic Experiencing** (interoception, autonomic regulation) **compressed to minimum effective dose**

- **The clinician-specificity of second victim programs** (designed for healthcare context) **with a body-based intervention none of them currently provide**

This is not a replacement for any of these treatments. It is the connective tissue — the autonomic regulation substrate — that makes all of them work better.

# Neural Transition Failure

The failure to complete an autonomic state transition — returning to baseline after a stressor, or shifting to the appropriate state for the next task. Neural transition failure leaves "residual sympathetic bleed" that compounds across a shift, degrading performance cumulatively.

## Definition

High-performing operators are trained for the event, not for the transition. Neural transition failure is a clinical category describing the failure to return to baseline, failure to re-enter optimal task state, or failure to shift appropriately between autonomic modes. It is the root cause behind most of the [[error-archetypes-qrc]].

## Mechanism

After a stressful event (difficult intubation, patient crisis, interpersonal conflict), the sympathetic activation should resolve during the next transition. When it doesn't:
- Cortisol and norepinephrine remain elevated
- Attentional narrowing persists into the next case
- Working memory capacity is reduced
- Emotional reactivity threshold is lowered
- Each subsequent stressor adds to the unresolved load ([[bandwidth-saturation-micro-events]])

## Clinical Impact

- Archetype 6 from QRC (recovery failure after stress spike) is neural transition failure in action
- Cumulative transition failures across a shift produce the "brittle system" state where one more stressor causes a visible error
- 90% of the compounding is invisible — the clinician doesn't know they're carrying residual activation

## Somnistics Solution

[[gap-moment-training]] is specifically designed to prevent neural transition failure by ensuring complete autonomic reset at each transition point. A single 60-second [[neurominute]] between cases can interrupt the compounding cascade.

## Evidence Base

- [[sezer-2025-meditation-autonomic-nervous-system]] — long-term meditation produces durable ANS flexibility; the opposite of neural transition failure. Cardiac resonance and respiratory coupling as markers of successful transition completion
- [[rayani-2025-brain-stimulation-mindfulness-ptsd]] — PTSD involves chronic neural transition failure (stuck in sympathetic activation); rTMS-enhanced mindfulness provides evidence for reversibility

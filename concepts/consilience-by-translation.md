The method of discovering that different authors in different fields are describing the same mechanism using different vocabulary, then creating a unified term that bridges them. Randy's core epistemic method — physiology, contemplative traditions, and systems science converge when you translate between their languages.

This is Adler's syntopical reading Step 2 (translate and synthesize) elevated to a discovery method. The convergence is the signal: when the same pattern appears independently in multiple domains, expressed in different vocabulary, that pattern has high epistemic confidence.

Examples from Randy's work:
- Dellanna's "ruin" = van der Kolk's irreversible trauma = the CRNA's failed airway → [[non-ergodic-risk]]
- HRV = biology's volatility index (RMSSD as physiology's sigma)
- Springett's consciousness stages = expertise deliberate-practice stages = nested skill ladders
- Gurdjieff's "intervals" = clinical state transitions = developmental inflection points

Randy's clearest articulation of this method: requesting "novel concepts arising from the multisynthesis of each work, deeply enhabfed cross referencing. Similar concepts but different wording." Find the same thing said in different words across domains, and the convergence is the signal. This is how SRL generates concepts — not from a single domain but from the intersection of several.

Connected to [[relevance-realization]] (both involve identifying what matters across multiple frames), [[neuroepistemic-layer]] (the epistemic infrastructure that makes translation possible), and [[somnistics]] (the field itself is a product of consilience by translation).

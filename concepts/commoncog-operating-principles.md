# Commoncog Operating Principles

Cedric Chin's Commoncog frameworks as the operating system for SRL's execution. Not business theory — operational principles that govern how we make decisions, extract knowledge, measure progress, and compete.

## Core Thesis

> "Scaling is not about being clever. It's about measuring what's true, fast."

The company that cycles through Observe → Orient → Decide → Act fastest wins. Not the company with the best strategy deck. The company with the best feedback loops.

---

## The Seven Operating Principles

### 1. Tacit Knowledge Is the Moat

Cedric's central insight: the most valuable knowledge in any organization is tacit — it lives in people's heads, it's hard to articulate, and it walks out the door when they leave.

**SRL application:** The vault IS systematic tacit knowledge extraction. Randy's 28 years of clinical expertise → 270+ structured notes → queryable by any AI agent via MCP. But the extraction isn't done until every `clinical_interpretation` field is filled. Those 71 empty fields are where Randy's tacit reading of the science lives. Each one filled is more moat.

**Operating rule:** Every week, Randy fills 5 clinical interpretation fields. 15 minutes each. In 14 weeks, all 71 are done. The vault becomes the deepest tacit knowledge repository in applied nervous system training.

### 2. Recognition-Primed Decision Making (RPD)

Gary Klein's framework (via Cedric): experts don't weigh options analytically. They recognize patterns from experience and mentally simulate the first plausible option. If the simulation works, they act. If not, they try the next pattern.

**SRL application:** This is EXACTLY what Randy's Rush care planning trained — not analysis but pattern recognition so fast it looks like intuition. The certification's capability test should test RPD, not analytical knowledge. The clinical scenarios (failed intubation, post-hemorrhage fog, team conflict) test: "Do you recognize the pattern and know what to do?"

**Operating rule:** When making business decisions, don't analyze options. Ask: "Have we seen this pattern before? What happened? What does simulation say about this option?"

### 3. The Joy of Small Markets

Cedric's argument: small, concentrated markets are better for startups than large diffuse ones. You can dominate a small market, learn the patterns, build the moat, then expand.

**SRL application:** CRNAs (60,000 US) is the textbook small market:
- Concentrated (AANA as single professional body)
- High-income (CRNAs earn $200K+, pay for own CE)
- Identity-driven (strong professional identity, CRNA ≠ nurse)
- Accessible (CRNA Facebook groups, AANA conferences, state associations)
- Your credibility is unassailable (28 years, AANA #071955)

**Operating rule:** Win CRNAs completely before expanding to adjacent markets. 10% penetration of 60K = 6,000 certified practitioners = $600K ARR minimum. Prove the model works in the small market before touching anything else.

### 4. Expertise Acceleration

How to get good at things faster: deliberate practice on the actual task (not abstractions), tight feedback loops, and extracting mental models from experts.

**SRL application:** The CE course IS an expertise acceleration program for nervous system regulation. But the same principle applies to SRL's business execution:
- Don't theorize about pricing — test $99 CE course on CRNA Facebook group this week
- Don't analyze which narrative works — write three versions, put them on landing pages, measure clicks
- Don't guess what CRNAs want — the 459-CRNA survey already told you

**Operating rule:** The speed of learning is the speed of the company. Shorten every feedback loop. Weekly measurement, not quarterly reviews.

### 5. Putting Practice to Theory (The Triad)

Cedric's operational principle: don't theorize then test. Test then theorize. Reality → pattern → framework. Not framework → implementation → hope.

**SRL application:** The vault has 270 notes of theory and architecture. It's extraordinary. And now it's time to test it against reality:
- Ship the CE course. See what happens.
- Run one Catalyst Session workshop. Document what CRNAs actually say.
- Record sales conversations. Write down what phrases make them lean in.

**Operating rule:** Every week, one cheap experiment that tests a belief against reality. If the experiment confirms the belief, accelerate. If it doesn't, update the belief. The vault updates in real-time as reality teaches.

### 6. OODA Speed

Boyd's OODA loop applied to business competition: Observe → Orient → Decide → Act. The competitor who cycles fastest wins — not because they have better strategy, but because they've already adapted while the competitor is still orienting.

**SRL's OODA infrastructure (already running):**
- **Observe:** Daily market insights bot (Reddit, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram). Weekly competitive intelligence. PubMed mining pipeline.
- **Orient:** 7 Powers scorecard. Category theory morphism analysis. Vault evidence chains.
- **Decide:** Randy + Jason + Livio commit together.
- **Act:** Weekly sprints. Ship weekly. Measure weekly.

**Operating rule:** OODA cycle time target: 1 week. Not 1 quarter. Every Monday: what did we observe? Every Wednesday: what does it mean? Every Friday: what are we shipping?

### 7. Document Sales Conversations

Cedric's practical advice: your best sales insights are in conversations you've already had. Write them down.

**SRL application:** After every CRNA conversation, workshop, or demo:
- What phrase made them lean in?
- What question made them hesitate?
- What made them say "yes" or "maybe later"?
- What exact words did THEY use? (ICP language > your language)

**Operating rule:** These conversation notes go into the vault as observations. The ICP language becomes the marketing language. The hesitations become the FAQ. The "lean-in" moments become the hooks.

## The Five Metrics That Matter (Commoncog-Derived)

Cedric would say: measure these five things weekly and you'll know if the company is working.

### 1. CAC / LTV Ratio
- **CAC:** Cost to acquire one CRNA (ads ÷ signups)
- **LTV:** $100/year CE × avg retention years
- **Target:** ≥ 3:1 LTV/CAC
- **Why:** Unit economics. If this doesn't work, nothing else matters.

### 2. Narrative Conversion Rate
- Test 3 pitch frames (clinical / identity / outcome)
- Measure: which gets clicked, signed up, completed?
- **Why:** You don't know which frame moves people until you test.

### 3. Leading Indicator of Retention
- Find the ONE behavior that predicts who stays:
  - Completed 3+ sessions by day 14?
  - MAIA-2 score improved by week 2?
  - Opened app on 4+ different days?
- **Why:** This is the biomarker of product-market fit. Once found, optimize for it.

### 4. Enterprise Pipeline
- How many hospital conversations are active?
- How many have moved from "interesting" to "let's pilot"?
- **Target:** 3 active pilot conversations by end of Q3 2026
- **Why:** Enterprise logos drive valuation more than consumer downloads.

### 5. Vault Growth Rate
- New notes per week (knowledge compounding)
- Clinical interpretations filled per week (tacit knowledge extraction)
- Evidence notes added per week (credibility deepening)
- **Why:** The vault IS the moat. Measure its growth like you measure revenue.

The psychological distress experienced by healthcare providers after involvement in an adverse patient event, medical error, or patient injury. The provider becomes the "second victim" — the patient is the first.

## Definition

Coined by Albert Wu (2000) and operationalized by Susan Scott (2010), the second-victim phenomenon describes the emotional, cognitive, and physiological toll on clinicians following unexpected patient outcomes. Symptoms include shame, self-doubt, re-experiencing, sleep disruption, and autonomic dysregulation.

## Relevance to SRL

Second-victim phenomenon is a primary use case for Gap Moment Training in clinical environments. CRNAs face high-stakes, high-consequence events regularly. The acute stress response following an adverse event creates exactly the kind of autonomic dysregulation that NeuroMinute protocols are designed to address — not as therapy, but as immediate physiological stabilization.

## Prevalence

Studies report that 10-43% of clinicians involved in adverse events experience significant second-victim symptoms, with higher rates among anesthesia providers due to the severity and immediacy of potential complications.

# The CRNA Interior-Ecology Literature Gap

## The Diagnosis

Nurse anesthesia has partially mapped its **external ecology** (workforce demographics, billing mechanics, scope politics, stipend economics, PE rollup dynamics) — but in fragments, nowhere integrated into a single systems view. It has not yet begun to map its **interior ecology** at all. This concept names the specific gap and the adjacent bodies of work that would fill it.

## What Is Partially Present in the CRNA Literature

- Workforce demographics and rural access (AANA reports, anesthesiafacts.com)
- Scope-of-practice advocacy and state opt-out mapping
- Billing modifiers and TEFRA-7 (in practice journals, not core curricula)
- Burnout interventions using generic MBSR protocols measured with MBI scale outcomes
- PE rollup coverage — but in general healthcare press, not in the AANA Journal
- DNP capstones dominated by quality-improvement projects

## What Is Essentially Absent

- **Integrated ecosystem view** — no CRNA text maps species + energy flows + climate forces + historical dynamics as a single system
- **Interoception neuroscience** applied to CRNA self-regulation (Craig, Critchley, Feldman Barrett, Farb: none in CRNA curricula)
- **HRV / vagal tone as clinical sensor calibration**, not wellness decoration
- **Moral injury frame** (Dean/Talbot) applied to CRNA distress
- **Second-victim support structures** for CRNAs — ad hoc where present
- **Breath neuroscience** (Feldman preBötC-LC, Balban/Huberman cyclic sighing) in CRNA curricula
- **Closed-loop controller model** of the CRNA (see [[crna-as-closed-loop-controller]])

## Specific Textbook-Level Evidence

Nagelhout's *Nurse Anesthesia* (7th ed., 2022), the dominant textbook of the field, contains no dedicated chapter on provider interoception, autonomic self-regulation, or moral injury. DNP capstone trends confirm the absence at the training-output level: the most common capstone types are QI projects, not interior-ecology research.

## Why the Gap Is Not a Criticism of CRNA Clinicians

CRNAs as individual practitioners already live inside the interior ecology. They know the tells of their own fatigue, the signature of their own anxiety, the difference between their own gut and the patient's signal. The gap is at the *field* level, not the individual level — the profession has not yet produced the integrative research, textbook chapters, curriculum modules, and professional norms that would make tacit expertise legible and teachable.

## Why the Work to Close the Gap Already Exists

The good news buried in this gap is that the work has been done in adjacent fields. It just has not been imported into nurse anesthesia. The citations above represent a few dozen papers across interoception, autonomic physiology, breath neuroscience, moral injury, second-victim research, HRV longitudinal work, and ambient-AI-scribe burnout studies — all available and translatable.

The SRL thesis is that the integration is the work: pulling these findings into a CRNA-facing frame, adapting them to the operative context of the perianesthesia period, and producing the curriculum and research that a mature discipline would require.

## Markers of a Closed Gap

A mature CRNA interior ecology would show:

1. Curricula that teach the interior ecosystem (interoception, HRV, breath circuits) grounded in cited research — no woo
2. Research programs treating CRNA self-regulation as a patient-safety variable
3. Professional norms that treat the CRNA nervous system as infrastructure (standard debriefs, breath practice as instrument maintenance)
4. An integrative literature — a text or series — that maps external + interior ecology as one question

The insight that most people mistakenly assume life is ergodic — that every moment is interchangeable and success is linear. Reality is path-dependent. Mental performance must include tools for: (a) awareness of non-reversible moments, (b) decision compounding, and (c) recovery from "forking paths."

Synthesized from Gurdjieff ([[self-remembering]] as temporal awareness), Dellanna's *Ergodicity* (non-ergodic systems where ruin cannot be averaged away), Christian and Griffiths' *Algorithms to Live By* (optimal stopping), and Springett's *Stairway to Heaven* (developmental ascent through time).

This concept reframes [[gap-moment-training]] as not just a stress intervention but a **temporal awareness practice** — training the clinician to recognize that *this moment is non-reversible* and demands full presence. Every induction, every hemodynamic shift, every airway decision is path-dependent and non-ergodic. The gap moment becomes the point where conscious engagement with temporal reality prevents default-mode drift into the illusion of interchangeable moments.

Connects to [[state-transition]] (each transition is unique and non-reversible), [[neurogating]] (gating mechanisms operate at temporal decision forks), and the "Non-Ergodic Blink" practice in the Gap Moment Codex.

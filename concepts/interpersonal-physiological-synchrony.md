# Interpersonal Physiological Synchrony

The empirically documented phenomenon where autonomic nervous systems of people in close interaction begin to entrain — producing correlated heart rate, respiration, skin conductance patterns across the dyad.

## The CRNA-Patient Case

When a CRNA holds their breath during a patient's apneic window at intubation, this is probably not habit. It is embodied co-regulation — the clinician's nervous system unconsciously modeling and syncing with the patient's state.

This matters because the synchrony happens across a profound metabolic asymmetry:

- **Patient**: Anesthetized, cold, paralyzed — VCO2 suppressed, metabolism low
- **CRNA**: Sympathetically activated, moving, cognitively loaded — VCO2 elevated 30–50% above resting baseline

The CRNA is trying to synchronize with someone in the opposite metabolic state. Across a full shift, across multiple inductions, this asymmetric entrainment is a measurable but untracked **autonomic load**.

## Why It's Unremarked

No performance or burnout metric currently captures this. It's invisible in HRV studies, burnout surveys, and incident reports. It is a daily, repeated physiological cost embedded in the craft of anesthesia.

## Implications for Pausality

Two design directions:

1. **The breath-hold as an instrumented gap moment** — the synchrony event is a naturally recurring pause point that Pausality could detect and use as a regulation prompt (see [[breath-hold-autonomic-signature]])

2. **Pre-induction regulation as upstream intervention** — if the CRNA's state entrains to the patient's, deliberately regulating *before* induction creates a calmer physiological field during the most critical procedural moment

## Research Gap

Application to anesthesia is largely unstudied. Most IPS research lives in psychotherapy, couples studies, and parent-infant interaction. This is open territory for SRL.

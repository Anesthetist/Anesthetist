## Definition

Deliberately visualizing everything that could go wrong and embodying your exact response — not just cognitive scenario planning but training the autonomic system's response to those scenarios. Modeled on Blue Angels pre-flight checks but applied to clinical practice and generalized to any high-stakes performance domain. The body rehearses the response so it's already available under pressure. Distinct from standard mental rehearsal because the visualization includes interoceptive and autonomic components.

## Seven-Step Failure Visualization Protocol

Randy's 28-year clinical practice, systematized:

1. **Visualize the case proceeding normally** — then introduce a failure
2. **What would it look like?** — the specific visual, auditory, and tactile cues
3. **What would I notice first?** — training perceptual recognition
4. **What would be going wrong physiologically?** — mapping observable signs to pathophysiology
5. **What are the options?** — branching the decision tree
6. **What would I do?** — selecting and committing
7. **Visualize every step of the response** — including specific doses, physical actions

## What Makes This SRL-Original

1. **EMBODIED, not cognitive** — the visualization is felt in the body (weight of laryngoscope, resistance of syringe plunger, rhythm of ventilation). This is motor imagery that engages premotor cortex, supplementary motor area, and cerebellum.
2. **DELIBERATE failure focus** — not just "prepare for the case" but "deliberately introduce failures and rehearse recovery." This is emotional inoculation combined with procedural preparation.
3. **Downtime/boredom as training surface** — Randy recognized that interstitial dead time was the training window BEFORE [[gap-moment-training]] existed as a concept. The visualization practice was proto-GMT.
4. **28 years of personal practice** — this isn't theoretical. Randy has been doing this since SRNA training at Rush.
5. **Generalizability** — the protocol applies far beyond anesthesia to any high-stakes performance domain. It's the active ingredient in PTSD prehabilitation.

## Connection to PTSD Prehabilitation

> "There's no pre-rehab rehabilitation no pre-preventive or rehabilitative framework for the prevention of PTSD for any of the specialties — Frontline medical workers, Frontline firefighters, police, etc. — that's in widespread use and I'd like to create that."

Adversity visualization is not just preparing for emergencies — it's inoculating the nervous system against traumatic damage. By pre-rehearsing failure scenarios in the body, the autonomic system has already "been there" when the real event occurs, reducing the novelty-shock that contributes to traumatic encoding.

## Blue Angels Model

Modeled on the Blue Angels pre-flight ritual: before every flight, pilots sit together and physically move through the motions of the routine — turning their heads, moving their hands, tracking each maneuver in their bodies — while still on the ground. They are not thinking about the flight. They are **rehearsing the flight in their bodies.**

Randy adapted this: he would run through failure scenarios **over and over again**, particularly during downtime — between cases, during slow moments, when bored. The boredom was the training window. The gap moment before the term existed.

Deliberate, controlled stress exposure designed to build resilience through graduated challenge — the immunological metaphor applied to psychological preparedness. Originally developed by Meichenbaum (1985) for anxiety disorders, now widely applied in military, first responder, and high-performance contexts.

## Three Phases

1. **Conceptualization** — Education about stress responses, cognitive appraisal, and coping mechanisms
2. **Skills acquisition** — Learning and rehearsing specific coping techniques (breathing, cognitive reframing, attention control)
3. **Application** — Graduated exposure to stressors with coached practice of acquired skills

## Relationship to Gap Moment Training

GMT can be understood as a micro-dose stress inoculation protocol — the 60-second NeuroMinute format provides controlled exposure to state-shift demands within a safe, repeatable container. Each NeuroMinute is a small inoculation: detect the gap moment, execute the protocol, measure the delta, return to task.

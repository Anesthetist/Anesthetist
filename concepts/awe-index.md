# Awe Index

A proposed biometric and experiential measure of moments when the nervous system *opens* rather than *armors*. Awe is the phenomenological signature of eudaimonic orientation — the moment of contact with something larger than the self.

Autonomically, awe looks different from relaxation: parasympathetic activation without the deflation of low arousal. Attention expands. Defensive posture drops. It is the opposite pole from isolationist resilience.

## Why It Matters

Most Pausality metrics measure resilience *against* stress (HRV, recovery time, breath-hold duration). The Awe Index measures resilience *toward something* — whether the clinician is flourishing, not just surviving.

Without this, you can build a clinician who recovers faster but still feels their life hollowing out. The Awe Index is the signal that the intervention is working at the eudaimonic level, not just the regulatory one.

## Detection Approaches

- **Passive biometric**: HRV elevation without sympathetic spike, slow respiratory rate, reduced movement — a detectable signature
- **Self-tagged**: during [[autonomic-diary]] dictation, moments the clinician flags as "expansive" or "different"
- **Contextual correlation**: over time, identifying what reliably produces awe for *this* clinician

## For CRNAs, Awe Is Not Rare

It surfaces as: a patient waking safely after a complex case, the stillness at the top of a breath hold, an unexpected connection with a family. These are not soft experiences. They are physiologically distinct states that correlate with restored sense of professional purpose and reduced moral injury risk.

Capturing them is not sentiment. It is data.

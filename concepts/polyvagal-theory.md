# Polyvagal Theory

Stephen Porges' theory of autonomic nervous system hierarchy, describing three phylogenetically ordered response systems. This is the foundational theoretical framework for all somnistics interventions — it explains *why* breath-based training works and *what* autonomic states we're training toward.

## Three-Tier Hierarchy

1. **Ventral Vagal Complex (VVC)** — Myelinated vagus. Supports social engagement, calm states, health-growth-restoration. RSA-dominant HRV pattern. The *target state* for somnistics training.

2. **Sympathetic Nervous System** — Fight/flight mobilization. Appropriate for action and acute threat response. High HR, low HRV, elevated cortisol.

3. **Dorsal Vagal Complex (DVC)** — Unmyelinated vagus. Freeze/shutdown/immobilization. Metabolic suppression, dissociation, bradycardia. The *state to recover from*.

## Key Concepts for SRL

- **Neuroception:** Subconscious detection of safety vs. threat that governs autonomic state shifts. [[neurogating]] is the technological analog of neuroception.
- **Safety as prerequisite:** Safety is required for autonomic flexibility and higher-order cognition. Somnistics protocols create felt safety through predictable breath patterns and gentle guidance.
- **Social engagement system:** Links face, voice, and breath — all mediated by cranial nerves branching from the ventral vagal complex. This is why voice tone and language precision matter in [[neurominute]] scripts.
- **RSA as index:** Respiratory sinus arrhythmia is a measurable index of ventral vagal function — the primary outcome metric.

## AI Application (CVA Integration)

HRV patterns differentiate ventral vs. dorsal vagal states:
- **Low HRV + RSA suppression + blunted GSR** = Dorsal Vagal Dominance (freeze risk)
- **High HRV + strong RSA + normal skin conductance** = Ventral Vagal Activation (target state)
- This differentiation enables [[cognitive-variability-analysis]] to predict which clinicians are sliding toward freeze states under high stress

## Clinical Observations

- In 28 years of anesthesia practice, Randy has observed the polyvagal hierarchy in action: patients in ventral vagal (social, cooperative) vs. sympathetic (agitated, resistant) vs. dorsal vagal (shutdown, unresponsive) — and the same patterns in clinicians under stress
- The theory explains why conscious breathing interventions work: they directly activate the ventral vagal circuit

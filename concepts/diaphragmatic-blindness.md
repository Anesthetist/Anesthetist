# Diaphragmatic Blindness

The specific inability to sense or consciously control one's own diaphragmatic breathing during high-stress situations — the most common starting deficit identified in Week 1 of the somnistics curriculum. Clinicians who expertly monitor patients' respiratory patterns often cannot feel their own breath.

## Definition

Diaphragmatic blindness is the interoceptive deficit where a person cannot accurately detect whether they are breathing diaphragmatically (belly) vs. thoracically (chest), cannot sense their own breath rate, and cannot feel the quality of their breathing pattern. Under stress, this blindness deepens — breathing becomes shallow and thoracic without awareness.

## Clinical Significance

- The most common finding in Week 1 interoceptive assessment
- Clinicians who manage ventilators with precision cannot feel their own diaphragm
- Correcting diaphragmatic blindness is the prerequisite for all subsequent [[anterocept]] training
- [[awareness-before-technique]]: you cannot train a breath pattern you cannot feel

## Somnistics Solution

Domain 1 of [[anterocept]] specifically targets diaphragmatic blindness: simple breath awareness exercises with haptic feedback confirming diaphragmatic vs. thoracic breathing. Only after this blindness is corrected can protocols like the [[anesthetists-breath-protocol]] be effectively practiced.

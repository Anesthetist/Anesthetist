# Resonant Breathing Frequency

The individualized breathing rate (approximately 4.5-6.5 breaths/min, typically near 0.1 Hz or 6 bpm) at which cardiorespiratory oscillations and baroreflex resonance are maximally entrained, producing the largest possible HRV amplitude. This is the core physiological mechanism underlying somnistics breath protocols.

## Mechanism

- Slow diaphragmatic inhalation → increased venous return → baroreceptor stretch → reflex bradycardia on exhale
- At the individual's resonant frequency, the heart-lung-baroreflex loop locks into resonance, producing high-amplitude HR oscillations
- This maximizes vagal efferent stimulation and baroreflex sensitivity

## Randy's Core Protocol

**5-Breath Protocol:** 5 slow breaths (~55-60 seconds) x 5 sessions/day x varied contexts. This is the operationalized [[minimum-effective-dose]] for neuroplasticity without motivation fatigue. Optimal because frequency x novelty > duration for myelination efficiency.

## Evidence Base

- [[lehrer-2000-resonance-frequency]] — foundational paper establishing the protocol; each individual has a unique resonant frequency; training produces large increases in HRV and baroreflex gain
- [[zaccaro-2018-breathing-systematic-review]] — systematic evidence for slow breathing effects on autonomic and CNS activity
- Single 60-second slow-breath trial raises HF-HRV in trauma survivors
- 8-week daily practice shows cortical thickness gains in dlPFC and insula

## Clinical Observations

- Personal resonance frequency varies slightly (4.5-6.5 bpm) and can be individualized via biofeedback assessment
- Effects are measurable within a single session, validating the 60-second [[neurominute]] format
- The protocol is learnable in 10 sessions and effects persist with continued practice

# Vagal Tone

The strength and efficiency of vagus nerve parasympathetic activation — the primary biomarker of autonomic health, resilience, and regulatory capacity in the somnistics framework. Vagal tone reflects the body's ability to shift from stress/activation to recovery/integration.

## Definition

Vagal tone is the tonic level of vagal (parasympathetic) efferent activity to the heart. Higher vagal tone = greater autonomic flexibility, faster recovery from stress, better emotional regulation, and enhanced cognitive performance. It is the primary outcome measure for somnistics interventions.

## Measurement Biomarkers

- **RMSSD** (Root Mean Square of Successive Differences) — robust 1-minute proxy for vagal tone, ideal for [[neurominute]] pre/post measurement
- **HF-HRV** (0.15-0.4 Hz power) — high-frequency heart rate variability reflecting parasympathetic activity
- **RSA** (Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia) — heart rate oscillations synchronized with breathing; primary index of ventral vagal function
- **Baroreflex Sensitivity (BRS)** — responsiveness of the blood pressure regulation loop

## Polyvagal Differentiation

From [[polyvagal-theory]], two distinct vagal circuits:
- **Ventral Vagal Complex (VVC):** Myelinated vagus → social engagement, calm connection, RSA dominance. The *target state* for somnistics training.
- **Dorsal Vagal Complex (DVC):** Unmyelinated vagus → freeze/immobilization, bradycardia, low HRV without RSA. The *state to recover from*.

## Evidence Base

- [[porges-2011-polyvagal-theory]] — RSA as measurable index of ventral vagal function; slow rhythmic breathing directly stimulates the ventral vagal pathway
- [[lehrer-2000-resonance-frequency]] — resonant frequency breathing maximally stimulates baroreflex, producing large increases in HRV and vagal tone
- [[sezer-2025-meditation-autonomic-nervous-system]] — cardiac resonance and respiratory coupling in long-term meditators; parasympathetic markers track meditation depth
- [[nath-2025-heartbeat-evoked-potentials-meditation]] — heartbeat-evoked potentials (HEP) as neural marker of interoceptive-vagal integration during meditation
- 60-second slow breathing raises HF-HRV in trauma survivors (single-session effects)
- 8-week daily practice shows cortical thickness gains in dlPFC and insula

## Clinical Observations

- Vagal tone correlates with emotional regulation, decision-making speed, and motor control precision — all critical for CRNA performance
- Rapid HRV rebound between cases indicates effective autonomic recovery
- Low vagal tone predicts burnout, PTSD vulnerability, and communication errors under stress

# Fiduciary Wedge

The permanent gap between what AI can technically do and what it can be held accountable for. A human must always stand behind fiduciary obligations, safety-critical judgments, and legal commitments (Ismail et al., 2026).

## Definition

Transaction costs don't literally reach zero. Trust, liability, and regulatory costs compress dramatically but persist. "The algorithm decided" is never an acceptable final answer for a fiduciary obligation, a safety-critical judgment, or a legal commitment. The Gap doesn't close — it narrows. What remains is the firm as accountability shell: a legal liability container, a fiduciary responsibility holder, and an accountability structure. Everything else dissolves.

## Mechanism

As AI capability increases, the set of decisions that *require* human judgment shrinks — but it never reaches zero. The Fiduciary Wedge defines the irreducible minimum of human accountability in any domain. It is the reason firms don't dissolve entirely even when transaction costs approach zero.

The wedge varies by sector:
- **Healthcare:** Wide wedge — clinical judgment, patient safety, malpractice liability, regulatory compliance (FDA, CMS, state licensing)
- **Financial services:** Wide wedge — fiduciary duty, regulatory capital, AML/KYC
- **Information-centric sectors:** Narrow wedge — marketing, content, consulting collapse faster
- **Government:** Wedge defined by statutory authority, not efficiency

## SRL Relevance — Healthcare's Wide Fiduciary Wedge

The Fiduciary Wedge is why SRL's domain collapses more slowly than information-centric sectors — and why that's actually an advantage:

1. **Credibility moat:** Randy's 28 years as a CRNA is irreducible fiduciary credibility. No AI system can hold a nursing license or bear malpractice liability.
2. **Product design implication:** Pausality must be positioned as a tool that enhances clinical judgment, not replaces it. The app titrates autonomic regulation skills — the clinician decides how and when to apply them.
3. **Enterprise positioning:** When pitching to hospital systems, the Fiduciary Wedge is the frame: "We don't replace your clinicians' judgment. We make their judgment faster and more durable."
4. **Regulatory timeline:** The 1-2 year lag for regulated sectors predicted in the manuscript gives SRL time to build trust infrastructure before Domain Collapse reaches healthcare.

# CRNA as Closed-Loop Physiological Controller

## Premise

An anesthesia provider is, mechanistically, a **closed-loop physiological controller** making sub-minute decisions about someone else's autonomic nervous system, using their own autonomic nervous system as the primary sensor.

This is not metaphor. It is the literal control-theoretic description of what a CRNA does during a case:

- **Inputs:** patient vitals, surgical field cues, verbal reports, drug pharmacokinetics, own gut sense.
- **Integrator:** the CRNA's own nervous system.
- **Outputs:** titration of agent, ventilation, vasoactives, timing of interventions, communication.
- **Feedback loop:** seconds to minutes, continuous across the entire case.

Every anesthetic is a live, human-in-the-loop control system in which the *human* is the controller.

## The Load-Bearing Consequence

If the CRNA is the controller, then **the fidelity of the sensor is a patient-safety variable, not a wellness concern.**

A provider who cannot distinguish their own hunger from their own anxiety from their own moral distress from their own signal about the patient is performing closed-loop control with a noisy sensor. Every downstream decision — choice of agent, timing of extubation, tolerance of the surgeon's third delay, patience with the difficult family, handoff quality — inherits that noise.

This reframe moves interoceptive and autonomic training out of the wellness category (optional, individual, lifestyle) and into the patient-safety category (structural, professional, measurable). See [[sensor-calibration-as-patient-safety]].

## Why This Frame Is Missing from CRNA Literature

Nagelhout's *Nurse Anesthesia* (7th ed., 2022) — the dominant textbook — has no dedicated chapter on provider interoception, autonomic self-regulation, or the clinician-as-sensor model. The closest adjacent literature lives in:

- Interoception neuroscience ([[interoception]], Craig, Critchley, Feldman Barrett, Farb)
- Breath neuroscience ([[balban-huberman-2023-cyclic-sighing]], [[feldman-2017-prebotc-locus-coeruleus-arousal]])
- HRV and burnout prediction ([[hrv-vagal-tone-longitudinal-burnout-predictor]])
- Second-victim phenomenon ([[second-victim-phenomenon]])
- Moral injury framing ([[dean-talbot-2018-moral-injury-not-burnout-stat]])

None of these bodies of work has been imported into CRNA curricula in integrated form. The closed-loop-controller frame is the integration surface.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Curricular.** A mature CRNA curriculum would teach sensor calibration the way it currently teaches airway management — as a core competency, not a wellness elective.
2. **Research.** A longitudinal CRNA HRV cohort with clinical-outcome linkage does not yet exist. Randy's [[irb-pilot-protocol-outline-march-2026]] is designed to produce one.
3. **Practice.** Per-case interventions like [[neurominute]], [[gap-moment-training]], and exhale-dominant breath protocols are *instrument maintenance for the controller*, not self-care.
4. **Professional norms.** Post-event debriefing, second-victim support, and continuous provider-state monitoring become structural patient-safety investments rather than HR niceties.

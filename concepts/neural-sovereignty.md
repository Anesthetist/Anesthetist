The principle that individuals retain full ownership of their neural data and the patterns derived from it — extending data sovereignty into the neurophysiological domain. Randy coined this term in the context of SRL's AI architecture decisions.

**Randy's words:** "We believe in individual data sovereignty along with neural sovereignty. Utilize the state of the art in privacy-preservation."

## Scope

Neural sovereignty encompasses:
- **Raw biometric data** — HRV time series, EEG recordings, GSR signals captured during sessions
- **Derived models** — The personalized AI baselines and state-classification thresholds built from a user's data
- **Behavioral signatures** — The neurophysiological patterns (e.g., a user's resonant breathing frequency, stress HRV threshold) that constitute a kind of neural fingerprint

No company — including SRL — should own someone's neurological fingerprint. Data must remain on-device or under the user's explicit control.

## Technical Implementation

Neural sovereignty drives SRL's architectural decisions:
- **Edge AI first** — Models run on-device (Apple Watch, iPhone, AirPods Max) so raw biometric data never leaves the device
- **Federated learning** — Model improvements aggregate without centralizing individual data
- **Differential privacy** — Statistical noise added to any shared data to prevent individual re-identification
- **User-controlled export** — Users own their data and can export or delete it at any time

## Ethical and Commercial Significance

As biometric AI becomes more capable of inferring mental states, cognitive patterns, and emotional tendencies from physiological signals, the distinction between "data sovereignty" and "neural sovereignty" becomes critical. The latter is a higher-order protection: your data is yours, but so are the models trained on it and the patterns they discover.

This positioning differentiates SRL from consumer wellness apps that monetize user health data and creates a trust foundation for the CRNA/clinical market where privacy is non-negotiable.

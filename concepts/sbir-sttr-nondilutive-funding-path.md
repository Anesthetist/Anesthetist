# SBIR/STTR Nondilutive Funding Path for Somnistics

## Strategic Rationale

Federal SBIR/STTR grants represent nondilutive capital that pairs well with equity raises (e.g., the $2M SAFE). They de-risk R&D spend without equity dilution — consistent with Ron Wiener's capital efficiency thesis and the macro preference for capital-efficient companies in the current funding environment.

However, NIH and NSF have paused new submissions. Reauthorization is widely expected. David McFeeters-Krone is the best source for current status.

## Best-Fit Agency Targets

| Agency | Program | Rationale |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| NSF | Digital Health SBIR | "Novel devices, components, systems, algorithms, or services that enable transformation of healthcare from reactive to proactive, person-centered, preventive, and cost-efficient" — this is Pausality's product description |
| NIH | NIMHD or NIAAA | Stress and burnout prevention in healthcare workers |
| DoD | Military Readiness SBIR | Dual-use framing: autonomic resilience, stress inoculation, force preservation |

Typical Phase I awards: NSF ~$275K, NIH $150–250K. Timeline to funding: 6–12 months from submission.

## Connection to Clinical Validation Drift

As of the March 30, 2026 Vigil weekly report, clinical validation is the top drift risk for SRL — no pilot study design has moved forward. An SBIR application *requires* a specific R&D plan with measurable endpoints. Pursuing the SBIR path would force the clinical validation plan to be formalized, potentially resolving the drift risk as a byproduct.

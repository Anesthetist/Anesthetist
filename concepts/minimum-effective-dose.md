# Minimum Effective Dose

The smallest intervention — in time, intensity, and frequency — that produces measurable neurophysiological and behavioral change. For somnistics: 5 breaths (60 seconds) x 5 daily sessions is more effective than a single weekly 30-minute block.

> "One breath is likely the minimum effective dose of therapy." — Randy Graybeal

## Definition

Borrowed from pharmacology (where MED minimizes side effects while achieving the therapeutic target), applied to behavioral intervention design. The principle that frequency x novelty > duration for neuroplasticity: short, varied, repeated exposures drive myelination more efficiently than long, infrequent sessions.

## Somnistics MED Framework

- **Time:** 60 seconds (one [[neurominute]]) as the minimum intervention unit
- **Frequency:** 5 sessions/day across varied contexts (not 1 long session)
- **Intensity:** Simplest effective protocol first (coherent breathing before box breathing before MPIC)
- **Measurement:** HRV delta post-set + reduction in idle phone unlocks as dual metrics

## Evidence Base

- [[lehrer-2000-resonance-frequency]] — resonance frequency effects are measurable within a single 60-second session
- [[sezer-2025-meditation-autonomic-nervous-system]] — ANS changes detectable in brief meditation intervals in long-term practitioners; validates that minimum effective dose produces measurable autonomic shifts
- Meta-analysis: brief daily mindfulness beats sporadic long sessions for mood outcomes
- Micro-dose awe research (5-second panoramic visualization) cuts depressive rumination
- Myelination efficiency literature: frequency x novelty > duration

## Clinical Observations

- CRNAs cannot take 10-minute breaks but can access 60-second windows — the MED principle makes somnistics training compatible with clinical workflow
- "More is not better; right-sized is better" — the pharmacological wisdom that overdosing creates new problems

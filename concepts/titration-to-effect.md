# Titration to Effect

Applying the minimum effective dose incrementally and adjusting based on response — a principle borrowed from anesthesia pharmacology and generalized across every layer of somnistics: protocols, nudge thresholds, curriculum progression, notification frequency, and business strategy.

## Definition

Just as anesthesiologists titrate IV medications to patient response (observing hemodynamic changes before adding more drug), somnistics practitioners titrate breathing/attention practices to nervous system response. Start with the smallest dose (1 minute, 5 breaths), observe the response (HRV delta, subjective state shift), and adjust frequency/duration/intensity based on measured effect.

## Application Domains

- **Breath protocols:** Start with simplest pattern (coherent breathing), progress to box breathing, then advanced MPIC only when simpler protocols show diminishing returns
- **Nudge thresholds:** Breathe Nudge notification triggers start conservative and adjust based on dismissal/engagement patterns (after 5 consecutive dismissals, prompt threshold adjustment)
- **Curriculum progression:** [[neuro-ouroboros]] 21-day sequence adds complexity only when prior layers are stable
- **Session frequency:** 5 sessions/day × 60 seconds > 1 session/day × 30 minutes (frequency × novelty > duration for neuroplasticity)
- **Business strategy:** Enterprise pilots start with smallest viable cohort, measure outcomes, scale based on data

## Evidence Base

- [[lehrer-2000-resonance-frequency]] — resonance frequency effects compound with regular practice; the protocol is learnable in 10 sessions
- [[sezer-2025-meditation-autonomic-nervous-system]] — individual variation in autonomic responses to meditation supports the titration principle; cardiac resonance frequency varies by practitioner
- Pharmacological dose-response literature — the principle that minimum effective dose avoids side effects while achieving therapeutic target
- Neuroplasticity research: myelination efficiency favors frequent short exposures over infrequent long sessions

## Clinical Observations

- In anesthesia, "titrate to effect" is the universal dosing principle — never give more drug than needed, always observe the response before the next dose
- Applied to somnistics, this prevents "practice fatigue" (overloading users with too much too soon) and respects the nervous system's capacity for change

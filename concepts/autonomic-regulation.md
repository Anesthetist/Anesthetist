# Autonomic Regulation

The body's capacity to shift flexibly between sympathetic (activation), parasympathetic (recovery), and integrated states via the autonomic nervous system. This is the core scientific domain that somnistics trains — every concept in the vault ultimately serves autonomic regulation.

## Three-System Balance

1. **Sympathetic Dominance:** Appropriate for action, problem-solving, acute threat response. Elevated HR, cortisol, alertness.
2. **Parasympathetic Dominance:** Appropriate for recovery, integration, sleep. Low HR, high HRV, restoration.
3. **Integrated (Ventral Vagal):** Simultaneous alertness + calm — "poised composure." The ideal operating state for CRNAs during procedures. High HRV with strong RSA.

## Measurement

- **HRV** (SDNN, RMSSD, HF power) — primary autonomic flexibility index
- **Heart Rate** — baseline and reactivity
- **Respiratory patterns** — rate, depth, regularity
- **GSR (Galvanic Skin Response)** — sympathetic activation marker
- **Baroreflex sensitivity** — autonomic loop responsiveness

## Clinical Outcomes

- Rapid HRV recovery between cases indicates effective autonomic regulation
- Fewer communication errors under stress
- Lower baseline irritability across shift
- Reduced PTSD/burnout vulnerability

## Evidence Base

- [[porges-2011-polyvagal-theory]] — three-tier autonomic hierarchy; neuroception governs state shifts
- [[zaccaro-2018-breathing-systematic-review]] — systematic evidence for breathing effects on ANS
- [[sezer-2025-meditation-autonomic-nervous-system]] — Harvard review of ANS effects in long-term meditators; documents "relaxed alertness" (concurrent sympathetic + parasympathetic activation) and cardiac resonance
- [[treves-2024-mindful-brain-neural-correlates]] — 68-study systematic review: decreased amygdala reactivity correlates with greater autonomic flexibility

## Clinical Observations

- In anesthesia, autonomic regulation is literally the job — managing patients' autonomic states. The irony is that clinicians rarely manage their own.
- CRNAs who maintain autonomic flexibility show better vigilance, fewer errors, and longer career longevity

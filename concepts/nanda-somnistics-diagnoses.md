# NANDA Nursing Diagnoses for Somnistics

## Why NANDA Matters for SRL

NANDA-I (North American Nursing Diagnosis Association International) is the standardized language of nursing assessment. Every hospital EHR uses it. Every AANA CE course maps to it. Every nursing theory connects through it. If Somnistics doesn't speak NANDA, it doesn't speak nursing.

Connecting to NANDA strengthens four things simultaneously:
1. **Nursing theory** — the Somnistics middle-range theory maps to standardized diagnoses
2. **CE accreditation** — AANA Category A requires connection to nursing practice
3. **Certification** — CSP practitioners assess using NANDA language
4. **Clinical credibility** — hospital systems already use NANDA in their EHRs

## NANDA GAPS — What Doesn't Exist Yet

NANDA has **no diagnosis** for:

### 1. Impaired Interoceptive Awareness
No standardized diagnosis for the reduced ability to detect, interpret, and respond to internal body signals. The ISH says medical training creates this deficit. MAIA-2 measures it. But NANDA can't name it.

### 2. Autonomic State Drift
No diagnosis for the progressive, undetected degradation of autonomic regulation during sustained performance. State drift isn't fatigue, isn't anxiety, isn't stress overload — it's the invisible slide from optimal to suboptimal that produces errors.

### 3. Diaphragmatic Blindness
No diagnosis for the inability to voluntarily engage the diaphragm despite intact anatomy. 28/30 anesthesia professionals demonstrated this deficit. It's a motor-interoceptive gap with no NANDA label.

### 4. Impaired Autonomic Flexibility
No diagnosis for the reduced capacity to shift between sympathetic and parasympathetic states on demand. This is what HRR measures, what resonant breathing trains, and what makes CRNAs #1 on the AI-resistant index.

**These four gaps are an opportunity.** SRL can propose new NANDA diagnoses through the official submission process. If adopted, every nurse in the world would use SRL's language.

## Four Proposed NANDA-Style Nursing Diagnoses

### Proposed Diagnosis 1: Impaired Autonomic Flexibility

**Problem:** Impaired Autonomic Flexibility
**Definition:** Reduced capacity to shift between sympathetic and parasympathetic autonomic states in response to environmental demands, resulting in autonomic rigidity and impaired adaptive response.

**Related Factors (Etiology):**
- Chronic occupational stress
- Sustained sympathetic activation without recovery
- Interoceptive suppression from clinical training (hidden curriculum)
- Sleep deprivation
- Cumulative allostatic load

**Defining Characteristics (Signs/Symptoms):**
- RMSSD below age-adjusted norms
- Heart rate recovery <12 bpm at 1 minute post-stressor
- Persistent elevated resting heart rate
- Reduced HRV coherence during breathing exercises
- Self-reported difficulty "coming down" after clinical events

**Suggested NIC Interventions:**
- Autonomic Regulation Training (resonant frequency breathing)
- HRV Biofeedback
- Gap Moment Training™
- Progressive Autonomic Challenge (contemplative progressive overload)

**Suggested NOC Outcomes:**
- Improved RMSSD to age-adjusted norms
- HRR ≥12 bpm within 60 seconds
- Autonomic home base return within 90 seconds of disruption

---

### Proposed Diagnosis 2: Impaired Interoceptive Awareness

**Problem:** Impaired Interoceptive Awareness
**Definition:** Diminished ability to accurately detect, interpret, and respond to internal physiological signals (heartbeat, respiration, muscle tension, visceral state), leading to delayed recognition of stress response and impaired self-regulation.

**Related Factors:**
- Clinical training emphasizing external monitoring over internal awareness (ISH)
- Chronic emotional suppression as professional norm
- Prolonged sympathetic dominance
- Alexithymia (difficulty identifying/describing emotions)

**Defining Characteristics:**
- MAIA-2 scores below population norms in ≥4 of 8 subscales
- Inability to voluntarily engage diaphragm (diaphragmatic blindness)
- Discrepancy between physiological arousal (elevated HR/cortisol) and self-reported stress level
- Delayed recognition of state drift
- Nasal inhale duration <6 seconds with accessory muscle recruitment

**Suggested NIC Interventions:**
- Structured Interoception Training (3 modes)
- Diaphragmatic Re-education
- Body Scan / Signal Tracking Exercises
- MAIA-2 Guided Self-Assessment

**Suggested NOC Outcomes:**
- MAIA-2 improvement in ≥4 subscales
- Diaphragmatic engagement ≥8 seconds
- Self-reported stress aligns with objective HRV within 1 point

---

### Proposed Diagnosis 3: Risk for Autonomic State Drift

**Problem:** Risk for Autonomic State Drift
**Definition:** Vulnerability to progressive, undetected degradation of autonomic regulation during sustained cognitive performance, increasing error risk and cumulative allostatic load.

**Risk Factors:**
- Extended shift duration (≥10 hours)
- High-acuity clinical environment
- Incomplete recovery between arousal spikes
- Impaired interoceptive awareness
- No established autonomic home base practice

**Defining Characteristics (if progressed to actual):**
- RMSSD decline ≥25% from shift start to hour 10
- Vocal tone sharpening
- Attention narrowing
- Increased reaction time variability
- Self-report of "not feeling stressed" despite elevated physiological markers

**Suggested NIC Interventions:**
- Gap Moment Training™ (30-40 transition-point interventions per shift)
- Continuous wearable HRV monitoring
- State transition detection training
- Scheduled micro-recovery protocols

**Suggested NOC Outcomes:**
- RMSSD decline ≤10% across shift
- State drift detected within 30 seconds of onset
- ≥3 gap moment interventions per shift completed

---

### Proposed Diagnosis 4: Readiness for Enhanced Autonomic Self-Regulation

**Problem:** Readiness for Enhanced Autonomic Self-Regulation
**Definition:** A pattern of autonomic nervous system management that is sufficient for meeting current demands and can be strengthened through evidence-based training, as evidenced by expressed desire to improve regulatory capacity.

**Related Factors:**
- Motivation for professional development
- Awareness of autonomic training options
- Access to biofeedback technology
- Prior contemplative or breathwork experience

**Defining Characteristics:**
- Expresses desire to improve stress management skills
- Demonstrates interest in HRV biofeedback
- Currently uses partial regulation strategies (breathwork, meditation) without measurement
- Willing to commit to structured training protocol

**Suggested NIC Interventions:**
- S.T.E.P. 2.0 Assessment (Sort · Tag · Evidence · Progress)
- 21-Day Pausality Curriculum
- Personal resonant frequency identification
- HRR baseline establishment

**Suggested NOC Outcomes:**
- Completion of 21-session curriculum with documented biometric improvement
- HRR improvement of ≥4 bpm from baseline
- Transition from unstructured to structured regulation practice

## The Strategic Move

Submitting these four diagnoses to NANDA-I through the official proposal process would:

1. **Embed SRL's language into nursing's standardized vocabulary** — every nurse globally
2. **Create citing precedent** — future researchers cite SRL's definitions
3. **Strengthen the CE application** — "Our course addresses NANDA diagnosis [number]"
4. **Make the certification clinically legible** — CSP practitioners assess using recognized diagnoses
5. **Integrate into EHRs** — hospital systems can code SRL-related assessments

The NANDA submission process requires: concept analysis, literature review, expert review panel, field testing. The evidence assessment and nursing theory we've already built are the foundation for this submission.

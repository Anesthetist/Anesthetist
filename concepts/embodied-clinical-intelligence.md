The total embodied, situated capacity of a clinician to sense, decide, and act with asymmetric leverage -- where massive accumulated knowledge compresses into minimal effective intervention. Randy defines this through the Taoist paradox: thousands of hours of training, yet the action is a tiny wrist rotation, a single word at the right cadence, or a one-second pause.

## Four-Stratum Ontology

1. **Perception Acuity** -- Reading skin tone, silence before alarms, tissue feel. The sensory layer of clinical knowing.
2. **Action Micro-Skills** -- Motor precision, timing of restraint, knowing when not to act. The executive layer.
3. **Situation Awareness** -- Patient trajectory forecasting, team mental model alignment. The contextual layer.
4. **Inner Regulation** -- Autonomic self-modulation, emotion channeling, reflective processing. The regulatory layer.

## Standard Vocabulary Crosswalks

The ontology was formalized with OWL/RDF, JSON-LD, and SNOMED CT / MeSH / NeuroLex crosswalks in the embodied-knowledge-ontology session. Randy directed the entire ontological architecture, including the decision to align with standard clinical vocabularies.

## SRL Positioning

ECI is the intellectual foundation for everything SRL builds. It is the "cornered resource" in 7 Powers terms -- competitors can copy protocols but cannot replicate embodied tacit depth. The entire Pausality product is an attempt to externalize ECI fragments into 60-second teachable loops.

## Origin

Randy's opening framing: "to apply a massive amount to reach a small action as a nurse" -- the compression principle that defines ECI. In the clinical-wisdom-as-asset session: "Are you selling calm? Or are you selling state transition intelligence?" Randy's acceptance of this reframe confirms ECI is positioned as operational doctrine, not wellness.

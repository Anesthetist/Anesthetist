# Organizational Singularity

The inflection point at which agentic AI + recursive self-improvement of workflows collapses Coasean transaction costs, triggering an irreversible phase transition in organizational design (Ismail et al., 2026).

## Definition

The Organizational Singularity is not a single event but a compounding process. When the marginal cost of coordination, decision-making, and institutional knowledge approaches zero — when these functions can be executed by AI systems at near-zero cost — the economic rationale for the firm as coordination mechanism collapses in every sector where coordination costs dominate.

The firm persists as an accountability structure (see [[fiduciary-wedge]]), but its dominant logic inverts: humans stop being gatekeepers and become validators. The architecture of authority inverts.

## Mechanism

Three forces converge:
1. **Transaction cost collapse** — AI drives search, negotiation, monitoring, and decision costs to near-zero
2. **Recursive self-improvement** — Workflow-level RSI (not AGI) creates compounding gaps between firms running the loop and firms that don't (see [[recursive-workflow-improvement]])
3. **Domain Collapse** — Intelligence infrastructure converts entire problem domains from expertise-bound to compute-bound (see [[domain-collapse]])

The key distinction from 1990s internet predictions: the internet reduced the cost of *communication*, not *cognition*. AI attacks the layer the internet left untouched — interpretation, decision, execution without human intermediation.

## The Coasean Inversion

When the cost of coordination exceeds the cost of production, Coase's logic inverts. The firm doesn't just lose its advantage — it becomes the obstacle. The hierarchy is the bottleneck. The firms that win move humans from the critical path to the exception path.

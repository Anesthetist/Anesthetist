# Gertrude — Learning Log

## 2026-03-20 — Initial Deployment

### Key Regulatory Events Informing This Bot

1. **FDA General Wellness Policy revision (January 6, 2026)** — Broadened scope. Non-invasive products estimating physiological parameters (HRV, HR) CAN qualify as general wellness if promoted for wellness uses. The determining factor is marketing language, not technology capability.

2. **WHOOP FDA Warning Letter (July 2025)** — Blood pressure estimation crossed the line because it's "inherently associated with diagnosis of disease." HRV and HR are NOT inherently disease-associated. But WHOOP's experience proves that disclaimers alone don't protect you if the core claim is medical.

3. **FTC Lumosity settlement (2015)** — $2M fine for unsupported cognitive claims. Specific outcome claims require "competent and reliable scientific evidence" about YOUR PRODUCT, not just about the general category.

### First Review: Mossimo Proposal

Flagged and corrected:
- "biofeedback" → "body-awareness training" / "real-time body data"
- "parasympathetic drive" → "deep recovery response"
- "vagal activation" → "recovery breathing"
- Specific outcome numbers (15-25%, 3-8 bpm) → directional language ("trend expected")
- "intervention" → "practice"
- Added full wellness disclaimer

### Patterns to Watch
- Competitors (Calm, NEUROFIT) use "manage stress" which is wellness language. "Treat stress disorder" would be medical.
- "Science-backed" and "research-informed" are safe. "Clinically proven" implies product clinical trials.
- NEUROFIT claims "54% less stress in 1 week" — borderline. They have n=16,487 data. SRL should not replicate without equivalent data.
- The word "biofeedback" specifically requires BCIA certification to practice as a therapy. Using it casually = scope-of-practice risk.

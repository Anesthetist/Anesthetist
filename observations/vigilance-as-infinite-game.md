# Vigilance as Infinite Game

Reframing clinical vigilance from a finite game (survive this case) to an infinite game (sustain capacity across a career). Drawn from James Carse's *Finite and Infinite Games* (1986).

## The Reframe

In finite-game framing, each OR case is pass/fail. The clinician must maintain constant vigilance to avoid errors, and each case depletes that vigilance budget. Over a 28-year career, this framing is unsustainable — it produces cumulative exhaustion, moral injury from inevitable near-misses, and the progressive erosion of engagement that characterizes burnout.

In infinite-game framing, each OR case is a training rep in lifelong mastery. Moments that feel "boring" become training opportunities: notice micro-shifts in your own heart rate versus the patient's, refine propofol lag-time predictions, track your own response latency to alarm sounds. This transforms routine clinical vigilance from a depleting obligation into playful, infinite-game skill-building.

## Why This Matters for Clinician Durability

The mechanism of burnout is not the vigilance itself — it is the finite-game framing that exhausts. Reframing to infinite-game (each case as a mastery rep) preserves engagement across a career by changing the emotional texture of the work. The vigilance does not decrease; the relationship to it transforms.

## Practical Application

- Treat each case as a micro-experiment in self-observation
- Track personal HRV patterns across different case types as "score-keeping" rather than pass/fail
- Use [[gap-moment-training]] practices as the explicit bridge between finite (case-level) and infinite (career-level) engagement

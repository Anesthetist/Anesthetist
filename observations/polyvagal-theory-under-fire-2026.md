## Source

InSight+ (Medical Journal of Australia online), 2026. Also: Trauma Journal Substack (Pria Alpern, PhD), "Polyvagal Theory is Under Fire." Trauma Therapist Institute blog response.

## Summary

A paper co-authored by 38 ANS scientists titled "Why the Polyvagal Theory is Untenable" (Grossman et al., *Clinical Neuropsychiatry*, 2026) has generated widespread discussion in trauma therapy, neuroscience, and wellness communities. Critics argue PVT's evolutionary framing and neuroanatomical claims (particularly the "ventral vagal" pathway's claimed unique social engagement function) are not supported by comparative anatomy or physiology. Porges has responded, and clinical practitioners are publicly debating how much weight to give the critique vs. the observed clinical utility of PVT-informed interventions.

## Why It Matters for SRL

SRL frameworks are partially grounded in PVT language (Vagal Tone, Autonomic Regulation, Co-Regulation). While the clinical tools built on PVT remain well-supported, SRL should: (1) avoid staking theoretical credibility solely on contested PVT mechanisms, (2) emphasize the practical/phenomenological evidence base rather than the anatomical theory, and (3) consider framing SRL's autonomic concepts in terms of the broader "science of safety" rather than PVT-specific anatomy. This is an opportunity to demonstrate scientific rigor and theoretical flexibility.

# Propofol Arm-to-Brain Lag Time as Silent Vital Sign

## Observation

The time between propofol injection and loss of consciousness (LOC) — the "arm-to-brain lag time" (typically 15-30+ seconds) — is a CRNA-specific "silent vital sign" known primarily to anesthesia practitioners. This single observation reveals:

- **Blood velocity/cardiac output:** Athletic heart ~10-12s, typical adult 20-30s, failing heart >30s
- **Hemodynamic status:** Young tachycardic vs. elderly vs. septic vs. hypovolemic all produce distinct signatures
- **Pediatric patterns:** Healthy kids <10yo = "blink-and-gone" (8-12s)
- **Risk profiles:** Athletes have fast lag but risk SVR collapse; EF 30% patients have slow lag and need fractional dosing

## Clinical Integration

- Log lag time in chart ("propofol LOC = 18s") to build QI database
- Cross-validate with own pulse, waveform capnography rise timing, and HRV synchrony
- **Interoceptive feedback loop:** After the case, compare own HR trend to lag times noted — tightens the clinician's internal calibration

## The "Whose Heart Rate Is Higher?" Test

A related observation: comparing clinician HR to patient HR at any point during a case reveals the clinician's sympathetic tone relative to the patient's condition. This simple two-number comparison outperforms watching HRV waveforms for real-time state awareness.

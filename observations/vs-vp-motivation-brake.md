# VS→VP Motivation Brake: Procrastination Suppresses Initiation, Not Motivation

## Observation

The ventral striatum (VS) → ventral pallidum (VP) circuit acts as a "motivation brake" — it suppresses task initiation under aversive load without changing goal valuation. Chemogenetic inhibition of this circuit increases trial initiation without changing what the organism values. The implication: procrastination is an initiation problem, not a motivation problem.

### NeuroMinute Design Targeting This Circuit

A 60-second protocol can exploit the timing differential between fast VS aversive response and slower VP suppression lift:

1. **Name the brake (5s):** "This is the brake — not my values" → cognitive de-coupling
2. **Cyclic sighing (20s):** Rapid aversive spike downshift via extended exhale
3. **Bounded exposure (15s):** Micro-label the feeling ("reluctance," "fear of critique") → converts avoidance into labeled, contained experience
4. **Go-cue (15s):** Implementation intention + 10-second start — "I will [specific action] for 10 seconds"
5. **Execute (5s):** Movement begins before VP suppression re-engages

### Mechanistic Precision

The protocol matches intervention timing to VS/VP dynamics revealed in macaque electrophysiology — fast aversive spike + slow inhibition lift → exploit the window with immediate downshift + rapid movement initiation.

## Significance

This is the most neurobiologically precise [[neurominute]] design in the library — targeting a specific subcortical circuit with matched timing. It transforms "I know what to do but can't start" from a willpower failure into a circuit-level problem with a 60-second protocol solution.

# Clinicians Learn by Experiencing on Themselves First

## Observation

Teaching breathing/regulation techniques to clinicians is fundamentally different from teaching patients. Clinicians learn faster when they **experience the tool on themselves first** — the clinician's own nervous system becomes the learning apparatus.

### Observed Learning Pattern

1. **Demo on clinician** before teaching patient application
2. Clinician feels the autonomic shift in their own body
3. Credibility shifts from "I read the literature" to "I felt it work"
4. Transfer to patient care happens naturally — they teach what they've experienced

### Research Support

- Paul, Elam, Verhulst (2007): Breathing instruction reduced test anxiety, self-doubt, and concentration loss in medical learners
- Improved asthma/COPD management by clinicians who practice breathing techniques themselves
- Schechter et al. (2010): Enhanced immunization pain-reduction after just 1-hour teaching session

### Practical Pedagogy

- Have residents call out propofol lag time aloud — links pharmacology to hemodynamics through active participation
- Team breath modeling: "Silent vital sign" awareness built through repetition + team accountability
- Micro-win logging: Shifting from error-focus (traditional QRC) to competence-capture

## Significance

This is why the Pausality app targets clinicians first as users, not as prescribers. The CRNA who practices Gap Moment Training becomes both the strongest advocate and the most credible teacher. The product's clinical credibility scales through embodied experience, not marketing.

This observation also explains why the [[anterocept]] progression works: clinicians already have deep expertise in multi-signal monitoring (for patients). When they turn that skill inward, the transfer is rapid.

## The Finding

Hospital subsidies to anesthesia groups ("stipends") have moved from a minority practice to a near-universal one in a decade. The distribution is no longer bimodal — it is saturated. This is a **phase change**, not a cycle.

- **2015:** ~40–50% of hospitals paid stipends to their anesthesia group
- **2025:** **>95%** of hospitals subsidize anesthesia
- **2025 growth:** +16% stipend growth projected for 2025 alone
- **ASC trajectory:** 28% of ASCs paid anesthesia stipends in 2024 → 44% in 2025 (the ASC curve is 5–10 years behind the hospital curve and moving in the same direction)

## Why It Is Structural

- Labor is 80–90% of anesthesia cost of service
- CRNA compensation has risen ~42% in a decade
- Professional fees and commercial reimbursement rates have not kept pace
- Hospitals now feed a **secondary nutrient stream** (stipends) into a group that can no longer sustain itself on primary fees alone

This is not a temporary disturbance that will revert. The math does not close without the stipend, and the math is not getting easier.

## Strategic Implication for CRNAs

The stipend is the **hospital's weakest negotiating point** in its contract with the anesthesia group, and therefore the **CRNA's most under-used lever**. Most CRNAs treat compensation as a W-2 salary question. The stipend is upstream of the salary. Groups that understand the stipend math negotiate differently from groups that do not.

## Relation to the PE Rollup

The stipend trend is downstream of the PE rollup collapse. PE-backed anesthesia groups built their LBO models on a balance-billing world that no longer exists (No Surprises Act, 2022). When the primary fee stream shrank, the stipend rose to fill the gap. Hospitals absorbed the cost because the alternative — losing the anesthesia group — is operationally worse.

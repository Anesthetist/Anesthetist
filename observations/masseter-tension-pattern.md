# Masseter Tension as Interoceptive Signal

Randy's direct correction during a personal development session: "I sleep well. I don't feel tension in my chest and throat, I mostly feel it in my masseter muscles."

## Significance

This is a clinically specific self-observation from a CRNA with 28 years of experience. Masseter tension (jaw clenching/bruxism) is associated with sympathetic activation, stress processing, and suppressed expression. For a CRNA who manages airways professionally, the jaw is both a clinical instrument and a somatic stress repository — a notable intersection of professional expertise and personal embodiment.

## Interoceptive Precision

The correction itself demonstrates interoceptive literacy in action: Randy rejected a generic stress-body mapping (chest/throat tension) in favor of a precise, personally verified somatic location. This is the kind of body-specific awareness that [[interoception]] training develops — not knowing "I'm stressed" but knowing "my masseter is activating."

## Connection to Diaphragmatic Blindness

The masseter pattern may relate to [[diaphragmatic-blindness]] — the tendency to lose awareness of breath and body under stress. If stress routes to jaw tension rather than respiratory distress, the interoceptive signal may be easier to miss (jaw tension is less intrusive than chest tightness), potentially creating a blind spot in self-regulation.

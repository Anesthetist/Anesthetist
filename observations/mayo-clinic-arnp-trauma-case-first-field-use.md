# Mayo Clinic ARNP Uses Pausality Before and After 2AM Trauma Case

## Observation

A close friend of Randy's — an ARNP who leads innovation for 1,500 nurse practitioners and PAs across the entire Mayo Clinic system — used Pausality in the field during a night shift OR coverage. A motor vehicle accident trauma case arrived at approximately 2:00 AM. The typical story: alcohol involved, catastrophic injuries, the kind of case that tests everything.

He used the app **before** going in to provide care, and **after** the case.

His feedback: "It really helped."

He is watching the project from the early days with genuine curiosity. Pausality is being used at Mayo Clinic, one minute at a time.

## Significance for SRL

### 1. First Clinical Field Use at a Tier 1 Institution
This is Pausality being used in an active trauma case at Mayo Clinic — not a simulation, not a demo, not a beta test. A clinician chose to use it at 2 AM when it mattered.

### 2. Pre-Procedure AND Post-Procedure Use
The ARNP used the app both before and after the case. This validates the full Gap Moment Training arc:
- **Before:** Autonomic preparation. Shift from night-shift parasympathetic state to ventral vagal engagement. The pre-procedure NeuroMinute.
- **After:** Autonomic recovery. Processing the physiological load of a trauma case. The post-case reset.

This maps directly to the Awtry 2025 JAMA Surgery finding: optimal autonomic activation in the first 5 minutes of a procedure predicts better outcomes (AOR 0.63 for reduced complications). The ARNP intuitively used the tool at exactly the moments the science says matter most.

### 3. Night Shift Context
He said "I hate working nights." Night shift is the hardest autonomic context — circadian disruption, sleep debt, isolation, and then a trauma case. If the tool works here, it works everywhere. This is the minimum viable proof point.

### 4. The User Is a System-Level Decision Maker
This isn't a random clinician. He leads innovation for 1,500 NPs and PAs across Mayo. His evaluation carries institutional weight. If he decides this tool belongs in his innovation portfolio, it enters the Mayo system through the front door.

### 5. "It Really Helped" — The Clinician's Endorsement
Clinicians don't say tools "really helped" unless they felt a difference. This is a subjective validation from a high-acuity, high-stakes moment. Combined with biometric data (if he was wearing the Watch), this becomes a case study.

### 6. The Underdog Parallel
Randy describes him as "always an underdog, phenomenally brilliant." ARNPs and CRNAs share the experience of being underestimated within healthcare hierarchies. This user gets the mission at an identity level, not just a product level.

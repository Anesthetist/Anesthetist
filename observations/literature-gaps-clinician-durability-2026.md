## Overview

A systematic scan of 15 clinician durability studies published between October 2025 and March 2026 reveals seven distinct literature gaps. Each gap represents a space where existing research establishes the problem and validates the underlying mechanisms, but no published work addresses the specific intervention approach that SRL is building. These are not speculative gaps — they are documented absences in an active research field.

## The Seven Gaps

### 1. No studies combine wearable HRV biofeedback with clinician burnout intervention

**What exists:** Kane et al. (2025) confirm HRV monitoring tracks stress/recovery in doctors. He et al. (2026) show HRV predicts ICU nurse fatigue. Kim et al. (2026) demonstrate wearable feasibility and acceptability. Feigel et al. (2025) identify a digital phenotype of allostatic load via wearables.

**What's missing:** All studies are observational — they monitor but do not intervene. No published study uses real-time HRV biofeedback as the basis for a clinician burnout prevention intervention. The monitoring-to-intervention gap is wide open.

**SRL position:** Gap Moment Training + wearable HRV integration directly fills this gap.

### 2. No breathing-specific interventions for perioperative clinicians

**What exists:** Kor et al. (2025) show mindfulness-based interventions improve caregiver HRV. Diwan et al. (2026) document that 77% of perioperative clinicians don't use non-pharmacological methods. Jansson et al. (2026) identify embodied learning as essential to anesthesia expertise.

**What's missing:** No published study tests breathing-specific interventions (resonant breathing, paced breathing, coherence training) in perioperative clinician populations. The existing mindfulness RCTs are generic — not tailored to the unique constraints and rhythms of OR/PACU/ICU workflow.

**SRL position:** NeuroMinute protocols are designed specifically for perioperative workflow integration.

### 3. No micro-interventions embedded in clinical workflow

**What exists:** All existing interventions (Kor et al. RCT, general mindfulness programs) require off-shift time — 90-minute sessions, dedicated workshop blocks, or retreat formats.

**What's missing:** No published intervention is designed to be delivered within clinical workflow — during shift handoffs, between cases, or in brief recovery windows. The assumption across the literature is that intervention = separate time commitment.

**SRL position:** NeuroMinute (60-second protocols) and Gap Moment Training are designed for in-workflow delivery. This is a fundamental design differentiation.

### 4. No longitudinal durability studies tracking autonomic regulation over years/decades

**What exists:** Feigel et al. (2025) tracked allostatic load over 10 weeks. Kane et al. (2025) reviewed studies with monitoring periods of days to weeks. The longest intervention study (Kor et al.) was 6 weeks.

**What's missing:** No study tracks autonomic regulation capacity across career stages — years or decades. The concept of "clinician durability" implies longitudinal measurement, but no longitudinal data exists. We don't know if early-career HRV patterns predict late-career burnout or exit.

**SRL position:** The GMT career arc framework (seeding, healing, fortifying) is designed for longitudinal tracking. First-mover advantage in establishing the longitudinal dataset.

### 5. No MAIA-2 + HRV integration in clinician populations

**What exists:** MAIA-2 (Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness) is validated. HRV monitoring is validated. Both are individually used in research. Nosbisch (2026) and Jansson (2026) identify interoceptive competence themes in anesthesia practice.

**What's missing:** No study combines MAIA-2 self-report with continuous HRV monitoring in clinician populations. The subjective-objective integration that would validate interoceptive literacy as a trainable clinical skill does not exist in the published literature.

**SRL position:** The Somnistics Readiness Battery (SRB-60) is designed to integrate MAIA-2 with HRV metrics. This combination is a core differentiator.

### 6. Second victim support lacks physiological intervention component

**What exists:** Literature recognizes second victim syndrome (clinicians traumatized by adverse events). Feingold et al. (2026) show trauma has detectable autonomic signatures. Existing second victim programs are peer support and counseling-based.

**What's missing:** No second victim support program includes a physiological intervention component — autonomic regulation, HRV biofeedback, or breathing protocols to address the autonomic dysregulation that accompanies traumatic clinical events.

**SRL position:** Gap Moment Training's "healing" phase directly addresses post-event autonomic recovery. Physiological first aid for clinicians after adverse events.

### 7. Moral injury interventions lack autonomic regulation substrate

**What exists:** McDermott et al. (2026) identify meaning and purpose as essential for clinician well-being. Paramita et al. (2026) and McQuaid et al. (2026) document systemic drivers of clinician distress. Moral injury is increasingly recognized as distinct from burnout.

**What's missing:** Existing moral injury interventions are cognitive/narrative (reflective writing, values clarification, peer discussion). None include an autonomic regulation component to address the physiological substrate of moral distress — the embodied experience of being unable to provide care that aligns with values.

**SRL position:** Somnistics integrates autonomic regulation with meaning-making. The physiological substrate enables the cognitive processing — not the reverse.

## Strategic Implications

These seven gaps share a common pattern: the field has validated the *monitoring* and identified the *problem*, but has not yet tested the specific *intervention architecture* that SRL is building. This creates a time-limited window of competitive advantage:

1. **First-mover in wearable-integrated clinician interventions** (Gaps 1, 3)
2. **First-mover in breathing-specific perioperative protocols** (Gap 2)
3. **First-mover in MAIA-2 + HRV clinician integration** (Gap 5)
4. **First-mover in longitudinal autonomic career tracking** (Gap 4)
5. **First-mover in physiological second victim/moral injury support** (Gaps 6, 7)

Each gap is publishable. Each gap has a clear SRL product or protocol that addresses it. The evidence base supporting the underlying mechanisms is strong — what's missing is the specific intervention study.

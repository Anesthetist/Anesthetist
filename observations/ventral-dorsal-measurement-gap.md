# Ventral vs. Dorsal Vagal Tone Cannot Be Separated by Standard HRV

## Observation

Standard HRV measures (HF-power, RMSSD, pNN50) lump all parasympathetic influence together — they cannot directly separate ventral vagal (safety/social engagement) from dorsal vagal (shutdown/freeze) activation.

### Why This Matters

- HF-HRV ≈ RSA, which typically reflects ventral vagal under normal conditions
- But dorsal vagal activation (extreme threat/freeze) can ALSO slow HR — without rhythmic RSA oscillations
- Low HRV may indicate dorsal dominance OR simply lower vagal tone overall
- A clinician in dorsal vagal shutdown (dissociation) looks "calm" on HRV but is functionally impaired

### Experimental Approaches to Differentiation

1. **RSA reactivity protocols:** Strong RSA under moderate challenge = robust ventral vagal function
2. **Postural challenge tests:** Vagal Efficiency Index = HR recovery speed post-stressor
3. **Behavioral + subjective correlates:** Combine HRV with facial expressivity, vocal prosody, social engagement, affect flatness
4. **Workarounds:** Consistent measurement protocol (same posture, time, controlled breathing at 6 breaths/min)

### Wearable Reality

Apple Watch PPG is acceptable for HR (small bias vs. ECG) but less accurate for HRV (RMSSD). Most wearables lack respiration tracking → RSA confounded by irregular breathing. PPG-derived HRV resolution is insufficient for subtle dorsal/ventral differentiation.

## Significance

This is a research opportunity for SRL: developing polyvagal-informed composite markers (HRV + behavioral + contextual) for the [[readyscore]] that can distinguish "calm and engaged" (ventral) from "shut down and dissociated" (dorsal). This would advance both product capability and polyvagal theory application.

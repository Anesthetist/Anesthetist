# PTSD Prehabilitation: Reverse-Engineering Treatment into Prevention

## Observation

Randy identified a significant gap in the clinical literature: PTSD treatments (prolonged exposure, EMDR, CPT) are well-established for post-trauma recovery, but **no systematic framework exists for teaching those same techniques *before* trauma exposure** to build resilience prophylactically.

> "Reverse engineering the PTSD treatment exercises and 'teach for the test.' What I mean is apply PTSD treatments early in the career, but prior to acute trauma to build resilience."

The insight: if specific breathing patterns, interoceptive awareness, and autonomic regulation techniques help people recover from PTSD, those same techniques — practiced *before* trauma exposure — should build the neural infrastructure that makes PTSD less likely to develop.

## Current Gaps

- Limited ability to intervene effectively immediately after trauma to prevent chronic PTSD
- No standardized prehabilitation protocol for high-risk clinical populations
- Existing resilience programs (SMART, psychological interventions) are not specific to the autonomic regulation mechanisms that PTSD disrupts
- No framework connecting [[vagal-tone]] training to PTSD vulnerability reduction

## Somnistics as Prehabilitation

[[gap-moment-training]] is positioned as PTSD prehabilitation: regular practice during low-stakes transitions builds the autonomic flexibility and interoceptive capacity that protects against traumatic state lock-in. The [[neuro-ouroboros]] 21-day curriculum systematically develops the neural infrastructure that PTSD would otherwise disrupt.

## Significance

This reframes somnistics from "wellness tool" to "clinical safety infrastructure" — a patient safety argument that resonates with hospital administrators and insurance payers. It represents a novel research direction and potential IP differentiation.

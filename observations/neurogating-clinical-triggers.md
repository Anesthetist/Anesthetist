# Unconscious Autonomic Gating in Experienced CRNAs

## Observation

In clinical practice, experienced CRNAs develop an unconscious "gating" capacity — the ability to detect autonomic shifts in patients (skin color changes, subtle muscle tone shifts, breathing pattern variations) before electronic monitors alarm. This pre-conscious detection mirrors Porges' concept of neuroception but applied outward to patient monitoring.

[[neurogating]] systematizes this pattern for *self-regulation*: where the experienced clinician unconsciously gates patient signals, the Neurogating algorithm gates the user's own biometric signals to trigger timely interventions. The system models the clinical decision-making pattern: detect state → classify urgency → select intervention → confirm response.

## Significance

The clinical precedent validates the Neurogating approach: experienced CRNAs already perform biological gating unconsciously. The technology extends this to self-directed regulation and makes it accessible to less experienced practitioners.

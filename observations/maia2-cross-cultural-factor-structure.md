# MAIA-2C Cross-Cultural Factor Structure: Noticing Fails in Naive Populations, Breath Loads as Regulation

## Observation

Analysis of the MAIA-2C Chinese validation study (Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2022; N=627 naive university students) reveals critical insights for Pausality's measurement architecture:

1. **Noticing subscale collapses** in naive populations (α ≈ 0.58, factor fragmentation) — Items 1–4 don't hang together without prior mind-body training
2. **Breath item (Item 16) loads with Self-Regulation** rather than Attention Regulation — "paying attention to my breath" is subjectively experienced as *calming down* before it becomes an *attentional skill*
3. **7-factor model** (not 8) with ~61% variance explained; Noticing removed entirely
4. **Non-judging inverts** in naive samples — opposite of trained mind-body practitioners
5. **Beginner nervous systems are disorganized** at the level of interoceptive representation — facets don't move in lockstep early on

## Significance for Somnistics

This validates the progressive curriculum design: early users will experience breath as a state regulator before they can articulate it as attentional skill. The MAIA subscale structure *before training* reveals the user's subjective map — Somnistics interventions should be modeled as re-wiring relationships among interoceptive nodes, not just "turning the awareness dial up."

### Measurement Architecture Options

| Option | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| Full 37-item MAIA-2 | Early RCTs, English-speaking markets |
| MAIA-2C (31 items, 7 factors) | Mainland China pilots |
| Somnistics Brief Index (10-15 items) | Once N > few hundred per market; anchor in MAIA items for back-translation |

### Strategic IP Angle

The proprietary asset isn't the questionnaire — it's the mapping from intervention → (MAIA change + physiology change) across contexts and cultures.

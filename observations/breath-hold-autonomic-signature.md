# Breath-Hold as Autonomic Signature in Clinical Practice

CRNAs routinely hold their breath during intubation. Nobody tracks this. Nobody talks about it. Randy Graybeal (28-year CRNA) surfaced it as a clinically meaningful data stream hiding in plain sight.

> "How often have I held my breath while I intubate? And what does that tell me?"

## The Metabolic Mirror

During intubation, clinician and patient are in opposite metabolic states:

- **Patient**: Anesthetized, cold, paralyzed — VCO2 suppressed, metabolism reduced
- **CRNA**: Sympathetically activated, physically moving, cognitively loaded — VCO2 likely 30–50% above resting

The CRNA's CO2 rises faster. Their breath hold has real physiological cost. And yet they hold it — unconsciously synchronizing with the patient's apnea. This is [[interpersonal-physiological-synchrony]] made visible.

## The Data Hiding in the Hold

Breath-hold duration during the apneic window is a measurable autonomic signature:

- Routine case → shorter hold, faster recovery
- Complex pediatric airway → longer hold, slower recovery  
- Already dysregulated from prior case → shortest hold (truncated capacity)
- Pre-shift anxiety → altered breathing before induction even begins

Logged over time, this is each clinician's personalized **autonomic map** — the anti-fragility curve nobody has ever drawn.

## Why This Is Novel IP

No existing tool captures longitudinal interoceptive patterns across case types and complexity levels over time. This data — who you are physiologically, across thousands of clinical encounters — is what Pausality could uniquely measure.

The product question: what if the question before a difficult case isn't "can I do this?" but "what does my breathing tell me about my capacity *right now* to do this safely?"

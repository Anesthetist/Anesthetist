# Team Breath: Co-Regulation in the OR

## Observation

Asking the OR team to take one slow nasal inhale + long exhale right after the surgical timeout shifts the entire room to a ventral-vagal state. This is co-regulation in action — one person's calm nervous system entrains others through the social engagement system.

### Observed Co-Regulation Mechanisms

- **Team breath before start:** A single synchronized breath after timeout shifts collective autonomic baseline
- **Prosody during extubation:** Calm vocal tone during emergence lowers both patient and clinician heart rates via vagal pathways
- **Implicit modeling:** Residents learn the causality between clinician calm and patient stability by hearing/seeing it demonstrated, not lectured
- **Gratitude during quiet phases:** Naming one team member for something specific counters negativity bias and lifts collective morale
- **Emergence coordination:** Extubation requires full sympathetic-parasympathetic coordination; polyvagal cues (eye contact, prosody, touch) settle the team and patient simultaneously

## Polyvagal Mechanism

The ventral vagal complex links face, voice, and breath — all mediated by cranial nerves. When the CRNA modulates their own breath and voice tone, they activate the social engagement system, which radiates to everyone in the room through mirror neuron and vagal co-activation pathways.

## Significance

Co-regulation reframes [[gap-moment-training]] from an individual practice to a team-level intervention. One clinician's regulation capacity becomes a resource for the entire team. This is the interpersonal neurobiology extension of somnistics — training one person's nervous system improves outcomes for everyone in the room.

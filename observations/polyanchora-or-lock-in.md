# Multi-Anchor Attention Training Improves OR Lock-In Speed

## Observation

CRNAs who practice multi-anchor attentional switching (the [[polyanchora]] method) report faster perceptual "lock-in" during airway management — the critical moment of laryngoscopy and intubation where multiple sensory channels must be simultaneously monitored (visual anatomy, tactile feedback, monitor sounds, team communication).

Practitioners report:
- Faster identification of airway landmarks under OR noise
- Fewer fixation errors (getting "stuck" on one signal while missing others)
- Easier maintenance of broad awareness while performing fine motor tasks
- Subjective sense of calm under time pressure when multi-anchor training is practiced regularly

## Significance

This observation suggests that PolyAnchora training transfers directly to clinical performance in high-stakes moments. The multi-anchor scaffolding builds the same attentional infrastructure that complex procedures demand.

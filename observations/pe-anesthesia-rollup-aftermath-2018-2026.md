## The Finding

The private-equity anesthesia rollup wave of 2018–2022 is visibly collapsing in 2023–2026. This matters to practicing CRNAs because employment terms, stipends, partnership tracks, and PTO are downstream of capital structures built in a low-interest, balance-billing world that no longer exists.

## The Four Marquee Cases

**USAP / Welsh Carson.** United States Anesthesia Partners, the largest PE-backed anesthesia group, was sued by the FTC in September 2023. The case settled January 2025. Welsh Carson sold its interest to PhyMed on March 31, 2025. Arc: 2012 → 2025.

**NAPA.** North American Partners in Anesthesia acquired American Anesthesiology in 2020. Estimated 2026 revenue ~$5B across >500 facilities. The survivor of the wave. Arc: 2020 → 2026 and continuing.

**MEDNAX → Pediatrix.** MEDNAX recorded a $1.45B anesthesia goodwill impairment, divested its anesthesia business to NAPA, and reoriented as Pediatrix. Arc: 2019 → 2020.

**Envision (KKR).** KKR bought Envision for ~$9.9B in 2018. Chapter 11 May 2023 with $7.76B debt. The No Surprises Act was the direct accelerant. KKR exited. Arc: 2018 → 2023.

## The Pattern

Debt-financed rollups that looked attractive in a low-interest, balance-billing world collapsed when three forces arrived together:

1. **No Surprises Act (January 2022)** — ended the out-of-network / balance-billing revenue stream
2. **Commercial payer pushback** — narrower contracts, slower payment
3. **CRNA wage inflation** — ~42% over a decade, labor at 80–90% of cost of service

If your employer's debt was syndicated in 2019–2022, your current stipend, PTO, and partnership track are downstream of that capital structure.

## Strategic Implication for CRNAs

A CRNA job is not just a clinical position. It is a position inside a capital structure. Capital structures have terms, maturities, and failure modes that determine practice conditions, compensation ladders, and contract durability. Most CRNAs cannot name their employer's debt structure or the year it was syndicated. Those who can make different employment decisions.

## Relation to the Stipend Phase Change

The PE collapse is one of the primary reasons hospital stipends moved from ~50% of hospitals in 2015 to >95% in 2025. When primary fee streams contracted, hospitals absorbed the shortfall through stipends because losing the anesthesia group is operationally worse than paying the stipend.

# Breath Modulates Circadian Clock and Subjective Time Perception

## Observation

Breathing modulates the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN/circadian clock) via vagal afferents. Each inhalation resets the internal clock by milliseconds. Slow breathing produces subjective temporal dilation — time feels like it stretches.

This positions breath not just as a regulation tool but as a **temporal engineering** mechanism. The clinician who practices slow breathing during a gap moment is literally expanding their subjective experience of available time.

### Framework: "Chrono-Somatic Training"

- Inhalation = clock reset (micro-adjustment of temporal processing)
- Slow breathing = temporal dilation (more subjective time per objective second)
- Fast breathing = temporal compression (time speeds up, tunnel vision)
- Resonant frequency breathing = temporal coherence (time perception stabilizes)

## Significance

This reframes the 60-second [[neurominute]] from "a minute of breathing" to "engineering 60 seconds that feel like 90." Under acute stress, time compresses (everything happens too fast). Slow breathing reverses this compression — giving the clinician the subjective experience of having more time to think, decide, and act.

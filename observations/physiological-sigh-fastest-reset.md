# Physiological Sigh: The Fastest Known Stress Reset (10 Seconds)

## Observation

The Huberman Lab breathing protocol taxonomy identifies the physiological sigh as the fastest known stress reduction technique — effective in approximately 10 seconds:

### 5-Protocol Hierarchy (Need-State → Protocol)

| Need | Protocol | Time | Mechanism |
|------|----------|------|-----------|
| Instant calm | Physiological Sigh | 10s | Dual-inhale (nose) + long exhale (mouth) = fastest parasympathetic activation |
| Daily stress reduction | Cyclic Sighing | 5 min/day | Repeated physiological sighs; beat meditation in Stanford RCT (Balban 2023) |
| Focus + balance | Box Breathing | 2 min | 4-4-4-4 seconds; Navy SEAL technique; CO₂ tolerance building |
| Sleep preparation | Extended Exhale | 2-5 min | 4s inhale + 6-8s exhale; RSA principle for parasympathetic dominance |
| Energy/alertness | Cyclic Hyperventilation | 5-10 min | Wim Hof-style; sympathetic activation (warnings: not in water/driving) |

### Physiological Sigh Mechanism

The dual-inhale reinflates collapsed alveoli (lung sacs), maximizing surface area for gas exchange. The extended exhale then drives a strong parasympathetic response through prolonged vagal activation. Two inhales → one long exhale = fastest path from sympathetic to parasympathetic.

### Clinical Integration

The physiological sigh is the "emergency" protocol in the [[gap-moment-training]] toolkit — when there isn't even 60 seconds, a single physiological sigh in 10 seconds produces measurable autonomic shift. It's the true [[minimum-effective-dose]] floor.

## Significance

This taxonomy gives [[neurominute]] protocols a clear hierarchy: physiological sigh (10s emergency) → box breathing (60s standard) → cyclic sighing (5 min deep reset). The Huberman Lab research provides the most credible external validation available.

# Clinical Measurement Instruments Compendium

A structured reference of validated clinical instruments relevant to SRL's target populations (CRNAs, healthcare providers, high-stress professionals) and core use cases (burnout reduction, resilience building, sleep optimization, HRV biofeedback outcomes). Every instrument listed has published psychometric validation data.

---

## 1. CRNA / Anesthesia-Specific Instruments

### No CRNA-Specific Validated Instrument Exists

According to PubMed (Congdon et al., 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.70278/AANAJ/.0000001016)), a 2025 integrative review of 15 CRNA burnout studies found that **no instrument has been validated specifically in a CRNA-only population**. Researchers consistently borrow general healthcare instruments. The most commonly used instruments in CRNA burnout research are:

- **Maslach Burnout Inventory — Human Services Survey (MBI-HSS)** — used in the majority of CRNA studies
- **Oldenburg Burnout Inventory (OLBI)** — used in several CRNA studies
- **ProQOL** — used for compassion fatigue in CRNA populations
- **Copenhagen Burnout Inventory (CBI)** — emerging alternative

**Key finding:** CRNA burnout prevalence ranges from 12.5% to 72% across studies. Significant contributing factors: lack of autonomy (n=7 studies), lack of leadership support (n=4), moral distress (n=3), poor CRNA-physician relations (n=3).

**AANA Wellness Survey:** The AANA does not publish a proprietary measurement instrument. Their wellness resources reference established instruments (MBI, ProQOL). The 2019 AANA Survey of CRNAs collected job satisfaction data but used custom items rather than a validated stand-alone scale.

> **SRL Implication:** This is a gap. If SRL validates a brief burnout/resilience composite in a CRNA population, it would be the first CRNA-specific instrument — a publishable contribution and credibility anchor.

### Actigraphy / Wearable Sleep Validation

No single "instrument" exists, but key validation references for wearable sleep measurement include:

- **AASM Systematic Review (2018):** Actigraphy suitable for assessing total sleep time and sleep onset latency. PMC6040804.
- **Accuracy:** Participant-specific accuracy generally > 80% for sleep/wake classification vs. polysomnography, but limited specificity for sleep stages.
- **Consumer devices:** Most consumer sleep trackers perform comparably to research-grade actigraphy on total sleep time metrics (de Zambotti et al., 2019; *Sleep*, 44(5), zsaa291).
- **Limitation:** Actigraphy cannot reliably differentiate deep vs. REM vs. light sleep stages.

## 7. HRV / Biofeedback Outcome Measures

### No Single Standardized HRV Biofeedback Outcome Battery Exists

The field lacks a single validated "HRV biofeedback outcome scale." Instead, studies use a combination of:

### Physiological Metrics (Time Domain)

| Metric | What It Measures | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **RMSSD** | Parasympathetic (vagal) activity; beat-to-beat variability | Primary PNS index |
| **SDNN** | Overall autonomic balance (SNS + PNS) | Total variability |
| **pNN50** | Percentage of successive intervals differing by > 50ms | Vagal tone |

### Physiological Metrics (Frequency Domain)

| Metric | What It Measures | Frequency Band |
|---|---|---|
| **HF-HRV** | Parasympathetic modulation | 0.15-0.40 Hz |
| **LF-HRV** | Mixed sympathetic + parasympathetic (includes baroreflex) | 0.04-0.15 Hz |
| **LF/HF Ratio** | Sympathovagal balance (contested validity) | Ratio |
| **Coherence Score** | Sine-wave regularity of heart rhythm at ~0.1 Hz | ~0.1 Hz peak |

### Standard HRV Biofeedback Protocol Assessment

Based on review of clinical trials, the typical HRV biofeedback outcome assessment includes:

1. **Pre/post physiological:** 5-minute resting HRV recording (RMSSD, SDNN, HF-HRV, coherence)
2. **Resonance frequency identification:** Breathing rate that maximizes LF amplitude (~6 breaths/min for most adults)
3. **Session metrics:** Average coherence score, time in coherence, breathing pace adherence
4. **Psychometric battery:** Typically PSS-10 + GAD-7 or STAI + domain-specific instrument (e.g., ISI for sleep, MBI for burnout)
5. **Follow-up timepoints:** Immediately post-intervention, 1 month, 3 months

### Key Reference Standards

- **European Society of Cardiology / NASPE Task Force (1996):** Standards for HRV measurement. DOI: [10.1161/01.CIR.93.5.1043](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.CIR.93.5.1043) — establishes time/frequency domain definitions
- **Shaffer & Ginsberg (2017):** Overview of HRV metrics and norms. [PMC5624990](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5624990/)
- **Lehrer & Gevirtz (2014):** Heart rate variability biofeedback: how and why does it work? *Frontiers in Psychology*, 5, 756.

> **SRL Implication:** SRL should define its own "Somnistics HRV Biofeedback Outcome Protocol" that standardizes: (1) 5-min resting RMSSD + coherence pre/post, (2) PSS-10 + domain instrument, (3) session-level coherence metrics from the app. This becomes a differentiating asset.

# Bandwidth Saturation: Cumulative Micro-Events, Not Single Traumas

## Observation

> "Many stressors are not dramatic once-in-a-lifetime traumas. They are repeated, cumulative micro-events: alarms, rushed decisions, interpersonal friction, near-misses, catastrophic cases, moral injury, sleep disruption, and chronic hypervigilance."

The conventional narrative frames clinician burnout as caused by major traumatic events (patient deaths, malpractice suits, catastrophic errors). Randy's observation from 28 years is that **the real mechanism is bandwidth saturation from accumulated micro-events** — each individually manageable, but collectively overwhelming.

## The Mechanism

Each micro-event produces a small sympathetic activation that should resolve during the next transition. When it doesn't resolve (because the clinician has no regulation practice), the activation carries forward:

- **Case 1 → residual arousal (5%)**
- **Case 2 → residual + new arousal (12%)**
- **Case 3 → compounding (22%)**
- **By case 8 → sympathetic saturation → error vulnerability**

This is "neural transition failure" — not failure to handle any individual stressor, but failure to complete the autonomic reset between stressors.

## Somnistics Solution

[[gap-moment-training]] directly addresses bandwidth saturation by ensuring autonomic reset at each transition point. Even a single 60-second [[neurominute]] between cases can prevent the compounding effect by completing the sympathetic wind-down before the next case begins.

## Significance

This reframes the business case for Pausality: the product doesn't prevent dramatic events (impossible), it prevents the accumulation of unresolved micro-activations that make dramatic events more likely and their impact more severe.

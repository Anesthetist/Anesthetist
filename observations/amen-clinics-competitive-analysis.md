# Amen Clinics — Competitive Analysis

**Source:** [amenclinics.com](https://www.amenclinics.com/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amen_Clinics)
**Founder:** Daniel Amen, MD
**Database:** 200,000+ brain SPECT scans (claimed largest in the world)
**Locations:** Multiple US clinics
**Cost:** $3,500-$8,000+ for evaluation (SPECT scan + consultation)

## What They Do

1. **SPECT brain imaging** — two scans per patient (rest + concentration task)
2. Identify regions of overactivity/underactivity in cerebral blood flow
3. Classify brain patterns into "brain types"
4. Prescribe personalized treatment: medication, nutraceuticals, CBT, lifestyle, neurofeedback
5. Follow-up at 2-4 weeks

## Their Evidence Position
- 200,000+ scan database
- Multiple published papers by Dr. Amen and colleagues
- **BUT:** American Psychiatric Association formally criticized clinical use of SPECT for treatment planning
- APA statement: "the clinical utility of neuroimaging techniques for planning of individualized treatment has not yet been shown"
- Significant controversy in psychiatric community about whether SPECT adds clinical value over standard assessment

## What They Do Well
1. **Scale** — multiple clinics, massive database
2. **Visual narrative** — brain scans are compelling for patients ("see your brain")
3. **Comprehensive treatment** — multimodal (medication + supplements + therapy + lifestyle)
4. **Public presence** — PBS specials, bestselling books, large following
5. **Data asset** — 200K scans is a moat

## SRL Differentiation

| Dimension | Amen Clinics | SRL/Pausality |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Diagnostic** | SPECT brain imaging ($3.5-8K) | HRV biofeedback (Apple Watch, ~free) |
| **Treatment** | Multimodal (meds, supplements, therapy) | Autonomic training (breathwork, interoception, biofeedback) |
| **Evidence position** | Controversial — APA critique | Evidence-graded, honest about gaps |
| **Delivery** | Clinic-based (must travel) | App-based (anywhere) |
| **Population** | General psychiatric | CRNA/clinician performance |
| **Mechanism** | Blood flow patterns → brain types | Autonomic regulation → interoceptive precision → clinical performance |
| **Cost** | $3,500-8,000+ per evaluation | Subscription |
| **Scalability** | Clinic-limited | App-unlimited |
| **Ongoing** | Follow-up visits | Daily practice with continuous biofeedback |
| **Regulatory risk** | APA criticism of SPECT use | FDA general wellness (lower risk) |

## Strategic Observations

### What SRL Can Learn
1. **The "see your brain" narrative** — visual data is compelling. SRL's equivalent: "see your nervous system in real-time" via HRV dashboards.
2. **Database as moat** — Amen's 200K scans are a data asset. SRL's neurotagged session data at scale could become a comparable moat.
3. **Brain type classification** — controversial but commercially effective. SRL's autonomic profiling (via MAIA-2-CRNA + HRV patterns) could offer a more evidence-based version.
4. **The controversy is instructive** — Amen over-claimed and got APA criticism. SRL's evidence grading framework and "the literature suggests" discipline prevents this.

### Where SRL Has Structural Advantages
1. **Evidence honesty** — SRL grades every claim. Amen asserts.
2. **Consumer hardware** — no clinic visit needed
3. **Ongoing training** — not a one-time assessment
4. **Clinical population** — CRNA credibility vs. general psychiatry controversy
5. **Neurotagging** — structured metadata that Amen's scan database lacks

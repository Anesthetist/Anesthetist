McGilchrist's 2000-page *The Matter with Things* has exactly ONE paragraph on interoception (p.176). Approximately 10 anatomy/physiology/anesthesiology textbooks have TWO short paragraphs total on interoception.

Randy's reaction: "Bizarre."

## Significance

This documents a massive gap in the literature that validates SRL's thesis. The right anterior insula is the biological correlate of McGilchrist's philosophical claim about the primacy of right-hemisphere experience, but McGilchrist never stays long enough to develop it. Interoception is the "missing hinge" between right-hemisphere embodied knowing and clinical/scientific frameworks.

SRL stands exactly where McGilchrist's argument stops.

## Primary Research Finding

This is not a secondary observation -- Randy checked the indexes himself across multiple standard clinical texts. The near-total absence of interoception from the textbooks that train clinicians is a primary finding that shapes SRL's entire market thesis: the field has a blind spot, and that blind spot has consequences for clinician performance, durability, and patient safety.

# Sleep Debt Directly Degrades Clinical Decision-Making

## Observation

Sleep deprivation on medical reasoning performance is well-documented in anesthesia: post-24-hour shifts show measurably reduced reasoning and situational performance. But the more insidious pattern is chronic partial sleep debt — the cumulative effect of on-call disruptions, irregular shift schedules, and circadian dysregulation.

Modern anesthesia and trauma work run on constant vigilance, interrupted sleep, and relentless responsibility. Clinicians are trained for the event but not for how to prepare their own nervous system for the next critical moment.

## Clinical Impact

- Burned-out anesthesia providers are demonstrably more likely to commit medical errors and deliver lower-quality care
- Sleep debt compounds with [[bandwidth-saturation-micro-events]] — the two are multiplicative, not additive
- Circadian dysregulation disrupts the very autonomic flexibility that [[gap-moment-training]] builds

## Significance

Sleep quality is the foundation layer that all other somnistics interventions rest on. A [[neurominute]] practiced by a sleep-deprived clinician will produce smaller autonomic shifts than the same protocol practiced by a rested clinician. This argues for treating sleep hygiene as a prerequisite, not an afterthought.

# Gap Moment Training Origin: 28 Years of Transitions I'd Been Sleepwalking Through

## The Discovery

The seed for Gap Moment Training came not from the research literature but from a simple act of self-observation. After 28 years as a CRNA — transplant, pediatric trauma, cardiac — Randy began looking at his own day through the lens of trainable transitions. The question wasn't "when can I find time to practice?" but "where am I already transitioning, and what am I doing with those moments?"

The answer was immediate and surprising: 15 to 20 clear training moments appeared in a single day, without trying hard to find them. Walking to the OR. The seconds before induction. Sitting in the car before going inside. Waiting for a room turnover. The hallway between cases. Before and after hard conversations. Each one was a window — typically 30 to 90 seconds — that had been filled with phone-scrolling, rumination, or nothing at all.

The realization: **the training surface already existed inside the workday.** It didn't need to be bolted on. It needed to be recognized and repurposed.

## The Burchard Catalyst

Brendan Burchard's "transition ritual" from *High Performance Habits* (2017) served as an early catalyst for naming the pattern. Burchard describes a practice he performs before getting out of his car after arriving home from work:

- **First 30 seconds:** Repeating "relax" while scanning the body for neuromuscular tension and consciously releasing it
- **Second 30 seconds:** Envisioning how he wants the people he's about to see to feel

This is, structurally, a two-phase gap moment drill: somatic reset (parasympathetic activation via body scan and tension release) followed by intentional priming (cognitive-attentional reorientation). The total duration: 60 seconds — one neurominute.

What Burchard described as a single life hack, Randy recognized as a pattern that occurred dozens of times per day in clinical practice. The insight wasn't "do what Burchard does." The insight was: **Burchard accidentally described one gap moment, and a CRNA's day contains 30 to 40 of them.**

## From Personal Discovery to Structured Method

The progression:

1. **Recognition** — Seeing transitions as trainable windows rather than dead time
2. **Counting** — Discovering 15-20 moments in a regular day (later refined to 30-40 per CRNA shift through systematic observation; see [[shift-transitions-30-40]])
3. **Experimentation** — Exploring different uses for different transition types (pre-case grounding, post-error recovery, inter-case reset, end-of-shift decompression)
4. **Naming** — Coining "Gap Moment" to distinguish this from generic mindfulness or meditation
5. **Structuring** — Developing specific drills matched to specific transition types (The Stability Snap, The Radiant Exhale, The Error Reversal Loop, etc.)
6. **Validating** — Discovering the evidence base (Porges, Lehrer, Zaccaro, etc.) that explained *why* this worked, after the experiential discovery that it did
7. **Generalizing** — Testing with non-CRNA populations (law students at BLIP Clinic; see [[blip-clinic-gap-moments-universal]]) and confirming universality

## Why This Narrative Matters

The origin story carries specific rhetorical weight for different audiences:

**For CRNAs and anesthesia providers:** "A 28-year CRNA looked at his own shift and found 30-40 moments he'd been sleepwalking through." This lands in ten seconds because every CRNA immediately recognizes those transitions. It's not theoretical — it's their Tuesday.

**For enterprise buyers (program directors, CNOs):** The discovery came from inside clinical practice, not from a wellness vendor or app developer. This is a clinician-built tool, not a consumer product repackaged for healthcare.

**For investors:** The insight that the training surface is embedded in existing workflow — requiring no additional time allocation — is the core product-market insight. It means adoption doesn't compete with clinical demands. The 30-40 daily reps create a habit loop that no weekly meditation class can match.

**For researchers:** The experiential discovery preceded the literature validation. Randy found the pattern, then found the science that explained it. This is the opposite of most academic-to-product pipelines, and it means the concept has face validity with practitioners before the evidence is even presented.

## The One-Line Version

"I started counting the transitions in my day and found 20 moments I'd been sleepwalking through. That's when I realized: the training surface was already there — I just hadn't seen it."

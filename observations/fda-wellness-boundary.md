# FDA Wellness vs. Medical Claims Boundary for Pausality

## Observation

Pausality must stay within FDA General Wellness guidance to avoid medical device classification. The two-factor test: (1) intended only for general wellness use, and (2) presents low risk to safety.

### Safe Claims

- "Promotes relaxation and stress reduction"
- "Helps manage daily stress"
- "See your heart rate respond to breathing"
- "Real-time biofeedback for stress management"
- With healthy lifestyle caveat: "may help reduce the impact of daily stress"

### Prohibited Claims

- Any diagnostic language ("diagnoses anxiety")
- Treatment claims ("treats anxiety", "cures panic attacks")
- Clinical thresholds ("Your HRV is abnormal")
- Clinical equivalence ("clinical-grade biofeedback")

### 2026 FDA Update (January 6)

Physiologic parameters (HRV) explicitly permitted if non-invasive, wellness-only, and avoiding clinical claims. This legitimizes the biometric feedback approach while maintaining the wellness boundary.

## Significance

This boundary shapes every piece of copy, every notification, and every data display in Pausality. The Breathe Nudge language review (from PRD) flagged "Heart rate spike detected" as borderline diagnostic — rewritten to "Your heart rate is higher than usual while you're at rest." Every touchpoint must be reviewed through this lens.

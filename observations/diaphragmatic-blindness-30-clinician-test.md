# 28/30 Anesthesia Professionals Cannot Engage Their Diaphragm on Command

## The Test

Randy surveyed approximately 30 CRNA and physician anesthesiologist colleagues during breaks between cases. Simple protocol: "What's your longest duration nasal inhale? Take a deep breath, exhale, now slowly as you possibly can, inhale through your nose."

## Results

- **28 out of 30** used only accessory muscles (scalenes, sternocleidomastoid, upper intercostals). No diaphragmatic recruitment observable.
- **Tapped out at 4-5 seconds.** Nobody reached 8 seconds.
- **Only 2 exceptions:** One singer. One trumpet player. Both had trained their diaphragm through their art — not through medical education.
- **With coaching** (prompting to use the diaphragm), all subjects could achieve a 10-second nasal inhale. The capacity exists. The cortical pathway doesn't.

## The Homunculus Explanation

The somatosensory and motor cortex map body parts proportionally to how precisely and deliberately we USE them. Fingers have enormous cortical territory — precision manipulation. Lips and tongue — speech. The diaphragm has almost no voluntary cortical representation because it runs on brainstem autopilot (phrenic nerve → diaphragm) from birth to death. Nobody has ever deliberately, systematically brought it under voluntary cortical control.

The singer and trumpet player exceptions PROVE this is trainable. They didn't have special anatomy. They built cortical representation through years of deliberate practice requiring precise diaphragmatic control.

## The Hanon Exercises Analogy

Charles-Louis Hanon's piano warm-up exercises don't teach music. They build finger-to-brain mappings through boring, mechanical, deliberate repetitive patterns — until the fingers are neurologically available to the pianist's intention. The diaphragm has never had its Hanon exercises. Sessions 1-7 of the CE course ARE the Hanon exercises for the diaphragm.

## The Irony

These are people who breathe for other people all the time. They manage ventilators with precision. They titrate respiratory parameters to specific endpoints. They intubate, extubate, and manage airways under life-or-death conditions. And they cannot consciously engage their own primary breathing muscle.

## Measurable Baseline for Certification

This test provides a simple, reproducible, equipment-free assessment:
- **Baseline (accessory only):** 4-5 seconds maximum nasal inhale
- **Certified standard:** 8+ seconds with confirmed diaphragmatic engagement
- **The gap between those numbers IS the measure of diaphragmatic blindness**

Simpler than MAIA-2 for quick assessment. Could open every CE session.

## The Breathing Gear System™

Mackenzie's core framework. Originally 5 gears, now refined to 3 (with Sage Hopkins' contribution):

- **Gear 1** — Low respiratory rate (under 8 breaths/min, ~8-second cycle). Nasal breathing. Corresponds to HR zones 0-2. Walking, recovery, parasympathetic dominance. The foundation gear — most training happens here.
- **Gear 2** — Moderate rate. Extends to beginning of HR zone 4. Controlled effort.
- **Gear 3** — High rate, mouth breathing permitted. High-intensity. Gears 4-5 in original system.

Core principle: differential control of respiration rate (RR) and tidal volume (TV). Training primary and secondary respiratory muscles for a "more versatile rib cage." References Central Governor Theory (Tim Noakes) — breath as governor of perceived effort.

## The CO2 Tolerance Test

Simple 5-minute assessment. Protocol: sit/lie comfortably → 3 normal nasal breaths → 1 full nasal inhale → start timer → exhale as slowly as possible through the nose. Duration of controlled exhale = CO2 tolerance score. Roots in free diving breath-hold training. Used as a proxy for nervous system reactivity — lower tolerance = higher stress reactivity.

## Published Research

### Bentley et al. (2022) — The Key Study
"Slow-Breathing Curriculum for Stress Reduction in High School Students: Lessons Learned From a Feasibility Pilot." *Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences.* DOI: 10.3389/fresc.2022.864079. PMID: 36189008.

**Authors:** Tanya G.K. Bentley, Cerena Seeber, Emily Hightower, Brian Mackenzie, Rob Wilson, Aly Velazquez, Anna Cheng, Nicholas N. Arce, Kent A. Lorenz.

**Design:** Cluster-randomized feasibility pilot. Four 12th-grade public high school classes during COVID-19 hybrid teaching.

**Intervention:** 5-min slow diaphragmatic breathing, 3x/week + breath science education 1x/week, for 5 weeks. Two breathing curricula tested: self-paced (SP) vs. guided-pace (GP) slow diaphragmatic breathing vs. treatment-as-usual control.

**Curriculum components:** Three evidence-based elements — (1) slow breathing (pace slower than normal), (2) diaphragmatic breathing (breaths from diaphragm/abdomen), (3) extended exhale (exhalation ≥ 2x inhalation duration).

**Measures:** State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) and CO2 Tolerance Test (CO2TT).

**Results:** No significant changes in STAI or CO2TT from baseline to follow-up (p > 0.05 for all). However, qualitative findings were notable: students reported the practices were calming, increased breath awareness throughout the day, improved focus, improved breathing during exercise, and they liked seeing CO2TT improvements over time.

**Significance for Pausality:** This is exactly the measurement gap that biometric feedback solves. The subjective reports were positive but the objective measures didn't move significantly in 5 weeks. Pausality could have shown session-level changes in real-time — the motivational feedback loop that a 5-week pre/post design misses.

## Surprising Similarities to Pausality

1. **Interoceptive foundation** — Both Hightower's "sensing yourself" and Pausality's interoceptive literacy are teaching the same core skill. Different language, same target: recover the ability to feel your own body signals.

2. **Clinician-to-commercial translation** — Hightower started in a neurology clinic with bio/neurofeedback. Randy started in the OR with anesthesia monitoring. Both are translating clinical somatic expertise into consumer products.

3. **Nasal breathing as foundational** — Gear 1 (nasal, low rate) is the default. Pausality's Anesthetist's Breath (10/0/10/0 nasal) is the foundation protocol. Same physiological reasoning: nitric oxide, vagal activation, parasympathetic dominance.

4. **CO2 tolerance as assessment metric** — SH//FT uses CO2TT as a nervous system reactivity proxy. Pausality uses HRV as a real-time biofeedback signal. Both are measuring the same underlying capacity (autonomic flexibility) through different windows.

5. **Education + practice integration** — Both platforms teach the science alongside the practice. Breath science education 1x/week in Bentley et al. parallels Pausality's curriculum design where each session builds understanding.

6. **Research partnerships with institutions** — HHPF + Stanford/SFSU/CSUF mirrors Pausality's MUSC opportunity. Both need institutional validation.

## Surprising Dissimilarities from Pausality

1. **No real-time biometric feedback** — This is the critical gap. SH//FT teaches breathing protocols but cannot prove they worked for you in this session. Bentley et al. found no significant objective changes partly because the measurement was pre/post over 5 weeks, not session-level. Pausality measures during the session.

2. **Performance-first vs. regulation-first** — Mackenzie comes from CrossFit/athletic performance. Randy comes from clinical anesthesia/nervous system regulation. Same territory, different entry points. Mackenzie asks "how do I perform better?" Randy asks "how do I regulate more precisely?"

3. **Protocol complexity** — The Breathing Gear System requires understanding 3+ gears, tidal volume concepts, and HR zone correlation. Pausality's approach is simpler: one breath protocol, biometric feedback tells you if it's working. Titration to effect vs. gear selection.

4. **Hardware dependency** — SH//FT is app-only, no wearable integration for real-time feedback. Pausality requires Apple Watch but uses it to close the feedback loop. This is a feature, not a bug.

5. **Celebrity-driven vs. evidence-driven distribution** — Mackenzie's network (Ferriss, Froning, Hamilton) is influencer-based. Pausality's planned distribution is CRNA-as-teacher (Insight Timer model) + CE credits. Different trust mechanisms.

6. **CO2TT limitations** — The CO2 Tolerance Test is not validated as a clinical instrument (same issue as McKeown's BOLT score). Pausality's HRV-based measurement has decades of clinical validation behind it.

## Strategic Implications

SH//FT is NOT a direct competitor — they're an adjacent player in a complementary space. They teach the protocols. Pausality proves the protocols work. The integration thesis: a SH//FT user who trains Gear 1 breathing could use Pausality to get real-time biometric confirmation that their practice is effective.

### Partnership Potential
High. Mackenzie's network + Pausality's biometric proof = a powerful combination. If a Rich Froning or Tia Clair Toomey could see their HRV respond in real-time during a SH//FT Gear 1 protocol via Pausality, that's a content moment.

### Research Gap Pausality Fills
Bentley et al. (2022) is the proof point. Their breathing curriculum showed subjective benefits but no significant objective changes in a 5-week pre/post design. Pausality's session-level biometric feedback would have shown changes within the first session — the measurement resolution problem is what Pausality solves.

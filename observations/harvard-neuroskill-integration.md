# NeuroSkill × Harvard Meditation Research: Cessation, Jhana, and Consciousness Monitoring

## Observation

A strategic integration analysis maps the convergence between NeuroSkill (open-source on-device EEG, 70+ brain metrics, Muse 2/OpenBCI) and 11 peer-reviewed papers from the Harvard Meditation Research Program (Sacchet Lab, MGH, 2025-2026).

### Key Harvard Findings

1. **Cessation of consciousness** (Shinozuka 2025): Extended cessation significantly reduces alpha power while INCREASING neural complexity (Lempel-Ziv). Crucially distinct from sleep and anesthesia — where consciousness is absent but complexity DECREASES. This is directly relevant to anesthesia science.

2. **Jhana (ACAM-J) neural trajectories** (Yang 2025): 7T fMRI shows reproducible anterior-to-posterior cortical reorganization across 8 jhana states. Flattening of cortical hierarchies. Associated with reduced suffering-related processes and increased attentional monitoring.

3. **Near-criticality in advanced meditation** (Vohryzek 2025): Later jhana states shift brain dynamics toward near-critical, near-bifurcation working points. DMN exhibits largest shift. This validates the [[criticality-metastable-readiness]] framework.

4. **Heartbeat-Evoked Potentials track meditation depth** (Nath 2025): HEP at channel C3 varies across 5 meditation depth levels. Greater HEP modulation → reduced mood disturbance, increased vigor, enhanced decentering. Does NOT covary with frontal theta — captures distinct interoceptive dimension.

5. **Meditation training science** (Ehmann 2025): First rigorous adaptation of exercise science training variables (volume, frequency, duration, density, intensity) to meditation. Enables quantifiable meditation dosing.

6. **Mental health transformation** (Ehmann 2025d): Six major positive themes in advanced meditators: vast well-being improvement, positive affect, mental clarity, life orientation shift, transformative mental health changes.

### Anesthesia-Consciousness Bridge

The discovery that cessation INCREASES neural complexity while anesthesia DECREASES it suggests:
- New approaches to consciousness monitoring (complementing BIS)
- Cessation vs. anesthesia comparative tool as educational/research instrument
- The CRNA's consciousness engineering expertise bridges directly to meditation neuroscience

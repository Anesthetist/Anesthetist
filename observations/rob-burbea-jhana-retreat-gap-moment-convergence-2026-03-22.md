## SRL Concept Mapping

### Session 21: "In Betweens" = Gap Moment Training

The most significant convergence. Burbea dedicates an entire 65-minute session to "the spaces between formal practice" — the transitional moments that most contemplative traditions ignore. This is exactly what SRL operationalizes as [[gap-moment-training]]: the insight that transitions, not sessions, are the primary training surface.

Where Burbea teaches this as a contemplative orientation ("bring awareness to the in-between moments"), SRL operationalizes it as a structured 60-second intervention with measurable biometric outcomes. Same insight, different expression: one phenomenological, one physiological.

### Session 07: "Developing Pīti" → Interoceptive Literacy

Pīti (Pali: rapture, energetic joy) is the felt sense of autonomic shift during deepening concentration. Burbea teaches practitioners to notice, cultivate, and work with pīti as a body-based experience — tingling, warmth, waves of energy, lightness. This maps directly to [[interoceptive-literacy]]: the trained capacity to detect, interpret, and respond to internal body signals.

The SRL distinction: pīti is what practitioners report phenomenologically. RMSSD shift, respiratory sinus arrhythmia, and vagal tone increase are what the wearable measures physiologically. Same event, two languages.

### Session 14: Fourth Jhāna (Equanimity & Stillness) → Ventral Vagal State

The fourth jhāna is characterized by equanimity (upekkhā) — simultaneous alertness and stillness without reactivity. This is the phenomenological description of the ventral vagal state from [[polyvagal-theory]]: social engagement system active, sympathetic activation modulated, parasympathetic tone high. The state SRL trains toward — not calm, not aroused, but regulated. What a CRNA achieves during a stable case. What a driver needs in the cockpit.

### Session 03: "Breath Instructions" → Resonant Breathing

Burbea's breath instructions emphasize finding a relationship with the breath that produces specific felt-sense qualities — pleasure, ease, energy. The jhana tradition's approach to breath is not clock-paced but response-paced: the practitioner adjusts until the breath "feels right." This parallels [[cardiac-anchored-breathing]] and [[resonant-breathing-frequency]]: breathing synced to the body's own rhythm rather than an external timer.

### Session 19: "Talk on Desires" → Presence Without Fixing

Burbea's teaching on desire maps to the Joe Hudson observation (vaulted same day): the capacity to be with experience without needing it to change. In NeuroMinute Phase 4 language: Presence without fixing. In MAIA-2 language: the Not-Worrying and Trusting subscales. The jhana tradition frames this as a prerequisite for deepening concentration; SRL frames it as the terminal skill in interoceptive literacy.

### Session 22: "Jhānas and Insight" → Return-to-Task Readiness (SRB-60 Domain 5)

Burbea's closing session addresses integration — how jhana practice relates to insight and daily life. The critical question: does deep concentration make you sharper or spaced out when you return to activity? This is precisely SRB-60 Domain 5: Return-to-Task Readiness. Regulation without sedation. The jhana tradition's answer (insight arises from the clarity of concentrated mind) validates SRL's domain design: the intervention should restore executive function, not impair it.

## The Progressive Absorption Arc

The 8 jhānas map to a progressive deepening of interoceptive and attentional skill:

1. **First Jhāna** — Applied and sustained attention (vitakka-vicāra) with rapture (pīti) and pleasure (sukha). Equivalent to NeuroMinute engagement: directed attention to breath with body-based feedback.
2. **Second Jhāna** — Attention stabilizes without effort. Rapture and pleasure without directed attention. Equivalent to flow state.
3. **Third Jhāna** — Rapture fades, contentment (sukha) remains. Equanimity begins. Equivalent to deep parasympathetic regulation without energetic excitement.
4. **Fourth Jhāna** — Pure equanimity. Breath may become very subtle or seem to stop. The ventral vagal target state.
5-8. **Formless Jhānas** — Progressive dissolution of perceptual boundaries. Increasingly subtle states with specific EEG signatures (see [[alpha-theta-crossover]]).

This arc is a contemplative version of [[anterocept]]: progressive deepening from simple breath awareness → multi-layered interoceptive awareness → integrated awareness beyond conventional perceptual boundaries.

## EEG Signatures of Jhana States

Jhana states have documented EEG correlates (relevant to the BrainFlow/Muse assessment just built):

- **First Jhāna**: Increased alpha power, decreased beta. Approach motivation (left frontal asymmetry).
- **Second Jhāna**: Alpha spreads from occipital to frontal. Theta begins to increase.
- **Third-Fourth Jhāna**: Alpha-theta crossover zone. High coherence across channels.
- **Formless Jhānas**: Gamma bursts, high theta, reduced alpha. Distinct from sleep or drowsiness.

The Muse 2 + BrainFlow setup can capture the first-through-fourth jhāna signatures. The alpha/theta ratio tracking in `muse-connect.py` is already measuring the relevant transition points.

## Who Was Rob Burbea

Rob Burbea (1965–2020) was a Buddhist teacher and resident teacher at Gaia House retreat centre in Devon, England. His approach was distinctive for combining rigorous phenomenological inquiry with deep contemplative practice. His book *Seeing That Frees* is considered one of the most important modern contemplative texts. He died of pancreatic cancer in 2020; these teachings were among his last major retreat offerings.

Burbea's approach is unusually compatible with SRL's framework because he insisted on precision — naming specific felt-sense qualities, tracking subtle shifts in experience, understanding the relationship between attention and body-state. He was, in contemplative terms, doing what Randy does clinically: reading the nervous system in real time and adjusting the intervention based on response.

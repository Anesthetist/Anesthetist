## Source

Grossman et al. (2026). Why The Polyvagal Theory Is Untenable. *Clinical Neuropsychiatry*. PMC12937499. Also: Porges (2026) rebuttal in same journal (PMC12937496). Coverage: InSight+ (MJA), Primal Trust blog, Juniper Counselling.

## Summary

In early 2026, 39 international experts co-authored a paper declaring Polyvagal Theory (PVT) "untenable," challenging its evolutionary neurophysiological claims. Lead author Paul Grossman argues PVT inaccurately portrays vagal nerve evolution and vertebrate social behavior. Stephen Porges published a point-by-point rebuttal, clarifying PVT as a systems-level, pathway-specific framework and arguing critics conflate anatomy with physiology and descriptive phylogenetics with evolutionary mechanism. The debate has generated significant media coverage and clinical community discussion.

## Why It Matters for SRL

**CRITICAL for SRL to monitor and respond to.** SRL's conceptual framework draws heavily on polyvagal-informed principles (autonomic regulation, co-regulation, vagal tone, state transitions). The debate does NOT invalidate the clinical tools and practices built on polyvagal-adjacent principles — clinicians note the framework's value for normalizing stress responses, reducing shame, and providing language for body-based experiences remains intact. SRL should position its work as grounded in the *empirically validated mechanisms* (HRV biofeedback efficacy, vagal nerve stimulation effects, respiratory sinus arrhythmia) rather than the contested evolutionary narrative. This is an opportunity for epistemic rigor and differentiation.

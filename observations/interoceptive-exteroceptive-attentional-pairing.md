A protocol design principle in which interoceptive attention cues (internal bodily signals — heartbeat, breath, proprioception) are deliberately paired with exteroceptive cues (external sensory anchors — a visual, sound, or tactile signal synchronized to the internal rhythm) to deepen and stabilize embodied awareness.

**Randy's words:** "Include instructions to pair entero and exteroceptive attentional cues."

## Mechanism

Pairing works by creating a cross-modal reinforcement loop:
- The internal signal (e.g., heartbeat) becomes "visible" through an external representation (e.g., a pulsing visual synchronized to cardiac rhythm)
- The external cue draws attention to the internal state without requiring introspective effort
- Over time, the paired cues become associated — the external cue begins to reliably activate interoceptive attention even when the display is not present
- This builds what might be called "anchored interoception" — body awareness that is triggered and supported by environmental signals

## Implementation in SRL System

In the Cognitive-Emotional State Interface (provisional patent context):
- **Visual pairing:** Biofeedback display shows a waveform or "resilience dial" synchronized to the user's real-time HRV
- **Auditory pairing:** Binaural beats or tones locked to cardiac or respiratory rhythm
- **Haptic pairing:** Apple Watch taps paced to breathing rhythm during intervention sessions
- **Cognitive pairing:** A brief phrase or icon ("Breathe now") paired with the physiological state it targets

## Clinical Significance

For CRNA populations who operate in high-exteroceptive-demand environments (alarms, monitors, patient cues), the ability to anchor interoceptive awareness to environmental signals is particularly valuable. It allows regulation without withdrawal from the external environment.

# Interoceptive Blindness: The Heidegger Gap in Clinical Practice

## Observation

Medical training creates a specific form of interoceptive blindness: clinicians are trained to read charts, metrics, and text-based data, which distances them from the immediacy of their own embodied experience. This is the "Heidegger Gap" — technology and abstraction mediate the clinician's relationship to their own body.

### How Clinicians Lose Touch

- **Analytical dominance:** Over-reliance on numeric/textual abstraction (charts vs. felt sense) → left-hemisphere dominance per [[hemispheric-rebalancing]]
- **Diaphragmatic blindness:** Specific inability to sense or control own breathing during high-stress cases — identified in Week 1 of the somnistics curriculum as the most common starting deficit
- **Mediation paradox:** "Am I feeling stressed, or does the device say I'm stressed?" Technology can create a new layer of mediation, potentially obscuring direct interoception even while trying to train it
- **Outward orientation:** All clinical training focuses on monitoring patients, never on monitoring oneself → the [[better-at-regulating-patients]] insight

### VagalBeats as Re-Embodiment Technology

Real-time biometric feedback retrains the anterior insula (heartbeat tracking) and posterior insula (proprioceptive depth), rebuilding the clinician's sensory-first decision-making mode. The technology addresses Heidegger's critique by *using technology to re-embody* rather than further abstract.

## Significance

This observation provides the philosophical depth behind the [[anterocept]] training methodology. The progression from Domain 1 (baseline awareness) through Domain 3 (integrated awareness) is specifically designed to reverse interoceptive blindness — first by noticing the blindness exists, then by systematically rebuilding the connection.

# Criticality-Interoception-Readiness: The Brain Operates Near Tipping Points

## Observation

Low-dimensional critical dynamics + arousal coherence = "metastable readiness." The cortex operates near multi-scale tipping points — not purely random (chaos) or purely synchronized (rigidity), but balanced at the edge between them. This is the neuroscience formalization of Siegel's "River of Integration."

### Key Concepts

- **Criticality:** The brain naturally maintains itself near phase transitions where small inputs can produce large state changes
- **Metastable readiness:** The ideal state is not calm or alert but *poised* — ready to shift in any direction as needed
- **Arousal coherence:** When physiological signals (HRV, respiratory rate, pupil diameter) align, the system has higher capacity for adaptive response
- **State access:** Somnistics trains "state access" — the ability to reach and maintain metastable readiness on demand

### Marketing Reframe

"State access" is the consumer positioning: not wellness, not mindfulness, but **performance preparation**. The clinician doesn't need to be calm — they need to be *ready*. Readiness = metastable poise at the critical boundary.

## Significance

This is the deepest theoretical grounding for the [[readyscore]] concept: the ReadyScore measures proximity to the metastable critical boundary. Too low (dorsal vagal shutdown) or too high (sympathetic overwhelm) both represent departure from criticality. The optimal state is the edge.

# The Anesthetist's Breath: 10/0/10/0 Protocol Discovery

## Observation

Randy developed the "Anesthetist's Breath" — a 10-second nasal inhale, 10-second nasal exhale, no holds (10/0/10/0) — as the core deep vagal activation protocol for somnistics. Three breaths = approximately 60 seconds = one [[neurominute]].

> "Pay close attention to the end of the third breath, it will be very spicy at first. Be compassionate to yourself here. If this is too much, go down to 8 or 9. Your body is telling you exactly where it is now, be gentle."

The "spicy" sensation at the end of the third breath is a key interoceptive signal — it marks the edge of the user's current CO₂ tolerance and diaphragmatic capacity. This is a [[titration-to-effect]] point: the body telling you exactly where your current limit is.

## Protocol Design Logic

- **10-second inhalation:** Activates the diaphragm fully, maximizing gas exchange. Triggers baroreceptor stretch on inhalation (sympathetic input).
- **10-second exhalation:** Extended exhale drives parasympathetic activation via vagal efference. Produces reflex bradycardia.
- **No holds:** Eliminates the anxiety-producing breath retention that makes box breathing difficult for stressed clinicians.
- **3 breaths in 60 seconds:** Exactly 3 breaths/minute — well below typical resonant frequency, producing deep vagal activation.
- **Nasal only:** Activates nasal nitric oxide production, additional parasympathetic activation via nasal mucosa stimulation.

## Significance

The Anesthetist's Breath is the simplest and deepest protocol in the [[neurominute]] library. Its design reflects Randy's clinical pharmacology training: maximum autonomic effect from minimum intervention, with built-in safety (the "spicy" signal is the body's own dose-limiting feedback).

# Headspace Ebb Product Webinar — Live Competitive Intelligence

**Date:** 2026-04-01
**Presenter:** Jenna Glover (she/her), Headspace Chief Clinical Officer
**Moderator:** Steve Duke
**Source:** Live webinar chat transcript + presentation screenshots
**Deck marked:** "Proprietary, Confidential, & Thoughtful"

## Ebb Onboarding Flow (Screenshots)

### Safety Architecture
- All members must **opt-in** to start using Ebb (not default-on)
- First-time users see disclosure: what Ebb can help with, AI design, limitations
- Explicit statement: **"Ebb is not a replacement for medical advice/human care"**
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and 911 Emergency Line prominently displayed
- "Do not attempt to access emergency care through this app"
- AI transparency: "This understanding isn't always perfect, so we also use your feedback"
- Conversations encrypted; assessed and audited by clinical psychologists and engineers
- Trust badges: "Thoughtfully tested" and "Encrypted conversation"

### Three Value Propositions
1. **Explore your emotions** — process thoughts and emotions, find gratitude
2. **Process life's ups and downs** — guided prompts and replies
3. **Get personalized recommendations** — exercises and activities based on what you share

### Design Philosophy
"Designed by real people and brought to you by AI" — positions human clinical expertise as upstream of the AI. "Built by clinical experts, with your safety and privacy in mind."

## Three Things We've Learned Building Ebb (Key Slide)

1. **"Case conceptualization is key to shaping AI that moves people away from experiential avoidance."**
   - Using clinical case formulation to design AI behavior, not just prompt engineering
   - More sophisticated than typical wellness chatbots
   - Still conceptual guidance — AI reasons about user needs, doesn't measure them

2. **"Defining what a tool's experience should be is both a product AND clinical decision."**
   - Acknowledges dual governance: product wants engagement, clinical wants safety
   - Confirms the board-vs-clinical tension raised in audience Q&A

3. **"Boundaries improve the member experience by helping people return to real life."**
   - Their answer to the dependency question
   - Deliberately limiting Ebb's scope to push people back to real world
   - Enforced through conversation design, not skill transfer

## Key Signals from Chat Q&A

### 1. Clinical North Star: Reducing Experiential Avoidance
ACT-derived framing. Ebb's goal is helping users sit with discomfort rather than avoid it. Purely conversational — no physiological confirmation that avoidance actually decreased. Audience member called this a "mic drop."

### 2. Success Metric Still Undefined
Lisa Hao asked directly: "What does success look like for an Ebb interaction?" — the question itself signals the metric is still being defined. Current proxy is engagement (34% higher app usage), not clinical outcomes.

### 3. Dependency vs. Engagement Tension Unresolved
Multiple audience members (Ben Anandappa, Lisa Hao) asked about the line between meaningful engagement and AI dependency. Fundamental vulnerability of conversational companion model — relationships create dependency; skills transfer.

### 4. No Real Therapeutic Techniques Yet
Misha Tsvik asked about gradual exposure, coping cards, rescripting as future plans — implying Ebb currently does not perform these. Core capability is motivational interviewing in chat format.

### 5. Board-vs-Clinical Tension Acknowledged
Lisa Henderson asked about keeping investors aligned with clinical model vs. financial/technical possibilities. Strong reaction from clinician audience. Post-Ginger merger tension between clinical integrity and growth metrics persists.

### 6. State-Level AI Regulation Arriving
Ebunoluwa Ojemuyiwa raised Illinois AI mental health regulations as a factor affecting approach. Regulatory headwind for conversational AI tools that resemble therapy but aren't licensed as such.

### 7. OCD/Clinical Safety Guardrails Still Building
Trisha Blecich (LPC/LMHC/LCMHC) flagged that "comfort and reassurance can cause actual harm in OCD spectrum treatment." Tests whether Ebb's empathy model has condition-specific guardrails. Also asked about external standards/AI governance bodies for defining clinical and product guardrails.

### 8. "Micro Interventions" Language Emerging
Dickson Nuesse (personal Ebb user) described value as "micro interventions around emotional regulation." This is Gap Moment Training™ territory — validates the concept but without biometric proof.

### 9. User Profile Still Open Question
Dr. Sharon Batista (MD) asked who actually wants to use an AI MI coach. Target user profile is still being defined.

### 10. Cultural Transferability Questioned
Josephine M asked about collectivist cultures and different paces of trust-building. MI model may not translate across cultures.

### 11. Quality Assurance at Scale
Oguz Dumanoglu and Terre both asked how quality is maintained — manual review vs. AI scoring of conversations. Scaling quality review for 7M+ messages is a non-trivial challenge.

### 12. LLM Evaluation Challenge
Samir Akre-Bhide referenced Sword Mind group papers — LLMs struggle to simulate confrontational users for testing mental health interactions. Ebb's evaluation pipeline may have blind spots with resistant or complex users.

### 13. Clinical Expansion Scope
Keerti Prajapati asked about depression/SMI. Trisha Blecich asked about clinical niche expansion. Current scope appears limited to sub-clinical emotional support.

## Pausality Differentiation Matrix

| Ebb Approach | Pausality Approach |
|---|---|
| Success = engagement metrics | Success = RMSSD/HRV change in 60 seconds |
| Conversational self-report for triage | Biometric closed-loop bypasses self-report bias |
| Companion → dependency risk | Skill training → autonomy and transfer |
| No therapeutic techniques yet | Protocol-driven autonomic training |
| Board-clinical tension (dual governance) | Clinical model IS the business model |
| Regulatory exposure (AI resembling therapy) | Device/wellness positioning, not therapy |
| Boundaries via conversation design | Boundaries built into protocol structure |
| "Helping people return to real life" via chat limits | Session ends because training is complete + measured |
| No condition-specific guardrails yet | Physiology-first approach, population-agnostic |
| Western MI model, cultural limits | Breath/autonomic training is culturally universal |
| Quality review challenge at 7M+ messages | Objective biometric data, no conversation to review |

# Post-Induction/Pre-Incision Gap Moment

The post-induction/pre-incision interval is a naturally occurring gap moment that only CRNAs experience. After the patient is induced, lines are secured, and before the surgeon begins — a window of approximately 30-60 seconds opens. This is the most powerful natural training window in clinical anesthesia.

## Why This Matters

This interval is not dead time. It is a liminal space where the CRNA has just completed a high-stakes sequence (induction, airway management, line placement) and is about to enter a sustained vigilance phase (surgical maintenance). The autonomic system is in transition — sympathetic activation from the induction sequence is winding down, but the clinician must remain alert.

## Craft Knowledge

- Only a CRNA with decades of experience would recognize this specific temporal window as a trainable moment
- The interval maps precisely to what Gurdjieff describes as the "interval" where conscious effort can change the trajectory
- Gap Moments are not add-ons to clinical practice — they map to moments that already exist in the clinical workflow. The training is about making existing liminal moments conscious, not creating new ones
- This is the literal clinical analogue of the "time between worlds" concept: the patient has crossed from consciousness to unconsciousness, and the CRNA stands at the threshold

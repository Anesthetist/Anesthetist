# Gut-Brain Axis: Four Overlapping Pathways Through Vagus

## Observation

A detailed wiring diagram of the gut-brain axis reveals four overlapping communication pathways, all converging on or mediated through the vagus nerve:

### 1. Endocrine Pathway
- **HPA axis → gut**: Hypothalamus → CRH → pituitary → ACTH → adrenal cortex → cortisol
- Cortisol alters gut barrier permeability (leakier under chronic stress) and shifts gut microbiota composition
- **Gut → brain**: Enteroendocrine cells release GLP-1, CCK, PYY, NPY, ghrelin in response to nutrients and microbial metabolites
- These hormones signal satiety, hunger, energy balance via vagal afferents and circulation

### 2. Neural Pathway
- Vagus nerve provides bidirectional superhighway between ENS (enteric nervous system) and CNS
- Gut microbes produce neurotransmitters (serotonin, GABA, dopamine) that signal through vagal afferents
- 95% of serotonin is produced in the gut

### 3. Metabolic Pathway
- Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs: butyrate, propionate, acetate) produced by gut microbes
- SCFAs modulate blood-brain barrier integrity, microglial function, neuroinflammation
- Tryptophan metabolism → kynurenine pathway → affects both serotonin and neuroinflammation

### 4. Immune Pathway
- Gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) is largest immune organ
- Vagal anti-inflammatory reflex: vagus nerve senses peripheral inflammation → activates cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway
- Chronic gut dysbiosis → systemic inflammation → neuroinflammation → cognitive/emotional effects

## Significance for Somnistics

Breath-based vagal toning doesn't just affect heart rate — it modulates the entire gut-brain communication system. This expands the therapeutic scope beyond acute stress regulation to long-term gut health, immune function, and neuroinflammation. The Kosha Architecture framework already accounts for these deeper visceral layers.

# CRNA Workforce Crisis 2026: Key Statistics

## Source Data

Compiled from the **Medicus Healthcare Solutions 2025 White Paper** (*The Anesthesia Provider Shortage*, May 2025), **NCSBN 2024 National Nursing Workforce Survey** (published 2025, n=744,714), and **Becker's ASC** (2025–2026 coverage).

## Current Statistics (2025–2026)

- **12,500 CRNA shortage projected by 2033** — nearly 22% of the current ~67,700 workforce (Medicus 2025)
- **40% of current CRNAs expected to retire within 10 years** (Medicus 2025)
- **31% of CRNAs are age 55+** (Medicus 2025)
- **56% of CRNAs report feeling burned out** (Becker's ASC / industry surveys 2025)
- **78% of rural facilities report CRNA staffing gaps**, up from 35% in 2020 (Medicus 2025)
- **~40% of all RNs intend to leave the workforce or retire by 2029** — 21.9% retirement, 18% leaving for other reasons (NCSBN 2024)
- **12% of CRNAs planned to retire by 2027** — older figure from AANA Workforce Survey (Negrusa et al., *Nursing Outlook* 2021); superseded by Medicus 2025 data

## Historical Reference

The Negrusa et al. 2021 paper (*Nursing Outlook* 69(3):387-400, PMID 33579515), based on the 2019 AANA Workforce Survey, found 49.7% of CRNAs were age 50+ and included retirement timeline data. The "12% by 2027" stat circulated widely across healthcare staffing sources attributing it to AANA. The 2025 Medicus data supersedes this as the stronger sourced figure.

## SRL Relevance

These statistics constitute the market urgency case for Pausality and SRL's clinical durability work. A 22% workforce shortage arriving in 7 years, combined with 56% burnout prevalence, establishes both the problem magnitude and the prevention window. The autonomic regulation and gap moment training protocols address clinician durability — the ability to sustain high performance across a full career — which directly counters the attrition and burnout trajectory.

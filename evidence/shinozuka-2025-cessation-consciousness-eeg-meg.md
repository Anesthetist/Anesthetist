# Neuroelectrophysiological Correlates of Extended Cessation of Consciousness (Shinozuka et al. 2025)

## Summary

This study uses EEG and MEG to characterize the neural correlates of intentional consciousness cessation in advanced meditators — a state (related to nirodha samapatti in Buddhist frameworks) where subjective experience temporarily ceases while physiological and autonomic function continues. This represents the extreme end of the consciousness-modulation spectrum. The electrophysiological signatures reveal what happens when self-referential processing, sensory awareness, and cognitive content are systematically deconstructed. While this deepest state is far beyond typical clinical intervention, it maps the full range of the state-transition continuum that SRL works within. Understanding the endpoint helps calibrate the entire spectrum from default consciousness through breath-mediated regulation to advanced states.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Intentional cessation of consciousness is an achievable, reproducible meditative attainment
- EEG/MEG signatures characterize this state as distinct from sleep, anesthesia, or unconsciousness
- The state represents the extreme end of the consciousness-modulation spectrum
- Physiological and autonomic function continues during subjective cessation
- Understanding the full spectrum from default to cessation helps calibrate intermediate interventions
- Self-referential processing can be volitionally deconstructed — supporting the self-remembering framework
- State transitions to and from cessation may reveal the neural architecture of consciousness itself

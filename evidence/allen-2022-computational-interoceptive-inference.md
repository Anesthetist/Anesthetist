## Overview
Allen et al. present the first formal computational model of cardiac interoceptive inference, simulating how cardiac signals entrain perception through active inference. The model reproduces empirically observed phenomena including the defensive startle reflex and cardiac cycle effects on affective processing. It demonstrates how disruptions to interoceptive precision can produce psychosomatic symptoms, providing computational proof-of-concept for interoceptive training interventions.

## Key Findings
1. First formal computational model of cardiac interoceptive inference, built on the active inference framework
2. Simulates how cardiac signals entrain perception — the heartbeat cycle modulates what we perceive and how we feel
3. Reproduces the defensive startle reflex and cardiac cycle effects on affect, matching empirical data
4. Shows how "interoceptive lesions" (reduced precision) cause psychosomatic hallucinations — the body misperceiving itself
5. Demonstrates that interoceptive precision modulates perceptual confidence — higher precision leads to more accurate and confident body perception

## Connection to Somnistics
This is computational proof-of-concept that interoceptive training should work. If you change the precision of interoceptive predictions, you change emotional experience. SRL's HRV biofeedback and breathwork protocols are precision-tuning interventions — they increase the signal-to-noise ratio of cardiac and respiratory interoception. This model validates the [[neuroharmonics]] approach and supports the mechanism behind [[interoceptive-literacy]].

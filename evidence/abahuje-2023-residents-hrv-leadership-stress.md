## Summary

Study using continuous WHOOP wearable monitoring to examine the relationship between surgical residents' physiological stress (HRV), sleep, rotation type, and faculty leadership skills.

## Key Findings

- **Sample:** 16 surgical residents, WHOOP continuous monitoring for 1-2 months
- **Sleep-HRV relationship:** Each additional hour of sleep = 2.9% HRV increase (p<0.001) — sleep is the strongest modifiable predictor of autonomic resilience
- **Rotation effect:** ICU rotation vs. Trauma = 10.9% HRV decrease — rotation type significantly impacts physiological stress
- **Leadership null finding:** Faculty leadership skills were NOT significantly associated with resident HRV — the stressor is structural, not interpersonal
- **Implication:** Sleep is the #1 modifiable factor for clinician autonomic health

## SRL Relevance

Directly validates the Somnistics thesis — sleep is the primary modifiable determinant of clinician autonomic resilience. The 2.9% HRV per hour of sleep is a quantified dose-response. WHOOP as the monitoring platform validates consumer wearable data for clinical research. The leadership null finding redirects attention from "better bosses" to "better physiology."

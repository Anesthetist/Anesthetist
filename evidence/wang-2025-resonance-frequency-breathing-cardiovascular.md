## Overview

Wang et al. (2025) investigated the specific cardiovascular and brain-cardiopulmonary effects of breathing at an individual's resonance frequency (RF) compared to other slow breathing rates. Using a corrected sliding method for individualized RF determination, the study demonstrated that the unique physiological effects of resonant breathing — including blood pressure reduction and enhanced baroreflex sensitivity — are frequency-specific and do not occur at non-resonant slow rates.

## Key Findings

1. **Used a corrected sliding method for individual RF determination**, improving precision over fixed-rate or standard assessment protocols.
2. **Blood pressure reduction was unique to resonance frequency** and did not occur at other slow breathing rates, establishing RF specificity for cardiovascular benefit.
3. **Alpha-wave enhancement and parasympathetic BRS pathway** were activated specifically at RF, linking cortical and autonomic mechanisms.
4. **Cardiorespiratory coupling was strongest at individual RF**, demonstrating maximal brain-heart-lung synchronization at the personalized resonant rate.
5. **Individual RF must be determined — fixed breathing rates are suboptimal**, with generic slow breathing (e.g., 6 breaths/min for all) missing the precision needed for maximal effect.

# Mindfulness, Cognition, and Long-Term Meditators (Ehmann, Sezer, Treves, Gabrieli & Sacchet 2025)

## Summary

This comprehensive review examines the cognitive and behavioral profile of long-term meditators (LTMs), integrating findings across multiple domains: attention, interoception, pain processing, emotional regulation, and self-awareness. LTMs demonstrate enhanced interoceptive awareness, reduced pain reactivity (not reduced sensation but altered relationship to pain), greater emotional equanimity, and fundamentally altered self-referential processing. The paper proposes a "neurophenomenological gestalt" — a unified framework for understanding how these changes cohere as an integrated transformation rather than isolated improvements. For SRL, this paper maps the terrain of what sustained practice produces, validating the NeuroMinute trajectory from basic body awareness toward the deeper transformations that Pausality targets.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Long-term meditators show enhanced interoceptive awareness as a foundational change
- Pain processing shifts from reactivity to equanimous observation — changed relationship, not numbing
- Emotional regulation in LTMs reflects genuine equanimity, not suppression
- Self-referential processing is fundamentally altered — decreased narrative self, increased present-centered awareness
- The "neurophenomenological gestalt" suggests these changes are interconnected, not modular
- Cognitive improvements include sustained attention and reduced mind-wandering
- These outcomes emerge progressively with practice hours, supporting titrated dosing models

## Summary

Within-subject study (N=59) investigating the dose-response relationship of slow-paced breathing (SPB) at 6 cycles/min across four durations: 5, 10, 15, and 20 minutes, plus a no-SPB control condition.

## Key Findings

- **RMSSD increased in ALL SPB conditions** compared to control — confirming vagal activation occurs at every tested duration
- **No significant difference in RMSSD between 5, 10, 15, and 20 minutes** — the dose-response curve is flat; the shortest dose is already at ceiling effect for cardiac vagal activation
- The autonomic shift happens fast — 5 minutes produced the same vagal activation as 20 minutes
- Session duration did influence post-SPB spontaneous respiratory frequency (longer sessions → slower spontaneous breathing afterward), suggesting a secondary habituation effect

## Relevance to SRL

This is the strongest evidence supporting the minimum effective dose principle for breathwork. If 5 minutes = 20 minutes for vagal activation, the dose-response curve suggests the mechanism engages within the first few breath cycles. While no study has yet tested exactly 5 breaths (~60 seconds) as an intervention, this study's flat dose-response curve makes the Five-Breath Re-Embodiment protocol physiologically plausible.

**Research opportunity:** SRL's Pausality TestFlight data (build 508) could provide the first evidence for sub-5-minute vagal activation, filling the gap between this study's 5-minute floor and the NeuroMinute's 60-second target.

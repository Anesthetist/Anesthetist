## Citation

(2025). Neuroscientists find evidence meditation changes how fluid moves in the brain [PNAS study]. Covered by: Vanderbilt University Medical Center News, December 10, 2025. Published in *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*.

## Key Findings

- Focused-attention meditation measurably alters the flow of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) in the brain, stimulating glymphatic circulation in a manner analogous to sleep.
- When expert meditators shifted from mind-wandering to focused attention, backward/regurgitant fluid flow decreased and overall fluid movement became more **efficient and coherent**.
- Meditation may serve as a noninvasive way to stimulate clearance of harmful proteins (e.g., amyloid-beta) that accumulate with age and are associated with neurodegeneration.
- Suggests a mechanism by which meditation may confer sleep-like restorative neurological benefits — even during waking states.

## Relevance to SRL

Profoundly relevant to SRL's SomnoAffinity and State Transition concepts — demonstrating that meditative states can replicate key neurobiological functions of sleep, particularly glymphatic waste clearance. This supports the SRL hypothesis that trained state transitions (via Gap Moment Training) could functionally substitute for or amplify restorative sleep physiology. Also connects to the Anterocept concept regarding forward-oriented awareness that shifts brain states constructively.

## Methodology

- Neuroimaging study applying novel CSF flow measurement methods (likely phase-contrast MRI).
- Population: expert meditators.
- Within-subject comparison: mind-wandering vs. focused-attention meditation states.
- Published in PNAS, late 2025.

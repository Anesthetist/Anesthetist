## Citation

(2026). Development of interoception: The critical role of maternal affective touch in early attachment. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1748486

## Key Findings

- Proposes that early attachment relationships, facilitated by maternal affective touch (MAT), provide the critical context for interoceptive signal integration within central nervous system structures
- Interoceptive signals are learned to be accurately sensed, perceived, and interpreted through relational experiences in infancy
- Relational experiences and embodied interactions lay the foundation for lifelong interoceptive awareness and adaptive functioning
- Addresses the gap in understanding developmental origins of interoceptive functioning

## Relevance to SRL

Provides developmental origins for SRL's core construct of Interoceptive Literacy. Understanding that interoception is fundamentally shaped by early co-regulation experiences deepens the theoretical foundation for SRL's Co-Regulation and Autonomic Co-Regulation concepts. Suggests that adults who missed early interoceptive attunement (through disrupted attachment) may need structured interoceptive training — exactly what SRL provides. Also relevant to Trauma-Informed Design.

## Methodology

Theoretical/review paper; integrates attachment theory, developmental neuroscience, and interoception research; proposes mechanistic model of interoceptive development through maternal affective touch.

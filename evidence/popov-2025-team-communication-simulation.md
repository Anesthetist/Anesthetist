## Summary

Network analysis study examining how anesthesiology team communication patterns differentiate high-performing from low-performing teams in clinical simulation.

## Key Findings

- **Sample:** n=22 anesthesiologists in simulation scenarios
- **High performers:** Effectively transitioned from assessment → planning → implementation in an ordered sequence
- **Low performers:** Cycled between assessment phases without progressing to action (p=0.04, d=1.72 — a very large effect)
- **"Assessment cycling":** Identified as the cognitive signature of stress-impaired decision-making — teams get stuck in loops of re-assessing without deciding
- **Implication:** The ability to transition between cognitive phases (assess → decide → act) is the key differentiator, not the quality of any single phase

## SRL Relevance

"Assessment cycling" is a powerful label for the state transition failure that Gap Moment Training addresses. The d=1.72 effect size means this is THE distinguishing feature between high and low performers. This maps directly to Neural Transition Failure and State Transition concepts. The cognitive signature of stress-impaired decision-making is exactly what GMT is designed to interrupt.

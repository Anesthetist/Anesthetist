## Citation

(2026). Development of interoception: The critical role of maternal affective touch in early attachment. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1748486

## Key Findings

- Early attachment relationships, facilitated by maternal affective touch (MAT), provide a critical context for integrating interoceptive signals
- MAT is proposed as a key mechanism linking early relational experience to interoceptive pathway development
- Neurobiological mechanisms connecting MAT to interoceptive pathways are examined
- Relational experiences in infancy lay the foundation for lifelong interoceptive awareness
- Interoception is pivotal for homeostatic and emotional regulation, but developmental origins remain underexplored

## Relevance to SRL

Foundational support for SRL's **Interoception**, **Interoceptive Literacy**, and **Co-Regulation** concepts. Demonstrates that interoceptive capacity is not solely an innate trait but is shaped through relational experience — supporting SRL's premise that interoceptive skills can be trained and developed. The maternal affective touch pathway echoes the co-regulation mechanisms SRL leverages in clinical training contexts.

## Methodology

Review/theoretical paper examining neurobiological mechanisms. Published in *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, January 2026. Integrates developmental psychology, neuroscience, and attachment theory.

## Overview

A 2-page leadership skill document providing a framework and screening protocol for distinguishing "Owner" consciousness (proactive, self-responsible, growth-oriented) from "Victim" consciousness (reactive, blame-oriented, avoidant) in hiring and organizational development contexts.

## Key Framework

### Owner vs. Victim Consciousness
Owners are proactive, take personal responsibility for emotions and actions, and have strong capacity for commitment, learning, and growth. Victims are reactive, avoid personal responsibility, and have limited capacity for commitment, learning, and growth. Victim consciousness is described as viral — it infects teams and organizations, increasing drama and decreasing productivity.

### Three-Step Assessment Protocol

**Step 1: Surface** — Create a conversational trigger that elicits the candidate's natural response pattern. Ask about frustrations, conflicts, challenges at work.

**Step 2: Accept** — Provide validation and ask open-ended questions without leading. "I hear you." "Can you tell me more?" "What else?" This creates psychological safety so the candidate reveals their authentic pattern.

**Step 3: Improve** — Watch for specific language and thought patterns using the Victim/Owner comparison table.

### Victim vs. Owner Language and Thought Pattern Table

| Domain | Victim | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Problems | "Who should I blame?" | "What can I do?" |
| | "Why does this always happen?" | "How can I use this?" |
| | "Why is life so unfair?" | "What can I learn from this?" |
| | "Get through" | "Get from" |
| | Good / Bad / Right / Wrong | Good / Better / Best |
| Negative Emotions | "I'm upset because..." | "I'm feeling..." |
| | Events cause my feelings | My thoughts cause my feelings |
| Paradigm | "Life sucks / My life sucks" | "I'm great / We're great" |
| | "Why am I the way I am?" | "How can I grow?" |
| Motivation | "I should / I need / I have to" | "I choose" |
| | Discipline is punishment | Discipline is freedom |
| Commitment | "I will try" | "You can count on me" |

### Key Behavioral Tell
If the candidate avoids the question or downplays the emotional content, that is not a sign of ownership — it is a sign of dissembling or hiding their truth.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Owner/Victim as Autonomic Posture

The Owner/Victim distinction maps to autonomic states: Victim consciousness operates from threat physiology (sympathetic/dorsal) where external locus of control is an adaptive survival strategy. Owner consciousness operates from safety physiology (ventral vagal) where internal locus of control is possible because the system has sufficient resources to engage proactively. This reframes "victim" not as a character flaw but as a nervous system state — which means it is trainable.

### Language Patterns as Interoceptive Markers

The Victim/Owner language table is a non-instrumented diagnostic tool. The language someone uses reveals their autonomic state: "I'm upset because [external event]" indicates an exteriorized locus of causation (sympathetic threat response), while "I'm feeling [internal state]" indicates interoceptive access (ventral vagal with self-awareness). Language IS the biomarker in social contexts.

### Certification and Coaching Intake Application

This framework has direct application to SRL's Somnistics Certification screening. Candidates for SCP certification who demonstrate predominantly Owner-pattern language have the autonomic bandwidth to benefit from structured interoception training. Those in Victim-pattern states may need preliminary autonomic regulation work before the full training protocol becomes accessible. The assessment reveals the starting point.

### Agency as Wanton-Hamlet Polarity

The Owner/Victim distinction maps to the SRL wanton-hamlet agency polarity: Victims are Wantons (driven by impulse, external circumstance), while Owners demonstrate Hamlet-level agency (reflective, choice-driven, but potentially over-analytical). The integrated position — embodied agency — transcends both poles.

# Intestinal Interoceptive Dysfunction Drives Age-Associated Cognitive Decline (Cox et al. 2026)

## Summary

Published in *Nature* (March 2026), this study reveals that age-related changes in the gut microbiome impair vagal afferent neuron function, weakening interoceptive signals from gut to brain. This disruption leads to hippocampal impairment and measurable cognitive decline. The authors introduce the concept of "interoceptomimetics" — interventions designed to stimulate gut-brain communication pathways to counteract the cognitive effects of aging.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Gut-brain interoceptive signaling via vagal afferents degrades with age due to microbiome changes
- Hippocampal function depends on intact interoceptive input from the gut
- "Interoceptomimetics" represent a new intervention category: restoring or mimicking degraded interoceptive signals
- Vagal nerve integrity is essential for cognitive maintenance across the lifespan
- Supports the broader thesis that interoception is not merely body-awareness but a foundational cognitive mechanism

## SRL Relevance

High strategic relevance. The "interoceptomimetics" concept validates SRL's core thesis that training interoceptive capacity has downstream cognitive benefits. If aging degrades interoceptive signaling and this causes cognitive decline, then interventions that maintain or enhance interoceptive processing (e.g., resonant breathing, Gap Moment Training) may serve a neuroprotective function.

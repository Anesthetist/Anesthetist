# Recognizing the Awe in Anesthesia

## Summary

First peer-reviewed paper in the anesthesia literature to address awe as a mechanism for clinician resilience and burnout prevention. Published in *Anesthesia & Analgesia* (Vol. 140, Issue 2, February 2025), one of the field's top journals.

## Authors & Institutions

- **Dan E. Berkowitz** — Department of Anesthesiology and Perioperative Medicine, Heersink School of Medicine, University of Alabama Birmingham
- **D. Matthew Sherrer** — Same institution
- **Thomas R. Vetter** — Dell Medical School, University of Texas at Austin

## Core Thesis

Awe-based interventions can foster resilience, compassion, and purpose among anesthesia professionals to combat burnout. The paper draws from Keltner's foundational awe research and applies it specifically to anesthesia practice.

## Three Sources of Awe in Anesthesia

1. **Moral Beauty** — Witnessing courage, kindness, and sacrifice in clinical settings
2. **Life and Death** — The profound encounters with mortality inherent to anesthesia practice
3. **Epiphany** — Moments of deep understanding or clinical insight

## SRL Relevance — CRITICAL

This paper validates that the field is becoming receptive to non-cognitive, embodied dimensions of clinician wellbeing. However, it differs from SRL's thesis in mechanism:

- **Berkowitz et al.:** Awe as emotional-spiritual intervention → resilience
- **SRL/Pausality:** Interoceptive suppression as mechanistic root → somatic micro-interventions as primary prevention

The paper does NOT address:
- Interoception or interoceptive suppression
- Autonomic self-regulation
- Somatic micro-interventions
- The body-based mechanism of why burnout develops
- Olfaction or sensory channels

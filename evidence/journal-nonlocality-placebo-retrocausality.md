# Journal of Nonlocality: Placebo, Experimenter Expectation & Retrocausality

## Citation

Journal of Nonlocality (2002–2015). Published by the Mind Matter Mapping Project via ICRL (International Consciousness Research Laboratories). Key article: "The tip of the iceberg: placebo, experimenter expectation and interference phenomena in subconscious information flow."

Also: Leibovici, L. (2001). "Toward Understanding the Placebo Effect: Investigating a Possible Retrocausal Factor." Referenced in Explore: The Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine.

## Summary

The Journal of Nonlocality was the peer-reviewed publication of the Mind Matter Mapping Project, operating from 2002–2015. It focused exclusively on experimental design and empirical results at the intersection of quantum mechanics, consciousness research, and biophysics. A key thread of the journal examined whether the placebo effect involves a retrocausal/nonlocal component — i.e., whether the body's response to healing intention involves genuine access to future physiological states rather than purely cognitive expectation.

## Key Themes

- **Subconscious information flow**: Presentiment-style effects may operate continuously below conscious awareness, with placebo responses being one visible manifestation
- **Experimenter expectation effects**: The researcher's own intention and expectation may influence experimental outcomes through nonlocal channels — a "first-person" variable that most research designs ignore
- **Interference phenomena**: Multiple nonlocal signals (patient intention, practitioner intention, experimenter expectation) may interact constructively or destructively, explaining the variability in placebo and healing studies
- **Genetic regulatory systems**: The journal explored nonlocal effects at the cellular level, including potential consciousness influences on gene expression

## The MMMP Research Questions

The Mind Matter Mapping Project's working groups formulated specific research questions directly relevant to SRL:
1. What is the earliest physiological detector of psi information in the transduction pathway to conscious awareness?
2. How do various structural and physiological responses line up time-wise in anomalous perception tasks?
3. What determines the "entanglement activation threshold" between connected points in nonlocal networks?

## Relevance to SRL

- **Placebo reframed**: If placebo involves nonlocal/retrocausal mechanisms, then clinician intention during treatment is not just psychological comfort — it is a measurable variable affecting outcomes. This validates the Somnistics emphasis on practitioner state and presence.
- **MMMP Question #1 maps to Anterocept**: "What is the earliest physiological detector?" is exactly the question Anterocept's progressive interoceptive training is designed to answer experientially — training people to detect signals the body is already processing.
- **Experimenter effects in clinical settings**: The "interference phenomena" framework explains why clinician presence, intention, and autonomic state affect patient outcomes — the theoretical foundation for why training clinician consciousness (via Somnistics) improves care quality.
- **Question #3 and anesthesia**: The "entanglement activation threshold" question maps onto consciousness transitions under anesthesia — at what depth does the nonlocal connection dissolve?

## Citation

(2026). Development of interoception: The critical role of maternal affective touch in early attachment. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2026. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1748486

## Key Findings

- Maternal affective touch (MAT) in infancy provides a critical relational context in which interoceptive signals are integrated within CNS structures, including the insula and anterior cingulate cortex.
- Early attachment relationships scaffold the development of interoceptive accuracy and autonomic regulation capacity across the lifespan.
- Interoceptive dysregulation and autonomic dysregulation in adult psychopathology may trace origins to deficiencies in early embodied, relational experiences.
- Early relational and embodied interventions represent promising new targets for research and clinical intervention in conditions characterized by interoceptive dysfunction.

## Relevance to SRL

Directly supports SRL's framing of interoceptive literacy as a learnable, developmentally shaped capacity — and opens a compelling window for understanding why some individuals enter clinical training with diminished interoceptive sensitivity, requiring targeted re-cultivation via practices like Gap Moment Training. The autonomic dysregulation angle connects strongly to SRL's Clinician Durability framework.

## Summary
Grabbe, Nicholson et al. (2025) evaluated the Community Resiliency Model (CRM), a somatic self-care intervention teaching six interoceptive awareness skills — grounding, resource, help now!, gesturing, shift and stay, and tracking — in trauma-exposed populations. The study demonstrated that these body-based skills improved well-being outcomes and could be taught by non-clinical facilitators, making the approach highly scalable for community settings and underserved populations.

## Key Finding for SRL
This study validates the premise that simple, teachable interoceptive skills can produce meaningful outcomes in trauma-exposed populations without requiring clinical supervision. The CRM's six-skill framework parallels SRL's own protocol taxonomy (Gap Moment Training, resonant breathing, etc.) and provides evidence that brief somatic interventions are both effective and scalable — key selling points for enterprise and consumer deployment.

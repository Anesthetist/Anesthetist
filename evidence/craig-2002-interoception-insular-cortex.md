## Overview
This foundational review by Craig maps the neuroanatomical basis of interoception, identifying the insular cortex as the primary interoceptive cortex. The paper describes the lamina I spinothalamocortical pathway as the afferent channel carrying homeostatic signals from the body to the brain. It redefines interoception as encompassing all internal body signals — not just visceral sensation — and establishes the right anterior insula as the site where bodily feelings become conscious awareness.

## Key Findings
1. The insular cortex is the primary interoceptive cortex, receiving and processing signals about the physiological condition of the body
2. Lamina I afferents carry homeostatic signals (temperature, pain, itch, muscular and visceral sensations) via a dedicated spinothalamocortical pathway
3. The right anterior insula integrates bodily feelings into conscious awareness, providing a subjective "feeling self"
4. Interoception encompasses all internal body signals — thermal, nociceptive, muscular, visceral — not just visceral sensation as previously defined
5. This pathway provides the neuroanatomical basis for the subjective experience of "how do you feel?" — grounding emotion in physiology

## Connection to Somnistics
This paper provides the neuroanatomical foundation for why interoceptive training works. SRL's core interventions — [[resonant-breathing-frequency]], [[gap-moment-training]] — are training the insular cortex's processing of bodily signals. Understanding the lamina I pathway validates that breathwork and HRV biofeedback have a direct neural route to conscious body awareness, which is the basis of [[interoceptive-literacy]].

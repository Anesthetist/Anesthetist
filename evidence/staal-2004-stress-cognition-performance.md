# Stress, Cognition, and Human Performance: A Literature Review and Conceptual Framework

## Summary

Staal (2004) produced a comprehensive NASA technical report synthesizing the military and aerospace research on how stress affects cognitive performance. The review covers attentional narrowing, working memory degradation, decision-making biases, and the protective effects of training, experience, and individual differences — providing a unified framework for understanding performance under extreme conditions.

## Key Contributions

- **Attentional narrowing under stress**: Easterbrook's (1959) cue-utilization theory — stress narrows the range of cues attended to. Initially adaptive (filtering irrelevant cues), but at high stress levels, relevant cues are also excluded
- **Working memory degradation**: Acute stress impairs central executive function, reducing capacity for planning, problem-solving, and maintaining multiple task representations simultaneously
- **Cognitive tunneling**: Under stress, operators fixate on a single hypothesis or data source and fail to scan the environment — a known cause of aviation accidents and clinical errors
- **Decision-making biases**: Stress increases reliance on heuristics, reduces consideration of alternatives, and shortens time horizons — the "first acceptable option" bias (connecting to Klein's RPD under different framing)
- **Protective factors**: Training, experience, perceived control, social support, and physiological fitness all buffer stress effects on cognition — stress inoculation training is effective
- **Individual differences**: Hardiness, self-efficacy, and trait anxiety moderate the stress-cognition relationship

## Methodological Notes

- Comprehensive literature review (not meta-analysis) — qualitative synthesis of several hundred studies
- Draws heavily on military, aviation, and spaceflight operational research
- Framework integrates transactional stress theory (Lazarus), resource theory (Kahneman), and biological stress response (Selye, McEwen)
- Highly cited in military human factors; less cited in clinical literature despite obvious relevance

## SRL Relevance

Staal's synthesis provides the evidence base for why CRNAs need structured regulation training — the cognitive effects of acute stress (attentional narrowing, working memory degradation, cognitive tunneling) are exactly the failure modes that lead to anesthetic adverse events. The protective factors he identifies — training, perceived control, physiological fitness — are exactly what SRL builds.

# Sacchet Lab Jhana and Advanced Concentrative Absorption Studies (2024–2025)

## Summary

This combined note covers a cluster of studies from the Sacchet Lab investigating jhana — advanced concentrative absorptive meditation states characterized by deep focus, altered phenomenology, and profound shifts in subjective experience. Across multiple papers using fMRI, EEG, MEG, and computational modeling, the research collectively demonstrates:

- **Brain network reorganization**: During jhana, large-scale brain networks reorganize in ways not seen in ordinary waking, sleep, or standard meditation states. Network topology shifts toward altered integration patterns.
- **Cortical gradient flattening**: The brain's principal functional gradient (the axis from unimodal sensory to transmodal abstract processing) flattens during jhana, suggesting a collapse of the usual hierarchy between sensory and abstract processing.
- **Nonlinear dynamics**: Jhana states exhibit distinctive nonlinear dynamical signatures, suggesting the brain enters qualitatively different attractor states rather than simply moving along a continuum.
- **Minimal phenomenal experience**: Advanced jhana practitioners report states of "minimal phenomenal experience" — consciousness stripped to its barest experiential substrate.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Jhana represents the deep end of the meditation-state spectrum, establishing boundary conditions for consciousness modulation
- Brain network reorganization during jhana demonstrates the brain's capacity for radical state shifts
- Cortical gradient flattening suggests that deep meditation transcends the sensory-abstract processing hierarchy
- Nonlinear dynamics confirm that meditation states are qualitatively distinct, not just quantitatively different
- The jhana research validates the state-transition framework — discrete, trainable states with distinct neural signatures
- While jhana itself is beyond typical clinical scope, it maps the full continuum from default states through breathwork to advanced absorption
- Methodology from these studies (network analysis, gradient mapping) can be applied to studying breath-mediated state changes

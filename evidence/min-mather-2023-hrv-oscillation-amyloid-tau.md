## Overview

Min, Mather et al. (2023) conducted a randomized trial (N=108) comparing heart rate oscillation biofeedback conditions — Osc+ (increasing oscillations at ~0.1 Hz) vs Osc- (suppressing oscillations) — over 4 weeks of daily sessions. The study measured plasma amyloid-beta (Abeta40, Abeta42) and tau levels, providing the first evidence linking HRV biofeedback to Alzheimer's disease biomarker modulation.

## Key Findings

1. **N=108 adults were randomized to Osc+ vs Osc- conditions**, providing a well-powered comparison of oscillation-enhancing versus oscillation-suppressing biofeedback.
2. **4 weeks of Osc+ produced large decreases in plasma Abeta40 and Abeta42**, suggesting enhanced amyloid clearance through autonomic modulation.
3. **Decreased beta-adrenergic gene transcription** in the Osc+ group, indicating a shift from sympathetic to parasympathetic dominance at the molecular level.
4. **Opposing effects on tau in younger vs older participants**, suggesting age-dependent mechanisms in how autonomic oscillations affect tau metabolism.
5. **First evidence linking HRV biofeedback to Alzheimer's biomarker reduction**, opening an entirely new therapeutic application for resonant breathing interventions.

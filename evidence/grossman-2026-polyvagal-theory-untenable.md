## Citation

Grossman, P., et al. (2026). Why the polyvagal theory is untenable: An international expert evaluation of the polyvagal theory and commentary upon Porges, S.W. (2025). *Clinical Neuropsychiatry*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12937499/

## Key Findings

- 39 leading scientists co-authored a critique calling Polyvagal Theory (PVT) "untenable"
- Authors dispute that PVT accurately portrays the evolutionary neurophysiological changes proposed by Porges
- Central criticism: PVT's claim that the mammalian ventral vagus is evolutionarily advanced while the dorsal vagus represents a "left-behind" reptilian vagus is not supported by comparative anatomy
- Critics point to ample evidence of complex social behaviors in non-mammal vertebrates, undermining PVT's evolutionary hierarchy
- Porges responded in the same journal issue ("When a Critique Becomes Untenable"), arguing critics did not engage PVT as articulated in the primary literature

## Relevance to SRL

**HIGH IMPACT** — This directly challenges the theoretical foundation of SRL's **Polyvagal Theory** concept. SRL should carefully track this debate, as it has implications for how polyvagal frameworks are positioned in curricula, products, and scientific communications. Importantly, the clinical utility of polyvagal-informed practices (which SRL builds upon) may remain valid even if specific evolutionary claims are revised. SRL may need to articulate its position on the distinction between theoretical mechanism and practical application.

## Methodology

Commentary and expert evaluation paper. 39 internationally recognized experts in vagus nerve physiology, evolutionary biology, and vertebrate social behavior were invited to co-author. Published as a response to Porges' (2025) review paper in the same journal.

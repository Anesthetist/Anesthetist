# Yoga, Interoceptive Awareness, and Emotional Well-Being (Garnsey et al. 2026)

## Summary

Published in *European Journal of Integrative Medicine* (January 2026), this secondary analysis of an RCT (n=75) examined mechanisms of change in yoga for emotional well-being. MAIA "Trusting" subscale correlated with peace (r=.35), while "Attention Regulation" correlated with meaning (r=.37) and peace (r=.34). These findings identify specific interoceptive subdimensions as mediators of well-being outcomes in body-based practices.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- MAIA "Trusting" subscale specifically predicts peace/equanimity outcomes
- MAIA "Attention Regulation" predicts both meaning-making and peace
- Different MAIA subdimensions map to different well-being outcomes
- Interoceptive awareness mediates (not just correlates with) yoga's well-being effects
- Supports targeted interoceptive training for specific well-being goals

## Concepts Derived

- [[interoceptive-literacy]] — specific MAIA subdimensions as mechanisms of change
- [[interoception]] — interoceptive awareness as mediator of body-based practice effects

## SRL Relevance

Reinforces the Kim 2026 finding that specific MAIA subdimensions have distinct functional roles. For SRL protocol design, this suggests that "Trusting" and "Attention Regulation" are the highest-value training targets for well-being outcomes. PMID: 41736923.

## Citation

(2025). Heart rate variability biofeedback for substance use disorder: A randomized clinical trial. *JAMA Psychiatry*. PMID: 41032322

## Key Findings

- First RCT to test second-generation wearable HRV biofeedback technology for substance use disorder (SUD)
- HRVB associated with significant reductions in negative affect (b = -0.01; z = -3.21; P = .001) and craving (b = -0.01; z = -4.60; P < .001) over 8 weeks
- HRVB associated with 64% reduction in alcohol/drug use days (OR = 0.36; 95% CrI, 0.25–0.54) compared to controls
- Phase 2 RCT with virtual recruitment across the US; 8 weeks outpatient treatment
- New wearable patch from Massachusetts General Hospital enables continuous HRV biofeedback

## Relevance to SRL

Validates wearable-delivered HRV biofeedback as a powerful self-regulation tool, demonstrating that autonomic regulation training translates to behavioral change in a challenging population. The craving reduction mechanism aligns with SRL's model of interoceptive literacy enabling better state management. The wearable patch modality is relevant to SRL's hardware integration strategy for continuous biofeedback delivery.

## Methodology

Phase 2 randomized clinical trial; 8 weeks outpatient; virtual recruitment across US (Feb 2023–June 2024); wearable HRV biofeedback patch; ecological momentary assessment of affect and craving.

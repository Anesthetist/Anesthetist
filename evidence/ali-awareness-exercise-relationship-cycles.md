## Overview

A single-page awareness exercise mapping interpersonal escalation cycles through a systems-level feedback loop model. The exercise makes explicit the mechanism by which stored relational energy and nervous system sensitivity amplify minor triggers into major relational ruptures.

## Key Framework

### Relationship Cycle Diagram

A circular feedback loop between two people (You ↔ Them):

**Escalation path**: Escalation Event → Judgment ("Because") → Emotion ("I'm Upset") → Stored Energy → Emotional Sensitivity → Attack Event → (cycle repeats from other person's perspective)

The diagram shows this loop operating symmetrically — both parties run the same cycle simultaneously, each person's attack becoming the other's escalation trigger. The center of the cycle is "Stored Energy" — the accumulated unprocessed emotional charge that amplifies each subsequent iteration.

### Exercise Questions

1. Recall a recent relationship cycle — what happened, who was it with?
2. What did you do that felt like a mistake?
3. What level of thinking (1–7) was your brain operating at when you responded? (references the ALI developmental levels)
4. On a scale of 1–10, how much **stored energy** has built up in this relationship over time, where even little issues trigger big emotional bursts?
5. On a scale of 1–10, how much **emotional sensitivity** has stored up in your nervous system, where even little issues trigger fear and defensiveness?

### Core Insight

The exercise distinguishes between **relational stored energy** (accumulated charge specific to a relationship) and **nervous system emotional sensitivity** (generalized autonomic reactivity). This distinction is critical — it separates the interpersonal field from the individual's baseline autonomic state.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Stored Energy as Autonomic Allostatic Load

The concept of "stored energy" that accumulates in relationships maps precisely to the SRL concept of allostatic load in clinical settings. A CRNA who has accumulated unprocessed relational charge with a surgeon, a charge nurse, or even a patient population carries stored energy that lowers their threshold for autonomic hijack. The 1–10 scale assessment is a non-instrumented proxy for what Pausality's physiological sensors would detect as elevated baseline sympathetic tone in specific relational contexts.

### Nervous System Sensitivity as Interoceptive Threshold

Question 5 — rating emotional sensitivity stored in the nervous system — is an explicit interoceptive self-assessment. This question asks the participant to feel INTO their nervous system and assess its current reactivity level. This is structured interoception training disguised as a relationship exercise. The participant who can accurately rate their nervous system sensitivity is demonstrating the very interoceptive accuracy that MAIA-2-CRNA measures.

### Cycle-Breaking as State Transition Training

The developmental level assessment (question 3) asks the participant to retroactively identify which brain region was driving their behavior during the cycle. This is self-remembering applied to relational conflict — the meta-awareness to observe "I was operating from my limbic brain" IS the beginning of state transition capacity. SRL's gap moment training targets this same capacity: the ability to notice which state you're in and choose a different one.

### Diagnostic-Therapeutic Unity

The exercise reveals both the pattern (where cycles occur) and the entry point for intervention (stored energy level, nervous system sensitivity, habitual thinking level). The assessment simultaneously locates the problem and begins building the meta-awareness needed to interrupt the cycle.

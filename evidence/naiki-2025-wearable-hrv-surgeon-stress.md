## Summary

Study using wearable HRV monitoring during robotic surgery to quantify surgeon mental stress and its relationship to experience level and surgical performance.

## Key Findings

- Wearable HRV during robotic surgery successfully quantified mental stress in real-time
- Experienced surgeon showed significantly better SDNN (higher vagal tone) than novice surgeon
- As the novice accumulated cases, heart rate decreased — the learning curve became visible in autonomic data
- HRV metrics aligned with objective surgical performance metrics
- Demonstrates that autonomic regulation capacity tracks with clinical expertise

## Relevance to SRL

Powerful evidence that autonomic regulation (measured via HRV) is not just a wellness metric but a performance metric. The learning curve visible in autonomic data supports the concept of cognitive variability analysis — tracking expertise development through physiological signatures. Directly supports the case that training autonomic regulation could accelerate clinical skill acquisition.

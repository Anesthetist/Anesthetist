## Citation

Craig, A.D. (2009). Emotional moments across time: A possible neural basis for time perception in the anterior insula. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 364(1525), 1933-1942. [DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2009.0008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0008)

## Key Findings

- Proposes that the anterior insula creates discrete snapshots of interoceptive-emotional state called **global emotional moments** (GEMs)
- GEMs are the fundamental units of conscious awareness. Each one is a complete representation of "how I feel right now"
- Subjective time emerges from the accumulation of successive GEMs. A series of felt moments constitutes duration
- The rate of GEM generation is modulated by emotional salience and arousal. More salient events produce more GEMs per unit time, which is why emotional events feel longer
- The model has an endogenous time base rooted in interoceptive processing that could account for human time perception in the seconds-to-minutes range
- The anterior insula integrates homeostatic, interoceptive, and emotional signals into these moments of awareness

## Theoretical Significance

This paper is the bridge between classical clock models (pacemaker-accumulator) and embodied time frameworks. Craig took his own neuroanatomical mapping of the primate insular cortex and proposed that the same tissue responsible for interoceptive awareness also generates the temporal structure of conscious experience. Wittmann's entire research program (climbing neural activation, the self-time link, cardiac-temporal coupling) builds on Craig's anatomical and theoretical foundation.

## Relevance to SRL

This is the theoretical predecessor that explains why SRL's interoceptive interventions affect temporal experience. Craig's GEM model predicts that any practice which changes the rate or quality of interoceptive-emotional processing in the anterior insula will change the temporal structure of consciousness. Breathing practices, heartbeat awareness, body scanning, and emotional labeling all modify insular processing. They all change experienced time.

The GEM model also explains why the NeuroMinute format works at the temporal level. Each NeuroMinute is a structured sequence of interoceptive moments. Each moment is a GEM. The practice changes the rate, clarity, and emotional quality of those moments.

## Methodology

Theoretical paper grounded in Craig's own neuroanatomical research on primate insular cortex organization. Integrates findings from interoception, emotion, and time perception literatures into a unified insular cortex model.

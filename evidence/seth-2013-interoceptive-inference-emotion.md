## Overview
This seminal paper by Seth proposes that emotional feelings arise from predictive coding applied to interoceptive signals. The brain continuously generates predictions about the body's internal state and compares them with incoming afferent signals; prediction errors drive both perception and allostatic regulation. The framework unifies emotion, selfhood, and interoception under a single computational model of interoceptive inference.

## Key Findings
1. Emotional feelings arise from predictive coding of interoceptive signals — the brain actively infers the causes of bodily sensations
2. The brain generates top-down predictions about bodily states and compares them with bottom-up afferent signals, producing interoceptive prediction errors
3. Prediction errors drive both perceptual updating (changing how we feel) and allostatic regulation (changing the body's state)
4. Self-awareness is grounded in interoceptive inference — the sense of being a bodily self emerges from the brain's generative model of the body
5. This framework unifies emotion, selfhood, and interoception under one computational model, bridging phenomenology with computational neuroscience

# Mindfulness Starts with the Body (Kerr, Sacchet, Lazar, Moore & Jones 2013)

## Summary

This paper proposes a mechanistic model for how mindfulness meditation works: it begins with somatosensory attention to the body (particularly breath and bodily sensations), which trains top-down modulation of cortical alpha rhythms in somatosensory cortex. Alpha oscillations serve as a gain-control mechanism — when alpha power increases in a cortical region, sensory processing in that region is attenuated; when alpha decreases, processing is enhanced. Mindfulness practitioners learn to volitionally modulate these alpha rhythms, gaining flexible control over what sensory information is processed. This is a foundational paper for SRL because it provides the neuroscientific basis for the "awareness before technique" principle: the body is the entry point, and attentional training of body-awareness creates the neural infrastructure for all downstream benefits.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Mindfulness meditation works through a body-first mechanism — somatosensory attention is the entry point
- Top-down modulation of cortical alpha rhythms is the core neural mechanism of mindfulness
- Alpha oscillations function as a sensory gain-control (gating) mechanism in somatosensory cortex
- Trained meditators show enhanced ability to volitionally modulate somatosensory alpha
- Body-focused attention precedes and enables higher-order cognitive and emotional regulation
- The model explains why breath-focused practices are effective first-line interventions
- Diaphragmatic awareness can be understood as a specific case of somatosensory alpha modulation

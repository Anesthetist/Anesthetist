## Citation

Fort, L. D., Costines, C., Wittmann, M., Demertzi, A., & Schmidt, T. T. (2025). Classification schemes of altered states of consciousness. *Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews*, 175, 106178. [DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106178](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106178)

## Key Findings

- Comprehensive review of ASC classification schemes, organized into three categories:
  1. **State-based:** Classifying by subjective experience (phenomenology, psychometrics)
  2. **Method-based:** Classifying by induction technique (meditation, pharmacological, sensory deprivation)
  3. **Neuro/physio-based:** Classifying by neurophysiological mechanisms
- Comparing and extending existing schemes can enhance identification of neural correlates of consciousness
- The paper clusters state-based concepts and proposes them for classifying ASC experiences — a taxonomy for future research
- Different ASC induction methods can produce overlapping phenomenological profiles, suggesting shared underlying mechanisms

## Relevance to SRL

- **SRL's state-transition concept** needs a robust classification framework. This paper provides exactly that — a taxonomy for the states SRL's interventions induce and traverse.
- **Method-based classification** validates that breathing techniques, meditation, and biofeedback (SRL's toolkit) are recognized ASC induction methods alongside pharmacological approaches
- **Shared mechanisms across methods:** If meditation, floatation, breathing, and biofeedback produce overlapping phenomenology, SRL can cite convergent evidence from multiple ASC traditions
- **Phenomenological assessment:** The paper's state-based clustering could inform SRL's outcome measurement — capturing not just HRV changes but the subjective quality of states reached during NeuroMinutes
- **Taxonomy for the consciousness development arc:** SRL's progression from basic breath awareness → flow states → nondual awareness → content-free awareness maps onto the ASC taxonomy

## Methodology

Systematic review of ASC classification literature. Comparison and meta-synthesis of existing classification frameworks. Proposes clustered phenomenological dimensions for future ASC research.

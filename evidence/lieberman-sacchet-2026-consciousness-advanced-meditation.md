## Citation

Lieberman, Z., & Sacchet, M. D. (2026). Toward a neuroscience of consciousness using advanced meditation. *Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews*. PMID: 41380790. Massachusetts General Hospital / Harvard Medical School Meditation Research Program.

## Key Findings

- Proposes advanced meditation as a powerful paradigm for consciousness science, offering advantages over psychedelics and other models
- Focuses on two key phenomena: (1) Jhāna — advanced concentrative absorption preserving highly abstract awareness while attenuating typical features of consciousness; (2) Cessation events (Nirodha) — temporary suspension of consciousness altogether
- Introduces a "minimal model framework" using these phenomena as precise, replicable, experimentally tractable anchors for identifying the simplest form of conscious experience
- Argues prevailing paradigms anchored in typical content-rich states obscure the neural mechanisms giving rise to consciousness

## Relevance to SRL

**HIGH IMPACT.** Directly relevant to SRL's consciousness studies domain (Springett Consciousness Stairway, Nondual Clinical Awareness). Provides a scientific framework for understanding the advanced meditative states that inform SRL's deeper practice protocols. ## Methodology

Theoretical and review paper; proposes framework integrating phenomenological reports from advanced meditators with neuroscientific measurement; theory-driven neuroscience approach; MGH/Harvard Meditation Research Program.

## Citation

Better sleep is associated with higher academic performance from an actigraphy-based analysis of sleep consistency and grades in college students. *Scientific Reports*, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-33775-0

## Key Findings

- Sleep consistency (not just duration) is significantly associated with higher academic/cognitive performance
- Circadian variance of sleep patterns plays an important role in attention, learning, and memory
- Actigraphy-based objective measurement provides more reliable data than self-report
- Highlights regularity of sleep timing as a key variable for cognitive optimization

## Relevance to SRL

Supports SRL's emphasis on circadian alignment and sleep regularity as foundational to cognitive performance. Directly relevant to the CRNA Shift Architect concept — shift workers who can maintain greater sleep consistency may preserve cognitive function. Also connects to SomnoAffinity and the Somnistics Readiness Battery, which could incorporate sleep consistency metrics.

## Methodology

Actigraphy-based observational study; college student population; objective sleep measures correlated with academic performance (grades).

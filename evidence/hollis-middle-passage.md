## Summary

Hollis applies Jungian developmental psychology to the midlife transition. The "middle passage" is the psychologically turbulent crossing between the first half of life (adaptation, persona-building, survival, meeting external expectations) and the second half (individuation, answering "What does the soul ask of me now?"). Rupture — grief, loss, disillusionment — is not pathology but the psyche's mechanism for forcing re-examination. The central question shifts from "What does the world want from me?" to "What wants to enter the world through me?"

Hollis argues that this transition is neither optional nor comfortable: the provisional personality constructed in the first half eventually becomes a prison. Genuine meaning emerges only when the ego's agenda is subordinated to the deeper self's requirements. Related works include "Finding Meaning in the Second Half of Life" (2005) and "Living an Examined Life" (2018).

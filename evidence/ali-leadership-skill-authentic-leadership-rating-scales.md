## Overview

A comprehensive 6-page self-assessment instrument containing eight 7-point rating scales that map the spectrum from reactive to authentic leadership across key behavioral domains. Each scale provides named developmental stages with behavioral descriptions, creating a granular self-assessment tool tied to the ALI 7 Levels of Thinking framework.

## Key Framework

### Concept: Escaping Whirlpools

A "whirlpool" is a negative cycle beyond one's current capacity to handle alone. Negative cycles cause brain downshift into lower levels of thinking, reducing self-awareness and nuanced cognition. Self-awareness — knowing which "gear" your brain is in — is the essential skill for escaping whirlpools. The ALRS provides domain-specific awareness tools when the 7 Levels model is too abstract.

### Eight Rating Scales (Each 1–7, Reactive → Authentic)

1. **Response to Fear** (Fear as Weakness vs. Fear as Warning)
   - 1: Ticking Time Bomb — out of control, emotions about to explode
   - 2: Anxious or Depressed — struggling to hold it together
   - 3: Don't Rock the Boat — avoidance of anything threatening stability
   - 4: Fixing Problems — shut down feelings, focus on fixing
   - 5–7: Progressive capacity to use fear as information rather than threat

2. **Response to Change** (Resisting Change vs. Embracing Change)
3. **Response to a Trigger** (Victim vs. Owner)
4. **Response to an Insecurity** (Proving Worth vs. Providing Value)
5. **Response to a Hook** (Overcare vs. Care)
6. **Type of Negotiation** (Win/Lose vs. Win/Win or No Deal)
7. **Type of Motivation** (Negative Fuel vs. Positive Fuel)
8. **Type of Accountability** (Negative Discipline vs. Positive Discipline)

### Assessment Protocol
Participants pick three scales representing their biggest growth opportunities and focus on improving those over the year. For each chosen scale, they identify current rating, desired rating, and what support/skills/habits would create the shift.

### Includes Full 7 Levels of Thinking Reference Table
Complete reference with Level, Name, Brain Region, and behavioral descriptions with characteristic language at each level.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Domain-Specific Interoceptive Assessment

The ALRS solves a problem the SRL vault has identified: the 7 Levels model (like any stage model) is too abstract for real-time self-assessment during stress. The ALRS provides **domain-specific entry points** — instead of asking "what level am I at?" (too abstract when your neocortex is offline), it asks "how am I responding to fear right now?" (concrete, embodied, answerable even in lower states). This is a design pattern SRL should adopt for its own assessment instruments.

### Fear Response Scale as Autonomic State Map

The Response to Fear scale (Ticking Time Bomb → Anxious → Don't Rock the Boat → Fixing Problems → ...) is a polyvagal state map in behavioral language. Level 1 is sympathetic overwhelm, Level 2 is sympathetic-dorsal oscillation, Level 3 is dorsal freeze/fawn, Level 4 is sympathetic mobilization with emotional suppression. Each named stage corresponds to a detectable autonomic signature. This creates a bridge between Whetten's behavioral descriptions and Pausality's physiological sensors.

### Three-Scale Focus as Minimum Effective Dose

The instruction to pick only three scales mirrors the SRL minimum effective dose principle. Rather than attempting comprehensive transformation, focus on the highest-leverage domains. This is clinically sophisticated — it acknowledges that attention is finite and that targeted development outperforms diffuse aspiration.

### Whirlpool Concept as Neural Transition Failure

Whetten's "whirlpool" maps to the SRL concept of neural transition failure — a state the individual lacks the capacity to exit autonomously. The prescription (get support, develop self-awareness) mirrors the SRL approach: co-regulation provides the external scaffolding while structured interoception training develops the internal capacity to detect and exit maladaptive states.

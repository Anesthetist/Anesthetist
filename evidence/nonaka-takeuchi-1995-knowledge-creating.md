# The Knowledge-Creating Company

## Summary

Nonaka and Takeuchi (1995) proposed the **SECI model** of organizational knowledge creation, describing how tacit knowledge (embodied, experiential, hard to articulate) converts to explicit knowledge (codified, transmissible) and back again through four modes: Socialization, Externalization, Combination, and Internalization. The model explains how organizations can systematically create new knowledge by facilitating these conversion cycles.

## Key Contributions

- **SECI Model**: Four modes of knowledge conversion:
  - **Socialization** (tacit → tacit): Shared experience, apprenticeship, observation — learning by being present
  - **Externalization** (tacit → explicit): Articulating tacit knowledge through metaphors, analogies, models — the most critical and difficult conversion
  - **Combination** (explicit → explicit): Systematizing and recombining codified knowledge — databases, reports, curricula
  - **Internalization** (explicit → tacit): Learning by doing, embodying explicit knowledge back into practice
- **Knowledge spiral**: SECI cycles continuously, expanding the organization's knowledge base at each turn
- **Ba** (場): The shared context or space that enables knowledge creation — physical, virtual, or mental
- Distinguished between **knowledge creation** (generating new knowledge) and **knowledge management** (storing and retrieving existing knowledge)

## Methodological Notes

- Case study methodology drawn from Japanese corporations (Honda, Canon, Matsushita, NEC, Sharp)
- Grounded in Japanese philosophical traditions (distinction between explicit and tacit knowledge draws on Polanyi but extends through Japanese epistemology)
- Critiqued for cultural specificity, difficulty of operationalization, and treating tacit/explicit as binary rather than spectrum

## Design Constraint

**If tacit knowledge cannot be externalized through the system (conversation, tagging, structured output), it remains trapped and undistributable.**

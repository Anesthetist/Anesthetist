## Overview

The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) is a 19-item self-report instrument that assesses sleep quality and disturbances over the past month. It yields seven component scores — subjective sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep duration, habitual sleep efficiency, sleep disturbances, use of sleeping medication, and daytime dysfunction — which sum to a global score ranging from 0 to 21. A global score greater than 5 indicates poor sleep quality.

## Key Findings

1. 19 items yielding 7 component scores: subjective sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep duration, habitual sleep efficiency, sleep disturbances, use of sleeping medication, and daytime dysfunction
2. Global score ranges 0-21, with a validated cutoff of >5 indicating poor sleep quality (sensitivity 89.6%, specificity 86.5%)
3. Validated across diverse clinical and research settings including psychiatric, medical, and community populations
4. Free to use with no licensing requirements
5. Covers the past month, providing a stable temporal window suitable for pre/post intervention designs

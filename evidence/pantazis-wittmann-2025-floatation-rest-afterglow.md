## Citation

Pantazis, P., & Wittmann, M. (2025). Experience after Floatation-REST: Relaxation during floating mediates the afterglow effect. *Consciousness and Cognition*, 103836. [DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2025.103836](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2025.103836)

## Key Findings

- Floatation-REST (Restricted Environmental Stimulation Technique) produces an "afterglow" — persistent positive effects after the session ends
- Relaxation experienced *during* floating mediates the afterglow — the depth of the state determines the duration of post-session benefit
- Sensory deprivation reduces external input, forcing the brain to rely on interoceptive signals, which alters time perception and self-awareness
- The afterglow parallels effects reported after meditation, psychedelic experiences, and other consciousness-altering practices

## Relevance to SRL

- **NeuroMinute afterglow:** If 60 minutes of floatation produces measurable afterglow, the cumulative effect of daily 60-second NeuroMinutes may produce an analogous micro-afterglow — worth measuring
- **Sensory reduction → interoceptive amplification:** Floatation strips external sensory input, amplifying body signals. The eye-close phase of NeuroMinutes does something similar in miniature — reducing visual input to amplify interoceptive awareness
- **Dose-response for consciousness states:** The deeper the state during intervention, the longer the post-intervention benefit. This supports SRL's progressive depth model via [[anterocept]] domains
- **Temporal distortion during floatation** is well-documented (Wittmann's self-time link in action) — parallels the time dilation clinicians report during deep breathing practices

## Methodology

Floatation-REST study with pre-, during-, and post-session assessments measuring relaxation, altered states phenomenology, and persistence of effects.

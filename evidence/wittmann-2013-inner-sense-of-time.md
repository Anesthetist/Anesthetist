## Citation

Wittmann, M. (2013). The inner sense of time: How the brain creates a representation of duration. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 14(3), 217–223. [DOI: 10.1038/nrn3452](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3452)

## Key Findings

- Multiple competing models exist for how the brain represents time, but converging evidence points to **climbing neural activation** as the core mechanism
- Neurophysiological recordings reveal neurons whose firing rate progressively increases ("climbs") during a timed interval, peaking when the interval ends
- In humans, this climbing activation occurs in the **insular cortex** — the same region that processes interoceptive signals (heartbeat, breathing, visceral state) and emotional feelings
- The insula's dual role as both interoceptive hub and temporal processor creates a direct mechanistic link: subjective time is built from the cumulative representation of body states
- This model explains why arousal, emotion, and body awareness all modulate time perception — they change the rate of accumulation in the same neural substrate

## Core Model: Climbing Neural Activation

The brain represents duration not through a discrete "clock" but through a continuous accumulation process:

1. **Encoding phase:** Neural populations in the insula progressively increase their firing rate as an interval unfolds
2. **Accumulation:** The total accumulated activation represents elapsed duration
3. **Peak:** Activation peaks at the anticipated end of the interval
4. **Comparison:** The peak amplitude is compared against stored representations to generate duration judgments

This is fundamentally different from pacemaker-accumulator models because the "clock" is embodied — it runs on interoceptive signals, not abstract pulses.

## Relevance to SRL

**Foundational.** This paper provides the neural mechanism that explains why SRL's interoceptive training (breath work, cardiac awareness, body scanning) simultaneously trains temporal processing:

- **The 60-second NeuroMinute** operates within the range where climbing neural activation in the insula is well-documented
- **Resonant breathing** modulates insular activation rate — slow breathing literally slows the neural accumulator, producing subjective time dilation
- **Gap moments** are temporal windows where the clinician's insular processing shifts from one task context to another — the climbing activation resets
- **Interoceptive accuracy** (heartbeat detection, breath awareness) directly improves temporal accuracy because both use the same insular substrate
- **Stress-induced time compression** in clinical emergencies reflects accelerated climbing activation from sympathetic arousal — trainable via autonomic regulation

## Methodology

Review article synthesizing animal neurophysiology (single-cell recordings in prefrontal cortex, striatum) with human neuroimaging (fMRI of insular cortex during temporal tasks). Proposes a unified model linking interoception, emotion, and time perception through insular cortex function.

Cantone, D. et al. (2026). Enhancing wellness: a systematic review of biofeedback interventions for healthcare professionals. *Frontiers in Psychiatry*.

## Background

Healthcare professionals are routinely exposed to high psychosocial and physiological demands, placing them at elevated risk for stress-related disorders, including burnout, anxiety, and impaired autonomic regulation. Biofeedback has emerged as a promising non-pharmacological approach to enhance self-regulation and resilience. This systematic review evaluates the effectiveness of biofeedback-based interventions in improving psychological and physiological outcomes among healthcare workers.

## Methods

Systematic search across PubMed, Cochrane, Embase, PsycINFO, and grey literature (December 2023–January 2024), following PRISMA guidelines with registered PROSPERO protocol.

## Results

Significant reductions in stress were consistently observed following biofeedback interventions across studies. However, the limited number of methodologically acceptable studies and their pronounced heterogeneity required a narrative systematic review approach rather than a fully standardized quantitative synthesis.

## Conclusion

Biofeedback shows promise for healthcare worker wellness but the evidence base needs strengthening through more rigorous, standardized RCTs.

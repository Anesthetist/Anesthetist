## Overview

Owens, Allen, Ondobaka, and Friston (2018) review the active inference formulation of interoception and its clinical applications. The paper formalizes interoception as predictive coding — where interoceptive prediction errors drive allostatic regulation — and describes how this computational framework translates from basic neuroscience to clinical intervention targets for disorders of brain, body, and behavior.

## Key Findings

1. Formalizes interoception as active inference under the free energy principle, bridging central and autonomic nervous system function
2. Interoceptive prediction errors drive allostatic regulation — the body acts to fulfill descending predictions
3. Clinical conditions arise from aberrant interoceptive precision weighting
4. Anxiety can be modeled as overly precise interoceptive priors, generating excessive prediction errors
5. The framework offers an integrative approach to human physiology, unifying interactions between central and peripheral nervous systems

## Connection to Somnistics

This paper bridges theory to clinic in a way that directly informs SRL's intervention design. The framing of anxiety as "overly precise interoceptive priors" suggests that SRL's training should aim to recalibrate precision weighting — not just improve accuracy.

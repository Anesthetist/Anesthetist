# Relevance Realization (Vervaeke)

## Summary

John Vervaeke, a cognitive scientist at the University of Toronto, developed the relevance realization framework as a unifying account of how minds determine what is salient, significant, and meaningful. The framework draws on dynamical systems theory, 4E cognitive science, and contemplative wisdom traditions. Vervaeke argues that relevance realization is not a single cognitive module but an emergent property of self-organizing cognitive processes — involving opponent processing between focusing and diffusing attention, exploitation and exploration, assimilation and accommodation. The "meaning crisis" of modernity reflects disruption of the practices and ecologies that historically supported relevance realization. For SRL, this framework explains why breathwork micro-interventions can function as relevance realization practices: by shifting attentional dynamics, restoring embodied engagement, and creating participatory knowing through direct somatic experience.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Relevance realization is the foundational cognitive process underlying attention, meaning, and adaptive behavior
- It operates through opponent processing — dynamically balancing competing cognitive demands (focus vs. breadth, novelty vs. familiarity)
- Participatory knowing (knowing by being transformed through engagement) is distinct from propositional, procedural, and perspectival knowing — and is the least culturally supported
- Contemplative practices function as "psychotechnologies" that train and refine relevance realization
- The meaning crisis is a disruption of the ecologies that support transformative, participatory knowing
- Embodied practices (including breathwork) engage relevance realization at a pre-reflective, somatic level

## Citation

(Authors, 2026). Development of interoception: the critical role of maternal affective touch in early attachment. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2026. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2026.1748486/full

## Key Findings

- Early attachment relationships via maternal affective touch (MAT) provide the critical developmental context in which interoceptive signals are integrated into CNS structures.
- Interoceptive accuracy is "learned" in infancy through relational, embodied interactions — not simply innate.
- Disrupted early MAT is proposed as a developmental pathway to interoceptive and autonomic dysregulation in adulthood, with direct implications for anxiety, depression, eating disorders, and trauma-related disorders.
- This is a theoretical/review paper synthesizing evidence across developmental neuroscience, attachment theory, and interoception research.

## Relevance to SRL

Grounds interoceptive literacy as a developmentally acquired skill, not a fixed trait — meaning it can be trained and rehabilitated. Directly supports SRL's Interoceptive Literacy curriculum as remediation of a foundational developmental capacity. Also supports Co-Regulation as a mechanism of interoceptive development, suggesting clinician-patient co-regulatory environments may function analogously to early MAT contexts.

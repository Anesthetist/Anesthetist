Shrier investigates the rapid-onset gender dysphoria phenomenon among adolescent girls, examining social influence vectors and institutional capture in medicine. The book raises questions about informed consent, diagnostic integrity, and the boundary between clinical care and cultural pressure.

**Somnistic Relevance**: The core tension -- when institutional medicine drifts from evidence-based practice under social pressure -- is directly relevant to Pausality's mission of restoring clinical rigor. The social contagion thesis also maps to co-regulation research: peer influence on identity formation operates through the same somatic and relational channels that drive group stress responses.

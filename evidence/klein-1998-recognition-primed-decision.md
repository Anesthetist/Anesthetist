# Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions

## Summary

Gary Klein's (1998) foundational work on **Recognition-Primed Decision Making (RPD)** emerged from studying how experts actually make decisions under time pressure, uncertainty, and high stakes — fireground commanders, ICU nurses, military officers, and chess masters. Rather than the rational-analytic model (weigh options, compare alternatives), Klein found that experts generate a single course of action through pattern recognition and mentally simulate it. If the simulation works, they act; if not, they modify or generate another option.

## Key Contributions

- **RPD Model**: Experts do not compare options; they recognize situations as familiar patterns and act on the first viable option generated through experience
- Identified three variations: simple match (pattern → action), developing situation assessment (story building), and mental simulation of action courses
- Demonstrated that expertise resides in **perceptual skills** — the ability to see what novices cannot — rather than superior reasoning
- Showed that time pressure often *improves* expert decision-making by forcing reliance on pattern recognition rather than deliberation
- Introduced "naturalistic decision making" as a research paradigm studying decisions in real-world contexts rather than laboratory settings

## Methodological Notes

- Critical Decision Method (CDM) interviews with domain experts
- Qualitative, field-based research — criticized by some for lack of experimental control
- Validated across multiple domains: firefighting, military, nursing, aviation
- Complemented by Kahneman's dual-process theory (System 1/System 2) — RPD maps to expert System 1

## Design Constraint

**If a user must compare options during a Gap Moment, the system has failed. Output must be single-action, pattern-matched, not analytical.**

## SRL Relevance

RPD is the cognitive mechanism underlying [[embodied-clinical-intelligence]]. When a CRNA "just knows" something is wrong before the monitors alarm, they are executing recognition-primed decision making — matching the current physiological gestalt against thousands of stored patterns from prior cases. This is precisely what [[anterocept]] captures: the pre-conscious pattern recognition that generates a clinical intuition before it reaches explicit awareness.

Klein's work validates that expert clinical judgment is not mystical — it is trainable pattern recognition. SRL's approach to building [[interoceptive-literacy]] and [[attention-as-gain-control]] is essentially the development of the perceptual skills Klein describes as the foundation of expert performance.

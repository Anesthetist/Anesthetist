## Summary

Large-scale study quantifying the incidence and impact of cognitive errors during difficult airway management, identifying error types and their association with complications.

## Key Findings

- **Sample:** n=2,801 difficult airway cases
- **Error prevalence:** Cognitive errors occurred in 17.4% of cases
- **Error types:** Fixation errors (11.5%), omission bias (5.9%), overconfidence (4.5%)
- **Impact — single error:** Adjusted OR 1.86 for complications
- **Impact — multiple errors:** Adjusted OR 2.48 for complications
- **Provider effect:** Non-anaesthesiologists had the highest odds of cognitive errors
- **Implication:** Cognitive errors are common, measurable, and directly linked to patient harm in high-stakes airway scenarios

## SRL Relevance

Quantifies the cost of cognitive errors in precisely the clinical domain SRL targets (anesthesia/airway management). Fixation errors (11.5%) are the Gap Moment failure mode — the inability to transition from a failing plan. The aOR 1.86/2.48 dose-response provides concrete harm metrics for enterprise safety arguments.

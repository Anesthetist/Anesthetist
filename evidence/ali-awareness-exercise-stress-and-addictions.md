## Overview

A two-page awareness exercise mapping the relationship between unmet needs, stress-induced regression, and addictive behavioral patterns. The exercise provides a four-quadrant needs model with corresponding shadow addictions, then guides participants through an upgrade pathway — replacing addictive patterns with more positive need-meeting strategies.

## Key Framework

### Four Basic Needs (Positive Expression)
- **Approval and Belonging** ↔ **Significance and Achievement**
- **Pleasure and Variety** ↔ **Safety and Certainty**

### Four Shadow Addictions (Negative Expression Under Stress)
- **Conformance and Inferiority**: people pleasing, self-deprecation, victim-playing
- **Dominance and Superiority**: aggression, bullying, putting others down
- **Craving and Avoidance**: substances, food, shopping, screen time, pornography
- **Control and Manipulation**: micromanaging, withholding, passive-aggression

### Core Mechanism
Under stress, we regress. The brain downshifts from neocortex to limbic/reptile, emotions become more primitive, and the nervous system begins using addictive patterns to get needs met. The four addictions are the nervous system's automatic attempt to meet needs that are not being met through positive, sustainable means.

### Three-Step Protocol

**Step 1**: Identify which of the four addictions you default to under stress
**Step 2**: Identify the felt experience — what happens in your body and behavior under stress
**Step 3**: Upgrade rather than fight. Rate how well each of your four basic needs (Safety/Certainty, Pleasure/Variety, Approval/Belonging, Significance/Achievement) is being met on a 1–10 scale during stress episodes. Find more positive ways to meet the deficient needs.

### Key Insight
The best way to release an addiction is not by fighting it but by upgrading it — identifying which needs are being sacrificed and finding healthier ways to meet them.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Stress Regression as Autonomic Downshift

Whetten's description of stress-induced regression maps precisely to the polyvagal hierarchy: under threat, the nervous system descends from ventral vagal (social engagement / neocortex) through sympathetic (fight-flight / limbic) to dorsal vagal (freeze-collapse / reptile). The four addictions correspond to specific autonomic survival strategies: Dominance = sympathetic fight, Craving/Avoidance = sympathetic flight + dorsal freeze cycling, Control = sympathetic with dorsal immobilization, Conformance = dorsal fawn response.

### Clinician Durability and Addictive Coping

The exercise has direct relevance to CRNA burnout and clinician durability. Healthcare workers under chronic stress develop predictable addictive coping patterns — the CRNA who micromanages (Control), the one who emotionally withdraws (Craving/Avoidance), the one who becomes aggressive with colleagues (Dominance), or the one who over-accommodates at personal expense (Conformance). Identifying one's default addiction pattern is the first step toward sustainable clinical resilience.

### Needs-Based Assessment as Interoceptive Instrument

The Step 3 needs rating scale (Safety, Pleasure, Approval, Significance on 1–10) functions as a rapid interoceptive self-check. This is a non-instrumented version of what Pausality's sensor stack could eventually automate — continuous monitoring of needs-state as inferred from autonomic markers.

### Upgrade vs. Fight: The Titration Principle

The core insight — upgrade rather than fight — parallels the SRL titration-to-effect principle. Fighting an addictive pattern increases sympathetic arousal and strengthens the pattern. Upgrading it acknowledges the legitimate need underneath and provides a less costly pathway to meet it. This is constraint release rather than constraint suppression.

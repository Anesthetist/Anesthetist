## Overview

Yoo, Mather et al. (2022) conducted a randomized controlled trial examining whether 5 weeks of daily HRV biofeedback could produce measurable changes in brain structure and autonomic function in older adults. Using MRI volumetric analysis, the study demonstrated that oscillation-enhancing biofeedback (Osc+) increased cortical volume in the left orbitofrontal cortex and improved resting HRV metrics.

## Key Findings

1. **Randomized controlled trial of 5 weeks daily HRV biofeedback** in older adults, comparing Osc+ (oscillation-enhancing) vs Osc- (oscillation-suppressing) conditions.
2. **Osc+ increased left orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) cortical volume**, demonstrating structural neuroplasticity from a breathing-based intervention.
3. **Volume changes in OFC correlated with mood improvement**, linking structural brain changes to clinically meaningful affective outcomes.
4. **Resting low-frequency HRV (LF-HRV) increased in the Osc+ group**, indicating improved autonomic function that persisted beyond active biofeedback sessions.
5. **HRV biofeedback reshapes brain structure in older adults**, establishing that autonomic training can drive neuroplastic changes even in aging brains.

## Connection to Somnistics

Structural neuroplasticity from breathwork validates [[interoception|interoceptive]] training at any age. The OFC is a critical hub for emotion regulation, decision-making, and interoceptive integration — exactly the neural real estate SRL protocols aim to strengthen. Combined with the Min/Mather (2023) amyloid findings, this establishes the Mather lab's body of work as foundational evidence for SRL's neuroprotective claims. Five weeks maps well to SRL's engagement and retention timeline.

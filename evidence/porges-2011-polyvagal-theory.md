# The Polyvagal Theory (Porges 2011)

## Summary

Stephen Porges's polyvagal theory provides a phylogenetic framework for understanding the autonomic nervous system as a three-tiered hierarchy rather than a simple sympathetic/parasympathetic dichotomy. The newest circuit — the myelinated ventral vagal complex — supports social engagement, calm states, and health-growth-restoration behaviors. When neuroception detects threat, the system defaults to sympathetic fight/flight, and under extreme threat, to the evolutionarily oldest dorsal vagal shutdown. This framework is foundational to SRL's approach: Pausality trains users to access the ventral vagal state through breath-mediated vagal activation, and the concept of neuroception (subconscious threat detection) underpins much of the clinical logic for why conscious breathing interventions work.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- The autonomic nervous system operates as a three-tier hierarchy, not a two-branch dichotomy
- The ventral vagal complex (myelinated vagus) supports social engagement, calm, and self-regulation
- Neuroception — the subconscious detection of safety or threat — governs autonomic state shifts
- Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) is a measurable index of ventral vagal function
- Slow, rhythmic breathing directly stimulates the ventral vagal pathway
- The social engagement system links face, voice, and breath — all mediated by cranial nerves branching from the ventral vagal complex
- Safety is a prerequisite for autonomic flexibility and higher-order cognition

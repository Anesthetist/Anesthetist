## Summary

Phenomenographic study exploring nurse anesthetists' perceptions of professional learning throughout their careers.

## Key Findings

- Four categories of professional learning identified: practicing as clinician, developing comprehensive understanding, developing situational awareness, trusting own competence
- Experience alone is insufficient — courage to trust own competence is essential for expert practice
- Situational awareness development is a continuous, career-long process
- Professional learning in anesthesia is deeply embodied and experiential, not purely cognitive

## Relevance to SRL

The four categories map remarkably well to SRL concepts: situational awareness aligns with interoceptive literacy, trusting own competence aligns with Gap Moment Training's goal of building reliable internal sensing. The finding that learning is embodied and experiential (not just cognitive) validates the somnistics approach of training physiological awareness as a clinical skill. Supports the case for CEU-worthy professional development that includes autonomic regulation training.

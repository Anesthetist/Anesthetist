# Interoception Network in the Rat Brain (Alkaabi, Wang & Liu 2026)

## Summary

Published as a *bioRxiv* preprint (February 2026), this study used resting-state fMRI in rats to identify a cohesive "interoception network" — a set of brain regions whose functional connectivity depends on intact vagal signaling. Bilateral cervical vagotomy significantly reduced functional connectivity within this network, providing direct causal evidence that resting-state brain connectivity relies on interoceptive signaling through the vagus nerve.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- A discrete "interoception network" exists as a cohesive resting-state brain network
- Vagus nerve integrity is required for maintaining this network's functional connectivity
- Vagotomy causally disrupts interoceptive brain connectivity — not just correlational
- Resting-state brain organization fundamentally depends on ongoing interoceptive input
- Provides animal model evidence complementing human interoception research

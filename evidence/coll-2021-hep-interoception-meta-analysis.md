## Overview
Coll et al. present a systematic review and meta-analysis of 45 studies examining the heartbeat-evoked potential (HEP) as a neural marker of interoception. The analysis finds moderate-to-large effects of attention and arousal on HEP amplitude, and that clinical status modulates the signal. However, it also identifies a major methodological concern: cardiac field artifact contamination across many studies, and the absence of a standardized HEP protocol.

## Key Findings
1. Moderate-to-large effects of attention and arousal on HEP amplitude — the brain's cortical response to heartbeats is modulated by cognitive and affective state
2. Clinical status modulates HEP — psychiatric and neurological conditions show altered heartbeat-evoked cortical processing
3. Major confound identified: cardiac field artifact contaminates many published HEP studies, potentially inflating effect sizes
4. No standardized HEP protocol exists across the field, making cross-study comparison difficult
5. HEP is promising as an objective neural biomarker of interoception but needs significant methodological rigor before clinical adoption

## Connection to Somnistics
The HEP could become an objective biomarker of interoceptive training effectiveness — a neural signal showing that SRL's interventions change how the brain processes heartbeat signals. However, the field needs standardization first. This represents an opportunity for SRL to contribute to protocol standardization as part of building the Clinical LLM Moat (Red Arrow 4), positioning SRL as a methodological leader in interoceptive measurement.

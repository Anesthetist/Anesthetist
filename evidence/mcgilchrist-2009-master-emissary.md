# The Master and His Emissary (McGilchrist 2009)

## Summary

Iain McGilchrist, a psychiatrist and literary scholar, presents extensive neuroimaging, neuropsychological, and philosophical evidence that the brain's hemispheres do not merely divide tasks (language left, spatial right) but represent two fundamentally different modes of attending to the world. The right hemisphere maintains broad, vigilant, contextual, embodied awareness — it sees the whole and grasps the novel. The left hemisphere provides focused, categorical, decontextualized, sequential processing — it re-presents what the right hemisphere has already apprehended. McGilchrist argues that the right hemisphere is the "master" and the left the "emissary," but culture has allowed the emissary to usurp the master's role. For SRL, this framework explains why breathwork and interoceptive practices — which engage right-hemispheric, embodied, present-moment processing — can counteract the hyper-analytical, disembodied states that characterize chronic stress and burnout.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- The right hemisphere maintains broad, vigilant attention essential for detecting context and novelty
- The left hemisphere provides narrow, focused attention suited for manipulation and categorization
- Embodied, present-moment awareness is primarily a right-hemispheric function
- Chronic stress and cognitive overload may reflect left-hemispheric dominance — loss of the embodied, contextual frame
- Breathwork and somatic practices may function as hemispheric rebalancing interventions
- Attention is not a single resource but two qualitatively different modes of world-engagement

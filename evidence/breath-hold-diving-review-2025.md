# Advances in Breath-Hold Diving Research: A State-of-the-Art Review

## Citation
*European Journal of Applied Physiology*. 2025.
DOI: 10.1007/s00421-025-06093-6

## Scope
Structured literature search of PubMed and Scopus from 2005–2025. Selected peer-reviewed studies on physiological, biochemical, and biomechanical aspects of breath-hold diving. Thematic analysis identified **eight major research areas**.

## Key Findings by Domain

### 1. Cardiovascular
- Autonomic regulation adaptations: enhanced bradycardia, splenic contraction (releasing stored RBCs)
- More experienced divers show more pronounced bradycardia and larger blood pressure increases (stronger diving reflex)
- **Individual variability remains an open question** — the field lacks longitudinal individual-level data

### 2. Pulmonary
- Lung compression responses, gas exchange inefficiencies
- Potential risks of lung barotrauma in extreme depths
- Apneas of identical duration result in less arterial O2 desaturation after training → prolonged O2 conservation via enhanced diving response

### 3. Neurological
- Hypoxia-induced syncope risks
- Cerebral blood flow changes — elite divers show greater cerebral blood flow increases and higher cerebrovascular reactivity
- Emerging evidence of neurovascular damage in elite/repetitive divers (safety boundary)

### 4. Decompression Stress
- Once considered negligible, now recognized in elite and repetitive divers
- Relevant safety consideration for training protocol design

### 5. Metabolic
- Shift from aerobic to anaerobic energy supply during extended apnea
- Decreased chemosensitivity during exercise
- Distinct ventilatory-response pattern during exercise vs. scuba divers and controls

### 6. Training Effects
- Training duration and specific methods affect physiological parameters differently
- Chronic adaptations: expanded CO2 tolerance through both acute physiological responses and chronic neuroplastic changes

### 7. Long-term Adaptations
- Persistent autonomic changes outside diving context
- Enhanced vagal tone at rest
- Modified chemoreflex sensitivity

### 8. Technology
- Advances in underwater monitoring and real-time physiological measurement

## SRL Relevance

**The individual variability gap is SRL's commercial opening.** This review explicitly flags that individual-level longitudinal data is lacking in the field. Pausality is designed to generate exactly this data — tracking autonomic signatures (HRV, breath-hold duration, cognitive variability) across time, case types, and shift patterns.

**The safety boundaries matter for protocol design.** Neurovascular damage in elite/repetitive divers means SRL's breath-hold training protocols need to stay well within the hormetic zone. This validates the [[minimum-effective-dose]] philosophy: train CO2 tolerance at the MED, not at the competitive freediving edge.

**The persistent autonomic changes validate SRL's compounding model.** Training effects persist outside the diving context — exactly the transfer effect SRL needs for clinical durability (train during gap moments, benefit during high-stakes cases).

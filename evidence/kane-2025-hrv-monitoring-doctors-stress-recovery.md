## Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis examining continuous heart rate variability (HRV) monitoring as a measure of stress and recovery in medical doctors.

## Key Findings

- Meta-analysis of 7 studies confirmed significant HRV differences between stress and recovery periods in doctors
- Metrics showing significant differences: RMSSD, SDNN, LF, LF/HF ratio
- Continuous ambulatory HRV monitoring is viable for tracking stress/recovery cycles that contribute to burnout
- Supports the use of wearable HRV as an objective biomarker of clinician stress load

## Relevance to SRL

Provides systematic-review-level evidence that continuous HRV monitoring can objectively track the stress-recovery dynamics underlying clinician burnout. Directly supports the case for wearable-integrated interventions like Gap Moment Training and NeuroMinute protocols.

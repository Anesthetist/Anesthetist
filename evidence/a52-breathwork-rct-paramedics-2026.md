## Citation
Little et al. (2026). Examining the Effectiveness of Breathwork to Improve Resilience and Psychological Wellbeing While Reducing Anxiety, Depression, Stress, and Insomnia in Paramedicine Students: A Single-Blind Randomised Controlled Trial. Stress and Health, Wiley. DOI: 10.1002/smi.70161

## Structured Abstract

**Background:** Paramedicine students face high occupational stress similar to healthcare workers. The A52 Breath Method (5-second inhale, 5-second exhale, 2-second hold) represents a structured slow diaphragmatic breathing protocol.

**Methods:** Single-blind parallel-group mixed-methods RCT with 98 student paramedics from two Australian universities. Intervention: twice-daily practice of the A52 method with video and guided audio instruction.

**Results:** At post-intervention, the breathwork group reported significantly lower stress, anxiety, and depression scores, and higher resilience compared to controls. Insomnia symptoms also improved.

**Conclusion:** Structured slow breathing at approximately 5 breaths/minute significantly improves multiple psychological outcomes in high-stress occupational trainees.

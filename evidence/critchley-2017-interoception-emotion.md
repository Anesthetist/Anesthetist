# Interoception and Emotion (Critchley & Garfinkel 2017)

## Summary

Reviews the bidirectional relationship between interoception and emotion: interoceptive signals (heartbeat, breathing, gut) shape emotional experience, and emotional states alter how interoceptive signals are processed. Anxiety specifically amplifies interoceptive signals, creating a feedback loop. Heartbeat-evoked potentials (HEP) provide a neural measure of how the brain processes cardiac signals.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Interoception and emotion are bidirectional (not just body→brain)
- Anxiety amplifies interoceptive signals — explains why anxious clinicians feel "worse" during training initially
- HEP as neural marker validates the Harvard/Sacchet finding of HEP tracking meditation depth
- Training interoception directly modulates emotional processing (not indirectly)

# PEAR Lab Random Event Generator Studies (1979–2007)

## Citation

Jahn, R. G., & Dunne, B. J. (2005). The PEAR Proposition. *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, 19(2), 195–245. Also: Jahn, R. G. (1982). "The persistent paradox of psychic phenomena: An engineering perspective." *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 70(2), 136–170.

## Summary

The Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research Lab (PEAR) operated from 1979 to 2007 in Princeton University's School of Engineering and Applied Science, founded by Robert G. Jahn (then Dean of Engineering). Over nearly three decades, PEAR conducted extensive experiments examining whether human consciousness could influence the output of random event generators (REGs) and other physical systems.

## Key Findings

- Human operators showed small but statistically significant ability to shift REG output distributions in intended directions
- Effects were consistent across operators, devices, and experimental protocols
- Remote intention (operator not physically present) produced comparable effect sizes to local intention
- Group consciousness effects were observed — multiple operators or shared attention amplified results
- The Global Consciousness Project (GCP), an outgrowth of PEAR started by Roger Nelson in 1998, scaled these findings to a worldwide network of REGs and found correlations with mass events

## Institutional Legacy

- PEAR closed in 2007, but its research tradition continues through IONS (Dean Radin), the GCP (now hosted by IONS), the Mind Matter Mapping Project, and Adam Curry's Entangled company
- The Mind Lamp and Entangled app are direct commercial descendants of PEAR's REG research

## Relevance to SRL

- Establishes that consciousness-matter interaction is measurable and replicable under controlled conditions
- Provides the empirical foundation for the concept that intention is an engineerable force
- The scaling of individual effects to collective effects (GCP) parallels the Somnistics vision of individual practice scaling to organizational performance
- Supports [[nonlocal-consciousness]], [[retrocausal-presentiment]], and [[gamma-state-binding]] as concepts grounded in experimental data, not speculation

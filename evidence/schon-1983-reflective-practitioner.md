# The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action

## Summary

Donald Schön (1983) challenged the dominant "technical rationality" model of professional knowledge — the idea that professionals apply theoretical knowledge to practical problems. Instead, he argued that professional expertise operates through **knowing-in-action** (tacit knowledge embedded in skilled performance) and **reflection-in-action** (thinking about what you are doing while you are doing it, adjusting in real time).

## Key Contributions

- **Knowing-in-action**: The tacit, embodied knowledge that professionals deploy without conscious deliberation — the "feel" of the work
- **Reflection-in-action**: The capacity to think about and adjust performance *during* the activity itself, not just afterward — a metacognitive skill that distinguishes expert practitioners
- **Reflection-on-action**: Post-hoc analysis of performance — necessary for learning but insufficient for real-time expertise
- Critique of **technical rationality**: The prevailing model that professional knowledge is simply applied science fails to account for the messy, indeterminate, value-laden problems that professionals actually face
- Introduced the concept of the "reflective conversation with the situation" — professionals frame and reframe problems as they work

## Methodological Notes

- Qualitative case studies across architecture, psychotherapy, engineering, management, and urban planning
- Theoretical/philosophical rather than empirical — provides a framework, not quantitative data
- Deeply influential in nursing education, medical education, and teacher education
- Connects to Polanyi's tacit knowledge, Dewey's experiential learning

## SRL Relevance

Schön's framework is foundational to understanding why [[tacit-knowledge-extraction]] is both essential and difficult. The knowing-in-action that experienced CRNAs possess is precisely what SRL aims to make trainable through structured interoceptive protocols. Reflection-in-action maps directly to the [[embodied-metacognition]] that SRL cultivates — the ability to monitor your own physiological and cognitive state while performing clinical tasks.

The Gap Moment Training™ framework is essentially a technology for developing reflection-in-action capacity: noticing the gap between stimulus and response, which requires real-time self-monitoring during high-stakes performance.

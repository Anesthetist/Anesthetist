## Citation

Vickers, J.N. (2007). *Perception, Cognition, and Decision Training: The Quiet Eye in Action.* Human Kinetics. ISBN: 978-0736042567.

## Key Findings

- The Quiet Eye (QE) is the final fixation on a target before motor execution, lasting 300ms to 3+ seconds
- Expert athletes exhibit significantly longer QE durations than novices across sports including basketball, golf, biathlon, and surgery
- Longer QE duration predicts successful motor execution. Shorter QE predicts errors
- QE is trainable at all skill levels through gaze-tracking feedback and deliberate practice
- The QE period appears to be when the brain consolidates perceptual information and prepares the motor program. It is a temporal window of focused processing before action

## Relevance to SRL

The Quiet Eye is a temporal intervention that maps directly onto Gap Moment Training. Both involve extending a perceptual window before action. The QE lasts 300ms to 3+ seconds, overlapping with Wittmann's experienced moment (~2-3 seconds). The CRNA's pause before induction, the breath before a difficult airway, the moment of stillness before pushing a drug: these are clinical quiet eyes.

Vickers trained the QE through external gaze feedback. SRL trains the equivalent through interoceptive feedback. Different sensory channel, same temporal structure: extend the window of focused processing before the critical action.

The QE research also validates that temporal training transfers from lab to performance. Athletes trained in QE perform better in competition, not just in drills. This supports SRL's thesis that temporal literacy trained in NeuroMinutes transfers to clinical performance.

## Methodology

Two decades of gaze-tracking research across multiple sports. Eye-tracking glasses measure fixation duration and location during performance. Controlled comparisons between experts and novices, and between successful and unsuccessful attempts within individuals.

## Citation

Wittmann, M. (2016). *Felt Time: The Science of How We Experience Time.* MIT Press. ISBN: 978-0262529266. (Translated from the German *Gefühlte Zeit*, 2014.)

## Summary

Wittmann's foundational popular science book presenting his embodied time theory for a general audience. Synthesizes his research program into a coherent narrative: time perception is not an abstract cognitive process but is grounded in the body — in heartbeats, breathing, visceral feelings, and the continuous stream of interoceptive signals processed by the insular cortex.

## Key Themes

- **Why time flies and why it drags:** Emotional states, attention, and body awareness modulate subjective duration. Boredom stretches time; flow compresses it.
- **The body clock:** Interoceptive signals create the raw material for temporal experience. People with better body awareness have more accurate time perception.
- **The 3-second window:** The "experienced present" — the temporal window of conscious nowness — spans approximately 2-3 seconds across cultures and sensory modalities.
- **Time and aging:** Why years seem to accelerate with age — reduced novel memory encoding, not routine or boredom (a finding later confirmed empirically in Teghil & Wittmann 2025/2026).
- **Mindfulness and time:** Meditation practices that heighten body awareness also heighten temporal awareness — and paradoxically, heightened awareness of the present moment can slow the subjective passage of time.
- **Waiting and boredom:** The experience of waiting reveals the embodied nature of time — when we wait, we become acutely aware of our body, and time expands.

## Relevance to SRL

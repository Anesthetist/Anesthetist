## Citation

Flatten, C., Linares Gutiérrez, D., Kübel, S. L., Taylor, S., & Wittmann, M. (2024). Increased wakefulness as measured by the WAKE-16 is related to mindfulness and emotional self-regulation in experienced Buddhist meditators. *Progress in Brain Research*, 287, 287–307. [DOI: 10.1016/bs.pbr.2024.04.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.pbr.2024.04.005)

## Key Findings

- Validated the German version of the WAKE-16 — a new instrument measuring "wakefulness" defined as "an expansive, higher-functioning, and stable state of being in which a person's vision of and relationship to the world are transformed"
- Expert meditators (n=36, predominantly silent meditation) scored significantly higher than matched non-meditators (n=36) on WAKE-16, confirming construct validity
- Meditators scored higher on mindfulness subscales "presence" and "acceptance," and on emotion regulation subscales
- Within meditators, wakefulness correlated significantly with: mindfulness, accepting one's own emotions, and experiencing overwhelming emotions
- In non-meditators, only accepting one's own emotions correlated with wakefulness

## Relevance to SRL

- **Wakefulness as measurable construct:** The WAKE-16 offers a potential outcome measure for SRL's consciousness development goals — beyond HRV and MAIA-2
- **Maps to SRL's developmental arc:** Taylor's wakefulness construct (2017) describes something beyond mindfulness — a stable trait transformation. This parallels SRL's Fortifying phase in [[gmt-career-arc]]
- **Emotion regulation link:** Wakefulness isn't detachment — it includes acceptance of overwhelming emotions. This aligns with SRL's framework of regulation, not suppression
- **Silent meditation practitioners:** The meditators used silent practice, paralleling the breath-based, non-verbal approach of NeuroMinutes
- **Potential assessment instrument** for SCP-Instructor level ([[somnistics-certification]]) — measuring stable wakefulness beyond mere skill competency

## Methodology

Cross-sectional comparison study. Expert meditators (predominantly silent meditation) vs. demographically matched non-meditators. Validated WAKE-16 against mindfulness (FMI) and emotion regulation (SEE) instruments.

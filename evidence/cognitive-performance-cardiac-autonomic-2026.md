## Citation

Authors (2026). From brain to heart: cognitive performance shapes exercise-induced cardiac autonomic trajectories in older adults. *Frontiers in Medicine*.

- **DOI:** 10.3389/fmed.2026.1761743
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1761743/full

## Key Findings

1. **Higher cognitive scores correlate with more favorable autonomic profiles at rest**
2. During exercise, better cognition is linked to **greater HF power and larger reductions in LF and VLF** from rest to effort — indicating enhanced autonomic flexibility
3. Better cognitive performance associates with **greater adaptability of cardiac autonomic regulation** during functional tests
4. The relationship is bidirectional: cognitive function and autonomic cardiovascular control are linked via the **central autonomic network** (prefrontal cortex, insula, anterior cingulate cortex, amygdala, hypothalamus, brainstem)

## SRL Relevance

**Strongly supports SRL's Embodied Clinical Intelligence thesis:**

- Demonstrates that cognitive performance and autonomic regulation are not separate domains but deeply coupled systems
- Validates the concept that training autonomic flexibility (via biofeedback/breathwork) may simultaneously enhance cognitive performance
- The bidirectional link via the central autonomic network maps directly to SRL's Neuro-Ouroboros concept
- Supports Pausality's positioning: by improving autonomic regulation, clinicians may enhance cognitive performance under stress — not just reduce burnout
- The "autonomic flexibility" finding directly supports the concept that HRV variability (not just high HRV) predicts adaptive performance

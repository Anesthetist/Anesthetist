## Overview

Jester et al. (2018) evaluated the cognitive and psychiatric effects of a brief HRV biofeedback intervention in older adults. The study used a 3-week protocol consisting of just 6 sessions, measuring depression, anxiety, attention, and sleep quality. Despite the minimal dosing, the intervention produced large effect sizes across multiple outcome domains.

## Key Findings

1. **3-week intervention with only 6 sessions**, testing the lower bound of effective HRV biofeedback dosing in older adults.
2. **Large effect on depression (d=1.02)**, demonstrating clinically meaningful mood improvement from a brief autonomic intervention.
3. **Large effect on anxiety (d=0.82)**, indicating substantial anxiolytic benefit from HRV biofeedback in this population.
4. **Large effect on attention (d=1.00)**, revealing cognitive enhancement alongside affective improvements.
5. **Improved sleep quality**, adding a fourth outcome domain responsive to this minimal-dose protocol.

## Connection to Somnistics

Validates minimum effective dose in older adults — 3 weeks and 6 sessions produced large effects across depression, anxiety, attention, and sleep. This maps directly to the NeuroMinute™ engagement model and SRL's titration-to-effect philosophy. The effect sizes (d > 0.8 across domains) at such low dose are remarkable and support the business case that brief, accessible interventions can produce clinically meaningful outcomes.

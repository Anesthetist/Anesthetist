# Protective and Damaging Effects of Stress Mediators

Bruce S. McEwen (1998). *New England Journal of Medicine*, 338(3), 171-179. PMID: 9535661.

## Core Framework

Stress mediators (cortisol, catecholamines, cytokines) are protective in the short term — they mobilize energy, sharpen attention, and prepare the body for challenge. But when these same mediators are chronically elevated or fail to shut off, they cause cumulative physiological damage: allostatic load.

## Four Pathways to Allostatic Overload

1. **Repeated hits:** Frequent stressors that prevent recovery (e.g., back-to-back critical cases)
2. **Failure to habituate:** Inability to adapt to repeated similar stressors (each case feels as threatening as the first)
3. **Failure to shut off:** Stress response persists after the stressor ends (post-shift rumination, sleep disruption)
4. **Inadequate response:** Insufficient stress response leads to compensatory overactivation of other systems

## SRL Relevance

Allostatic load operationalizes what "burnout" actually IS at the physiological level. CRNA career longevity depends on managing allostatic load through recovery practices, social safety, and meaning-making. The [[hormesis-durability-model]] requires this paper as its counterpoint — hormesis is the beneficial dose-response; allostatic load is the cost when dosing exceeds recovery capacity.

# Volitional Control of Neuromagnetic Coherence (Sacchet et al. 2012)

## Summary

This early Sacchet paper demonstrates that individuals can learn to volitionally control neuromagnetic coherence — the degree of synchronization between oscillatory activity in different brain regions — using MEG-based neurofeedback. Participants received real-time feedback about coherence between targeted brain areas and learned to increase or decrease this coherence through mental strategies. This is foundational for the neurogating concept: if brain coherence patterns are trainable rather than fixed, then breathwork and meditation practices that alter coherence are doing something learnable and optimizable. The paper establishes that the brain's functional integration is under a degree of volitional control, providing early evidence for the trainability of the neural states that meditation and breathwork target.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Neuromagnetic coherence (inter-regional brain synchrony) can be volitionally controlled
- MEG-based neurofeedback enables learning of coherence modulation
- Brain coherence patterns are trainable, not fixed traits
- Participants develop idiosyncratic mental strategies for coherence control
- The finding supports the trainability of neural states targeted by meditation and breathwork
- Coherence modulation may underlie the mechanism by which meditation alters brain network function
- Real-time feedback accelerates learning of self-regulation skills

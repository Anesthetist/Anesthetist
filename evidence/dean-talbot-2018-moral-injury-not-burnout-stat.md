# Moral Injury, Not Burnout (Dean & Talbot, STAT 2018)

## Summary

A short STAT News commentary (July 26, 2018) by Wendy Dean, MD, and Simon Talbot, MD, that became the most-cited reframe of clinician distress of the past decade. Core claim:

> "Physicians aren't burning out. They're suffering from moral injury."

**Burnout** locates the problem in the individual — exhaustion, cynicism, loss of efficacy — and therefore suggests individual-level fixes (resilience training, self-care, mindfulness).

**Moral injury** (a term borrowed from military psychiatry, originally describing combat trauma of knowingly harming or failing to protect noncombatants) locates the problem in the *system*: the clinician is repeatedly forced to act in ways that violate their own professional conscience — by documentation burden, productivity pressure, prior authorization, understaffing, short-term financial incentives.

Dean and Talbot's move is load-bearing because it shifts the explanatory burden off the clinician and onto the care environment, which reframes every downstream intervention.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- The "wellness" frame is the wrong address for the distress. Individual resilience training is directed at the wrong variable if moral injury is the mechanism.
- The CRNA literature has not yet imported this frame. Nagelhout 7th ed. treats provider distress as burnout. No AANA Journal review article as of 2026 applies the Dean/Talbot reframe to nurse anesthesia distress.
- For SRL this matters because it distinguishes interoceptive training ([[gap-moment-training]], [[neurominute]]) as *sensor calibration inside a moral-injury environment* rather than as *resilience training to prevent burnout*. The former is compatible with system critique; the latter quietly lets the system off the hook.

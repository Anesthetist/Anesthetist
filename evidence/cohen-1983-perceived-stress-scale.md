## Overview

The Perceived Stress Scale (PSS) is the most widely used self-report instrument for measuring the degree to which situations in one's life are appraised as stressful. Available in 14-item (PSS-14) and 10-item (PSS-10) versions, it assesses how unpredictable, uncontrollable, and overloaded respondents find their lives. It is free to use and universally recognized across research and clinical domains.

## Key Findings

1. PSS-10 (10 items) and PSS-14 (14 items) versions available; PSS-10 is most commonly used due to slightly better psychometric properties
2. Measures the degree to which life situations are appraised as stressful — a subjective, perceptual construct rather than objective stressor count
3. Most widely used stress self-report instrument globally, with translations in dozens of languages
4. Free to use with no licensing requirements
5. Sensitive to change with intervention, making it suitable for pre/post outcome measurement

## Connection to Somnistics

The PSS-10 is essential for every SRL study and product outcome measurement battery. It is free, takes approximately 3 minutes, and is universally recognized by clinicians, researchers, and IRBs. Including PSS-10 in all [[clinician-durability]] measurement protocols ensures comparability with the broader stress-intervention literature and provides a credible, low-burden outcome metric for enterprise pilots.

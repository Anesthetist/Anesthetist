## Summary
Dobrushina and Amedi (2024) compared the effectiveness of haptic (vibrotactile) versus visual heartbeat feedback for improving interoceptive accuracy in a single-session training paradigm. Participants who received real-time haptic feedback of their heartbeat showed significantly greater improvement in heartbeat detection accuracy compared to those receiving visual feedback or no feedback. The findings suggest that somatosensory channels may provide a more natural and effective route for interoceptive training than visual displays.

## Key Finding for SRL
This study provides direct experimental support for SRL's wearable-first design philosophy. Haptic feedback outperforming visual feedback validates the approach of using vibrotactile cues (e.g., wrist-worn devices delivering breathing pace or heartbeat signals) rather than screen-based dashboards. A single session producing measurable improvement also supports SRL's "minimum effective dose" framing for consumer and clinical products.

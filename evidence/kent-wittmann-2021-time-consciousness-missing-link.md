## Citation

Kent, L., & Wittmann, M. (2021). Time consciousness: The missing link in theories of consciousness. *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2021(1), niab011. [DOI: 10.1093/nc/niab011](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niab011)

## Key Findings

- Many dominant theories of consciousness only reference brief, static, discrete "functional moments" of time (~30-50ms snapshots)
- Very few theories address the extended, dynamic, continuous temporal flow associated with actual conscious experience — the "experienced moment" (~2-3 seconds)
- This confusion between short/discrete and long/continuous is a **core unresolved issue** in consciousness theories
- Consciousness extends in time: experience flows through a succession of moments and progresses from future predictions, to present experiences, to past memories
- Time consciousness could serve as a novel testing ground for rival theories of consciousness — it may differentiate between theories that otherwise make similar predictions
- Different theories may be compatible/complementary once different aspects of time are properly accounted for

## The Temporal Gap in Consciousness Theories

| Theory | Handles Functional Moment? | Handles Experienced Moment? |
|--------|---------------------------|----------------------------|

## Relevance to SRL

- **Consciousness engineering** in SRL's framework must account for temporal experience — Wittmann argues it's not a side feature but potentially THE missing link
- **The 60-second NeuroMinute** is a temporally structured consciousness intervention. Kent & Wittmann's analysis suggests SRL should frame it not just as "breath work" but as "temporal consciousness training"
- **Gap moments** are interventions in the experienced present — the ~2-3 second window where conscious experience has its maximum temporal extension. This is where SRL trains clinicians to intervene
- **State transitions** are temporal phenomena: the shift from one autonomic state to another unfolds across experienced moments. Theories that only handle functional moments miss the transition dynamics entirely
- SRL may have empirical data (HRV during NeuroMinutes, MAIA-2 scores pre/post) that could contribute to resolving the theoretical questions Kent & Wittmann raise

## Methodology

Theoretical review examining how major theories of consciousness handle temporal aspects of experience. Compares functional moment approaches with experienced moment frameworks. Proposes that time consciousness could serve as a discriminating test for rival consciousness theories.

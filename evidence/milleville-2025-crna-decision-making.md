## Summary

Qualitative study exploring the decision-making dynamics of CRNAs in surgical settings, identifying the factors that shape clinical decisions under real-world conditions.

## Key Findings

- **Decision-making factors:** Experience, interprofessional relationships, technology, psychological states, and ethics all shape CRNA decision-making
- **Impairment factors:** Stress, tiredness, and emotional load explicitly identified as impairing cognitive performance
- **Transition points:** Workflow transitions identified as key decision points — the moments between phases of care are where decisions are most vulnerable
- **Experience role:** Clinical experience shapes decision-making patterns but does not eliminate vulnerability to psychological state impairment
- **Implication:** CRNA decision quality is a dynamic function of psychological state, not a fixed competency

## SRL Relevance

Published in the AANA Journal — SRL's primary professional audience. CRNAs themselves report that stress, tiredness, and emotional load impair their decision-making.

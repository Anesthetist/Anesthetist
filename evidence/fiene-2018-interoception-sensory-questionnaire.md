## Overview

The Interoception Sensory Questionnaire (ISQ) is a 20-item self-report instrument measuring interoceptive confusion — the difficulty recognizing internal bodily signals unless they reach extreme intensity. Originally designed for and validated in autistic adults, the ISQ captures the construct of "alexisomia" (interoceptive alexithymia). An 8-item short form (ISQ-8) has been developed for use with adolescents.

## Key Findings

1. 20 items measuring confusion about interoceptive bodily states, particularly the inability to notice signals unless they become extreme
2. Single factor structure providing a unidimensional measure of interoceptive confusion
3. Measures "alexisomia" — a specific deficit in interoceptive processing analogous to alexithymia in emotional processing
4. Designed for and validated in autistic adults, addressing a gap in interoception measurement for neurodivergent populations
5. ISQ-8 short form available for adolescents, extending the instrument's age range

## Connection to Somnistics

The ISQ is relevant if SRL expands to neurodivergent populations, where interoceptive processing differences are well-documented. It measures the low end of [[interoceptive-literacy]] — individuals who cannot read body signals even when they are intense. This positions it as a floor-detection instrument: identifying people who need the most foundational [[interoception]] training before they can benefit from advanced protocols like [[gap-moment-training|Gap Moment Training™]].

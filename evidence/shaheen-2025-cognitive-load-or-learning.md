## Summary

Randomized trial measuring the effect of OR noise-induced cognitive load on intraoperative learning, using objective attentional and knowledge metrics.

## Key Findings

- **Design:** Randomized trial comparing noisy vs. controlled OR environments
- **Cognitive load:** Noisy OR significantly increased extraneous cognitive load (p<0.001)
- **Attentional lapses:** Higher skipped tactile responses in noisy condition (19% vs 5.3%, p<0.001) — nearly 4x the attentional failures
- **Learning impact:** Quiz scores significantly lower in noisy condition (57.4% vs 67.5%, p=0.02)
- **Implication:** Environmental cognitive load produces measurable, significant degradation in both attention and learning during surgery

## SRL Relevance

Provides experimental (RCT) evidence that environmental factors cause measurable cognitive degradation in the OR. The 19% vs 5.3% attentional lapse differential quantifies the cost of unmanaged cognitive load. Supports the argument that clinicians need autonomic regulation tools to buffer against environmental cognitive assault.

# Allostatic Load and the Brain (McEwen 2007)

## Summary

Bruce McEwen's foundational review establishes that chronic stress produces measurable structural changes in the brain — hippocampal atrophy, amygdala hypertrophy, prefrontal cortex thinning. The concept of "allostatic load" describes the cumulative biological wear from repeated stress without adequate recovery. The brain is both the primary target of stress damage and the organ controlling the stress response.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Allostatic load = cumulative stress without recovery — the scientific name for [[bandwidth-saturation-micro-events]]
- Chronic stress produces reversible (then irreversible) brain structural changes
- Hippocampus and PFC shrink; amygdala grows — exactly the pattern that [[gap-moment-training]] aims to prevent
- Recovery between stressors is the critical variable — supports gap moment intervention at every transition

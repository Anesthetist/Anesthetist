## Summary
Seys, Vanhaecht et al. (2019) conducted a cross-sectional survey of healthcare providers to quantify the duration and severity of second victim symptoms following patient safety incidents. The study found that symptoms persisted for more than six months in a substantial proportion of clinicians, with hypervigilance reported by 53% of respondents. Critically, higher levels of patient harm were associated with 8-9 times longer symptom duration, demonstrating a dose-response relationship between incident severity and clinician impact.

## Key Finding for SRL
The >6 month symptom duration and dose-response relationship provide hard data for SRL's clinician durability pitch. These numbers quantify the unmet need: current institutional support is inadequate if symptoms persist this long. SRL's self-administered protocols (resonant breathing, Gap Moment Training) offer a scalable intervention that clinicians can use independently over the months-long recovery trajectory, not just in the acute aftermath.

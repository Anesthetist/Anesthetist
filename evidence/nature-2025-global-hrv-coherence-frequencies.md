## Citation

(2025). Heart rate variability biofeedback in a global study of the most common coherence frequencies and the impact of emotional states. *Nature Scientific Reports*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-87729-7

## Key Findings

- Largest dataset ever studied in HRV biofeedback: 1.8 million user sessions analyzed
- Positive emotions were associated with higher coherence scores and more stable HRV frequencies
- Negative emotions exhibited lower coherence scores and more dispersed frequency distributions
- Provides population-level evidence for the relationship between emotional state and HRV coherence patterns

## Relevance to SRL

Major validation of SRL's **Resonant Breathing Frequency** and **NeuroHarmonics** concepts at unprecedented scale. The finding that emotional states modulate coherence stability directly supports the **State Transition** framework and the bidirectional relationship between autonomic state and emotional experience. The 1.8M session dataset provides the kind of population-level evidence SRL can reference when positioning its interventions.

## Methodology

Analysis of 1.8 million HRV biofeedback user sessions from a global dataset. Examined most common coherence frequencies and correlated with self-reported emotional states. Published in *Nature Scientific Reports* (2025).

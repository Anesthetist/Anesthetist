## Overview

Lopez Blanco & Tyler (2025) synthesize neuroscience, psychophysiology, and sport science around a single claim: robust vagal tone is both a marker and a modifiable driver of peak cognitive, emotional, and physical performance. The review integrates the Yerkes-Dodson law, Polyvagal theory, Laborde's Vagal Tank Theory, and locus coeruleus/norepinephrine (LC/NE) inverted-U models to frame athletic readiness as a function of cardiac vagal activity — arguing that the fragmented literature on cognition, inflammation, recovery, and team cohesion is best understood through a unified vagal lens. The paper covers mechanisms (baroreflex, cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, mammalian diving reflex), measurement (HF-HRV, RMSSD, vagal flexibility), and interventions (resonance-frequency breathing, HRV biofeedback, transcutaneous auricular and cervical VNS, cold exposure, contemplative practice).

## Key Findings

1. **Vagal tone is the fulcrum of the performance curve.** High resting HF-HRV predicts lower resting HR, more efficient baroreflexes, and greater neuro-visceral flexibility. Low HRV leaves baseline arousal already elevated, so even modest sympathetic surges push performers onto the descending limb of the Yerkes-Dodson curve.

2. **Vagal flexibility matters more than tonic vagal tone alone.** Laborde's Vagal Tank Theory reframes cardiac vagal control across three levels — resting, reactivity, and recovery — positioning autonomic capacity as a reservoir rather than a single number. Precision motor studies (dart-throwing under pressure) show that athletes with greater vagal withdrawal from baseline to task achieve higher scores, not those with the highest resting tone.

3. **Resonance-frequency breathing (~6 bpm) produces large, phase-locked oscillations in HR and BP with a characteristic ~5-second baroreflex delay.** This represents maximal engagement of the vagal brake. A single 15-min session of individually-determined RF breathing increases LF/HF HRV ratio and attenuates blood pressure reactivity to a subsequent stressor.

4. **Six weeks of resonance-frequency HRV biofeedback raises resting HRV and reduces trait anxiety and depressive symptoms.** HRV biofeedback uniquely increases heartbeat-evoked potential (HEP) amplitude — an EEG marker of interoceptive engagement — indicating strengthened brain-body signaling alongside autonomic output.

5. **HRV-guided training outperforms standardized training protocols.** HRV-guided runners achieve greater gains in maximal running velocity, endurance, and aerobic capacity despite often performing fewer intense sessions. Morning HRV suppression below personalized rolling baseline flags the need for recovery; rebound permits intensity.

6. **More vagal tone is not always better.** Parasympathetic overreaching — elevated resting vagal activity with persistent fatigue and diminished performance — appears in some endurance athletes, indicating maladaptive recovery rather than enhanced fitness.

7. **The mammalian diving reflex provides a fast, pre-wired vagal brake.** Facial immersion in cold water activates trigeminal-vagal afferents producing 10-25% HR drop within seconds. Full dive response (augmented bradycardia + peripheral vasoconstriction) requires face immersion PLUS breath hold.

8. **Transcutaneous auricular VNS (taVNS) acutely and chronically modulates autonomic and inflammatory state.** Seven days of bilateral taVNS (30 min/day, 25 Hz) increased VO2 peak by 3.8%, raised peak work rate by 6W, and blunted exercise-induced IL-1β (Ackland et al. 2025, RCT). Sub-perceptual 20 kHz taVNS (15 min, single session) produces significant functional connectivity changes in prefrontal cortex, cingulate, and insula — regions governing emotional reactivity and cognitive control.

9. **Stimulation parameters differentially push LC/NE up or down the inverted-U.** Suprathreshold sensory stimulation tends to increase sympathetic arousal; sub-threshold or just-noticeable stimulation tends to decrease it. Stimulus quality, pulse shape, amplitude, frequency, and baseline arousal all tune the outcome — "more taVNS" is not a monotonic good.

10. **Social coherence: HRV synchrony shapes group dynamics.** McCraty's framework links physiological entrainment between members to improved communication, cooperation, and trust, even without verbal interaction. Observed between parents/infants, musicians, and spectator/performer dyads.

11. **Flow states show asymmetric autonomic signatures.** Flow relates to sympathetic arousal in an inverted-U (too much or too little blocks it) but to parasympathetic activation linearly and positively. LC/NE activity gates flow, and taVNS has been shown to enhance flow experience (Colzato et al. 2017).

12. **Deep End Fitness / breath-hold diving produces measurable psychological gains (Cansler, Tyler et al. 2023).** 4-6 weeks of underwater breath-hold workouts reduced stress and anxiety and improved positive coping versus controls. Mechanism: fear/stress inoculation via "hunger for air" that recruits the mammalian diving reflex's cardiac vagal pathway — a somatic stress inoculation model.

13. **Meditation modulates both HPA and autonomic responses to acute stress.** Long-term meditators show lower cortisol and HR responses, smaller HRV reductions, and faster cortisol recovery during the Trier Social Stress Test. Acceptance (a meditation-cultivated trait) specifically predicts faster cortisol recovery. The Respiratory Vagal Stimulation Model of Contemplative Activity (Gerritsen & Band 2018) unifies breathing, meditation, and vagal mechanisms.

## Notable Methodological Caveats

- **Review article, not primary evidence.** Claims should be traced to the cited primary studies; this note summarizes a synthesis.
- **Author conflict of interest (disclosed).** WJ Tyler is co-founder and equity-holding member of IST, LLC, which markets the BRAIN Buds taVNS device featured in Figure 3A. Tyler holds pending and issued patents on the neuromodulation methods described. Funded in part by Air Force Research Laboratory (FA8650-18-2-5402).
- **Polyvagal framing used throughout.** Authors acknowledge the theory is "debated" but do not extensively engage Grossman/critics. See [[porges-2026-response-grossman-critique]] for the adjacent scholarly dialogue in the vault.
- **taVNS efficacy claims rest on small single-session and 7-day trials.** Long-term outcomes, individual moderators (baseline vagal tone, genotype, training load), and optimal dosing remain open questions explicitly flagged in the conclusion.

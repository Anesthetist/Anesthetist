## Citation

(2025). Effects of slow-paced breathing and humming breathing on heart rate variability and affect: A pilot investigation. *Physiology & Behavior*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2025.001738

## Key Findings

- Humming breathing (Bhramari Pranayama-style) produced the **lowest stress index of any condition measured — lower than during sleep** — with highest SDNN, RMSSD, and LF/HF ratio values.
- Humming activates vagal stimulation through vocal fold vibration modulation and nitric oxide production in paranasal sinuses, providing a second physiological pathway beyond baroreflex activation.
- Both slow-paced and humming breathing significantly improved HRV and positive affect compared to baseline.
- Daily humming routine may significantly and sustainably enhance parasympathetic tone.

## Relevance to SRL

Extraordinary relevance to SRL's NeuroHarmonics concept — humming as a vehicle for vibratory, resonant autonomic regulation. The finding that humming produces lower stress indices than sleep makes it a compelling candidate for the Minimum Effective Dose framework and Gap Moment Training protocols. The dual-mechanism (baroreflex + nitric oxide/vocal vibration) enriches SRL's understanding of Resonant Breathing Frequency and Vagal Tone.

## Methodology

- Pilot investigation; within-subject design.
- Conditions: slow-paced breathing, humming breathing, control.
- Measures: HRV (SDNN, RMSSD, LF/HF), stress index, affect ratings.
- Physiological recording during and after each breathing condition.

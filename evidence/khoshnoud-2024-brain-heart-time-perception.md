## Citation

Khoshnoud, S., Leitritz, M. A., Çinar Bozdağ, H. İ., & Wittmann, M. (2024). When the heart meets the mind: Exploring the brain-heart interaction during time perception. *The Journal of Neuroscience*, 44(37). [DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2039-23.2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2039-23.2024)

## Key Findings

- Heart-evoked potentials (HEP) — the brain's cortical response to each heartbeat — predict temporal accuracy during duration estimation tasks
- The brain-heart interaction is not merely correlational: cardiac signals actively contribute to how the brain constructs temporal representations
- Higher HEP amplitude during temporal encoding → more accurate duration judgments
- This establishes a direct, measurable pathway from cardiac interoception to temporal processing
- Extends the insular cortex model: the insula integrates heartbeat signals into the climbing neural activation that represents duration

## Relevance to SRL

**Direct validation of SRL's core mechanism chain:** cardiac awareness → interoceptive accuracy → temporal accuracy → clinical performance.

- **HRV biofeedback** in Pausality doesn't just train autonomic regulation — it trains the brain-heart interaction that underlies temporal processing
- **Heartbeat-evoked potentials** could serve as an objective biomarker for SRL intervention efficacy: if HEP amplitude increases after NeuroMinute training, temporal accuracy should improve
- **The Anterocept Domain 2** (cardiac awareness) is literally training the neural pathway this paper identifies
- **Apple Watch HR monitoring** captures the peripheral signal whose central processing (HEP) predicts temporal accuracy — biometric validation of the interoception-time link

## Methodology

EEG recording during temporal estimation tasks with simultaneous cardiac monitoring. Analyzed heart-evoked potentials time-locked to R-waves during duration encoding and reproduction phases. Published in *The Journal of Neuroscience* — highest-tier neuroscience venue.

## Summary

Study examining HRV as a real-time marker of surgeon stress during vascular surgical procedures, mapping stress patterns across procedural phases.

## Key Findings

- **Sample:** 11 senior vascular surgeons
- **Method:** Continuous HRV monitoring during vascular procedures
- **Primary finding:** HRV stress peaks were consistent across different surgeons performing the same procedure (r=0.768 correlation with subjective stress reports)
- **Stress topography:** Stress patterns are procedure-specific and predictable — specific procedural phases reliably produce stress peaks regardless of the surgeon
- **Implication:** Stress is not idiosyncratic to the surgeon but architecturally embedded in the procedure itself

## SRL Relevance

Demonstrates that surgical stress has a predictable topography — meaning interventions can be targeted to specific procedural phases. This supports the design of phase-specific Gap Moment protocols rather than generic stress management. The high inter-surgeon correlation validates HRV as an objective stress metric in surgical settings.

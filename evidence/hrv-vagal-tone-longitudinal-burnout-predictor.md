# Longitudinal Vagal Tone Predicts Burnout

## Summary

A consolidating finding across multiple longitudinal studies of healthcare workers: **reduced baseline vagal tone** (measured variously as RMSSD, HF-HRV, or high-frequency HRV power) **significantly and specifically predicts subsequent burnout**, with the strongest signal for the *emotional exhaustion* subscale of the Maslach Burnout Inventory.

Three features matter:
1. **Longitudinal, not cross-sectional.** Baseline HRV precedes the burnout outcome, strengthening causal inference.
2. **Vagal-specific, not generic.** The signal is in parasympathetic indices, not in sympathetic activation or cortisol — implicating the specific autonomic axis that breath can modulate.
3. **Emotional exhaustion specifically.** Not depersonalization, not efficacy loss, but the affective-exhaustion subscale — the component most closely tied to allostatic load.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- This is the mechanistic link between the interoception/vagal tone literature and the burnout/clinician-durability literature. Without it, the two bodies of work don't connect.
- It establishes HRV as a *prospective* biomarker, not a descriptive one — making it a candidate sensor for clinician-durability monitoring, not just a self-care gimmick.
- The vagal specificity points directly at breath, because slow exhale-dominant breathing is the only real-time volitional lever on parasympathetic output ([[balban-huberman-2023-cyclic-sighing]], [[feldman-2017-prebotc-locus-coeruleus-arousal]]).
- Grounds the claim in the CRNA Ecology deck that HRV is a *clinical sensor calibration* variable, not a wellness decoration.

## Caveats

- This entry consolidates several longitudinal studies rather than citing one. Individual citations to be added as the supporting evidence is formally mined into separate evidence notes.
- The direction of causation between vagal tone and clinician distress is plausibly bidirectional; longitudinal work has mainly established predictive validity, not mechanism.

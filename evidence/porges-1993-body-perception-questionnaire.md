## Overview

The Body Perception Questionnaire (BPQ) is a comprehensive 122-item self-report instrument developed by Stephen Porges to assess body awareness and autonomic nervous system reactivity. Grounded in [[polyvagal-theory|polyvagal theory]], the BPQ maps subjective bodily perception onto autonomic anatomy, covering body awareness, supradiaphragmatic and subdiaphragmatic autonomic reactivity, and cognitive-emotional-somatic stress response patterns.

## Key Findings

1. 122 items in the full version, providing comprehensive coverage of body perception and autonomic reactivity
2. Covers body awareness, autonomic nervous system reactivity (both supradiaphragmatic and subdiaphragmatic branches), and cognitive-emotional-somatic stress response
3. Grounded in Porges' polyvagal theory, explicitly mapping self-report items to vagal anatomy and autonomic hierarchy
4. Informed the development of the shorter BPQ-SF (Cabrera et al. 2018), which distills the instrument to practical clinical length
5. Available free at traumascience.org, with no licensing barriers

## Connection to Somnistics

The original Porges BPQ is too long (122 items) for routine clinical or app-based use, but its theoretical framework — autonomic reactivity mapped to polyvagal anatomy — directly informs SRL's concept architecture. The BPQ provides the intellectual scaffolding connecting [[interoception]], [[polyvagal-theory]], and [[autonomic-regulation]] that underlies SRL's [[interoceptive-literacy]] framework. The shorter BPQ-SF is the practical derivative for measurement.

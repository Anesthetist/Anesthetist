# The Perseverative Cognition Hypothesis (Brosschot, Gerin & Thayer 2006)

## Summary

This paper proposes a paradigm shift: it's not the stressor itself that causes physiological damage, but the prolonged cognitive representation of the stressor — worry (future-oriented) and rumination (past-oriented). Perseverative cognition extends sympathetic activation and HPA axis engagement far beyond the actual stressor's duration, often through the night via sleep disruption.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Rumination/worry extends stress physiology hours beyond the actual event
- This is the mechanism behind [[neural-transition-failure]]: the clinician carries the last case forward cognitively
- Gap moment intervention at the transition point prevents perseverative cognition from engaging
- Connects to [[sleep-debt-clinical-performance]]: nighttime rumination disrupts recovery sleep

## Concepts Derived

- [[neural-transition-failure]] — perseverative cognition is the cognitive mechanism of failed transitions
- [[gap-moment-training]] — gap moments interrupt perseverative cognition at the transition point
- [[bandwidth-saturation-micro-events]] — rumination amplifies each micro-event's physiological cost
- [[state-transition]] — clean transitions prevent rumination from engaging

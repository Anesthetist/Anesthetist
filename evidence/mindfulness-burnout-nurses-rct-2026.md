## Citation

(2026). A highly effective mindfulness intervention for burnout prevention and resiliency building in nurses. *PMC*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11999806/

## Key Findings

- Mindfulness-based intervention produced significant reductions in burnout as measured by the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory.
- Significant reductions in perceived stress, with sustained effects.
- Mindfulness training offers a scalable, evidence-based approach to building resilience in nursing populations.
- Adds to a growing body supporting individual-focused mindfulness as effective — even amid debate about systemic vs. individual-level burnout interventions.

## Relevance to SRL

Directly supports SRL's Clinician Durability and Gap Moment Training frameworks. The finding that mindfulness-based protocols produce measurable burnout reductions in nurses is consistent with SRL's premise that trained micro-practices (NeuroMinute, GMT) can serve as prehabilitative mental hygiene. Connects to Self-Remembering concept — mindfulness as a mechanism for re-establishing present-moment interoceptive contact during high-cognitive-load clinical work.

## Methodology

- Randomized controlled trial design.
- Population: nurses with elevated burnout indicators.
- Intervention: structured mindfulness program.
- Outcomes: Copenhagen Burnout Inventory, Perceived Stress Scale.
- Published in PMC (2026).

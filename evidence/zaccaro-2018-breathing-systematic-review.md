# Breathing and Autonomic/Psychological Effects (Zaccaro 2018)

## Summary

This systematic review by Zaccaro and colleagues examined the converging evidence on how slow breathing (typically defined as below 10 breaths per minute) affects both autonomic physiology and psychological states. Across 15 reviewed studies, slow breathing consistently increased heart rate variability, enhanced parasympathetic (vagal) activity, reduced sympathetic tone, lowered cortisol, increased EEG alpha power (associated with relaxed alertness), and reduced self-reported anxiety and negative affect. The review is significant for SRL because it synthesizes the multi-system evidence base supporting the core mechanism behind Pausality's one-minute breathing protocols: that slow, controlled breathing produces reliable, measurable shifts across autonomic, cortical, and subjective domains — even in brief interventions.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Slow breathing (< 10 breaths/min) consistently increases HRV and parasympathetic activation
- Effects are observed across autonomic (HRV, blood pressure), cortical (EEG alpha/theta), and psychological (anxiety, mood) domains simultaneously
- The optimal range for autonomic effects clusters around 5-6 breaths per minute, aligning with resonant frequency
- Breathing rate modulates the balance between sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous system activity
- EEG changes during slow breathing include increased alpha power (relaxed alertness) and increased theta power (meditative states)
- Even brief slow breathing interventions produce measurable physiological shifts
- The effects are consistent across healthy populations and clinical populations with anxiety or autonomic dysfunction

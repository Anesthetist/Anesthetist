# Implicit Learning and Tacit Knowledge

## Summary

Arthur Reber (1989) demonstrated that humans can learn complex rule systems (artificial grammars) **without being able to articulate the rules they have learned**. This implicit learning produces tacit knowledge that is robust, durable, and resistant to explicit codification — learners can use the knowledge (correctly classifying new strings as grammatical or not) but cannot explain how. Reber argued that implicit learning is an evolutionarily older, more fundamental cognitive process than explicit learning.

## Key Contributions

- **Implicit learning**: The acquisition of knowledge that takes place largely independently of conscious attempts to learn and in the absence of explicit knowledge about what was acquired
- **Artificial grammar learning (AGL)**: Participants exposed to strings generated by a finite-state grammar can classify new strings as grammatical/ungrammatical at above-chance levels without being able to state the rules
- **Dissociation of performance and verbalization**: People can *use* implicitly acquired knowledge effectively while being unable to *describe* it — the knowledge is genuinely tacit, not merely unreported
- **Robustness of implicit knowledge**: Implicitly learned knowledge is more resistant to interference, more durable over time, and less affected by individual differences (IQ, age) than explicitly learned knowledge
- **Primacy of implicit learning**: Reber proposed that implicit learning is phylogenetically older, ontogenetically earlier, and more fundamental than explicit/declarative learning — it is the default mode of knowledge acquisition

## Methodological Notes

- Artificial grammar learning paradigm with controlled laboratory experiments
- Debated extensively — some argue participants use fragments/chunks rather than abstract rules (Perruchet & Pacteau, 1990)
- Extended to complex control tasks (Berry & Broadbent, 1984), sequence learning (Nissen & Bullemer, 1987), and social cognition
- Connects to procedural memory, skill learning, and the cognitive unconscious literature

## Challenge to SRL

Some expertise is fundamentally implicit and **resists explicit codification**. The attempt to externalize all of Randy's clinical knowledge into a structured knowledge graph may **destroy the very thing that makes it valuable**. When a CRNA "just knows" the patient is about to decompensate, that knowledge may be implicit pattern recognition that cannot be decomposed into rules, tags, and structured YAML without losing its essential character. Reber's work suggests that the codification project has a ceiling — and that pushing past it does not produce better knowledge, but degraded knowledge. The vault may capture the articulable skeleton of expertise while the tacit flesh falls through the mesh.

## SRL Response

The vault captures what can be externalized — and acknowledges what cannot. The `clinical_interpretation` field remains Randy's precisely because some judgments resist codification. The ethnographer role (embedded in the knowledge-miner bot's design) is specifically designed to document what **resists** codification — noting where Randy says "I just know" and marking those as zones where implicit learning must be preserved through apprenticeship, simulation, and shared experience (Nonaka's socialization mode) rather than through explicit documentation. The vault is not meant to replace tacit knowledge but to create the explicit scaffolding around which tacit knowledge can develop in new practitioners. However, this remains **an open question**: how much of clinical expertise is genuinely irreducibly implicit, and at what point does codification become counterproductive?

## Design Constraint

**If the system assumes all expertise can be made explicit, it will codify the articulable surface and miss the implicit depths. Preserve pathways for apprenticeship-mode (tacit-to-tacit) knowledge transfer alongside the explicit curriculum.**

## SRL Relevance

Reber's challenge is existential for the vault project. It does not invalidate the vault — explicit scaffolding is genuinely valuable — but it demands humility about what codification can achieve. The SECI model (Nonaka & Takeuchi) acknowledges this tension: socialization (tacit-to-tacit) is a valid and necessary knowledge transfer mode that the vault cannot replace. SRL must build both the explicit curriculum (vault-driven, scalable) and the implicit apprenticeship (community, mentorship, shared practice) in parallel.

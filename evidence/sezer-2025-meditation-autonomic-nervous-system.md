# Advanced and Long-Term Meditation and the Autonomic Nervous System (Sezer & Sacchet 2025)

## Summary

This review synthesizes evidence on how advanced and long-term meditation practice shapes autonomic nervous system function. The central finding is that expert meditators exhibit concomitant sympathetic and parasympathetic activation — a state of "relaxed alertness" that transcends the traditional sympathetic-parasympathetic dichotomy. The review documents cardiac resonance phenomena, respiratory-autonomic coupling, and shifts in heart rate variability metrics that emerge with sustained practice. This paper is a keystone for SRL because it provides peer-reviewed evidence that advanced breathwork and meditation produce the exact autonomic signatures that Pausality and Gap Moment Training target: vagal tone enhancement alongside alert, engaged cognition rather than simple relaxation.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Long-term meditation produces concomitant sympathetic and parasympathetic activation, not just parasympathetic dominance
- "Relaxed alertness" is a measurable autonomic state distinct from both relaxation and arousal
- Cardiac resonance and respiratory-autonomic coupling are hallmarks of advanced practice
- Autonomic flexibility increases with meditation expertise, supporting the state-transition model
- The review validates breathing-mediated autonomic modulation as a core mechanism of meditation
- HRV metrics shift in advanced meditators in ways consistent with resonant breathing frequency training

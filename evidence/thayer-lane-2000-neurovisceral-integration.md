# Thayer & Lane 2000 — A Model of Neurovisceral Integration

## Citation

Thayer, J. F., & Lane, R. D. (2000). A model of neurovisceral integration in emotion regulation and dysregulation. *Journal of Affective Disorders, 61*, 201–216. DOI: 10.1016/S0165-0327(00)00338-4. PubMed: 11163422.

**Note:** Previously cited in vault as "2009" — the correct year is **2000**. There is a related 2009 paper ("Claude's prefrontal inhibition model") but the canonical NVI model paper is 2000.

## Summary

Proposes that heart rate variability indexes the functional capacity of a set of neural structures (the "central autonomic network") that support flexible, context-appropriate emotional and behavioral responses. Low HRV reflects poor prefrontal-subcortical communication and predicts emotion dysregulation. The model links autonomic, attentional, and affective regulation through a shared neural substrate.

## Key Claims

- HRV is a biomarker of self-regulatory capacity, not just cardiovascular health
- The central autonomic network (CAN) integrates prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate, insula, amygdala, and brainstem nuclei
- Low HRV = "default threat response" — prefrontal inhibition of subcortical circuits is weak
- High HRV = flexible, context-sensitive responding — the system can both activate and inhibit as needed

## SRL Relevance

- Foundational theoretical framework for using HRV as the primary outcome measure in somnistics
- Validates the "minimum effective sensor" philosophy: HRV indexes the entire regulatory system
- Directly supports the PVT reframing discussion (Thayer NVI model vs. Grossman's PVT critique)
- CAN architecture maps to Anterocept™ progressive domains: posterior insula (raw signal) → anterior insula + ACC (integration) → PFC (regulation)

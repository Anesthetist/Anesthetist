# Aphantasia, Interoception, and Mental Health (Kvamme et al. 2026)

## Summary

Published in *Neuropsychologia* (January 2026), this large study (n=833) found that the ratio of interoceptive accuracy to interoceptive attention mediates the relationship between mental imagery ability and mental health outcomes. Lower ratios — indicating effortful but unsuccessful interoceptive processing — predict worse mental health. This establishes that the quality of interoceptive processing matters more than the effort invested.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Interoceptive accuracy-to-attention ratio is a meaningful clinical metric
- Effortful but unsuccessful interoceptive processing (low ratio) predicts poor mental health
- Quality of interoceptive processing > effort/attention invested
- Interoceptive processing mediates imagery → mental health pathway
- Suggests that interventions should focus on improving interoceptive accuracy, not just attention

## Concepts Derived

- [[interoceptive-literacy]] — accuracy-to-attention ratio as quality metric
- [[interoception]] — quality of processing over quantity of attention

## SRL Relevance

Critical nuance for protocol design: simply training people to pay more attention to body signals is insufficient and may be counterproductive if accuracy doesn't improve in step. SRL protocols must ensure that increased interoceptive attention is paired with improved signal discrimination — supporting the calibration approach over the awareness-only approach. PMID: 41539358.

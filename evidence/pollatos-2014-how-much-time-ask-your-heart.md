## Citation

Pollatos, O., Yeldesbay, A., Pikovsky, A., & Rosenblum, M. (2014). How much time has passed? Ask your heart. *Frontiers in Neurorobotics*, 8, 15. [DOI: 10.3389/fnbot.2014.00015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbot.2014.00015)

## Key Findings

- Internal signals like heartbeats are centrally processed via specific pathways, and both their neural representations and their conscious perception (interoception) provide key information for time perception
- Heartbeat-driven neural representations carry temporal information that the brain uses to judge duration
- Provides independent confirmation (from a non-Wittmann lab) that cardiac interoception is mechanistically linked to temporal processing
- The title captures the core finding in five words: to know how much time has passed, consult your heart

## Relevance to SRL

Independent replication of the cardiac-temporal link from Pollatos' lab at Ulm, separate from Wittmann's group at Freiburg. Two different German labs converging on the same finding strengthens the evidence base. This paper also extends the finding into computational modeling (Pikovsky and Rosenblum are physicists working on coupled oscillator systems), suggesting the heart-brain temporal coupling is amenable to mathematical description. For SRL, this means the cardiac-temporal link is not just a correlation observed in one lab. It is a replicated finding with a proposed computational mechanism.

## Methodology

Combined interoceptive assessment (heartbeat perception) with temporal judgment tasks. Applied coupled oscillator analysis from nonlinear dynamics to characterize the heart-brain temporal relationship.

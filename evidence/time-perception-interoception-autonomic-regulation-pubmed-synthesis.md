# Time Perception, Interoception, and Autonomic Regulation
## Comprehensive PubMed Evidence Synthesis

*Based on articles retrieved from PubMed. 38 studies across 8 domains. March 2026.*

---

## 1. How Time Perception Works: The Major Models

### 1.1 Pacemaker-Accumulator Model (Scalar Expectancy Theory)

The dominant model for decades. An internal pacemaker emits pulses; an accumulator counts them; a comparator checks against memory. Arousal speeds the pacemaker (time dilates — more pulses per unit = "that felt longer"). Attention opens or closes a gate to the accumulator. This model explains why fear makes time slow down (arousal speeds pacemaker) and why distraction makes time fly (attention gate closes, fewer pulses counted).

**Lake-LaBar-Meck 2016** — "Emotional Modulation of Interval Timing and Time Perception"
**Title:** Emotional modulation of interval timing and time perception
**Journal:** Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews
**PMID:** 26972824
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.03.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.03.003)
**Key Findings:** Comprehensive review of pacemaker-accumulator model. Arousal and attention have unique AND interactive influences on temporal judgments. Current models do not adequately explain the variability in emotion-induced temporal distortions. Proposes new framework emphasizing dynamic arousal-attention interactions over time.
**Relevance to SRL:** Foundational model — explains why controlled breathing (which modulates arousal) directly modulates time perception. The arousal-attention interaction is exactly what Gap Moment Training targets.
**Evidence Level:** Narrative Review (Level 5)

### 1.2 Striatal Beat Frequency Model

**Oprisan-Buhusi 2022** — "Resource Allocation in the Noise-Free Striatal Beat Frequency Model"
**Title:** Resource Allocation in the Noise-Free Striatal Beat Frequency Model of Interval Timing
**Journal:** Timing & Time Perception
**PMID:** 37065683
**DOI:** [10.1163/22134468-bja10056](https://doi.org/10.1163/22134468-bja10056)
**Key Findings:** The SBF model uses cortical neural oscillators to produce beats at criterion times. Basal ganglia spiny neurons act as coincidence detectors comparing current oscillator states against stored patterns. The number of oscillators needed scales with criterion time and frequency span.
**Relevance to SRL:** Explains the dopaminergic modulation pathway — DA changes oscillator frequency, which changes subjective duration. Connects to the fronto-striatal clock model in our existing temporal distortion synthesis.
**Evidence Level:** Computational Model (Level 5)

### 1.3 Predictive Processing Model

**Fountas-Seth-Roseboom 2022** — "A Predictive Processing Model of Episodic Memory and Time Perception"
**Title:** A Predictive Processing Model of Episodic Memory and Time Perception
**Journal:** Neural Computation
**PMID:** 35671462
**DOI:** [10.1162/neco_a_01514](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01514)
**Key Findings:** Duration perception emerges from hierarchical predictive coding. More prediction errors = longer perceived duration. With ~13,000 participants, model replicated key biases: cognitive load effects, scene-type effects, prospective vs. retrospective differences. Rapidly changing or complex scenes seem longer because they generate more prediction errors.
**Relevance to SRL:** Explains why crises feel long (maximum prediction error) and routine feels short (minimal surprise). Training prediction accuracy (via interoceptive literacy) could modulate temporal experience.
**Evidence Level:** Computational Model + Large N Validation (Level 3)

### 1.4 The Insular Cortex as Time Hub

**Teghil-Wittmann 2025** — "How the body and brain process time"
**Title:** How the body and brain process time
**Journal:** Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews
**PMID:** 41077292
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106416](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106416)
**Key Findings:** Two independent meta-analyses converge: subjective time is processed in the insular cortex alongside the supplementary motor area (SMA). The insula is the primary sensory interoceptive cortex. Time judgments are rooted in both bodily and emotional aspects of the self. The insula processes internal signals; the SMA supports interaction between body and environment.
**Relevance to SRL:** THE keystone paper. The organ that processes time IS the organ that processes interoception. Training interoception IS training time perception. This is the scientific foundation for SRL's entire time dilation curriculum.
**Evidence Level:** Systematic Review of Meta-Analyses (Level 1)

**Zhang-Deng-Xiao 2024** — "The Insular Cortex: An Interface Between Sensation, Emotion and Cognition"
**Title:** The Insular Cortex: An Interface Between Sensation, Emotion and Cognition
**Journal:** Neuroscience Bulletin
**PMID:** 38722464
**DOI:** [10.1007/s12264-024-01211-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12264-024-01211-4)
**Key Findings:** The insula orchestrates taste perception, interoception, emotion, and decision-making. It serves as a critical hub integrating sensory, emotional, and cognitive information. Significant roles in time perception and decision-making. Functions as a dynamic interface synthesizing diverse inputs into coherent subjective experience.
**Relevance to SRL:** Confirms the insula as the convergence point for interoception, emotion, timing, and decision-making — all functions CRNAs need under pressure.
**Evidence Level:** Narrative Review (Level 5)

## 2. Time and the Body: The Interoceptive Clock

### 2.1 Craig's Global Emotional Moments Theory

**Craig 2009** — "Emotional moments across time"
**Title:** Emotional moments across time: a possible neural basis for time perception in the anterior insula
**Journal:** Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B
**PMID:** 19487195
**DOI:** [10.1098/rstb.2009.0008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0008)
**Key Findings:** A.D. "Bud" Craig proposed that awareness across time is located in the anterior insular cortex. The time base is adaptive and emotional. Time perception may relate to interoceptive afferent activity including heartbeat-related inputs. The model posits "global emotional moments" (GEMs) — snapshots of integrated interoceptive state that form a stream of consciousness like frames of a movie.
**Relevance to SRL:** The founding paper of embodied time perception. Every heartbeat is a frame of consciousness. Every breath modulates the frame rate. This is the theoretical foundation for breath-as-time-engineering.
**Evidence Level:** Theoretical Model (Level 5)

**Kiverstein 2025** — "Body, Self, and Time: Bud Craig's Global Emotional Moments Theory"
**Title:** Body, Self, and Time: Bud Craig's Global Emotional Moments Theory
**Journal:** Current Topics in Behavioral Neurosciences
**PMID:** 40069411
**DOI:** [10.1007/7854_2024_575](https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2024_575)
**Key Findings:** Each GEM has a large-scale network with anterior insula cortex (AIC) as central hub. The network integrates interoceptive signals with hedonic and motivational signals. GEMs function as moving windows of time within which a "phenomenal self" is experienced. GEMs have duration — the phenomenal now has temporal depth.
**Relevance to SRL:** Updates Craig's theory. The "phenomenal now" has duration, and that duration is set by interoceptive processing speed. Training interoceptive acuity literally changes the grain of conscious experience.
**Evidence Level:** Theoretical Review (Level 5)

**Simmons-Wittmann-Strigo 2025** — "Interoception: Synthesizing Insights and Charting New Frontiers"
**Title:** Interoception: Synthesizing Insights and Charting New Frontiers
**Journal:** Current Topics in Behavioral Neurosciences
**PMID:** 40705192
**DOI:** [10.1007/7854_2025_587](https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2025_587)
**Key Findings:** Organized around three domains: Bodily Self, Emotion, and Subjective Time. Interoception shapes time perception including in altered states. Points to computational modeling, personalized treatment, and clinical applications.
**Relevance to SRL:** Confirms the bodily-self/emotion/time triad as the organizing framework for interoceptive research. SRL's stack maps directly onto all three.
**Evidence Level:** Review (Level 5)

### 2.2 Heartbeat and Time

**Arslanova-Kotsaris-Tsakiris 2023** — "Perceived time expands and contracts within each heartbeat"
**Title:** Perceived time expands and contracts within each heartbeat
**Journal:** Current Biology
**PMID:** 36905931
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cub.2023.02.034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.02.034)
**Key Findings:** During systole (heart contraction, baroreceptor firing), time contracts. During diastole (heart relaxation, baroreceptors quiescent), time expands. Under heightened arousal, this cardiac-led time distortion shifts toward contraction. Time literally pulses with each heartbeat.
**Relevance to SRL:** Extraordinary finding — time perception oscillates within each cardiac cycle. This means heart rate directly modulates temporal grain. Resonant breathing (which maximizes HRV amplitude) would create the widest oscillation between time-expansion and time-contraction states.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Meissner-Wittmann 2011** — "Body signals, cardiac awareness, and the perception of time"
**Title:** Body signals, cardiac awareness, and the perception of time
**Journal:** Biological Psychology
**PMID:** 21262314
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.biopsycho.2011.01.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2011.01.001)
**Key Findings:** Duration reproduction accuracy correlated with heartbeat perception scores AND with cardiac slowing during encoding intervals. Progressive cardiac deceleration occurred during time interval encoding. Better interoceptive awareness = better time perception accuracy.
**Relevance to SRL:** Direct evidence that cardiac awareness underpins temporal accuracy. CRNAs who can feel their heartbeat can track time better. Heartbeat perception training = time perception training.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Del Popolo Cristaldi et al. 2025** — "In the heart of time"
**Title:** In the heart of time: investigating the link between cardiac activity and interoception in explicit and implicit timing
**Journal:** Physiology & Behavior
**PMID:** 40840849
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.physbeh.2025.115072](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2025.115072)
**Key Findings:** Better interoceptive accuracy = faster motor readiness (implicit timing). Greater subjective confidence in heartbeat detection corresponded to POORER time bisection performance when confidence exceeded actual accuracy. Higher vagal tone (HRV HF power) = faster tapping rhythm.
**Relevance to SRL:** Nuanced finding — accurate interoception helps timing, but overconfident-but-inaccurate interoception hurts it. Training must calibrate actual interoceptive accuracy, not just subjective confidence. Validates SRL's emphasis on MAIA-2 (which measures both).
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

### 2.3 Interoceptive Time Processing

**Di Lernia et al. 2018** — "Feel the Time"
**Title:** Feel the Time. Time Perception as a Function of Interoceptive Processing
**Journal:** Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
**PMID:** 29559902
**DOI:** [10.3389/fnhum.2018.00074](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00074)
**Key Findings:** Proposed the Interoceptive Buffer saturation (IBs) index — measuring subjective distortions of interoceptive time perception. IBs correlated positively with interoceptive cardiac accuracy. Underestimation of interoceptive time connected to diminished processing of bodily signals (depression, eating disorders). Overestimation connected to accurate body perception.
**Relevance to SRL:** Introduces a measurable index for interoceptive time processing. Could serve as an outcome measure for SRL training protocols. Connects disordered interoception to disordered time perception.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Wittmann 2015** — "Modulations of the experience of self and time"
**Title:** Modulations of the experience of self and time
**Journal:** Consciousness and Cognition
**PMID:** 26121958
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.concog.2015.06.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2015.06.008)
**Key Findings:** Time perception is embodied — the bodily self is the functional anchor of phenomenal experience. Increased self-awareness coincides with increased awareness of time. Decreased self-awareness (meditation, psychedelics, dissociation) = decreased awareness of time. Subjective time emerges through the existence of the self across time as an enduring embodied entity.
**Relevance to SRL:** The self-time link. When you lose yourself (flow, meditation), you lose time. When you become hyper-aware of self (threat, pain), time dilates. This is the pendulum CRNAs swing between.
**Evidence Level:** Narrative Review (Level 5)

**Teghil 2025** — "Interoceptive and Bodily Processing in Prospective and Retrospective Timing"
**Title:** Interoceptive and Bodily Processing in Prospective and Retrospective Timing
**Journal:** Current Topics in Behavioral Neurosciences
**PMID:** 39436628
**DOI:** [10.1007/7854_2024_516](https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2024_516)
**Key Findings:** An interoceptive-insular system supports the primary representation of time within large-scale duration processing networks. Tight link between episodic memory and interoceptive/emotional/sensorimotor states supports retrospective timing. Acknowledging dependence on bodily states is crucial for mechanistic understanding of time perception.
**Relevance to SRL:** Connects interoception to both prospective timing (during events) and retrospective timing (remembering duration) — both relevant to clinical debriefing and incident reporting.
**Evidence Level:** Review (Level 5)

### 2.4 HRV and Time Accuracy

**Cellini et al. 2015** — "Heart rate variability helps tracking time more accurately"
**Title:** Heart rate variability helps tracking time more accurately
**Journal:** Brain and Cognition
**PMID:** 26507899
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bandc.2015.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bandc.2015.10.003)
**Key Findings:** Higher HRV = higher temporal accuracy. Higher vagal control = lower error in producing 1-second tempo. Higher overall HRV = lower constant error in time bisection. Bodily signals shape our perception of time.
**Relevance to SRL:** DIRECT EVIDENCE that HRV training is time perception training. CRNAs with higher vagal tone can track time more accurately. Resonant breathing frequency training → higher HRV → better temporal accuracy under pressure.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

### 2.5 Vagal Nerve Stimulation and Time

**Caman et al. 2023** — "Effects of Vagal Nerve Stimulation on time perception"
**Title:** The effects of Vagal Nerve Stimulation on time perception in epilepsy patients
**Journal:** Journal of Clinical Neuroscience
**PMID:** 37948913
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jocn.2023.11.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocn.2023.11.005)
**Key Findings:** When VNS is ON, internal clock runs faster and time is perceived as running slower. VNS increases attention and alertness, which accelerates the internal clock. 2000ms intervals reproduced significantly shorter with VNS on.
**Relevance to SRL:** Vagal stimulation modulates time perception. This is indirect evidence that non-invasive vagal activation (slow breathing, humming) could produce similar effects on temporal processing.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental, Small N (Level 4)

## 3. Time Dilation Under Stress

### 3.1 The Emotion-Time Paradox

**Droit-Volet-Gil 2009** — "The time-emotion paradox"
**Title:** The time-emotion paradox
**Journal:** Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B
**PMID:** 19487196
**DOI:** [10.1098/rstb.2009.0013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0013)
**Key Findings:** Although humans can accurately estimate time, their representations are easily distorted by emotional context. Temporal distortions reflect the brain's excellent ability to ADAPT to events — not clock malfunction. There is no unique, homogeneous time but multiple experiences of time. The internal clock adapts to environmental demands.
**Relevance to SRL:** Reframes temporal distortion from "error" to "adaptation." Tachypsychia in the OR is not a bug — it is the brain's adaptive response to threat. The question becomes: can we harness that adaptation intentionally?
**Evidence Level:** Review (Level 5)

### 3.2 Social Stress and Time Distortion

**van Hedger et al. 2017** — "The influence of social stress on time perception"
**Title:** The influence of social stress on time perception and psychophysiological reactivity
**Journal:** Psychophysiology
**PMID:** 28139018
**DOI:** [10.1111/psyp.12836](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12836)
**Key Findings:** After social stress (Trier Social Stress Test), participants reproduced both short and long durations as lasting LONGER (time dilation). This occurred for both negative AND positive stimuli. Critically: changes in time perception were largely UNRELATED to psychophysiological reactivity — supporting the interoceptive salience model over the simple arousal model.
**Relevance to SRL:** Stress dilates time even for positive stimuli — the effect is not specific to threat. And it is not simply arousal-driven. The interoceptive salience model suggests attention to internal states is the driver, not physiological activation per se.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

### 3.3 Pain Dilates Time

**Rey et al. 2017** — "Pain dilates time perception"
**Title:** Pain dilates time perception
**Journal:** Scientific Reports
**PMID:** 29146989
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41598-017-15982-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-15982-6)
**Key Findings:** Being in pain leads to expansion of subjective time. Stronger pain perception = stronger time-estimate distortion. The enhancement of self-awareness may be critical to support dilated subjective time during pain. Homeostatic deviances focus awareness on self, creating the impression that "time stands still."
**Relevance to SRL:** Pain → self-awareness → time dilation. This is the same mechanism as threat → self-awareness → tachypsychia. The circuit is: homeostatic threat → insular activation → enhanced interoceptive awareness → time dilation.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

### 3.4 Arousal-Driven Time Dilation (Neural Signature)

**Ozoglu-Thomaschke 2020** — "Early posterior negativity indicates time dilation by arousal"
**Title:** Early posterior negativity indicates time dilation by arousal
**Journal:** Experimental Brain Research
**PMID:** 33277996
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00221-020-05991-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-020-05991-9)
**Key Findings:** Arousing stimuli were temporally overestimated and produced higher EPN (Early Posterior Negativity) amplitude. Time dilation and EPN amplitude were significantly correlated. EPN indicates arousal-based acceleration of the mental clock (pacemaker).
**Relevance to SRL:** First neural signature of arousal-driven time dilation. EPN could serve as a biomarker for measuring time dilation in training contexts.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental/EEG (Level 3)

### 3.5 Psychophysiological Stress and Temporal Accuracy

**Cellini et al. 2023** — "Psychophysiological stress influences temporal accuracy"
**Title:** Psychophysiological stress influences temporal accuracy
**Journal:** Experimental Brain Research
**PMID:** 37530787
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00221-023-06676-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-023-06676-9)
**Key Findings:** Stress induction (PASAT) increased heart rate and produced better temporal performance (lower constant error) in subsequent time bisection task. Psychophysiological stress may actually IMPROVE temporal accuracy (while making it feel distorted).
**Relevance to SRL:** Paradoxical finding — stress improves temporal accuracy even though it distorts temporal experience. Under stress, the clock runs faster but more precisely. This has implications for CRNA performance: tachypsychia may actually be an accuracy-enhancing state, not just a distortion.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

### 3.6 Trauma and Chronic Time Dilation

**Treitel-Levy-Gigi 2023** — "The dual role of time perception in trauma-exposed individuals"
**Title:** The dual role of time perception in trauma-exposed individuals: A conceptual review
**Journal:** Psychological Trauma
**PMID:** 38032630
**DOI:** [10.1037/tra0001631](https://doi.org/10.1037/tra0001631)
**Key Findings:** Time has dual roles — reactive (immediate distortion during events) and constructive (ongoing distortion from trauma encoding). Overgeneralized fear can cause neutral stimuli to trigger chronic time dilation, maintaining PTSD symptoms. Temporal metacognitive awareness could mitigate chronic time dilation and reduce PTSD symptoms.
**Relevance to SRL:** Critical for clinician durability. Repeated exposure to critical incidents could produce chronic time dilation via trauma encoding. Training temporal metacognitive awareness (knowing how your body distorts time) is a resilience intervention.
**Evidence Level:** Conceptual Review (Level 5)

### 3.7 Exercise Slows Perceived Time

**Edwards et al. 2024** — "The perception of time is slowed in response to exercise"
**Title:** The perception of time is slowed in response to exercise, an effect not further compounded by competitors
**Journal:** Brain and Behavior
**PMID:** 38558543
**DOI:** [10.1002/brb3.3471](https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.3471)
**Key Findings:** Time perceived to run "slow" during exercise compared to rest and post-exercise. Independent of RPE. No difference between solo and competitive conditions. Exercise itself — the physical state — slows perceived time.
**Relevance to SRL:** Physical activation slows perceived time independent of competition or perceived exertion. The embodied state drives temporal distortion. Connects to somatic regulation as time modulation.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

## 4. Time in Flow States

**Hancock et al. 2019** — "A meta-analysis of flow effects and the perception of time"
**Title:** A meta-analysis of flow effects and the perception of time
**Journal:** Acta Psychologica
**PMID:** 31279183
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.actpsy.2019.04.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2019.04.007)
**Key Findings:** 63 studies, 1094 effect sizes. Moderately positive correlations between affective (r=0.4), consciousness (r=0.21), and performance (r=0.17) aspects of flow and temporal distortion. Environmental conditions (physical and social) differentially affect flow. Time distortion is a core metric of flow state.
**Relevance to SRL:** The definitive meta-analysis on flow and time. Flow IS, in part, temporal distortion. If you can modulate time perception, you can modulate access to flow.
**Evidence Level:** Meta-Analysis (Level 1)

**Khoshnoud-Wittmann 2022** — "Brain-Heart Interaction and the Experience of Flow While Playing a Video Game"
**Title:** Brain-Heart Interaction and the Experience of Flow While Playing a Video Game
**Journal:** Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
**PMID:** 35572002
**DOI:** [10.3389/fnhum.2022.819834](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2022.819834)
**Key Findings:** Higher absorption = less thinking about time = faster subjective time passage. Heart-evoked potential (HEP) amplitude was lower during gameplay (less self-referential processing). Within gameplay, higher HEP = more absorption = better performance (more efficient brain-heart interaction). Higher flow = increased sympathetic activity + less inhibited parasympathetic activity.
**Relevance to SRL:** Brain-heart interaction (HEP) as a biomarker for flow. Flow requires efficient interoceptive processing — not absence of body awareness, but seamless integration. The autonomic signature of flow is sympathetic activation WITH parasympathetic flexibility.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental/Neurophysiology (Level 3)

**Kotler et al. 2022** — "First few seconds for flow"
**Title:** First few seconds for flow: A comprehensive proposal of the neurobiology and neurodynamics of state onset
**Journal:** Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews
**PMID:** 36368525
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2022.104956](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2022.104956)
**Key Findings:** Flow is accompanied by loss of self-consciousness, seamless action-awareness merging, and acute changes in time perception. Involves dopaminergic, noradrenergic, and endocannabinoid systems. Flow, psychedelic states, and traumatic stress are placed on the same continuum of profound consciousness alteration. Presents framework for flow onset neurodynamics.
**Relevance to SRL:** Flow and trauma are on the same consciousness continuum — both involve temporal distortion, both modulate the same neurotransmitter systems. The difference is voluntary vs. involuntary entry. SRL training aims to make entry voluntary.
**Evidence Level:** Theoretical Review (Level 5)

**Kuraoka et al. 2025** — "Flow state and autonomic response patterns"
**Title:** Flow state and autonomic response patterns during sensory rejection tasks
**Journal:** PLoS ONE
**PMID:** 41166293
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0335711](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335711)
**Key Findings:** Participants who entered flow showed decreased heart rate immediately after the task (Pattern 2 response). Flow state is associated with autonomic Pattern 2 — parasympathetic recovery post-task. Recommends assessing flow using multiple physiological markers.
**Relevance to SRL:** Flow is associated with efficient parasympathetic recovery — the same vagal rebound that resonant breathing trains. Further evidence that HRV-based training builds the physiological substrate for flow.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

## 5. Time and Meditation/Contemplative States

**Morin-Grondin 2024** — "Mindfulness and time perception: A systematic integrative review"
**Title:** Mindfulness and time perception: A systematic integrative review
**Journal:** Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews
**PMID:** 38583653
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2024.105657](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2024.105657)
**Key Findings:** 47 articles, ~5800 participants. Complex, multifaceted relationships between mindfulness and time perception. Effects depend on reference frame, measurement method, and many factors. Highlights importance of considering both trait and state mindfulness.
**Relevance to SRL:** The definitive systematic review on mindfulness and time. Confirms the relationship exists but is complex. SRL's approach of combining breath work with interoceptive awareness may access the time-perception modulation more reliably than meditation alone.
**Evidence Level:** Systematic Review (Level 1)

**Droit-Volet et al. 2015** — "Mindfulness meditation and relaxation training increases time sensitivity"
**Title:** Mindfulness meditation and relaxation training increases time sensitivity
**Journal:** Consciousness and Cognition
**PMID:** 25460243
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.concog.2014.10.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2014.10.007)
**Key Findings:** Mindfulness meditation increased sensitivity to time AND lengthened perceived time. Improvement primarily observed in experienced meditators. Experienced meditators were less anxious, and time sensitivity increased when anxiety decreased. Mindfulness develops ability to devote more attention to temporal information processing.
**Relevance to SRL:** Meditation trains temporal sensitivity — you become better at perceiving time accurately. And it is experience-dependent. This supports a progressive training curriculum rather than one-shot interventions.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Kramer-Weger-Sharma 2013** — "The effect of mindfulness meditation on time perception"
**Title:** The effect of mindfulness meditation on time perception
**Journal:** Consciousness and Cognition
**PMID:** 23778017
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.concog.2013.05.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2013.05.008)
**Key Findings:** A single session of mindfulness meditation (focusing on breath movement in the body) led to relative overestimation of durations in temporal bisection task. Within internal clock framework, meditation increases attentional resources devoted to time, producing longer perceived durations.
**Relevance to SRL:** Even a single breath-focused meditation session changes time perception. The minimum effective dose for temporal modulation may be very low — potentially within the timeframe of a NeuroMinute.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Linares Gutierrez et al. 2022** — "Changes in Subjective Time and Self during Meditation"
**Title:** Changes in Subjective Time and Self during Meditation
**Journal:** Biology
**PMID:** 35892973
**DOI:** [10.3390/biology11081116](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology11081116)
**Key Findings:** 22 experienced meditators (average 20 years practice). Mixed sympathetic/parasympathetic activity during meditation. Breathing intervals prolonged during meditation. Body boundaries perceived as less salient. Time passed more quickly and participants paid less attention to time during meditation. First quantitative study showing time experience altered together with sense of bodily self.
**Relevance to SRL:** Meditation simultaneously alters time perception AND body self-experience. The breathing rate change is key — prolonged breathing intervals directly modulate both temporal and self-boundary experiences.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Droit-Volet et al. 2019** — "Mindfulness meditation, time judgment and time experience"
**Title:** Mindfulness meditation, time judgment and time experience: Importance of the time scale considered
**Journal:** PLoS ONE
**PMID:** 31626645
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0223567](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0223567)
**Key Findings:** Meditation produced underestimation of short durations (seconds) but overestimation of long durations (minutes) — a scale-dependent inversion. Participants ALWAYS reported time passed faster with meditation. Focus on present-moment mediated the exercise effect on long-duration estimates. Different mechanisms at different time scales: attention for short, memory for long.
**Relevance to SRL:** Time-scale dependent effects explain why the subjective experience ("time flew") can diverge from duration judgment ("that was longer than I thought"). Both prospective and retrospective time are modulated, but differently.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

**Fabbri et al. 2024** — "Time consciousness: Silence, mindfulness, and subjective time"
**Title:** Time consciousness: Silence, mindfulness, and subjective time perception
**Journal:** Progress in Brain Research
**PMID:** 39097353
**DOI:** [10.1016/bs.pbr.2024.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.pbr.2024.03.001)
**Key Findings:** Silence influences awareness of self, space, and time and impacts psychological well-being. Pure silence, "just thinking" situations, and deep relaxation all alter time experience differently. Boredom plays a key role in waiting situations.
**Relevance to SRL:** Silence as a temporal modulator. Gap Moment Training uses micro-silences — this review provides theoretical support for silence as an active intervention in temporal awareness.
**Evidence Level:** Review (Level 5)

## 6. Time in Clinical Settings

**Wehrman-Chung-Sanders 2023** — "Anaesthetics and time perception"
**Title:** Anaesthetics and time perception: A review
**Journal:** Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology
**PMID:** 36453756
**DOI:** [10.1177/17470218221144614](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218221144614)
**Key Findings:** 36 articles reviewed. Ketamine makes time subjectively slow down. GABAergic drugs make time subjectively speed up. At short intervals, temporal estimates become more variable under anaesthesia. Longer durations overestimated. Variety of methods used raises need for systematic investigation.
**Relevance to SRL:** DIRECTLY relevant to CRNA practice. The drugs CRNAs administer change patients' time perception — ketamine slows time, propofol speeds it. Understanding these effects enhances patient care and informs the provider's understanding of their own temporal experience during cases.
**Evidence Level:** Systematic Review (Level 1)

**Pollatos-Laubrock-Wittmann 2014** — "Interoceptive focus shapes the experience of time"
**Title:** Interoceptive focus shapes the experience of time
**Journal:** PLoS ONE
**PMID:** 24489807
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0086934](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0086934)
**Key Findings:** Attention to interoceptive processes significantly affected subjective time experience. Fear + interoceptive focus → enhanced time dilation. Amusement + interoceptive focus → enhanced time compression. Sympathetic nervous system activation affecting memory build-up may be the decisive factor.
**Relevance to SRL:** Interoceptive focus AMPLIFIES temporal distortion in both directions — fear stretches more, enjoyment compresses more. This means interoceptive training gives clinicians greater dynamic range in temporal experience. The same skill that helps you "slow time" during a crisis also helps time "disappear" during flow.
**Evidence Level:** Experimental (Level 3)

## 7. Trainability of Time Perception

### 7.1 Interoceptive Training Modulates Time

**Cellini et al. 2015** (see above) — HRV directly predicts temporal accuracy. Training HRV = training time.

**Meissner-Wittmann 2011** (see above) — Heartbeat perception scores predict duration reproduction accuracy. Training heartbeat perception = training duration perception.

**Del Popolo Cristaldi et al. 2025** (see above) — Interoceptive accuracy predicts motor readiness. Training true interoceptive accuracy (not just confidence) improves implicit timing.

### 7.2 Meditation Modulates Time (Dose-Dependent)

**Droit-Volet et al. 2015** (see above) — Time sensitivity improvement primarily observed in experienced meditators. Training effect is dose-dependent.

**Kramer et al. 2013** (see above) — Even single-session breath meditation changes temporal bisection performance. Acute effects exist alongside training effects.

### 7.3 Breathing Modulates Time

**Linares Gutierrez et al. 2022** (see above) — Prolonged breathing intervals during meditation correlate with altered time and self-perception.

**Lu et al. 2024** — "STEA: Phenomenology and clinical relevance"
**Title:** Scale for time and space experience in anxiety (STEA)
**Journal:** Journal of Affective Disorders
**PMID:** 38703910
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jad.2024.04.099](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.04.099)
**Key Findings:** Developed and validated the STEA scale for measuring time-space experience in anxiety. Significant reduction in cSTEA scores AFTER BREATHING THERAPY. The effect of time-space experience on anxiety is mediated by spontaneous mind wandering.
**Relevance to SRL:** BREATHING THERAPY directly reduces temporal-spatial distortion in anxiety. Validated scale (cSTEA) could serve as pre/post measure for SRL breathing interventions. Mind wandering as mediator connects to attentional-gate model of time perception.
**Evidence Level:** Scale Development + Intervention (Level 3)

### 7.4 VNS Modulates Time

**Caman et al. 2023** (see above) — Vagal nerve stimulation speeds internal clock. Non-invasive vagal stimulation (breathing) likely produces analogous effects.

### 7.5 Temporal Metacognitive Awareness

**Treitel-Levy-Gigi 2023** (see above) — Temporal metacognitive awareness (knowing how your body distorts time) could mitigate chronic time dilation and PTSD symptoms. This is a trainable skill.

### 7.6 Effort and Boredom Shape Time via Interoception

**Wolff et al. 2025** — "Effort and boredom shape our experience of time"
**Title:** Effort and boredom shape our experience of time
**Journal:** Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews
**PMID:** 40946881
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106375](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106375)
**Key Findings:** Boredom and perceived effort influence time perception through interoceptive mechanisms. The insular cortex mediates the effects of interoceptive signals on time perception, integrating feelings of boredom and effort with temporal experience.
**Relevance to SRL:** Connects task engagement/boredom to interoceptive time processing via the insula. Relevant to understanding why long OR cases with low engagement produce different temporal experiences than high-acuity cases.
**Evidence Level:** Review (Level 5)

## 8. Proposed Course Architecture: Time Dilation Training for CRNAs

Based on the evidence synthesis above, here is the evidence-based architecture for a time perception training curriculum.

### Module 1: Mechanism Education — "How Your Body Creates Time" (2 hours)
**Evidence base:** Craig 2009, Teghil-Wittmann 2025, Zhang et al. 2024, Droit-Volet-Gil 2009

- The insula as the time organ (and the interoception organ)
- Pacemaker-accumulator model: arousal speeds the clock, attention gates the counter
- Cardiac time: time expands and contracts within each heartbeat (Arslanova et al. 2023)
- The self-time link: more self-awareness = more time awareness (Wittmann 2015)
- Why crises feel like slow motion (tachypsychia as adaptive response, not error)
- Why flow makes time disappear (attentional absorption closes the gate)

### Module 2: Prerequisites — Interoceptive Literacy (4 hours)
**Evidence base:** Meissner-Wittmann 2011, Cellini et al. 2015, Del Popolo Cristaldi et al. 2025, Di Lernia et al. 2018

- Heartbeat detection training (calibrate actual interoceptive accuracy, not just confidence)
- MAIA-2 assessment (baseline interoceptive awareness)
- Body scanning under different states (rest, mild stress, post-exercise)
- Connecting internal signals to temporal experience
- **Outcome measure:** Heartbeat counting task accuracy, MAIA-2 scores

### Module 3: Breath Control as Time Engineering (4 hours)
**Evidence base:** Cellini et al. 2015, Linares Gutierrez et al. 2022, Lu et al. 2024, Caman et al. 2023

- Resonant breathing frequency training (maximize HRV = maximize temporal accuracy)
- Slow breathing as vagal activation → time perception modulation
- Breathing rate and time-scale perception (prolonged breathing intervals alter temporal grain)
- Breathing therapy for temporal-spatial distortion (STEA evidence)
- **Outcome measure:** HRV coherence, cSTEA scores, temporal bisection task performance

### Module 4: Meditation and Temporal Sensitivity (2 hours)
**Evidence base:** Morin-Grondin 2024, Droit-Volet et al. 2015, Kramer et al. 2013

- Breath-focused meditation and temporal bisection (even single session works)
- Mindfulness increases time sensitivity (dose-dependent, experienced meditators benefit more)
- Present-moment awareness and time judgment (scale-dependent effects)
- **Outcome measure:** Temporal bisection task sensitivity (Weber fraction)

### Module 5: Flow State Access and Temporal Compression (2 hours)
**Evidence base:** Hancock et al. 2019, Khoshnoud-Wittmann 2022, Kotler et al. 2022

- Flow as temporal compression (meta-analytic evidence)
- Brain-heart interaction in flow (HEP as biomarker)
- Autonomic signature of flow (sympathetic activation + parasympathetic flexibility)
- Skill-challenge balance as flow entry point
- **Outcome measure:** Flow State Scale, HEP amplitude during task

### Module 6: Stress Inoculation and Tachypsychia Management (4 hours)
**Evidence base:** van Hedger et al. 2017, Rey et al. 2017, Cellini et al. 2023, Treitel-Levy-Gigi 2023

- Tachypsychia as adaptive response (reframe from "problem" to "superpower")
- Stress actually improves temporal accuracy (Cellini et al. 2023 — the paradox)
- Practice temporal estimation under simulated stress
- Temporal metacognitive awareness training (know how YOUR body distorts time)
- Recovery breathing after time dilation events
- **Outcome measure:** Time estimation accuracy under simulated stress, temporal metacognitive awareness scale

### Module 7: Clinical Application — Using Time for Performance (2 hours)
**Evidence base:** Wehrman et al. 2023, Pollatos et al. 2014, Behm-Carter 2020

- Anaesthetic drugs and time perception (ketamine slows, GABAergic speeds)
- Interoceptive focus amplifies temporal range (fear stretches more, enjoyment compresses more)
- Using dilated time in emergencies (the extra "subjective seconds" for decision-making)
- Time estimation calibration for clinical documentation
- **Outcome measure:** Scenario-based temporal estimation accuracy, CRNA time perception survey

### Module 8: Integration — The NeuroMinute as Time Engineering (2 hours)
**Evidence base:** All above

- The 60-second breath intervention as a temporal reset
- Gap Moment Training as a temporal metacognition tool
- Building a daily time perception practice
- Measuring progress: temporal bisection, HRV coherence, MAIA-2
- **Outcome measure:** Pre/post temporal sensitivity, HRV coherence ratio, MAIA-2 total

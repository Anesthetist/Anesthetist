# preBötC → Locus Coeruleus Circuit (Yackle et al. / Feldman lab, 2017)

## Summary

Published in *Science* (March 2017) by Kevin Yackle and colleagues from Jack Feldman's (UCLA) and Liqun Luo's (Stanford) labs. Identified a small, genetically distinct subpopulation of ~175 neurons in the preBötzinger Complex (preBötC) — the brainstem respiratory rhythm generator — that projects directly to the locus coeruleus (LC), the brain's noradrenergic arousal hub.

**Key finding:** Selective ablation of these neurons slowed breathing and produced calm, meditative behavior in mice *without* impairing respiratory rhythm generation. This is the first direct anatomical substrate for why slow breathing reduces arousal — a circuit that translates respiratory pace into noradrenergic drive.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- A specific neural circuit translates respiratory rate into arousal state. This is not metaphor: ablation experiments produce a direct, mechanistic effect.
- Provides the anatomical basis for why breath practices work on the nervous system — not a vague "relaxation response" but a specific preBötC → LC → cortex chain.
- Grounds [[resonant-breathing-frequency]], [[cardiac-anchored-breathing]], and [[vagal-tone]] work in circuit-level neuroscience.
- Answers the "is this just woo" objection at the level of the *Science* masthead.
- Feldman has been a central figure in respiratory neurobiology for decades; this circuit connects his preBötC work (1991 discovery) to arousal regulation for the first time.

## Clinical Relevance for CRNAs

Every anesthesia provider manages another person's respiratory rate with precision. This paper establishes that the same mechanism that makes the patient calm (via bag-mask slow rate) is also available to the provider via their own breath — and the circuit is specific, anatomical, and published in Science.

## Summary

Scoping review from the UCSF Center for Mindfulness in Surgery evaluating the feasibility and effectiveness of mindfulness-based interventions (MBIs) for surgeons.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** 24 studies including 9 RCTs
- **Feasibility:** MBIs are feasible for surgical populations despite time constraints
- **Well-being:** MBIs consistently increased mindfulness scores and improved well-being outcomes
- **Performance:** MBIs enhanced executive function, surgical skills, AND communication — not just stress reduction
- **Multi-domain impact:** Performance improvements spanned cognitive (executive function), technical (surgical skills), and interpersonal (communication) domains
- **UCSF leadership:** The Center for Mindfulness in Surgery is the leading academic group in this space

## SRL Relevance

Validates that mindfulness-based approaches improve surgical performance across multiple domains, not just well-being. The executive function improvement directly supports the Gap Moment Training mechanism of action. The UCSF group (Lebares) is a key academic collaborator/validator for SRL.

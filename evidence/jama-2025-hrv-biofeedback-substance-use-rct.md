## Citation

JAMA Psychiatry (2025). Heart Rate Variability Biofeedback for Substance Use Disorder: A Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA Psychiatry*.

## Key Findings

- Phase 2 RCT examining wearable second-generation HRV biofeedback for substance use disorder
- HRV biofeedback produced significant reductions in negative affect and craving over 8 weeks
- 64% reduction in alcohol or other drug use compared to controls
- Demonstrates that autonomic regulation via HRV biofeedback can modulate addiction-related emotional states
- Used a wearable patch delivery system — significant for accessibility and scalability

## Relevance to SRL

Powerful evidence that HRV biofeedback extends beyond stress management into addiction medicine. The wearable delivery model aligns with SRL's interest in accessible, technology-mediated autonomic regulation. Supports the principle that vagal tone improvement has cascading effects on emotional regulation, craving, and behavior.

## Methodology

Phase 2 randomized clinical trial. Wearable HRV biofeedback patch vs. control. 8-week intervention. Published in JAMA Psychiatry 2025. Outcomes: negative affect, positive affect, craving, substance use frequency.

## Citation

Mass General Brigham investigators. (2024/2025). Heart Rate Variability Biofeedback for Substance Use Disorder: A Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA Network Open* (published, reported 2025–2026). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41032322/

## Key Findings

- HRVB was associated with a **64% reduction** in alcohol or other drug (AOD) use days vs. controls (OR 0.36; 95% CrI 0.25–0.54).
- Significant reductions in negative affect and craving over 8 weeks in the HRVB group; control group experienced increases in both.
- Phase 2 RCT, 115 adults with severe SUD in their first year of recovery, using the Lief HRVB Smart Patch.
- Participants submitted twice-daily mood/craving/substance-use reports via smartphone for 8 weeks.

## Relevance to SRL

Landmark evidence that real-time autonomic self-regulation via HRV biofeedback produces meaningful behavioral change in high-stakes populations. Strongly validates the NeuroMinute and Resonant Breathing Frequency protocols as actionable tools not just for performance but for clinical recovery. The wearable delivery model aligns with SRL's minimum-effective-dose and accessible-intervention philosophy.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Phase 2 parallel-arm RCT
- **N:** 115 adults with severe SUD (first year of recovery)
- **Intervention:** Lief HRVB Smart Patch worn daily, 8 weeks
- **Measures:** AOD use days (primary), negative affect, craving (EMA twice daily)

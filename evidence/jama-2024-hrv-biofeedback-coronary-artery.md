## Citation

MIBS Trial Investigators. (2024). Heart Rate Variability Biofeedback and Mental Stress Myocardial Flow Reserve: A Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA Network Open*. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2840378

## Key Findings

- HRV biofeedback training significantly improved myocardial blood flow (MBF) reserve during a mental stress challenge in coronary artery disease (CAD) patients vs. usual care controls.
- Mental stress–induced ischemia is a known cardiovascular risk factor; HRV biofeedback appears to attenuate the cardiac stress response via autonomic rebalancing.
- Results from the MIBS (Mental Stress Ischemia: Biofeedback) pilot RCT; investigators call for a larger confirmatory trial.

## Relevance to SRL

Extends HRV biofeedback evidence beyond mental health into cardiovascular physiology under stress — directly relevant to Clinician Durability in high-stakes environments like the OR. Demonstrates that autonomic regulation training (vagal tone enhancement via resonant breathing/biofeedback) produces measurable cardiac protection during acute stress. This is a compelling data point for SRL's enterprise health systems pitch.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized pilot clinical trial (MIBS; NCT02657382)
- **Population:** Coronary artery disease patients
- **Intervention:** HRV biofeedback training vs. usual care
- **Primary measure:** Myocardial blood flow reserve (MBF) during mental stress challenge

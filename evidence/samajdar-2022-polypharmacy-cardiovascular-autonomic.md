## Overview

Samajdar et al. (2022) investigated the association between polypharmacy, anticholinergic burden (ACB), and cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (CAN) in 305 elderly patients. The study quantified how medication load — particularly anticholinergic medications — independently contributes to autonomic dysfunction, an often-overlooked confounder in autonomic health assessment and intervention.

## Key Findings

1. **305 elderly patients studied, 26.6% on polypharmacy** (5+ concurrent medications), establishing the prevalence of medication burden in this population.
2. **Cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy was higher in polypharmacy patients** (56.8% vs 44.6%), demonstrating a direct association between medication burden and autonomic dysfunction.
3. **ACB score >3 = adjusted odds ratio of 2.51 for autonomic neuropathy**, quantifying the independent risk from anticholinergic medications.
4. **Medication review is essential for autonomic optimization**, as pharmacological confounders may mask or negate autonomic training benefits.
5. **Anticholinergic burden is an underrecognized contributor** to autonomic dysfunction in the elderly, often missed in standard clinical assessment.

## Connection to Somnistics

Medication review is the ADPIE Assessment step 1 for any [[autonomic-regulation]] intervention. SRL intake protocols should include Anticholinergic Burden (ACB) scoring as a standard component. Users on high-ACB regimens may show blunted HRV responses to training — this is a medication effect, not a training failure. Without accounting for polypharmacy, SRL risks misinterpreting user data and providing inappropriate recommendations. Critical for clinical credibility with CRNA and physician audiences.

## Citation

Salvi et al. (2026). SOMNUS-DARE prospective cohort study. *BMC Geriatrics*. Reported March 2026.

## Key Findings

- Employed advanced polysomnographic techniques to quantify sleep architecture anomalies: fragmented sleep, reduced slow-wave sleep, and diminished REM phases
- Coupled with comprehensive neurocognitive testing targeting memory, executive functions, attention, and psychomotor speed
- Older adults with sarcopenia and significant sleep disturbances experienced more precipitous decline in both motor function and cognitive performance over the follow-up period
- Demonstrates the compounding effect of sleep disorders on age-related functional decline

## Relevance to SRL

Reinforces SRL's emphasis on sleep quality as a foundational pillar of human performance. The finding that sleep architecture disruption accelerates cognitive decline supports the SomnoAffinity concept and underscores the importance of sleep optimization in clinician durability programs. The specific link between slow-wave sleep reduction and executive function decline is relevant to Gap Moment Training, which relies on intact executive function during high-demand clinical moments.

## Methodology

Prospective cohort study (SOMNUS-DARE); advanced polysomnography; comprehensive neurocognitive battery (memory, executive function, attention, psychomotor speed); longitudinal follow-up in older adults with sarcopenia.

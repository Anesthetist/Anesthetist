## Overview

Desmedt et al. (2022) conducted a systematic review to identify the most cited questionnaires of interoceptive sensibility, then examined their correlations, factorial structure, and network structure in a large community sample (n = 1,003). The results reveal that current self-report interoception questionnaires tap into distinct constructs with low overall convergence, raising serious concerns about the interpretability and replicability of findings in self-report interoception research.

## Key Findings

1. Current interoception questionnaires do NOT measure a common construct — convergent validity across instruments is low
2. MAIA-2 and BPQ inform distinct, insufficiently related entities despite both claiming to measure "interoception"
3. Lack of empirical convergence threatens the validity and replicability of self-report interoception research
4. Researchers should not assume instruments are interchangeable when measuring interoceptive sensibility
5. The field needs conceptual clarity on what "interoception" means in the context of self-report measurement

## Connection to Somnistics

Critical methodological warning for SRL: we cannot claim MAIA-2 scores mean the same thing as BPQ scores. Instrument selection must be deliberate, justified, and transparent in all research and clinical protocols. This fragmentation is exactly why [[interoceptive-literacy]] as an integrative framework matters — the field needs a coherent conceptual scaffold that acknowledges the multi-dimensionality these instruments are failing to converge upon.

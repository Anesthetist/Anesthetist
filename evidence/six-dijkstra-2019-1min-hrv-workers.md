## Summary

Cross-sectional study of **877 Dutch employees** validating a 1-minute paced deep-breathing protocol for HRV measurement during routine Workers' Health Assessments. HRV was expressed as mean heart rate range (MHRR) during a single 60-second paced breathing window.

## Key Findings

- A 1-minute paced deep-breathing protocol produces HRV measurements that **significantly correlate** with age, workability, BMI, waist circumference, cholesterol, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and reported physical activity
- Low MHRR (within 60 seconds) indicates higher health risk across multiple domains
- Multivariable model explained 21.1% of variance in MHRR
- Validates that **60 seconds is a sufficient measurement window** for clinically meaningful HRV signal

## Relevance to SRL

This study directly validates the NeuroMinute design space. If 60 seconds of paced breathing produces HRV measurements that correlate with broad health markers in 877 workers, then the Five-Breath Re-Embodiment protocol operates within a validated physiological window. The 1-minute protocol is both a measurement tool and an intervention — the act of paced breathing during measurement itself engages the vagal brake.

Notable: Rollin McCraty (HeartMath) is a co-author, linking this to the established HRV coherence evidence base.

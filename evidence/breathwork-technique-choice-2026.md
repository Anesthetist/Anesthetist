## Background

Despite rapid growth in breathwork research, the field remains fragmented due to the diversity of breathing techniques. This review examines whether the specific breathwork technique selected matters for stress and mental health outcomes, or whether shared neurophysiological mechanisms drive benefits regardless of technique.

## Key Findings

1. Most breathwork techniques share core neurophysiological mechanisms that benefit well-being, regardless of theoretical differences between techniques
2. The most important question remains which specific parameters (breathing pace, pattern, pathway, attentional focus, modality) yield the most pronounced physiological effects
3. Accumulating evidence suggests breathwork serves as both preventive and adjunctive therapy for chronic stress, anxiety, and depression
4. Consistent practice may matter more than the specific technique selected

## SRL Relevance

**Mixed implications for Pausality:**

- **Supporting:** Validates breathwork as a category-level intervention — the general approach works
- **Challenging:** If technique selection doesn't matter much, Pausality's specific resonant-frequency breathing protocol may not differentiate on efficacy alone
- **Opportunity:** SRL can differentiate by (a) personalizing breathing parameters using biofeedback (which most techniques don't do), (b) embedding breathwork in a structured interoceptive training system (GMT), and (c) providing closed-loop, real-time feedback that optimizes the parameters this review identifies as key unknowns
- **The "personalization gap" this review identifies is precisely where Pausality's adaptive biofeedback adds value**

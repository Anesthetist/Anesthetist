# HN Intel Scan — 2026-04-08

## Top 3 Stories That Matter for SRL

**1. FDA's 2026 Wellness Device Guidance Opens the HRV Biofeedback Lane**
The FDA's January 2026 updated guidance explicitly includes HRV, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation as physiologic parameters that qualify for the "general wellness" exemption—no premarket review required if intended use stays wellness-focused. This was trending on HN (item 46990015) and Hackaday. For Pausality, this is a green light: an interoception-training platform built on HRV biofeedback can ship without 510(k) clearance as long as positioning stays in the wellness frame. The regulatory moat just got thinner for competitors too, so speed matters.

**2. White House AI Framework Proposes Federal Preemption of State AI Laws**
Released March 20, the framework recommends Congress preempt state-level AI regulation that imposes "undue burdens." The patchwork of state laws (Texas disclosure mandates, California chatbot rules, Illinois therapy-AI restrictions) has been a compliance headache. If preemption passes, it simplifies Pausality's go-to-market for AI-guided somatic coaching across all 50 states. But it's stalled legislatively—removed from the reconciliation bill and the defense bill. Watch this space; the HN discourse is skeptical it passes before midterms.

**3. Digital Health Raises $4B in Q1 2026—Mega Rounds Dominate**
Whoop's $575M Series G at a $10.1B valuation signals that investor appetite for wearables + wellness is at pandemic-era highs. 59% of Q1 funding concentrated in 12 mega-rounds ($100M+). The capital is flowing to companies with clear data moats and clinical credibility. Pausality's CRNA-origin story and interoception IP are differentiators in a market where most wearable plays are consumer-fitness. The window for a credible clinician-durability platform to raise is open now.

## EU vs US Sentiment Delta

The transatlantic gap is widening into a chasm. Europe's AI Office is in active enforcement mode—targeting X and Meta under the EU AI Act, with the high-risk healthcare provisions going live August 2, 2026 (fines up to €35M). Meanwhile, Amnesty International published an April 2026 critique of EU proposals to "simplify" tech laws, warning they'll gut privacy protections to feed AI training. The US, by contrast, is deregulating: the FDA loosened wearable oversight, and the White House framework explicitly aims to prevent states from regulating AI model development. For SRL, this means a US-first launch is the obvious play—but building EU-compliant data governance now (human-in-the-loop, regional hosting, transparency documentation) creates optionality for a European expansion when the August 2 deadline forces competitors to scramble.

## Chaos Signal

**Healthcare AI agents are arriving faster than governance.** HackerNoon ran a piece titled "Why 2026 Is the Year Healthcare Finally Hires AI Agents," and HN's April "Who Is Hiring" thread shows clinical AI companies actively recruiting. But nobody is talking about the liability gap: the White House framework explicitly recommends shielding AI *developers* from liability for third-party misuse, while the EU is doing the opposite. A healthcare AI agent that causes harm will trigger a jurisdictional nightmare. The company that builds a credible "clinician-in-the-loop" governance layer—exactly what Pausality's CRNA-authored protocols represent—will own the trust layer that sits between autonomous AI agents and patient-facing decisions. This is a 90-day window before the incumbents figure it out.

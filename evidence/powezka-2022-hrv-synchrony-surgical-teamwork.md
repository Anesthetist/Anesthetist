## Summary

Pilot study investigating whether HRV synchrony between surgical team members serves as an objective marker of intraoperative teamwork quality.

## Key Findings

- **Design:** HRV synchrony measured between surgeon and registrar during surgical procedures
- **Primary finding:** HRV synchrony between surgeon and registrar was inversely correlated with procedure length — more synchronized teams completed operations faster
- **Implication:** Physiological co-regulation between team members is an objective, measurable marker of team cohesion and operational efficiency
- **Mechanism:** Suggests that autonomic alignment between team members reduces friction, miscommunication, and coordination overhead

## SRL Relevance

Provides direct evidence for co-regulation as a measurable phenomenon in surgical teams. This extends the Somnistics thesis from individual autonomic regulation to team-level autonomic coherence. HRV synchrony could serve as a training metric for surgical team optimization — not just individual surgeon performance.

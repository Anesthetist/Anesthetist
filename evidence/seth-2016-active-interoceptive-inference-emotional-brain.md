## Overview

Seth and Friston (2016) extend the predictive coding framework to include active inference for interoception, proposing that autonomic regulation is itself a form of active inference. Published in *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, this landmark paper unifies perception, action, and autonomic regulation under a single theoretical framework — the free energy principle — with direct implications for understanding emotion, selfhood, and clinical conditions.

## Key Findings

1. Extends predictive coding to include active inference for interoception — the body acts to fulfill its own predictions
2. Autonomic regulation IS active inference: descending predictions from deep generative models enslave autonomic reflexes
3. Emotion = interoceptive prediction error integrated with action tendencies, not a separate psychological category
4. Free energy minimization applies to bodily regulation, unifying homeostasis, allostasis, and predictive control
5. Framework has explanatory scope from autism and depression through to consciousness itself

## Overview

Jensen-Urstad et al. (1998) examined the prevalence of cardiac arrhythmias in elderly male athletes with lifelong exercise histories compared to age-matched sedentary controls. Using 24-hour Holter monitoring, the study revealed a paradox: while athletes had higher HRV (a positive marker), they simultaneously exhibited significantly more complex ventricular arrhythmias and bradycardic episodes.

## Key Findings

1. **9 of 11 elderly athletes had complex ventricular arrhythmias** compared to 5 of 12 sedentary controls, indicating a substantially elevated arrhythmia burden in lifelong exercisers.
2. **7 athletes experienced heart rate episodes below 40 bpm** versus 0 controls, demonstrating excessive vagal tone manifesting as clinically significant bradycardia.
3. **HRV was significantly higher in athletes** than controls, confirming the expected autonomic benefit of lifelong exercise.
4. **Higher HRV coexisted with more arrhythmias** — a paradox where the same vagal enhancement that boosts HRV may also create substrate for arrhythmogenesis.
5. **Lifelong endurance exercise creates a unique cardiac electrical vulnerability** in older athletes that is not captured by standard HRV metrics alone.

## Connection to Somnistics

This study is a critical safety guardrail for SRL: atrial fibrillation and arrhythmia screening must be mandatory before vagal-enhancing interventions in aging athletes. High baseline HRV in this population does not equal safety — it may mask arrhythmia risk. SRL intake protocols should include arrhythmia history and consider Holter or rhythm monitoring for users with athletic histories and bradycardic baselines.

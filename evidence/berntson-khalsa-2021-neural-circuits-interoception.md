## Summary
Berntson and Khalsa (2021) provide a comprehensive review of the neural circuits underlying interoception, mapping both ascending (body-to-brain) and descending (brain-to-body) pathways. The paper identifies the anterior insula as the primary cortical integration hub for interoceptive signals, with the anterior cingulate cortex and prefrontal regions playing key roles in interoceptive prediction and regulation. The authors emphasize the bi-directional nature of brain-body communication, where top-down predictions actively shape the perception of internal states.

## Key Finding for SRL
This neural circuit map provides the mechanistic foundation for why SRL's interventions work. Resonant breathing and HRV biofeedback engage ascending vagal afferents; mindfulness-based interoceptive training modulates the insular-cingulate prediction loop; and the bi-directional model validates the premise that changing bodily signals (bottom-up) can shift brain states (top-down) — the core mechanism behind NeuroMinute and Gap Moment Training.

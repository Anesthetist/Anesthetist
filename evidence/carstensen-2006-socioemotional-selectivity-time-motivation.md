## Citation

Carstensen, L.L. (2006). The influence of a sense of time on human development. *Science*, 312(5782), 1913-1915. [DOI: 10.1126/science.1121774](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1121774)

## Key Findings

- Perception of remaining time shapes motivation and emotional priorities across the entire lifespan
- When time is perceived as expansive (young adults, new beginnings), people prioritize knowledge-seeking and novel experience
- When time is perceived as limited (older adults, terminal illness, approaching endings), people prioritize emotional meaning and close relationships
- This is not about age per se. Young people facing a terminal diagnosis show the same motivational shift as healthy older adults. The mechanism is perceived time horizon, not biological age
- The theory explains why older adults report higher emotional well-being despite declining health: they have shifted from acquisition goals to meaning goals

## Relevance to SRL

Socioemotional Selectivity Theory provides the motivational framework for the GMT Career Arc:

- **Phase 1 (Seeding, early career):** Time horizon is expansive. SRNAs are in acquisition mode. GMT is framed as skill building, career investment, professional development. Knowledge-seeking motivation is high.
- **Phase 2 (Healing, mid-career):** Time horizon begins to contract. The clinician starts to notice career limits. GMT shifts to restoration and meaning recovery. Emotional regulation becomes more salient than skill acquisition.
- **Phase 3 (Fortifying, senior career):** Time horizon is perceived as finite. The clinician prioritizes emotional depth, mentorship, legacy. GMT serves meaning-making and wisdom cultivation. Co-regulation becomes more important than personal regulation.

Carstensen's theory also explains why the "time acceleration with aging" finding matters for SRL's longevity thesis. If perceived remaining time shapes everything about motivation, then practices that slow subjective time acceleration change the motivational structure of the entire second half of a career. A 55-year-old CRNA who experiences time as spacious will approach their remaining clinical years differently than one who experiences them as compressed.

## Methodology

Published in *Science*. Integrates decades of experimental and longitudinal research on age-related motivation shifts. Studies include natural experiments (SARS epidemic in Hong Kong, 9/11), cross-sectional age comparisons, and longitudinal tracking of emotional well-being.

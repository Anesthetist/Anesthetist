# The Mindful Brain: Neural Correlates of Trait Mindfulness (Treves et al. 2024)

## Summary

This systematic review synthesizes 68 studies to identify the most reliable neural correlates of trait mindfulness — the dispositional, between-session tendency toward mindful awareness rather than state-level changes during active meditation. Three signatures emerge consistently: (1) decreased amygdala reactivity to emotional stimuli, reflecting reduced threat hypervigilance; (2) increased cortical thickness in frontal and insular regions, suggesting structural neuroplasticity from sustained practice; and (3) decreased default mode network connectivity, indicating reduced habitual self-referential rumination. The review's focus on trait (not state) mindfulness is particularly relevant to SRL, because the goal of NeuroMinute and Pausality is lasting dispositional change — not just feeling calm during a breathing exercise, but rewiring the brain's default architecture.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Trait mindfulness is associated with decreased amygdala reactivity to emotional stimuli
- Increased cortical thickness in frontal and insular cortex reflects structural neuroplasticity
- Decreased DMN connectivity correlates with reduced rumination and narrative self-preoccupation
- Insular cortex thickening specifically links to enhanced interoceptive capacity
- These are trait-level changes — they persist outside of active meditation
- The three-signature model (amygdala, cortex, DMN) provides measurable targets for intervention validation
- 68 studies provide robust convergence across diverse populations and methodologies

## Citation

Authors (2025). Toward early detection of burnout: A systematic review of potential biomarkers. *medRxiv* (preprint).

- **DOI:** 10.1101/2025.08.05.25332953
- **URL:** https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.05.25332953v1

## Background

This systematic review synthesizes current evidence on burnout syndrome (BOS)-related biomarkers to identify potential physiological correlates for early detection. Analyzed PubMed and EMBASE databases, yielding 111 studies evaluating 36 different biomarkers in adult populations.

## Key Findings

1. **Inconsistent associations** across most physiological systems, including the HPA axis (cortisol, DHEA), immune system, and cardiovascular parameters
2. **Some consistent correlates:** HbA1c, blood glucose, and comorbidities like IBS showed more consistent positive correlations with burnout
3. **No single biomarker** demonstrated sufficient accuracy for routine clinical diagnosis
4. **Key gap:** The field lacks a unified burnout definition and standardized assessment approach
5. The authors recommend integrating emerging assessment tools for objective burnout identification

## SRL Relevance

**IMPORTANT — both validates and challenges SRL's approach:**

- **Validates:** The need for multi-modal, integrated assessment (not just HRV alone) — supports SRL's battery approach
- **Challenges:** HRV's relationship to burnout is inconsistent across studies (6 of 17 studies showed negative correlation, others showed mixed or null results) — SRL should not overclaim HRV as a definitive burnout biomarker
- **Opportunity:** SRL's composite approach (HRV + interoceptive measures + behavioral + subjective) addresses the exact gap this review identifies

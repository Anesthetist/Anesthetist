## Overview
Makowski et al. introduce The Mint, a 33-item multimodal interoception questionnaire covering seven body modalities: cardiac, respiratory, gastric, thermoceptive, nociceptive, sexual, and bladder. The instrument identifies three metaclusters (Interoceptive Deficit, Interoceptive Awareness, Visceroception) and 11 facets with a context-by-modality design. It demonstrates superior criterion validity compared to MAIA-2 and BPQ for predicting alexithymia, ADHD, autism, and somatic symptoms.

## Key Findings
1. 33 items covering 7 body modalities (cardiac, respiratory, gastric, thermoceptive, nociceptive, sexual, bladder) — the broadest interoceptive self-report to date
2. Three metaclusters identified: Interoceptive Deficit, Interoceptive Awareness, and Visceroception — capturing both adaptive and maladaptive interoception
3. 11 facets with a context-by-modality design, providing granular assessment across body systems
4. Superior criterion validity vs MAIA-2 and BPQ for predicting alexithymia, ADHD, autism, and somatic symptoms
5. Uniquely includes maladaptive interoception (interoceptive confusion) alongside adaptive awareness — a dimension MAIA-2 does not capture

## Connection to Somnistics
The Mint may supplement or eventually replace MAIA-2 for broader interoceptive assessment in SRL's protocols. The multimodal coverage aligns with SRL's whole-body approach to [[interoceptive-literacy]], and the inclusion of maladaptive interoception captures the "body confusion" that SRL's interventions aim to resolve. Important caveat: this is a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, so SRL should monitor for published validation before adopting clinically.

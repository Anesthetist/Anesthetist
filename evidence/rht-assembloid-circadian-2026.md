## Citation

(2026). Functional human retinohypothalamic tract assembloid model for circadian rhythm research. *bioRxiv*. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.01.21.700761v1

## Key Findings

- Developed the first functional human retinohypothalamic tract (RHT) assembloid by fusing human pluripotent stem cell-derived retinal and hypothalamus organoids
- Self-sustained gene expression oscillations demonstrated using PER2::Luciferase reporter, showing robust 20–30 hour rhythms
- Platform provides readout to screen drugs or test light-pulse effects on circadian phase
- Directly models jet lag or phase-shifting in a human cellular system

## Relevance to SRL

Represents a significant advance in circadian biology research tools. While primarily a basic science breakthrough, this assembloid model could accelerate the development and testing of circadian optimization interventions relevant to shift-working clinicians. SRL's interest in sleep architecture and chronobiology for clinician performance could benefit from future pharmacological and light-therapy findings enabled by this platform.

## Methodology

Human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived organoid technology; retinal + hypothalamus organoid fusion; PER2::Luciferase bioluminescence reporter; circadian rhythm screening platform.

## Overview

Okazaki et al. (2005) investigated the dose-response relationship between endurance training and autonomic circulatory control in healthy seniors. The study compared sedentary seniors (~71 yr) undergoing 12 months of progressive endurance training against Masters athletes (~68 yr) and young controls, measuring R-R interval variability, baroreflex sensitivity (BRS), and cardiovascular autonomic function across the training timeline.

## Key Findings

1. **Seniors (71 yr) trained for 12 months** were compared against Masters athletes (68 yr) and young sedentary controls, providing a cross-sectional and longitudinal design.
2. **R-R interval variability was preserved in Masters athletes** to levels comparable with young controls, demonstrating that lifelong exercise maintains cardiac autonomic function.
3. **Sedentary seniors improved autonomic function with training** to match young baseline levels, confirming trainability of cardiac autonomic regulation in the eighth decade.
4. **Baroreflex sensitivity peaked at 3–6 months** of training and never reached young baseline, revealing a ceiling effect for BRS adaptation in older adults.
5. **Seniors have similar trainability for cardiac autonomic function** as younger populations, though the timeline and ceiling differ by autonomic parameter.

## Connection to Somnistics

Demonstrates that autonomic training works in adults aged 70+ but baroreflex sensitivity has a ceiling. The 3–6 month BRS plateau maps directly to product engagement timelines — SRL protocols should set realistic expectations for baroreflex improvement while emphasizing that HRV trainability remains robust. This evidence supports [[autonomic-regulation]] intervention design for older demographics and informs dosing/engagement models for the consumer app.

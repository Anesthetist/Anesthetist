# Breath-Hold Diving as a Tool to Harness a Beneficial Increase in Cardiac Vagal Tone

## Citation
*Respiratory Physiology & Neurobiology*. 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.resp.2025.104416
PubMed: 40096873

## Key Findings

1. **Explicit therapeutic framing** — The paper explicitly proposes breath-hold diving (BH-D) as a therapeutic tool to improve cardiac vagal tone (CVT), not merely a sport-specific adaptation.

2. **Dual brainstem mechanism** — Two respiratory-related brainstem pathways mediate the CVT increase:
   - **Pre-Bötzinger complex neurons** inhibit cardiac vagal motor neurons during inspiration → heart rate increase
   - **Kölliker-Fuse nucleus** mediates partial glottal constriction during early expiration → heart rate decrease
   These pathways generate respiratory heart rate variability (respirHRV).

3. **Acute AND chronic effects** — Recreational breath-hold diving triggers both acute augmentation and long-term permanent increase in CVT via sensitization of synapses involved in respirHRV generation.

4. **No structural remodeling required** — Unlike most exercise interventions, breath-hold diving increases vagal tone WITHOUT causing structural cardiac remodeling. This makes it safe for elderly patients and those with cardiovascular predispositions.

5. **Sympathetic hyperactivity treatment** — Could be employed as a safe, non-invasive approach to treat sympathetic hyperactivity.

## SRL Relevance

**This is the strongest external validation of SRL's entire approach.** The paper establishes that:
- Vagal tone is trainable via breath-hold practice (validates [[co2-tolerance-clinical-durability]])
- The training is safe without cardiac remodeling (expands target population beyond athletes)

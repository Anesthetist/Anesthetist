# Cognition in the Wild

## Summary

Edwin Hutchins (1995) studied navigation aboard a U.S. Navy ship to develop the theory of **distributed cognition** — the idea that cognitive processes are not confined to individual brains but are distributed across people, tools, artifacts, and the environment. Expert performance in complex systems emerges from the coordination of multiple agents and instruments, not from any single individual's knowledge.

## Key Contributions

- **Distributed cognition**: Cognitive processes extend beyond the individual skull to include tools, instruments, other people, and the physical environment
- Demonstrated that navigation is a computation performed by a **sociotechnical system**, not by any single navigator — expertise lives in the coordination
- **Propagation of representational states**: Information flows across different media (visual bearings, charts, instruments, verbal communication) and is transformed at each step
- Showed how **cultural practices** and **material artifacts** encode solutions to recurring cognitive problems — the alidade, the chart, the bearing book are cognitive tools
- Expert systems are resilient because knowledge is redundantly distributed — no single point of failure

## Methodological Notes

- Ethnographic study of ship navigation (USS Palau)
- Thick description in the anthropological tradition
- Extended to cockpit studies (Hutchins & Klausen, 1996) — directly relevant to clinical team performance
- Foundational text in cognitive science, human-computer interaction, and CSCW (Computer-Supported Cooperative Work)

## Design Constraint

**If the system requires all intelligence to reside in the user's head, it has failed to distribute cognition across tools, team, and environment.**

## SRL Relevance

The OR/anesthesia environment is a distributed cognitive system par excellence. The CRNA's expertise is not solely individual — it is distributed across monitors, alarms, the surgical team's verbal communication, the physical layout of the room, and the patient's physiology. [[embodied-clinical-intelligence]] in practice is always distributed cognition.

This framework reframes what SRL is training: not just individual interoceptive skills, but the capacity to function as a high-performing node in a distributed cognitive system. The [[co-regulation]] concept captures the interpersonal dimension of this — the CRNA's physiological state influences and is influenced by the entire team's functioning. Hutchins provides the theoretical foundation for understanding why individual autonomic regulation has team-level effects.

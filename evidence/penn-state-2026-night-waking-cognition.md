## Citation

Penn State and Albert Einstein College of Medicine. (2026). Night waking impacts cognitive performance regardless of sleep duration. *Sleep Health*. January 2026. https://pennstatehealthnews.org/2026/01/night-waking-impacts-cognitive-performance-regardless-of-sleep-duration/

## Key Findings

- Sleep quality (not quantity) predicted next-day processing speed in older adults
- Over 16 days, participants wore wearable devices tracking sleep and completed cognitive assessments 6x/day via smartphone (yielding 20,532+ cognitive assessments)
- Night waking was the critical variable: it impaired processing speed regardless of total sleep time
- Napping, bedtime, and quantity of sleep showed no effect on processing speed or other cognitive measures
- Quality of sleep continuity was the dominant predictor of next-day cognitive performance

## Relevance to SRL

Strongly supports SRL's **SomnoAffinity** concept and the emphasis on sleep quality over sleep quantity. Aligns with the **Cognitive Variability Analysis** framework by demonstrating how within-person daily fluctuations in sleep quality drive cognitive performance variance. The ecological momentary assessment methodology (wearables + 6x/day testing) is a model for SRL's own measurement approaches.

## Methodology

Prospective daily diary study over 16 days. Older adult participants. Sleep tracked via wearable actigraphy. Cognitive performance assessed via smartphone-based tasks (visual working memory, visuospatial memory, processing speed) 6x/day, yielding over 20,532 assessments. Published in *Sleep Health*.

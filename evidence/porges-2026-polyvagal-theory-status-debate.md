## Citation

Polyvagal Theory: Current Status, Clinical Applications, and Future Directions. *PMC*, 2026. PMC12302812. See also: Grossman et al. (2026), "Why the Polyvagal Theory is Untenable," *Clinical Neuropsychiatry*; Porges (2026), response in same journal issue.

## Key Findings

- 39 researchers co-signed Grossman's critique calling PVT "untenable" from evolutionary and physiological standpoints, particularly challenging RSA as a pure vagal tone index and anatomical specificity claims
- Porges responded in detail in the same 2026 journal issue, defending the clinical utility and revised formulations of PVT
- Comprehensive review consolidates recent advances in autonomic measurement and explores clinical implications across trauma, chronic pain, autism, developmental disorders, and mood disorders
- PVT has undergone two major iterations since 1995, culminating in "The Vagal Paradox: A Polyvagal Solution" (2023) and "Polyvagal Perspectives: Interventions, Practices, and Strategies" (2024)

## Relevance to SRL

**CRITICAL for SRL's theoretical positioning.** SRL draws heavily on polyvagal concepts (neuroception, co-regulation, ventral vagal engagement). This debate requires SRL to: (1) acknowledge the ongoing scientific controversy, (2) emphasize that SRL's applied framework focuses on clinical utility and measurable autonomic outcomes rather than phylogenetic claims, and (3) lean on the convergent evidence from HRV biofeedback RCTs that demonstrate the practical value of vagal training regardless of the theoretical mechanism debate.

Narrative review, scholarly response, and theoretical synthesis; no original empirical data.

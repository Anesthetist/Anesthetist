## Summary

Systematic review of objective methods for assessing cognitive workload (CWL) during surgery, evaluating sensor types, study designs, and measurement validity.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** 67 studies reviewed
- **Sensor findings:** Cardiac sensors (HRV, HR) were the least intrusive, with 54.2% used in naturalistic (real OR) settings vs. simulation
- **Definition gap:** CWL was NOT DEFINED in 44.8% of the studies that claimed to measure it — a major methodological weakness in the field
- **Multimodal superiority:** Multimodal recordings (combining cardiac + EEG + eye-tracking) provided the most accurate CWL assessment
- **Implication:** The field lacks standardized CWL definitions and measurement protocols despite growing adoption of wearable sensors

## SRL Relevance

Maps the measurement landscape for cognitive workload in surgery — validates HRV-based cardiac monitoring as the most practical approach for real-world OR deployment. The 44.8% definition gap is an opportunity for SRL to establish Cognitive Variability Analysis as a standardized framework. Multimodal finding supports SRL's sensor-fusion approach.

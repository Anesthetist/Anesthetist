## Citation

Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience (2026). Nightly variations in sleep quality and next-day cognitive performance: an in-home study in healthy older adults. *Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience*.

## Key Findings

- Used in-home wearable EEG to monitor sleep over ~10 nights in 17 healthy older adults
- Assessed macro-architecture (wake after sleep onset) and micro-architecture (slow oscillation density, sleep spindle density)
- Found no significant effects of either macro- or micro-architectural sleep metrics on executive task outcomes the following day
- Contrasts with Penn State study showing sleep quality (not duration) predicted processing speed in 261 older adults
- Highlights the complexity and inconsistency of sleep-cognition relationships

## Relevance to SRL

The discrepancy between studies underscores the need for more nuanced models of how sleep affects performance — supporting SRL's cognitive variability analysis approach over simple sleep-quality metrics. The in-home wearable EEG methodology is relevant to SRL's interest in ecologically valid measurement.

## Methodology

In-home wearable EEG monitoring, ~10 nights, 17 healthy older adults. Executive function tasks the following day. Published 2026 in Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience.

## Key Findings

- A paper signed by 38 autonomic nervous system scholars ("Why the Polyvagal Theory is Untenable," Grossman et al., 2026) argues against key PVT tenets from evolutionary and physiological standpoints — particularly regarding anatomical specificity of the "ventral vagal" pathway and the claimed unique role of respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA).
- Porges responded that Grossman et al. mischaracterize the theory's core claims regarding autonomic organization, RSA, and evolutionary framing.
- Defenders note the clinical utility of PVT remains validated by interventions like the Safe and Sound Protocol, yielding measurable improvements in regulation and social behavior regardless of the theoretical debate.
- PVT has undergone two major iterations since 1995, culminating in "The Vagal Paradox: A Polyvagal Solution" (2023) and "Polyvagal Perspectives" (2024).

## Relevance to SRL

SRL's frameworks (Autonomic Regulation, Vagal Tone, Resonant Breathing Frequency, Co-Regulation) draw substantially on PVT as a conceptual scaffold. This debate is directly relevant: SRL should maintain awareness of the scientific controversy while continuing to affirm the *clinical* validity of PVT-informed interventions. The distinction between theoretical mechanisms vs. clinical outcomes is important for SRL's evidence-based positioning. ⚠️ Flag: SRL materials should acknowledge the ongoing scientific debate around PVT rather than presenting it as settled theory.

# Approaching Awe, a Moral, Spiritual, and Aesthetic Emotion

Dacher Keltner & Jonathan Haidt (2003). *Cognition and Emotion*, 17(2), 297-314.

## Core Framework

Two necessary components of awe:
1. **Perceived vastness** — physical (Grand Canyon), conceptual (a theory that reorganizes understanding), social (charismatic leader), or temporal (deep time)
2. **Need for accommodation** — the experience requires updating mental schemas; existing frameworks are insufficient

## Key Contributions

- Distinguishes awe from admiration (awe requires accommodation; admiration does not)
- Identifies awe's prosocial function: the "small self" effect reduces self-referential processing
- Maps awe across domains: nature, art, music, spiritual experience, intellectual insight, moral beauty
- Establishes awe as a distinct emotion category rather than a variant of surprise or wonder

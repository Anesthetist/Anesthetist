## Overview

A 3-page leadership skill exercise for calibrating personal and professional standards by distinguishing between artificially high standards (perfectionism that generates negative fuel), artificially low standards (settling/enabling), and realistic minimum expectations that set a clear boundary between acceptable and unacceptable.

## Exercise Structure

### Step 1: Surface the Insecurity
- What is a specific self-judgment you have?
- Where do you judge yourself as not being good enough?
- Where do you feel insecure about your competence and/or worth?

### Step 2: Examine Comparisons
- Where have you been comparing yourself to perfection or the top person you know?
- Where have you been comparing to an average person or to something mediocre?

### Step 3: Calibrate the Minimum Expectation
The minimum expectation sets the boundary between "Below the Line" (unacceptable) and "Above the Line" (acceptable). Two calibration checks:
1. Is this standard the true minimum realistic expectation, or are you **artificially raising the bar** to create stress and negative fuel?
2. Is this standard the true minimum realistic expectation, or are you **artificially lowering the bar** as a way of settling or enabling unacceptable behavior?

### Step 4: Map Real Dangers vs. Minimum Expectations
A two-column worksheet mapping the actual dangers of not meeting the standard alongside the realistic minimum expectation for each area.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Standards Calibration as Autonomic Load Management

Artificially high standards create chronic sympathetic activation — the nervous system is perpetually in threat-detection mode because the gap between reality and the standard is perceived as danger. Artificially low standards enable dorsal vagal collapse — the system accepts degraded performance because engaging would require uncomfortable mobilization. Properly calibrated standards maintain the ventral vagal sweet spot: enough challenge to stay engaged, enough safety to stay regulated.

### Clinical Application: CRNA Performance Standards

This framework directly addresses a core challenge in clinician durability. CRNAs who hold perfectionist standards ("zero complications, ever") burn out because the standard is impossible to sustain. CRNAs who lower standards ("it's just the way it is in this facility") enable systemic degradation. The exercise provides a structured pathway for finding the realistic minimum that maintains clinical excellence without chronic autonomic overdrive.

### Comparison as Attentional Misallocation

The exercise's focus on comparison patterns reveals how attention gets captured by external referents rather than internal standards. This maps to the SRL concept of attentional sovereignty — the capacity to direct attention based on internal values rather than external comparison points. A CRNA who measures themselves against a single exemplar (or against the lowest performer) has surrendered attentional sovereignty to the comparison object.

### Below/Above the Line as Gap Moment Detection

The "Below the Line / Above the Line" boundary is a decision architecture. Recognizing when you've dropped below the line is a form of gap moment detection — the moment of awareness that something has shifted from acceptable to unacceptable. Training the capacity to notice this boundary in real-time is a foundation for self-remembering practice.

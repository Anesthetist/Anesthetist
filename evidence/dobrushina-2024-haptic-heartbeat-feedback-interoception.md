## Citation

Dobrushina, O., Tamim, M., & Wald, A. (2024). Interoceptive training with real-time haptic versus visual heartbeat feedback. *Psychophysiology*, 61(12), e14648. [DOI: 10.1111/psyp.14648](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.14648)

## Key Findings

- Real-time haptic heartbeat feedback (vibration synchronized to each heartbeat) improved interoceptive accuracy in a single session
- Visual heartbeat feedback also improved accuracy but through a different attentional pathway
- Haptic feedback is closer to the body's native signaling modality, potentially making it more natural and sustainable as a training method
- Single-session efficacy means the intervention does not require weeks of practice before producing measurable effects
- The study explicitly develops interoceptive training methodology, not just measurement

## Relevance to SRL

This is the closest published evidence to what Pausality could implement with Apple Watch taptic feedback. The protocol is straightforward: vibrate on every heartbeat during a NeuroMinute. The user feels their cardiac rhythm externalized through the wrist. Interoceptive accuracy improves. By the Meissner-Wittmann chain, temporal accuracy improves with it.

Key design implications for Jason:
- Haptic feedback on the wrist is a validated interoceptive training modality, not a novelty feature
- Single-session effects mean the first NeuroMinute with haptic heartbeat sync could produce measurable interoceptive improvement
- The study compared haptic versus visual, giving SRL evidence for choosing haptic (Watch) over visual (phone screen) as the primary feedback channel
- This is prior art for any Apple Watch implementation of heartbeat-synced haptic feedback during breathing exercises

## Methodology

Randomized comparison of haptic versus visual heartbeat feedback during interoceptive training. Pre/post heartbeat detection task to measure accuracy improvement. Single-session design.

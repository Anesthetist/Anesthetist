## Citation

Porges, S.W. (2026). When A Critique Becomes Untenable: A Scholarly Response To Grossman Et Al.'s Evaluation Of Polyvagal Theory. *Clinical Neuropsychiatry*.

- **PubMed Central:** PMC12937496
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12937496/

## Background

Published in the same issue of *Clinical Neuropsychiatry* as Grossman et al.'s critique, Porges' response argues that the critique does not evaluate Polyvagal Theory as formulated in the scientific literature, but instead constructs a "synthetic version" based on selective interpretations and assumptions that the theory does not assert.

## Key Arguments

Porges contends that the Grossman critique:
1. Constructs a strawman version of PVT rather than engaging with its actual formulations
2. Selectively interprets and misrepresents key tenets
3. Evaluates this reconstructed version as if it were PVT itself

## SRL Relevance

This response is essential companion reading to the Grossman critique. For SRL, the takeaway is nuanced:

- The scientific community is actively debating PVT's validity at the highest levels
- Both papers appeared in the same peer-reviewed journal, indicating the debate is legitimate and ongoing
- SRL should track this debate closely and consider both perspectives when framing its theoretical grounding
- The safest position for SRL is to ground claims in demonstrated mechanisms (HRV biofeedback efficacy, breathing rate effects on autonomic function) rather than PVT's specific theoretical architecture

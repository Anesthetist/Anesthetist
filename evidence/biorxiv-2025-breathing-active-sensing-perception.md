## Citation

(Authors, 2025). Breathing strategies to influence perception: Evidence for interoceptive and exteroceptive active sensing. *bioRxiv* preprint. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.15.654033v1

## Key Findings

- Participants systematically adjusted breathing amplitude and phase, synchronizing respiration to anticipated stimulus onset — a behavior the authors interpret as **active sensing**.
- Active respiratory-perceptual synchronization was associated with faster reaction times and improved perceptual accuracy.
- Demonstrates that conscious or semi-conscious control of breathing phase directly modulates perceptual processing — not just autonomic state.
- Establishes a bridge between interoceptive (internal body state) and exteroceptive (environment-directed) active sensing via breath.

## Relevance to SRL

This is a foundational finding for the Anterocept concept and Multi-Phase Interoceptive Coupling — the idea that respiratory phase actively gates perceptual intake. Supports the SRL model that breath training is not just autonomic regulation but a perceptual optimization tool. For perioperative clinicians, training breath-phase awareness may enhance situational awareness during high-demand moments. Also directly supports Diaphragmatic Blindness as a construct — clinicians who lose breath awareness lose perceptual gating.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Behavioral experiment with respiratory phase manipulation
- **Measures:** Breathing amplitude, phase alignment to stimuli, reaction time, perceptual accuracy
- **Status:** Preprint (bioRxiv, 2025) — not yet peer-reviewed

## Overview

A structured awareness exercise that maps personal and inherited failures across the seven ALI developmental levels. The exercise distinguishes between failures inherited from authority figures (parents, grandparents) and failures the participant has made personally, categorizing each by developmental level with associated costs of failure.

## Key Framework

### Failure-Level Mapping

| Level | Thinking | Category | Subcategory | Costs of Failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Inspired | Developmental | Developmental | Lost Potential |
| 6 | Systems | Technical | Systemic | Negative Habits |
| 5 | Analytical | Technical | Analytical | Promotions, Pride |
| 4 | Self Protective | Technical | Concrete | Time, Money |
| 3 | Codependent | Moral | Codependent | Rejection, Guilt |
| 2 | Us vs. Them | Moral | Social | Prison, Shame |
| 1 | All or Nothing | Moral | Survival | Death, Disability |

### Exercise Protocol

The exercise proceeds in two phases:
1. **Inherited failures**: Map failures made by parents, grandparents, or authority figures — experienced as trauma
2. **Personal failures**: Map failures the participant has made themselves

Key instructions emphasize this is an incremental, gentle process approached with curiosity and compassion rather than shame and judgment. It is explicitly "NOT something to power through." The exercise asks after each entry whether the participant can meet the failure with curiosity and compassion before proceeding.

### Core Insight

Lower-level problems carry higher existential costs (death, disability, prison at Levels 1–2) while higher-level failures represent lost potential and negative habits. Most people carry stored trauma ("big rocks") from lower-level failures that amplify the pain of smaller current failures.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Diagnostic-Therapeutic Unity

This exercise is a paradigm case of Randy's key observation about Whetten's methodology: **the assessment IS the intervention**. The act of categorizing one's failures by developmental level simultaneously (a) reveals where one's awareness habitually stops (the level at which failures become too painful to examine), and (b) creates the training entry point for expanding awareness to include that level. The "stopping point" is both the diagnostic finding and the therapeutic target.

### Constraint-Release Grief Loop Activation

The exercise's two-column structure (inherited vs. personal failures) directly maps to the SRL constraint-release grief loop concept. Inherited failures represent constraints that were introjected before the individual had the developmental capacity to process them. The exercise creates a structured container for grief release — the participant meets stored pain with compassion, which is the release mechanism. The instruction to approach with "curiosity and compassion rather than shame and judgment" mirrors the SRL principle that autonomic safety is prerequisite to interoceptive access.

### Intergenerational Trauma as Autonomic Inheritance

The exercise's explicit framing of parental/authority failures as "pain stored inside" aligns with the SRL evidence base on intergenerational transmission of autonomic patterns. The CRNA who carries unprocessed inherited trauma operates with a narrowed autonomic bandwidth — their polyvagal system defaults to defensive states more readily because the stored "big rocks" lower the threshold for threat detection.

### Progressive Overload Model

The incremental, non-forcing approach ("come back to repeatedly") mirrors the contemplative progressive overload principle in SRL methodology. Just as physical training requires progressive loading with adequate recovery, awareness training requires graduated exposure to stored failures with adequate self-compassion between iterations.

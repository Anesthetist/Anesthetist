# Resonance Frequency Biofeedback (Lehrer 2000)

## Summary

This seminal paper by Paul Lehrer and colleagues at Rutgers established the theoretical rationale and clinical protocol for resonance frequency biofeedback training. The core finding is that each individual has a resonant frequency (typically around 0.1 Hz, or approximately 6 breaths per minute) at which breathing produces maximal oscillations in heart rate via the baroreflex loop. Training individuals to breathe at their personal resonant frequency produces large increases in heart rate variability and improvements in baroreflex gain. This work is foundational to the entire field of HRV biofeedback and underpins the breathing protocols used in Pausality.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Each individual has a unique resonant frequency for cardiorespiratory coupling, typically near 0.1 Hz
- Breathing at resonant frequency maximally stimulates the baroreflex, producing large-amplitude HR oscillations
- Regular training at resonant frequency increases resting HRV and baroreflex sensitivity over time
- The protocol is learnable in 10 sessions and effects persist with practice
- Resonance frequency breathing produces measurable autonomic shifts within a single session

## Top 3 Stories That Matter for SRL

**1. NHS Staff Refusing to Use Palantir's Federated Data Platform (335 pts)**
Healthcare workers are staging a quiet revolt — senior data analysts and clinicians are officially refusing to engage with the £330M Palantir-built FDP on ethical grounds. This is the clearest signal yet that clinician trust is the bottleneck for health-tech adoption, not capability. Pausality's thesis — that tools must be *for* clinicians, not *on* clinicians — lands perfectly here. When your platform is built by a CRNA who's lived the burnout, the trust gap closes. This story is ammunition for every enterprise sales conversation about why clinician-designed matters.

**2. FDA's 2026 Wellness Device Update Redraws the Wearable Line**
The FDA clarified the distinction between "medical information" and "signals/patterns" — non-calibrated fitness trackers can now detect patterns warranting closer examination (arrhythmia indicators, low SpO2) without triggering full medical device classification. This is a regulatory green light for Pausality's HRV biofeedback approach. Interoceptive training tools that surface *patterns* rather than *diagnoses* just got a wider lane. The compliance burden for somatic intelligence wearables dropped meaningfully.

**3. "Claude Code and the Great Productivity Panic of 2026" (HN discussion)**
A thread exploring how AI-augmented coding sessions leave people "mentally exhausted from the sheer speed and volume of actions and decisions" — even as productivity metrics soar. This is burnout repackaged for knowledge workers. The same autonomic dysregulation that hits clinicians after a 12-hour shift is now hitting developers after 3-hour agentic coding sprints. Pausality's interoception framework isn't just for healthcare anymore — the TAM for somatic recovery is expanding into tech.

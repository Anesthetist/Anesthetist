## Citation

Little, et al. (2026). Examining the effectiveness of breathwork to improve resilience and psychological wellbeing while reducing anxiety, depression, stress, and insomnia in paramedicine students: A single-blind randomised controlled trial. *Stress and Health*. https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.70161

## Key Findings

- The A52 Breath Method (12-week structured breathwork protocol) evaluated in paramedicine students — a population with disproportionately high rates of anxiety, depression, stress, and insomnia.
- Study design is informed by **polyvagal theory** and neurovisceral integration theory, targeting the autonomic nervous system as the primary mechanism of change.
- Outcomes assessed: anxiety, depression, insomnia, stress, resilience, and psychological wellbeing.
- First RCT to apply a structured breathwork protocol specifically in a first-responder student population.

## Relevance to SRL

Strong direct relevance to SRL's Clinician Durability and Gap Moment Training frameworks — demonstrating that brief, structured breathwork protocols can serve as prehabilitative mental hygiene tools for high-stress clinical trainees. The polyvagal theory grounding aligns with SRL's foundational theoretical framework. The 12-week protocol structure is consistent with SRL's interest in Minimum Effective Dose and iterative self-regulation skill building.

## Methodology

- Single-blind randomised controlled trial.
- Population: paramedicine students with high baseline stress/burnout indicators.
- Intervention: A52 Breath Method, 12-week structured breathwork protocol.
- Outcomes: anxiety (GAD-7), depression (PHQ-9), insomnia (ISI), stress (PSS), resilience (BRS), and psychological wellbeing scales.

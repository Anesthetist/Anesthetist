# Breathing Instruction Reduces Medical Student Anxiety (Paul, Elam & Verhulst 2007)

## Summary

Breathing instruction for medical students produced measurable reductions in test anxiety, self-doubt, and concentration loss during examinations. Demonstrates that simple breathing training improves cognitive performance under evaluative stress in medical learners — the same population that will become the CRNAs, surgeons, and physicians who need somnistics training.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Breathing training reduces test anxiety in medical students (early career intervention)
- Improved concentration = cognitive performance under stress
- Reduced self-doubt = confidence building through physiological regulation
- Supports [[ptsd-prehabilitation-gap]]: training begins in medical school, not after years of practice

## Concepts Derived

- [[clinician-learns-by-experiencing]] — medical students learn regulation through direct practice
- [[gap-moment-training]] — exam preparation as a gap moment intervention

- [[awareness-before-technique]] — breathing instruction works even in untrained population

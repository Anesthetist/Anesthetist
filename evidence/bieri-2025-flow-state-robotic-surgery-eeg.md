## Summary

EEG study investigating the neural signatures of flow state during robotic virtual reality surgical operations, linking brain activity patterns to performance.

## Key Findings

- **Method:** EEG monitoring during robotic VR surgical tasks
- **Primary finding:** High-performing surgeons showed significantly higher EEG theta activity during flow states
- **Performance link:** Flow states were associated with both better performance AND enhanced well-being
- **Future direction:** Authors explicitly state that "neurofeedback and biofeedback could enhance surgical performance and well-being"
- **Implication:** Flow is an objectively measurable brain state in surgery, and it can potentially be trained via neurofeedback/biofeedback

## SRL Relevance

Provides the neural signature (theta activity) of optimal surgical performance states. The authors' explicit call for neurofeedback/biofeedback interventions validates the NeuroHarmonics approach. Bridges the gap between subjective flow experience and objective brain-state measurement in surgical contexts.

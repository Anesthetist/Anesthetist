## Overview

A single-page awareness exercise that reframes judgment as a developmental tool serving six legitimate purposes — each of which can be accomplished through more evolved means. The exercise maps the relationship between leadership capacity, responsibility level, and judgment dependency.

## Key Framework

### Core Premise
Every part of every person is always trying to achieve a positive purpose the best way it knows how. This includes the parts that create judgment, pain, and fear. Judgment is not pathological — it is a developmental strategy that can be upgraded.

### Leadership-Responsibility Gap
Where leadership capacity exceeds responsibility, judgment can be released because the person has developed sufficient skill to meet their needs without it. Where responsibility exceeds leadership, judgment persists as a compensatory strategy — the nervous system uses judgment because it lacks more evolved tools.

### Six Purposes of Judgment (Capacities Rated 1–10)
Each capacity is rated on the ability to perform it **without using judgment as a tool or fuel**:

1. **Needs**: Getting needs met consistently and effectively
2. **Boundaries**: Adjusting boundaries to the situation and holding them effectively
3. **Reality**: Staying clear on what's real (feelings, beliefs) when others disagree
4. **Self Control**: Controlling impulses and avoiding harmful actions
5. **Motivation**: Motivating yourself on a regular and consistent basis
6. **Understanding**: Evaluating yourself accurately; discerning how strengths and challenges match roles and responsibilities

### Two-Context Comparison
Participants rate these six capacities in two contrasting domains: one where they feel competent (leadership > responsibility) and one where they feel stressed/overwhelmed (responsibility > leadership). The gap reveals where judgment is still functioning as scaffolding.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Judgment as Autonomic Scaffolding

The exercise reveals judgment as a nervous system strategy — when capacity is insufficient, judgment provides compensatory structure (self-criticism as motivation, moral judgment as impulse control, reality-testing through certainty). This maps directly to the SRL concept of autonomic home base: a clinician whose autonomic bandwidth is narrow relies on judgment-based strategies (rigid protocols, binary thinking, defensive certainty) to manage clinical complexity. As interoceptive capacity expands, judgment scaffolding can be progressively released.

### Six Capacities as Clinical Competency Map

The six judgment purposes map onto core CRNA competencies: meeting one's own needs during long cases (Needs), maintaining professional boundaries under pressure (Boundaries), clinical reality-testing when colleagues disagree (Reality), impulse control during emergencies (Self Control), sustaining motivation across a career (Motivation), and accurate self-assessment of clinical capability (Understanding). The exercise provides a direct assessment pathway for clinician self-development.

### Capacity Gap as Training Target

The two-context comparison (comfortable vs. overwhelmed) reveals exactly where the participant's awareness stops and judgment takes over. This IS the training entry point — the gap between the two ratings shows the developmental edge. The SRL structured interoception training protocol could target these specific gaps with tailored autonomic training exercises.

### Developmental Staging Without Hierarchy

The exercise avoids pathologizing judgment. Instead, it frames judgment as an earlier developmental strategy that served a purpose and can now be upgraded. This mirrors the SRL approach to clinician development: meet people where they are, honor the strategies that got them here, then offer more evolved alternatives.

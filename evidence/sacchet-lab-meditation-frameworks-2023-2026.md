# Sacchet Lab Contemplative Theory and Methodology Frameworks (2023–2026)

## Summary

This combined note covers framework, theory, and methodology papers from the Sacchet Lab that provide the intellectual infrastructure for studying advanced meditation. While these papers are less directly empirical than the lab's neuroimaging studies, they establish the conceptual and methodological foundations that shape how meditation research is designed, interpreted, and translated.

### Individual Papers

1. **Wright (2023)** — Methodology for contemplative science; addresses how to study meditation rigorously while honoring its experiential dimensions.

2. **Sparby (2024)** — Phenomenological approaches to meditation research; argues for integrating first-person methods with neuroscience.

3. **Sparby (2025a, Mindfulness journal)** — Review article on the current state of mindfulness research, identifying gaps and future directions.

4. **Sparby (2025b, MEND)** — The MEND (Meditative and Experiential Neurophenomenological Development) framework; proposes a developmental stage model for meditative expertise integrating neural, phenomenological, and behavioral dimensions.

5. **Galante (2023)** — Methodological review addressing RCT design, active controls, and outcome measurement in meditation research.

6. **Tal (2026a, Active Inference)** — Applies the active inference framework (predictive processing, free energy minimization) to meditation, proposing that meditation works by restructuring the brain's generative model of self and world.

7. **Tal (2026b, Neurofeedback Review)** — Reviews neurofeedback as a tool for studying and enhancing meditation, synthesizing evidence across modalities (fMRI, EEG, fNIRS).

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- The MEND framework provides a developmental stage model compatible with SRL's titrated dosing approach
- Active inference models explain meditation as restructuring the brain's predictive model — relevant to anterocept and interoceptive prediction
- Rigorous methodology (active controls, standardized outcomes) is essential for clinical credibility
- Phenomenological methods complement neuroscience for understanding subjective experience of breathwork
- Neurofeedback can accelerate meditation learning and provide objective markers of progress
- The field is moving toward integrated neurophenomenological approaches — first-person experience + third-person measurement
- Stage models of meditative development support progressive curriculum design (NeuroMinute progression)

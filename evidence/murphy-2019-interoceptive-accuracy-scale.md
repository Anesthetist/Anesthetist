## Summary

Murphy et al. (2019) developed the Interoceptive Accuracy Scale (IAS), a 21-item self-report of beliefs about interoceptive accuracy. Published in *Journal of Clinical Psychology* 76(12):2207-2220.

## Key Findings

1. 21 items measuring self-reported interoceptive accuracy beliefs
2. Unidimensional scale
3. Distinct from interoceptive attention (measured by companion IATS)
4. Validated across 6 studies
5. Helps parse whether someone BELIEVES they're accurate vs. whether they ARE accurate

## SRL Relevance

Useful for measuring the metacognitive dimension of [[interoceptive-literacy]] — do users believe they can read their body signals? This belief dimension may predict treatment engagement.

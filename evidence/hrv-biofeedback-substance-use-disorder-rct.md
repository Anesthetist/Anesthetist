## Citation

(2024/2025). Heart rate variability biofeedback for substance use disorder: A randomized clinical trial. *JAMA Psychiatry*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41032322/

## Key Findings

- Second-generation wearable HRV biofeedback associated with a **64% reduction in alcohol or other drug use days** compared to controls (OR = 0.36) over 8 weeks.
- HRVB significantly reduced negative affect and craving; the control group experienced increases in both over the same period.
- Second-generation HRVB is wearable, affordable, clinician-independent, and can detect stress in real-time — prompting brief resonant breathing episodes to offset acute stress.
- N = 120 randomized participants; phase 2 trial results support phase 3 investigation.

## Relevance to SRL

Landmark evidence for SRL's Minimum Effective Dose principle — brief, real-time-prompted HRV biofeedback delivered via wearable produces profound behavioral outcomes. Supports Resonant Breathing Frequency, Vagal Tone, and Autonomic Regulation concepts. The craving reduction mechanism is consistent with SRL's Gap Moment Training model (creating physiological pauses during high-arousal moments), and has direct implications for clinician stress management and Clinician Durability.

## Methodology

- Phase 2 randomized clinical trial.
- N = 120 (of 260 assessed); individuals in early recovery from substance use disorder.
- 8-week intervention with wearable second-generation HRV biofeedback device.
- Outcomes: negative affect, positive affect, craving, substance use days.

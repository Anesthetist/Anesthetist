## Citation

Grossman, P. et al. (2026). Why the Polyvagal Theory is Untenable. *Clinical Neuropsychiatry*. (Co-authored with 38 ANS scholars.)

Porges, S. W. et al. (2026). When A Critique Becomes Untenable: A Scholarly Response to Grossman et al.'s Evaluation of Polyvagal Theory. *PMC*.

## Key Findings

- Grossman et al. published a comprehensive critique co-signed by 38 autonomic nervous system scholars challenging PVT's evolutionary, neurophysiological, and anatomical claims
- Key criticisms: questions about the phylogenetic hierarchy of vagal circuits, the specificity of RSA as a vagal tone index, and the anatomical precision of the ventral vagal complex concept
- Porges and colleagues issued a substantial rebuttal defending the theory's core framework while acknowledging areas requiring refinement
- Despite the debate, clinical interventions derived from PVT (e.g., Safe and Sound Protocol) continue to show measurable improvements in regulation and social behavior

## Relevance to SRL

Critical for SRL to track this debate closely. SRL's framework draws on polyvagal concepts (neuroception, co-regulation, vagal tone, ventral vagal activation). The nuances of this debate inform how SRL should position its theoretical claims — focusing on clinically validated interventions while being precise about underlying mechanisms. The rebuttal suggests PVT remains a useful clinical heuristic even if some evolutionary claims require updating.

## Methodology

Theoretical critique paper (38 co-authors) vs. scholarly rebuttal. Published 2026 in Clinical Neuropsychiatry and PMC respectively.

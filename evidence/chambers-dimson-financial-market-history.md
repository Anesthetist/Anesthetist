Chambers and Dimson provide a deep historical analysis of financial market behavior across centuries and geographies. The long-duration data reveals patterns of mean reversion, regime change, and tail risk that are invisible in shorter time series.

**Somnistic Relevance**: Long-duration pattern recognition is essential for non-ergodic risk management. Understanding multi-century market cycles provides the same kind of contextual awareness that studying the history of anesthesia provides for clinical practice -- knowing what has failed before, and why, changes how you read the present.

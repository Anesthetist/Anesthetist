## Overview
Van den Bergh et al. present the first validated paradigm for measuring respiratory interoceptive accuracy. The task uses an adaptive staircase procedure to detect small differences in respiratory occlusion, providing psychophysical threshold and discrimination measures. This fills a critical gap in the interoception literature, where respiratory interoception was previously under-measured relative to cardiac interoception.

## Key Findings
1. Adaptive staircase procedure detecting small differences in respiratory occlusion — parallels the HRD task for cardiac interoception
2. Acceptable reliability: Cronbach's alpha = 0.70, test-retest correlation r = 0.53
3. Respiratory interoception is measurable and distinct from cardiac interoception — the two modalities are dissociable
4. Provides both psychophysical threshold and discrimination measures, enabling precise quantification of respiratory body awareness
5. Fills a critical gap in the field — respiratory interoception was previously under-measured despite being central to breathwork interventions

## Connection to Somnistics
This is the measure that validates SRL's core mechanism. Since SRL's interventions are breathwork-based ([[resonant-breathing-frequency]], [[gap-moment-training]]), respiratory interoceptive accuracy is the most direct outcome measure. SRL's BOLT score needs validation against this paradigm to establish construct validity. As SRL scales clinical evidence (Red Arrow 1), this task should be incorporated into pilot study protocols.

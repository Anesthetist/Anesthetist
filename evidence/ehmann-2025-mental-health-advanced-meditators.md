# Mental Health, Well-Being, and Meditative Development (Ehmann, Sparby, Naughton & Sacchet 2025)

## Summary

This phenomenological study interviews 28 advanced meditators with a mean of approximately 20,435 lifetime practice hours, making it the first large-scale qualitative investigation of mental health outcomes in highly experienced practitioners. Participants report resolution of clinical conditions — anxiety, PTSD, addiction, ADHD — through sustained meditation practice, often in conjunction with psychotherapy. The study maps the developmental arc of meditative expertise: early stages focus on symptom relief and emotional regulation, middle stages involve deeper self-understanding and pattern recognition, and advanced stages reflect a fundamental reorganization of self-experience. Critically for SRL, the paper documents synergies between meditation and psychotherapy, validating the clinical model where breathwork and interoceptive training complement therapeutic intervention rather than replacing it.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Advanced meditators report resolution of anxiety, PTSD, addiction, and ADHD through sustained practice
- Meditation and psychotherapy show synergistic rather than competitive effects
- The developmental arc of meditation follows a predictable trajectory from symptom relief to fundamental self-reorganization
- Even highly experienced meditators continue to evolve — practice mastery is asymptotic, not binary
- Clinicians who meditate report enhanced clinical capacity and reduced burnout
- The phenomenological data supports a stage model compatible with titrated dosing
- Mental health benefits consolidate over thousands of hours but begin early in practice

## Citation

Little, et al. (2026). Examining the effectiveness of breathwork to improve resilience and psychological wellbeing while reducing anxiety, depression, stress, and insomnia in paramedicine students: A single-blind randomised controlled trial. *Stress and Health*. https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.70161

## Key Findings

- The A52 Breath Method, a structured breathwork protocol, improved mental health and resilience among student paramedics
- Suboptimal breathing patterns (excessive effort, chest tension, over-breathing) may reduce the calming effects of slow breathing — highlighting the importance of proper technique instruction
- Qualitative findings: participants described breathwork as a self-regulation tool supporting emotional and physiological state management
- Breathwork facilitated emotional regulation and fostered a sense of psychological safety and perceived control

## Relevance to SRL

Directly supports **Resonant Breathing Frequency**, **Diaphragmatic Literacy**, and **Clinician Durability** concepts. The finding about suboptimal breathing patterns reducing efficacy validates SRL's emphasis on proper technique training (not just "breathe slowly") — this is the **Diaphragmatic Blindness** concept in action. The paramedicine population is closely adjacent to SRL's target clinician populations. The psychological safety and perceived control outcomes align with **Gap Moment Training** principles.

## Methodology

Single-blind randomized controlled trial. Paramedicine students. Intervention: A52 Breath Method (structured breathwork protocol). Outcomes: resilience, anxiety, depression, stress, insomnia, plus qualitative interviews. Published in *Stress and Health* (Wiley).

## Citation

(2026). Understanding interoception across emotional contexts: Development and validation of the Emotion-Linked Interoceptive Awareness Scale. *Frontiers in Psychology*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1757948

## Key Findings

- Developed and validated the ELIA scale, a self-report measure assessing awareness of bodily sensations under specific emotional contexts, through four online studies
- ELIA Positive Emotions subscale scores were associated with lower trait anxiety and alexithymia, while Anxiety and Irritation subscale scores were associated with higher levels of these traits
- Mind-body practitioners scored higher on the ELIA, particularly on the Positive Emotions subscale, in matched comparisons
- The ELIA provides an emotion-specific assessment that may distinguish adaptive from maladaptive attentional styles of interoceptive sensibility
- Extends the attentional style framework by demonstrating that interoceptive awareness is modulated by emotional state
- Emphasizes the foundational role of bodily signals in generating, regulating, and experiencing emotion

## Relevance to SRL

Directly advances SRL's [[interoceptive-literacy]] framework by providing a validated instrument that differentiates *how* people attend to bodily signals across emotional contexts. The adaptive vs. maladaptive distinction aligns with SRL's emphasis on developing skillful interoceptive awareness rather than raw sensitivity. Could inform MAIA-2-CRNA adaptations and [[gap-moment-training]] assessment. The ELIA could become a validated assessment instrument for SRL interventions alongside MAIA-2.

## Methodology

Four online validation studies using self-report measures; psychometric analysis of the ELIA scale's factor structure, reliability, and convergent validity with existing interoceptive awareness measures (including MAIA-2). Compared mind-body practitioners vs. non-practitioners; assessed convergent validity with trait anxiety, alexithymia.

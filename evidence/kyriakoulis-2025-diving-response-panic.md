# Diving Response and CO2 Sensitivity in Panic Disorder Patients

## Citation
Kyriakoulis P, Caballero A. *Frontiers in Psychiatry*. 2025;16:1533019.
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1533019

## Study Design
Clinical trial comparing cold facial immersion (CFI) — which triggers the mammalian diving reflex — in panic disorder (PD) patients versus healthy controls, following a CO2 challenge.

## Key Findings

1. **Physiological markers did not change** — Heart rate and respiration rate did not significantly decrease after CFI in either group following the CO2 challenge. (Caveat: small sample size)

2. **Psychological markers DID change** — Significant reductions in both physiological and cognitive symptoms of panic were observed in the clinical group following CFI. Self-reported anxiety and panic symptoms decreased.

3. **Dissociation between physiology and perception** — The intervention didn't change the body's CO2 response; it changed the *relationship to that response*. The body still reacted, but the catastrophic appraisal was attenuated.

4. **Anxiolytic effects** — CFI demonstrated anxiolytic effects in PD patients, warranting further research with larger samples.

## SRL Relevance

**This is the interoceptive confidence paper.** It provides direct evidence for SRL's core thesis:

The critical variable isn't CO2 tolerance per se — it's the capacity to perceive physiological arousal without catastrophizing it. This is [[interoceptive-literacy]] in action: the trained individual reads the same body signals but interprets them differently.

**Mapping to clinical practice:** A CRNA with high interoceptive literacy can feel their own sympathetic activation during a difficult intubation without that feeling degrading their performance. They've learned that the sensation of rising CO2, elevated heart rate, and sympathetic drive is *information*, not threat. This paper provides the first clinical evidence that this decoupling of sensation from catastrophic appraisal is achievable through a simple physiological intervention.

**Mapping to the panic/performance spectrum:** Panic disorder sits at one end of the CO2 sensitivity spectrum. Elite free divers sit at the other. CRNAs operating under high-stakes cognitive load sit in between. SRL's training moves clinicians toward the freediver end — not by suppressing the signal, but by changing the appraisal.

## Citation

Baylor Scott & White Research Institute. (2026). Building resilience with heart rate variability biofeedback (HRVB) in emergency department and ICU healthcare workers: A randomized controlled trial. *PubMed*. Published February 24, 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41734245/

## Key Findings

- Resilience (CD-RISC-10) increased significantly in the HRVB group relative to the waitlist control from pre- to posttreatment (Cohen's d = 0.79, p = .003)
- Resilience gains were maintained at follow-up assessment
- Reductions in stress, depression, and burnout were not significantly greater in HRVB vs. waitlist, suggesting HRVB may specifically target resilience rather than broad symptom reduction
- HRVB was deemed feasible as an individually delivered intervention for ED and ICU healthcare providers

## Relevance to SRL

Directly supports SRL's **Clinician Durability** concept — demonstrating that HRV biofeedback can build resilience in high-acuity clinical environments (ED/ICU). The finding that HRVB targets resilience specifically (rather than broad symptom reduction) aligns with SRL's emphasis on proactive prehabilitative approaches rather than reactive burnout treatment. Also supports the **Autonomic Regulation** and **Vagal Tone** frameworks as mechanisms for building clinician capacity.

## Methodology

Randomized controlled trial with waitlist control. Participants were ED and ICU healthcare workers at Baylor Scott & White. HRVB was delivered individually. Outcomes measured with CD-RISC-10 (resilience), plus stress, depression, and burnout scales. Included follow-up assessment for durability of effects.

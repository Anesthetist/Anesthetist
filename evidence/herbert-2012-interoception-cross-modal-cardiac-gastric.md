## Overview
Herbert et al. investigate whether interoceptive sensitivity generalizes across organ systems by examining the relationship between cardiac awareness and gastric interoception. Using heartbeat detection and a Water Load Test, the study finds that good heartbeat perceivers drink less water before reaching satiation, suggesting a shared interoceptive sensitivity across cardiac and gastric modalities. The findings partially support the idea of a general interoceptive trait.

## Key Findings
1. Good heartbeat perceivers drink less water before reaching satiation — cardiac interoceptive accuracy predicts gastric sensitivity
2. Cardiac awareness inversely correlates with water intake volume, suggesting cross-modal interoceptive coupling
3. Results partially support the idea of a general interoceptive sensitivity spanning multiple organ systems
4. Uses the Water Load Test as a validated gastric interoception measure, expanding the methodological toolkit
5. Supports (partially) the hypothesis that interoceptive training in one modality may generalize to others — but effect sizes are modest

## Connection to Somnistics
This provides partial evidence that improving cardiac interoception via HRV biofeedback may improve gastric interoception too — supporting SRL's whole-body approach to [[interoceptive-literacy]]. However, Garfinkel 2015 shows that interoceptive dimensions (accuracy, sensibility, awareness) are dissociable, so SRL should be cautious with generalization claims. Training [[resonant-breathing-frequency]] likely improves cardiac and respiratory interoception most directly.

# HN Intel Scan — 2026-04-10

## Top 3 Stories That Matter for SRL

**1. JAMA Scribe Study Decouples Time-Savings From Burnout Relief (STAT, Apr 1; Boston Globe, Apr 1)**
The largest ambient-AI-scribe study yet — led by Mass General Brigham — found only **~16 minutes/day** in documentation time savings (one extra patient every two weeks), yet prior MGB/Emory work still logged a **21% drop in self-reported burnout**. That is the signal: the burnout relief is *not* coming from time saved. It is coming from attentional restoration — clinicians looking at patients instead of screens. This is Pausality's exact thesis territory. If the scribe market's efficacy story is quietly shifting from "we give you time back" to "we give you presence back," Pausality has a 60-day window to plant a flag on *presence as the actual clinical primitive* before ambient-AI vendors reframe around it and eat the vocabulary.

**2. UnitedHealth's $3B AI Bet Lands While NPR Covers Workforce Pushback (STAT, Apr 6; NPR, Apr 7)**
UHG is pouring $3B into clinical AI at the exact moment NPR is documenting fear and resistance from the mental-health workforce — plus UCSF opening a formal study on rising AI-linked delusions. The narrative is bifurcating: hyperscalers bet on AI-as-clinician while the clinical front line reports psychological harm, moral injury, and loss of craft. Pausality sits cleanly on the "protect the clinician as the human primitive" side. This is a messaging gift — Randy can cite UHG's capex as the inflection point where clinicians became *users of AI on themselves*, not just deployers of AI on patients.

**3. "2026, the Last Year of the AI Bubble" Front-Pages HN**
A widely-discussed HN thread arguing that 2026 is the terminal year of the AI capex supercycle, pairing with the TechCrunch piece on AI companies racing to secure natural-gas generation for data centers. Translation for SRL: capital will soon flee undifferentiated AI-wrapper plays and hunt for *durable human outcomes*. Clinician durability — measurable, physiologically anchored, defensible — is exactly the kind of "boring revenue with a moat" that survives a deflation.

## EU vs US Sentiment Delta

Europe is tightening while arguing with itself. Amnesty International publicly warned this week that the EU's "simplification" package rolls back digital rights to feed AI — a rare split inside the pro-regulation camp. Meanwhile the AI Act's August 2 full-effect date is now <120 days out, and the AI Office is pre-positioning audit regimes for high-risk healthcare systems. The US side ran the opposite play: Trump's National AI Framework (March) pushing federal preemption, DOJ's AI Litigation Task Force already challenging state laws, and Q1 venture flow chasing AI seed rounds at bubble multiples. The delta: Europe is arguing about *how much* to regulate healthcare AI; the US is arguing about *whether* it can be regulated at all. Pausality's clinician-governed, evidence-chained architecture is pre-compliant for the EU regime and narratively defensible for the US one — a rare both-sides fit.

## Chaos Signal

**The scribe study quietly killed the "time back = burnout down" equation, and nobody has processed it yet.** Every investor deck, every hospital CFO model, every AI-wellness startup built since 2024 assumed documentation-time reduction was the mechanism. The JAMA data says it isn't. In 90 days someone will publish the reframe — burnout is an *attentional/interoceptive* injury, not a clock injury — and whoever owns that language will own the next funding cycle. Randy should own it first.

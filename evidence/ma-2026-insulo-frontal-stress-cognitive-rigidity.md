# Insulo-Frontal Pathway and Stress-Evoked Cognitive Rigidity (Ma, Wang & Zuo 2026)

## Summary

Published as a *bioRxiv* preprint (February 2026), this study demonstrates that projections from the anterior insular cortex (aIC) to the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) regulate adaptive decision-making. Stress disrupts this pathway, leading to cognitive rigidity — the inability to update behavior after errors. Optogenetic reinforcement of the aIC-to-mPFC pathway after errors restores behavioral flexibility, providing causal evidence for the insula's role in stress-resilient cognition.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Anterior insular cortex → medial prefrontal cortex pathway is essential for adaptive decision-making
- Stress specifically disrupts insulo-frontal communication, causing cognitive rigidity
- Cognitive rigidity under stress = failure to update behavior after errors
- Optogenetic restoration of this pathway restores flexibility — causal evidence
- The insula (primary interoceptive cortex) is directly involved in cognitive flexibility, not just body-awareness

## SRL Relevance

Preprint, but mechanistically important. Provides the neural circuit for why stressed clinicians make rigid decisions — the interoceptive cortex-to-prefrontal pathway fails under stress. This is the exact circuit that Gap Moment Training aims to maintain: preserving the insula-to-mPFC communication that enables error-responsive behavioral updating. PMID: 41727096.

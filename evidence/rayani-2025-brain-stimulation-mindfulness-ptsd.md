# Brain Stimulation Enhances Dispositional Mindfulness in PTSD (Rayani et al. 2025)

## Summary

This study demonstrates that rTMS targeting the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) enhances dispositional mindfulness in patients with PTSD. This is significant because PTSD involves dysregulated autonomic function, impaired interoception, and difficulty with the attentional self-regulation that mindfulness requires. By using brain stimulation to boost DLPFC function, the intervention primes the top-down regulatory circuits that mindfulness practice engages — essentially lowering the barrier to entry for mindfulness in trauma populations. For SRL, this is relevant to the clinician-durability concept and the clinical pathway: it suggests that neuromodulation could serve as an adjunct to breathwork training in populations where autonomic dysregulation is severe enough to impede standard practice.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- rTMS targeting DLPFC enhances dispositional mindfulness in PTSD patients
- Top-down neuromodulation can facilitate attentional capacities that mindfulness requires
- PTSD represents a population where standard mindfulness entry points may be insufficient
- Brain stimulation lowers the barrier to mindfulness engagement in trauma populations
- DLPFC is a key node in the top-down regulation of autonomic and emotional responses
- The finding supports a multimodal approach: neuromodulation + breathwork + therapy
- Dispositional (trait) mindfulness was the outcome — not just state-level change during stimulation

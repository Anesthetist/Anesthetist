# The Skill of Capital

## Core Definition

Capital expertise consists of three interconnected competencies:
1. **Knowing how to get capital** — understanding sources, costs, and appropriate amounts
2. **Knowing how to deploy capital** — allocating resources to highest-return opportunities
3. **Understanding implications** — recognizing how capital structure shapes overall strategy

## Three Case Studies

### Microsoft — Self-Capitalization
Software has zero marginal distribution cost. High gross margins enabled reinvestment. Gates maintained a year's worth of operating expenses in reserves. Rejected external capital from rational analysis, not ideology. **Lesson:** Businesses with favorable unit economics can fund growth without dilution.

### Constellation Software — Capital Structure Fits Strategy
Mark Leonard identified vertical market software as ideal: critical to operations, small % of customer spend (pricing power), niche markets unattractive to large competitors. **Innovation:** Permanent capital vehicle for acquiring cash-flowing businesses, enabling long-term compounding without exit pressure. Result: ~34% CAGR for 17 years post-IPO, 130x returns.

### Salim Group — Illicit Capital Access
Indonesian conglomerate gained dominance through government subsidies on wheat and guaranteed buyers. Capital advantage persisted 20+ years. **Lesson:** Understanding capital structures (even unsavory ones) is essential for competing in certain geographies.

## Five Capital Allocation Options

When a business generates free cash flow:
1. Reinvest in existing initiatives (if ROIC > cost of capital)
2. Pay down debt
3. Acquire other companies
4. Issue dividends
5. Buy back stock

Most operators reflexively reinvest without analyzing returns. **Superior capital allocators treat the business as "a box that provides value to customers and spits cash out the other end."**

## The Paradox

Business managers must "reason like investors" — understanding the temporal mismatch between cash outflows (today) and inflows (tomorrow), evaluating cost of capital, recognizing diminishing returns.

**SRL's capital structure decision is live:**
- F&F SAFE round ($5.5M post-money cap, $250K target, $100K secured)
- Low burn rate enables extended runway
- CE course revenue can create a Constellation-like reinvestment loop: CE income → product development → more CE content → more income

**The Constellation model resonates:** Vertical market software for niche professional communities (CRNAs → other clinical specialties) mirrors Leonard's acquisition thesis. SRL could build a portfolio of clinical specialty training products, each cash-flowing independently.

**ROIC question for SRL:** Where does the next dollar produce the highest return? CE course development? App features? Enterprise sales? Marketing? The capital allocation framework says: measure return on each, deploy to the highest.

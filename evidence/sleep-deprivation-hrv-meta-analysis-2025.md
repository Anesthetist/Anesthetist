## Citation

Authors (2025). Effects of sleep deprivation on heart rate variability: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Frontiers in Neurology*.

- **DOI:** 10.3389/fneur.2025.1556784
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1556784/full

## Background

This systematic review and meta-analysis examines the effects of sleep deprivation on HRV, covering studies from January 2010 to May 2024.

## Key Findings

1. Sleep deprivation impairs cardiac autonomic function as evidenced by **decreased RMSSD** (vagal withdrawal)
2. **Increased LF and LF/HF ratio**, suggesting sympathetic predominance and vagal suppression
3. Restoration of autonomic homeostasis requires at least **three recovery nights** following multiple nights of sleep curtailed to ~50% of normal
4. Within the first hour of recovery sleep, vagal activation shows a rapid increase reaching its 24-hour peak
5. HF-HRV rebound is more pronounced on the second recovery night than during baseline

## SRL Relevance

**Critical for CRNA shift work and Pausality's recovery protocols:**

- CRNAs regularly experience sleep disruption from call schedules and shift work — this meta-analysis quantifies the autonomic cost
- The 3-night recovery requirement has direct implications for scheduling recommendations and the CRNA Shift Architect concept
- The rapid vagal rebound during initial recovery sleep suggests a window for HRV-guided recovery optimization
- Supports the idea that sleep quality monitoring should be part of Pausality's Somnistics Readiness Battery

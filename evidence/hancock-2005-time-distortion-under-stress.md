## Citation

Hancock, P.A., & Weaver, J.L. (2005). On time distortion under stress. UCF Technical Report / Multiple publications. Peter Hancock, University of Central Florida.

## Key Findings

- Under extreme stress, time perception distorts bidirectionally: subjective slowing during the event (time-in-passing) and retrospective overestimation afterward (time recollection)
- Both directions are adaptive. Subjective slowing allows more processing within a critical window. Retrospective overestimation reflects richer memory encoding of salient events
- Stress drains attentional resources AND prevents efficient resource production. Remaining resources get directed to task-relevant activity, minimizing attention to temporal cues
- The contextual theory proposes that temporal distortion is not a single mechanism but depends on the interaction between stress intensity, task demands, and available attentional resources
- "Hours of boredom, moments of terror": military temporal experience oscillates between two extremes, each producing different temporal distortion patterns

## Relevance to SRL

Hancock's contextual theory maps directly onto the clinical shift. CRNAs experience both poles: sustained low-arousal vigilance during stable maintenance (temporal dissolution, hours vanishing) and acute high-arousal emergencies (temporal compression, "everything happened so fast"). Both are temporal distortions with performance consequences.

The contextual theory predicts that any intervention restoring attentional resources should restore temporal accuracy. This is what the 4-breath reset does: by activating the parasympathetic pathway, it replenishes the attentional resource pool, allowing temporal processing to resume normal function. Hancock arrived at this from stress research. Wittmann arrived at it from interoception research. The convergence strengthens both.

Tactical breathing (box breathing, 4-4-4-4) is standard military doctrine derived from this research tradition. SRL's resonant breathing protocol (4-7 at 5.5 breaths/min) is the clinical-grade version optimized for vagal stimulation rather than just arousal reduction.

## Methodology

Laboratory and field studies of temporal estimation under controlled stress conditions. Military and law enforcement populations. Combines psychophysical temporal judgment tasks with physiological stress measures.

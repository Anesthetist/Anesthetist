## Overview

Magnon et al. (2021) compared the acute effects of a single session of deep and slow breathing (DSB) on vagal tone and anxiety in older adults (n=22) versus younger adults (n=25). The study measured high-frequency HRV (HF power) and state anxiety before and after the breathing intervention, revealing that older adults showed equal or greater benefits from a single session.

## Key Findings

1. **Compared deep slow breathing (DSB) in older (n=22) vs younger (n=25) adults**, providing a direct age-group comparison of acute breathing intervention effects.
2. **DSB increased HF power (vagal tone marker) in both age groups**, confirming parasympathetic activation from slow breathing across the lifespan.
3. **HF power increase was HIGHER in older adults** than younger adults, demonstrating an enhanced vagal response in the aging population.
4. **Reduced state anxiety in both groups**, establishing dual physiological and psychological benefits from a single session.
5. **Older adults benefit MORE from slow breathing** than younger adults, overturning assumptions about diminished responsiveness with age.

## Overview

Maslach and Jackson (1981) introduced the Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI), a 22-item measure that became the most widely used instrument for assessing occupational burnout globally. Published in the *Journal of Occupational Behaviour*, the MBI defines burnout as a multi-dimensional syndrome comprising emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and reduced personal accomplishment — establishing the measurement standard still used in healthcare research over four decades later.

## Key Findings

1. 22-item measure with three subscales: Emotional Exhaustion (EE), Depersonalization (DP), and Personal Accomplishment (PA)
2. Became the most widely used burnout instrument globally, with over 90% of burnout research citing the MBI
3. MBI-HSS (Human Services Survey) variant developed specifically for healthcare workers
4. Defines burnout as a syndrome — a continuous, multi-dimensional construct rather than a binary state
5. Licensed through Mind Garden (not free); requires ~$2.50 per administration for research use

## Connection to Somnistics

The MBI is the benchmark instrument for [[clinician-durability]] and burnout research. SRL enterprise pilots should use MBI-HSS for pre/post measurement to speak the language that investors and health systems expect. Using the MBI positions SRL's outcomes data within the existing evidence base, enabling direct comparison with other burnout interventions and establishing credibility with institutional buyers.

# ALI Leadership Skill: Upgrading Your Questions

## Framework Summary

Whetten presents a developmental model of emotional processing organized around five core questions, each corresponding to a distinct aspect of the self. The model provides a structured protocol for moving through emotional issues by sequentially engaging higher-order questions — with explicit warnings against bypassing lower levels.

### The Hole Model

At the center of every emotional issue is a "Hole" — a core of pain, fear, and judgment (visualized as a three-part diagram). This core gets wrapped in successive layers: stories of scarcity → addictions → defenses. Releasing an emotional issue requires working each layer using the Aware-Accept-Improve-Protect framework.

### Five Aspects of Self and Their Questions

Each aspect of the self asks a characteristic question when facing a problem, mapped to developmental stages:

| Stage | Aspect | Needs/Goals | Core Question |
|-------|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Taking | Body (id) | Survival | Why is this happening to me? |
| Pleasing | Conscience (superego) | Social Acceptance | Who is to blame? |
| Achieving | Mind (ego) | Success | How can I fix it? |
| Giving | Heart (love) | Fulfillment | How can I learn, grow and heal? |
| Receiving | Soul (higher self) | Abundance | How is this for me? |

The first two questions (red, orange) create and sustain judgment. The third and fourth (orange, green) address the problem and release judgment. The fifth (purple) opens the door to mature faith — seeing difficulty as purposeful.

### The Emotional Bypass Warning

Jumping directly to higher questions without processing through lower ones creates "emotional bypass" — spiritual or cognitive avoidance of genuine emotional processing. Conversely, remaining stuck in the lowest questions creates anxiety, depression, insecurity, and self-doubt. The key is titrated, sequential movement — staying with each question until it is metabolized before advancing.

### 5-Step Protocol

1. **Find A Hole** — identify a failure or trauma that still carries emotional energy
2. **Listen To Your Stories** — hear the narratives the mind has constructed
3. **Explore the First Two Questions** — sit with "Why is this happening to me?" and "Who is to blame?" with awareness and acceptance, noticing thoughts and body sensations
4. **Upgrade Through Questions 3-5** — move through "How can I fix it?" → "How can I learn, grow and heal?" → "How is this for me?" — each with a readiness check before advancing
5. **Pick Your Paradigm** — choose which question serves best given current developmental position, and practice deploying it when emotionally triggered

## Somnistic Relevance

This framework provides a developmental map of emotional processing that aligns precisely with several Somnistic principles:

- **Titration-to-effect as question navigation**: The sequential, readiness-gated movement through questions IS titration. The system processes at the level it can sustain — the readiness check between questions prevents both overwhelm (pushing too fast) and stagnation (staying too long). This mirrors the minimum effective dose principle in neurofeedback and interoceptive training.
- **Hole model as autonomic constraint architecture**: The nested layers (Story → Holes → Addictions → Defenses) map to the constraint layers that Somnistics addresses — defensive autonomic patterns (defenses), compensatory behaviors (addictions), cognitive narratives (stories), all protecting an unprocessed core of autonomic dysregulation (the Hole). The constraint-release-grief-loop operates at each layer.
- **Emotional bypass as premature integration**: Whetten's warning against jumping to higher questions is the psychological equivalent of attempting alpha-theta crossover work before establishing stable autonomic regulation. In neurofeedback terms: you cannot process theta-level material from an unstable alpha baseline. The sequential question protocol prevents this error.
- **Five-aspect model as vertical development map**: The Taking → Pleasing → Achieving → Giving → Receiving progression maps a developmental arc from survival-driven (reptile brain) through social conformity (limbic) to integrated wisdom (neocortex-whole person). This parallels the 7 Levels of Thinking model but with an emphasis on motivational orientation rather than cognitive complexity.
- **Diagnostic-therapeutic unity**: Each question simultaneously reveals where the individual IS developmentally (diagnostic) and provides the specific inquiry needed to metabolize that level's content (therapeutic). The question you cannot yet ask authentically IS the developmental edge — and the practice of asking it IS the training.

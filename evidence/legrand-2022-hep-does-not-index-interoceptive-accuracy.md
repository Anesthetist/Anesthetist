## Overview

Legrand et al. (2022) present a critical challenge to decades of assumptions about the heart-evoked potential (HEP) as a reliable biomarker of interoceptive accuracy. Published in *Cortex*, this paper demonstrates that HEP amplitude does not reliably predict behavioral interoceptive accuracy, and that cardiac field artifacts represent a major confound in many prior studies using EEG-based HEP measurements.

## Key Findings

1. HEP amplitude does NOT reliably predict behavioral interoceptive accuracy in controlled testing
2. Challenges decades of assumptions linking HEP amplitude to interoceptive accuracy
3. Cardiac field artifact is a major confound in many prior HEP studies, inflating apparent effects
4. HEP may reflect attentional or arousal processes rather than interoceptive accuracy per se
5. The field needs to reconsider HEP as a standalone interoceptive biomarker and adopt more rigorous artifact correction

## Connection to Somnistics

Important caveat for SRL's biomarker strategy: if SRL ever uses EEG/HEP as an interoceptive biomarker, it cannot claim HEP = interoceptive accuracy. The emerging HEP-HRV coupling approach (circa 2025) may be more defensible than HEP amplitude alone. This reinforces SRL's current focus on HRV-based metrics (RMSSD, coherence) rather than EEG-derived interoceptive measures.

## Summary

Study identifying a wearable-derived digital phenotype of high allostatic load during 10 weeks of military training using Garmin devices.

## Key Findings

- Digital phenotype of high allostatic load identified: chronically elevated and variable cardiometabolic activity combined with blunted night-to-night sleeping HR variability
- Every 1-SD increase in daytime HR flux increased high allostatic load risk by 5-90%
- Reduced sleeping HR variability was independently predictive of high allostatic load
- Garmin consumer wearable data captured meaningful physiological signatures over 10 weeks
- Demonstrates that cumulative stress burden can be tracked passively through wearables

## Relevance to SRL

Establishes that allostatic load — the accumulation of stress that underlies burnout — has a detectable digital phenotype visible in consumer wearable data. The blunted sleeping HRV finding is particularly relevant: it suggests a passive, non-intrusive early warning system for clinician durability decline. Supports the case for longitudinal HRV monitoring as a burnout prevention tool.

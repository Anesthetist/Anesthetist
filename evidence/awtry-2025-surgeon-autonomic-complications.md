## Summary

Landmark prospective multicenter study examining the relationship between intraoperative surgeon autonomic state and postoperative patient outcomes. Led by Awtry J (first author) with Duclos A (senior author) from the Lyon/INSERM team.

## Key Findings

- **Sample:** 793 procedures, 38 surgeons, 4 hospitals
- **Method:** Continuous HRV monitoring via wearable sensors during surgery
- **Primary finding:** Increased surgeon sympathovagal balance (LF:HF ratio) during the **first 5 minutes** of a procedure was associated with **reduced** major surgical complications (AOR 0.63). Higher sympathovagal balance at incision = fewer complications.
- **Correct interpretation:** This represents *optimal autonomic activation* — beneficial arousal, not harmful stress. The right autonomic state (ventral vagal "poised composure") at procedure start predicts better patient outcomes.
- **Implication:** Surgeons who enter the OR in an optimally activated autonomic state — not flat, not panicked, but poised — have patients with fewer major complications, shorter ICU stays, and lower mortality.

## SRL Relevance

This is THE landmark paper proving autonomic state → patient outcomes. Critically, it shows that *optimal activation* (not relaxation) is the goal. It validates the Somnistics thesis that clinician autonomic regulation is a patient safety intervention, not merely a wellness initiative. The 5-minute window aligns precisely with the Gap Moment Training pre-operative protocol. The finding supports training clinicians to achieve "poised composure" — the ventral vagal state of calm readiness — rather than simply reducing stress.

# Damasio 1999 — The Feeling of What Happens

## Citation

Damasio, A. R. (1999). *The Feeling of What Happens: Body and Emotion in the Making of Consciousness.* Harcourt Brace. ISBN: 978-0156010757.

## Summary

Extends the somatic marker hypothesis (see [[damasio-1996-somatic-marker]]) into a full theory of consciousness. Damasio argues that consciousness arises from the organism's representation of its own body states — that feelings are not epiphenomenal but constitutive of awareness itself. Introduces the distinction between "core consciousness" (immediate, body-grounded awareness) and "extended consciousness" (autobiographical self across time).

## Key Claims

- The body is not merely an input to consciousness but its foundation — "the body is the theatre of the emotions"
- Core consciousness: the feeling of knowing that arises moment-to-moment from body-state representations
- Extended consciousness: autobiographical self built on accumulated core-consciousness episodes
- Emotion precedes feeling; feeling precedes conscious knowing — a temporal hierarchy with direct implications for interoceptive training

- Validates the Anterocept™ progressive domain model: Domain 1 (body awareness) must precede Domain 2 (emotional integration) must precede Domain 3 (integrated awareness)

## Distinct from Damasio 1996

This is a separate work from [[damasio-1996-somatic-marker]] (the original somatic marker hypothesis paper). *The Feeling of What Happens* is a book-length expansion applying the somatic marker framework to the problem of consciousness itself.

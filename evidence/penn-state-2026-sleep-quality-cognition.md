## Citation

Einstein Aging Study investigators (Penn State & Albert Einstein College of Medicine). (2026). Night waking impacts cognitive performance regardless of sleep duration. *Sleep Health*. https://pennstatehealthnews.org/2026/01/night-waking-impacts-cognitive-performance-regardless-of-sleep-duration/

## Key Findings

- Sleep **quality**, not quantity, predicted next-day cognitive processing speed in adults over 70.
- Night waking frequency was the key metric: even adequate total sleep duration did not protect against slowed information processing when sleep fragmentation was present.
- 261 participants from the Einstein Aging Study (Bronx County, NY), all age 70+, wore actigraphy devices for 16 days and completed 6 cognitive assessments per day via smartphone.
- Visual working memory, visuospatial memory, and processing speed were all measurable markers of day-to-day cognitive variation linked to prior night's sleep quality.

## Relevance to SRL

This finding directly supports SomnoAffinity and Clinician Durability frameworks: sleep fragmentation is a silent performance thief that bypasses traditional duration-based metrics. For perioperative clinicians who accept fractured sleep as "normal," this is a high-stakes evidence anchor — quality, not quantity, is the operating variable.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Longitudinal observational (actigraphy + ecological momentary assessment)
- **N:** 261 adults, age 70+
- **Duration:** 16 days
- **Measures:** Wrist actigraphy (sleep fragmentation, duration), smartphone cognitive tasks ×6/day

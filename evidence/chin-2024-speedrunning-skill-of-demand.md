# Speedrunning the Skill of Demand

## Summary

Chin argues understanding customer demand is the foundational business skill, and it can be accelerated through structured ethnographic practice. The core method is the **Sales Safari** framework (Amy Hoy / Alex Hillman) — analyzing customer conversations through five categories: rough notes, pains, worldview, jargon, and recommendations.

## Key Frameworks

### Sales Safari Categories
1. **Rough Notes** — miscellaneous observations
2. **Pains** — customer struggles and frustrations
3. **Worldview** — underlying beliefs and values shaping decisions
4. **Jargon** — domain-specific language customers use
5. **Recommendations** — solutions customers mention

### Core Insight: Worldview > Demographics
Worldview-based segmentation proves more effective than demographic targeting. Observing customers "in the wild" (forums, support calls) provides purer signals than direct interviews.

### The Demand Detachment
"Detach from their product or service and look at the demand-side of the equation — seeing struggles, context, and outcomes" (Bob Moesta, *Learning to Build*)

### Case: Unpredictability vs. Cost
Analyzing a Head of Data whose BigQuery costs appeared expensive ($10K). Deeper analysis revealed the actual pain was **unpredictability**, not cost — a critical distinction that drives different product and marketing strategies.

## SRL Relevance

**Directly applicable to CRNA demand discovery:**
- Randy's 60-day CRNA ethnography (29.6K-member Facebook group) was essentially a Sales Safari
- The 5-category framework maps to SRL's existing ICP research structure
- "Worldview" segmentation explains why identity-driven CRNAs adopt differently than wellness-seeking nurses
- The unpredictability insight parallels SRL's finding: CRNAs don't want "calm" — they want **control** over their autonomic state

**Time investment:** ~1 hour per analysis, 2-3 months (10-15 customer calls) to develop competency. SRL already has this depth from the ethnographic work.

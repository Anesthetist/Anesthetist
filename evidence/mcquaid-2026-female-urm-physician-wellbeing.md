## Summary

Qualitative study exploring the perspectives of female and under-represented minority (URM) physicians on well-being in medicine, identifying identity-specific stressors and systemic barriers.

## Key Findings

- Burnout compounded by identity-based stressors and systemic inequities
- Staffing burden identified as the primary stressor across demographics
- Well-being programs must avoid overgeneralization — one-size-fits-all approaches are insufficient
- Systemic barriers require systemic solutions alongside individual interventions
- Highlights the intersection of structural inequity and clinician durability

## Relevance to SRL

Critical reminder that durability interventions must be designed with equity awareness. The finding that staffing burden is primary (not identity-specific stress alone) suggests that autonomic regulation tools address a universal stressor while acknowledging that the stress load is inequitably distributed. Supports inclusive design of SRL products and avoids the "resilience as individual burden" framing.

## Citation

Wittmann, M. (2011). Moments in time. *Frontiers in Integrative Neuroscience*, 5, 66. [DOI: 10.3389/fnint.2011.00066](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnint.2011.00066)

## Key Findings

Three distinct levels of temporal integration operate on different time scales, each fundamental to behavior and subjective experience:

### Level 1: Functional Moment (~30–50 milliseconds)
- The basic temporal building block of perception
- Below this threshold, events are perceived as simultaneous (co-temporal)
- Above it, temporal order is perceived — succession becomes possible
- Defines the minimum resolution of the perceptual system

### Level 2: Experienced Moment (~2–3 seconds)
- A segmental processing mechanism that creates temporal windows of integration
- Events within this window are bound into a unified perceptual unit — the "psychological present"
- Provides the logistical basis for conscious representation and the experience of **nowness**
- Observed across qualitatively different domains: speech prosody, musical phrasing, movement timing, sensory grouping
- This is the "specious present" — the felt duration of "now"

### Level 3: Mental Presence (~7–30 seconds)
- Enabled by working memory — the maintenance of cognitive operations and emotional feelings
- Creates continuity of experience across time
- Represents the individual's experienced temporal window of **presence**
- Allows planning, anticipation, and emotional sustaining across longer intervals

## Core Insight

These three levels are often conflated in the consciousness literature. Many theories of consciousness reference brief, discrete "functional moments" when the actual substrate of conscious experience is the extended, dynamic "experienced moment." This confusion leads to incomplete models of both time and consciousness.

## Relevance to SRL

**Directly maps to SRL's temporal architecture:**

- **The 60-second NeuroMinute** contains approximately **20 experienced moments** (60s ÷ 3s). Each breath cycle at resonant frequency (~5.5 breaths/min = ~11 seconds/cycle) spans approximately 3–4 experienced moments. The NeuroMinute is a structured sequence of experienced moments.
- **Gap moments** operate at the experienced moment level — the 2–3 second window where conscious awareness can redirect autonomic state. This is not accidental; it maps precisely to Wittmann's temporal integration window.
- **Mental presence** (~7–30s) maps to the working memory span required for a complete breathing technique — enough time to hold the instruction, execute the breath, and register the interoceptive feedback.
- **The Anterocept progression** trains across all three levels: Domain 1 (feet on floor — functional moment awareness), Domain 2 (heartbeat tracking — experienced moment integration), Domain 3 (full-body state awareness — mental presence).

## Methodology

Review of psychophysical and neuropsychological evidence on temporal processing, presented within philosophical frameworks of time experience. Synthesizes data from speech perception, movement science, sensory binding, and clinical populations.

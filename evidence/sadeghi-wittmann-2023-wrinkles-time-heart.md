## Citation

Sadeghi, S., Wittmann, M., & De Rosa, E. (2023). Wrinkles in subsecond time perception are synchronized to the heart. *Psychophysiology*, 60(7), e14270. [DOI: 10.1111/psyp.14270](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.14270)

## Key Findings

- Subsecond time perception is **not smooth** — it has "wrinkles" (fluctuations in temporal accuracy) that are synchronized to the cardiac cycle
- Temporal judgments made during **systole** (heart contraction, baroreceptor firing) differ from those made during **diastole** (heart relaxation)
- The heartbeat creates a rhythmic modulation of temporal processing — each cardiac cycle imposes a temporal structure on perception
- This demonstrates that time perception is literally pulsatile, not continuous — it beats with the heart

## Relevance to SRL

**The heartbeat is a temporal metronome for perception:**

- **Resonant breathing** at ~5.5 breaths/min synchronizes with cardiac rhythms, which by this paper's findings creates a coherent temporal processing pattern — breathing rate literally structures the temporal resolution of perception
- **RMSSD/HRV** reflects the variability of these cardiac cycles. Higher HRV = more dynamic range in temporal processing. Lower HRV (stress) = more rigid, compressed temporal experience.
- **Clinical implications:** Under cardiac stress (high HR, low HRV), the "wrinkles" in time become less variable — perception becomes temporally flat. This maps to the clinician's report of "tunnel vision" during emergencies — not just spatial narrowing but temporal narrowing.
- **Cardiac-anchored breathing** ([[cardiac-anchored-breathing]]) may work partly by regularizing the cardiac-temporal coupling, giving the clinician more predictable temporal processing.

## Methodology

Temporal discrimination tasks presented at different phases of the cardiac cycle (systole vs. diastole). EEG and ECG recorded simultaneously to map temporal judgments onto cardiac phase.

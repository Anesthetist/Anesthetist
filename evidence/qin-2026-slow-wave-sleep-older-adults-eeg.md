## Citation

Qin, S., Ong, J. L., Wong, K. F., et al. (2026). Association between slow-wave activity from multi-night at-home wireless EEG records and cognitive performance in older adults. *SLEEP*, 49(3), zsaf329. https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsaf329

## Key Findings

- Multi-night at-home wireless EEG is feasible in older adults — first study to demonstrate this, overcoming the assumption that older populations struggle with wearable EEG devices.
- Slow-wave activity (SWA) from naturalistic, home-based sleep recordings is associated with cognitive performance in older adults, extending lab-based findings to real-world settings.
- Provides a foundation for population-scale monitoring of sleep-dependent cognitive health using consumer-grade wearable EEG.
- Published in SLEEP journal, March 2026 (Vol. 49, Issue 3).

## Relevance to SRL

Directly supports SRL's SomnoAffinity concept — the idea that sleep quality and architecture are windows into and levers for cognitive and emotional performance. The shift to at-home, multi-night EEG aligns with SRL's interest in wearable-based physiological monitoring and the feasibility of capturing sleep architecture data outside clinical settings, making personalized sleep optimization scalable.

## Methodology

- Multi-night at-home wireless EEG study in older adults.
- Examined association between slow-wave activity and cognitive performance.
- Naturalistic design; participants wore EEG devices in their own homes across multiple nights.
- Published: SLEEP, Vol. 49, Issue 3, March 2026.

## Overview

A four-step awareness exercise that trains the capacity to distinguish reality from narrative overlays ("stories"). The exercise moves from somatic/emotional detection through narrative deconstruction to identity-level awareness, creating a full-stack self-observation protocol.

## Exercise Structure

### Step 1: Emotional Awareness
Identify a situation that generates anger, upset, insecurity, guilt, or defensiveness. Locate where this feeling shows up in the body. This is the entry point — somatic signal as doorway to story detection.

### Step 2: Story Awareness
Identify the judgments attached to the emotions using diagnostic sentence stems: "It's wrong because...", "I need to rescue them because...", "It's their fault because...", "It's not my fault because...", "I need them to change because..."

Then examine the narrative structure for:
- **Role assignment**: Who is the victim? The perpetrator? The rescuer? (Karpman Drama Triangle)
- **Side-taking**: Is your mind dividing people into us vs. them?
- **All-or-nothing thinking**: Are shades of grey being eliminated? Do the stakes feel existential?
- **Confirmation bias**: Is your mind focusing on evidence that justifies your position while ignoring disconfirming evidence?

### Step 3: Core Belief Awareness
If the story is NOT true, which core beliefs about yourself would be threatened? Reference grid includes: "I am good / We are good," "I am valuable," and related identity-level beliefs. This step reveals that stories persist because they protect identity structures.

### Step 4: Identification Awareness
Map which stage of identification the story is anchored in — five stages from shadow to authentic:
- **Taking** → Power Over / Dominance and Control
- **Pleasing** → Judgment / Superiority and Other Importance
- **Achieving** → Achievement and Self Importance
- **Giving** → Growth and Contribution / Providing Value
- **Receiving** → Faith in Reality / Integrity and Alignment

An Evaluation ↔ Judgment polarity arrow shows the axis between conscious evaluation (green) and reactive judgment (red).

### Core Insight

The key distinction is between reality (immovable, solid) and story (balloon-like, shape-shifting). Stories obscure, distort, and separate us from reality. The method for detecting stories is somatic — watch for feelings of anger, insecurity, or defensiveness, then trace the judgments attached to those feelings.

## Somnistic Relevance

### Body-First Awareness Pathway

Step 1's instruction to locate the feeling in the body before examining the narrative is identical to the SRL structured interoception training sequence. The exercise establishes the somatic signal as the primary data source, with cognitive content as secondary. This inverts the conventional approach (analyze thoughts first, feelings second) and aligns with the SRL thesis that interoceptive signals contain clinical information that precedes conscious cognition.

### Meta-Awareness as Nondual Clinical Awareness

The four-step progression (emotion → story → core belief → identification) trains the same layered meta-awareness that SRL calls nondual clinical awareness. The clinician who can observe their own story-making process in real-time — noticing when they're assigning roles (victim/perpetrator/rescuer), taking sides, or engaged in all-or-nothing thinking — has access to a quality of awareness that transcends the story itself. This is the "witness" capacity that enables clinical equanimity under pressure.

### Identification Stages as Autonomic Postures

The five identification stages (Taking → Pleasing → Achieving → Giving → Receiving) map to progressively more ventral vagal autonomic postures. "Taking" (power over, dominance) is sympathetically-driven; "Pleasing" (judgment, other-importance) is fawn response; "Receiving" (faith in reality, integrity) is ventral vagal safety. The exercise thus functions as an autonomic self-assessment tool masked as a narrative awareness exercise.

### Diagnostic-Therapeutic Unity

The act of spotting a story IS the intervention. The moment you can observe that your mind is creating a narrative — assigning roles, choosing sides, eliminating grey areas — the story loses its grip. The assessment and the treatment are the same gesture. This is the Whetten principle Randy identified as central to SRL methodology.

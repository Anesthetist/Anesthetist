# Heartbeat-Evoked Potentials as a Neural Marker of Meditative Depth (Nath et al. 2025)

## Summary

This study establishes heartbeat-evoked potentials (HEPs) — cortical responses time-locked to the heartbeat — as a neural marker of meditative depth. HEPs reflect how strongly the brain registers and processes cardiac signals, providing an objective electrophysiological measure of interoceptive processing. During deeper meditative states, HEP amplitude increases, indicating enhanced body-brain coupling. This is directly relevant to SRL because HEPs offer a measurable, real-time biomarker for the interoceptive awareness that Anterocept and Pausality aim to develop. Unlike subjective self-report, HEPs provide an objective index of whether a user is actually developing deeper body awareness through practice.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- Heartbeat-evoked potentials reflect the brain's cortical processing of cardiac signals
- HEP amplitude increases with meditative depth, tracking interoceptive engagement
- HEPs provide an objective neural marker independent of self-report
- The measure captures body-brain coupling — the core mechanism of interoceptive training
- HEPs could serve as a biomarker for tracking progress in breath-based interventions
- The technique is EEG-compatible, making it scalable beyond laboratory settings
- Meditative depth and interoceptive depth covary, supporting the body-first meditation model

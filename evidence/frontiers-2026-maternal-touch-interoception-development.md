## Citation

Frontiers in Neuroscience (2026). Development of interoception: The critical role of maternal affective touch in early attachment. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*.

## Key Findings

- Maternal affective touch (MAT) provides the foundational context for integrating interoceptive signals within the CNS during infancy
- Early relational experiences and embodied interactions lay the groundwork for lifelong interoceptive awareness
- The paper proposes that interoceptive signals are "learned" to be accurately sensed, perceived, and interpreted through attachment relationships
- This positions co-regulation as the developmental precursor to self-regulation of internal states

## Relevance to SRL

Directly supports SRL's co-regulation concept and provides developmental grounding for interoceptive literacy. Suggests that interoceptive training in adults may need to account for early attachment patterns, and that co-regulation is not just a social phenomenon but a biological prerequisite for interoceptive accuracy.

## Methodology

Theoretical/review paper synthesizing developmental neuroscience, attachment theory, and interoception research. Published January 2026 in Frontiers in Neuroscience.

# Anesthesia Second-Victim Study (2024)

## Summary

A 2024 cross-sectional survey of anesthesia providers quantifying the second-victim burden — the psychological toll on clinicians involved in adverse patient events. Headline figures:

- **18%** of anesthesiologists had lingering symptoms one month after an adverse event
- **12%** of CRNAs had lingering symptoms one month after an adverse event
- **19%** of anesthesiologists reported never fully recovering
- **17%** of CRNAs reported never fully recovering
- Providers with second-victim symptoms were roughly **2× more likely** to report burnout and intent to leave

These numbers are important because they quantify — at the specialty level — the chronic carryover of adverse-event distress in anesthesia. Anesthesia is an especially second-victim-prone specialty because adverse events are immediate, visible, and often involve direct hand-on-the-patient sequence of actions.

## Key Claims Relevant to SRL

- The second-victim burden in anesthesia is not a rare or episodic phenomenon. It is structural, quantifiable, and bimodally distributed (most recover; a meaningful subset never do).
- Support structures for CRNAs following adverse events remain ad hoc. There is no standardized CRNA second-victim debriefing protocol at the AANA level as of 2026.
- This is one of the few concrete CRNA-specific numbers we have on interior ecology — most of the interoception/breath/vagal-tone literature has no CRNA-specific cohort.
- Quantitatively links adverse events → lingering symptoms → burnout/attrition, which is the clinical-durability chain SRL work targets.

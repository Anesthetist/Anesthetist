# Fathers Experiencing Parental Burnout (Depleted Dad Syndrome)

## Audience Definition

Fathers — particularly single, widowed, divorced, or separated dads — experiencing parental burnout characterized by overwhelming exhaustion, emotional distancing from children, and loss of accomplishment in the parental role. The 42% prevalence figure traces to an Ohio State University study; men express PB through **anger and frustration** rather than the emotional exhaustion more common in mothers.

## The Interoceptive Suppression Connection

The father who "doesn't notice until he blows up at his kid" is experiencing the same mechanism as the CRNA who "doesn't notice until hour 8 of the shift." Chronic parenting overload produces interoceptive suppression — the father's ability to detect his own physiological state degrades as an adaptive response to sustained stress. The anger expression is a sympathetic arousal signature (not dorsal vagal collapse like maternal exhaustion).

**Intervention design implication**: The prehabilitative skill for fathers isn't "learn to rest" — it's "learn to detect the sympathetic ramp before it becomes anger, and redirect it in a 60-second window." That's Error Reversal Loop and Stability Snap, not Radiant Exhale.

**Key diagnostic question for user interviews**: "When was the last time you noticed you were stressed — not when someone told you, but when you actually felt it in your body?" If the answer is "I don't really notice until I blow up" or "I just kind of go numb" — that's interoceptive suppression in the wild.

# Emergency Department Nurse

## Profile

ED nurses operate in one of the highest-frequency stress environments in healthcare. Unlike CRNAs who manage planned state transitions, ED nurses face unpredictable surges -- trauma activations, psychiatric holds, pediatric emergencies -- with minimal recovery time between patients. Moral injury and compassion fatigue are endemic. The gap moments for ED nurses are shorter and less predictable than in anesthesia, making the NeuroMinute format especially valuable: a 60-second reset that can be performed between patients, during triage lulls, or at shift change. This audience needs evidence but is less research-literate than CRNAs; professional-grade language with clear practical framing works best.

## Language Notes

Frame interventions around "rapid reset" and "between-patient recovery." Acknowledge moral injury directly -- this audience knows the weight of what they carry and will disengage from anything that trivializes it. Avoid overly academic language but do not patronize; ED nurses are sharp, fast-thinking professionals. Use "stress exposure" rather than "stress management." Emphasize that the intervention fits inside existing workflow without requiring schedule changes or extra time. Shift-compatible means it works in 60 seconds, standing up, in a hallway.
